1. **Auspiced Arrangement with Council**  
   The grant program expressly permits organisations that are not themselves incorporated or registered charities, provided they operate under the auspices of a recognised public entity such as a local council. As long as the council’s insurance and financial oversight cover the activities you propose, you remain eligible to apply. Please ensure that the formal agreement (e.g., a memorandum of understanding or council endorsement letter) is attached to your submission.

2. **Grant Amount**  
   For the current round, the minimum award is **$5,000** and the maximum is **$25,000** per project. The exact figure you receive will be determined by the scope of work, the demonstrated need, and the overall budget you submit. Projects that combine multiple eligible components (e.g., water storage and shade structures) are viewed favorably and may approach the upper limit, assuming a well‑justified cost breakdown.

3. **Eligible Infrastructure Costs**  
   Infrastructure that directly enhances climate‑related resilience is eligible, including rainwater harvesting systems, shade structures, solar‑powered pumps, and related civil works. The costs must be *directly attributable* to the project and supported by quotations, invoices, or contracts. Indirect expenses such as staff salaries are allowable only when they are integral to project delivery and clearly itemised in the budget.

4. **Required Supporting Documentation**  
   To substantiate your application, please include the following:  
   - A signed **auspice agreement** or letter of support from the council confirming insurance coverage and oversight.

   - **Quotes or tenders** from suppliers/contractors for each major component.  
   - A **risk assessment** and **resilience rationale** explaining how the proposed works will mitigate climate‑related impacts on the garden.  
   - Evidence of **community benefit**, such as letters of endorsement from local residents, schools, or other stakeholders.  
   - Proof of **financial capacity** (e.g., recent financial statements or council’s audited accounts) to demonstrate that the project can be sustainably managed.
