**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines state that eligible applicants include not only incorporated non‑profit entities and registered charities, but also unincorporated community groups that operate under the formal auspices of a local government body. When a council holds the requisite insurances, holds any necessary licences, and assumes legal responsibility for the group’s activities, the group is considered an eligible applicant. In such cases, the council’s involvement is documented in a formal auspice agreement, which should be attached to the application. Please ensure that your council can provide a written statement confirming that it will act as the fiscal sponsor and retain the relevant public liability and professional indemnity insurances for the duration of the project.

**Grant amount – minimum and maximum**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, funding levels are determined by the scope, impact, and budget completeness of each proposal. Historically, awards have ranged from approximately $5,000 to $50,000, but we have supported projects with budgets well beyond that ceiling when the justification and community benefit are compelling. Your projected costs for rainwater tanks and a shade structure appear modest relative to these benchmarks, suggesting that a grant in the $10,000–$20,000 range would be realistic, provided the budget is well justified and fully costed.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes—capital infrastructure that enhances the sustainability and resilience of community facilities is expressly eligible. Rainwater harvesting systems, permeable paving, solar‑powered lighting, and weather‑resilient shade structures all fall under the “environmental sustainability and climate adaptation” category of our funding criteria. To demonstrate eligibility, please include detailed specifications (e.g., tank capacity, material, durability ratings), cost breakdowns, and evidence of how each item directly supports continuous operation during hot summer periods.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
We recommend assembling the following documents to present a complete application:

1. **Project Description Form** – a concise narrative (maximum 2 pages) outlining the problem, solution, expected outcomes, and timeline.  
2. **Budget Table** – itemised costs with unit prices, quantities, and any contingency. Include installation, permits, and ongoing maintenance where applicable.  
3. **Auspice Agreement** – a signed letter from your council confirming its role as fiscal sponsor, its insurance coverage, and its endorsement of the project.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page overview of your group, its volunteer composition, and any previous community projects.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and balance sheet) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
6. **Letters of Support** – at least two letters from community stakeholders (e.g., local businesses, schools, or resident groups) affirming the need for the project and willingness to assist post‑implementation.  
7. **Risk Assessment and Mitigation Plan** – a brief outline of potential safety, environmental, or operational risks and how they will be managed throughout the project lifecycle.  
8. **Proof of Insurance** – if your council can provide a copy of the public liability policy that names the group as an insured party, please include it.
