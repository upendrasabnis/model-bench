## 1. Foundations of Grant Writing  

### 1.1 Understanding the Grant Landscape  
- **Public vs. Private**: Public grants (federal, state) have strict eligibility criteria and competitive application cycles. Private foundations often have specific thematic priorities and flexible timelines.  
- **Corporate Giving**: Corporate foundations may focus on community impact or employee volunteer programs.  
- **Community Foundations**: Localized grantmakers often support projects that directly benefit the region.  

### 1.2 Core Grant Writing Principles  
1. **Alignment** – Your project must align with the funder’s mission and priorities.  
2. **Clarity** – Use plain language; avoid jargon unless the audience expects it.  
3. **Evidence** – Anchor claims in data, research, or proven outcomes.  
4. **Feasibility** – Demonstrate realistic timelines, budgets, and staffing.  
5. **Impact** – Show how the project creates measurable change.  

### 1.3 Building a Grant‑Writing Team  
| Role | Key Responsibilities | Typical Personnel | Tips |
|------|----------------------|-------------------|------|
| **Grant Lead** | Oversees strategy, ensures alignment | Program Director | Deep knowledge of funder |
| **Program Officer** | Provides subject‑matter expertise | Project Manager | Builds narrative |
| **Finance Officer** | Prepares budgets, tracks costs | CFO/Accountant | Validates cost assumptions |
| **Evaluation Specialist** | Designs outcome measures | Data Analyst | Ensures data integrity |
| **Copy‑Editor** | Polishes language, edits | Professional Editor | Maintains style guide |
| **Legal/Compliance** | Reviews policies, ensures compliance | Compliance Officer | Avoids legal pitfalls |

## 3. The Needs Statement  

### 3.1 Defining the Issue Clearly  
- **Problem Statement**: “X% of low‑income youth in City Z experience unemployment.”  
- **Scope**: Identify geographic, demographic, and temporal boundaries.  

### 3.2 Using Data & Storytelling  
- **Quantitative Data**: Census data, local health statistics.  
- **Qualitative Data**: Interviews, focus groups.  
- **Narrative Hook**: “When 12‑year‑old Maya couldn't afford a school lunch, she missed a crucial learning day…”  

### 3.3 Linking Need to Your Mission  
- **Mission Statement**: “Empower under‑served communities through education.”  
- **Bridge**: Show how the need directly feeds into your mission’s core.  

### 3.4 Sample Needs Statements  
> *“In City X, 45% of adults lack basic digital skills, hindering employment prospects. Our program will provide digital literacy workshops to bridge this gap, aligning with our mission to promote economic empowerment.”*

## 4. Program Narrative & Objectives  

### 4.1 Writing a Clear, Concise Narrative  
1. **Hook**: Start with a compelling fact or anecdote.  
2. **Problem**: Expand on the needs statement.  
3. **Solution**: Describe your program’s approach.  
4. **Benefits**: Outline expected outcomes.  

### 4.2 SMART Objectives Framework  
- **Specific**: “Train 200 adults in basic coding.”  
- **Measurable**: “Increase coding proficiency by 30%.”  
- **Achievable**: “Use existing facility and volunteers.”  
- **Relevant**: “Aligns with workforce development goals.”  
- **Time‑bound**: “Within 12 months.”  

### 4.3 Outcome Mapping  
| Inputs | Activities | Outputs | Outcomes | Impact |  
|--------|------------|---------|----------|--------|  

- **Inputs**: Staff, venue, curriculum.  
- **Activities**: 8 weekly workshops.  
- **Outputs**: 200 participants, 200 certificates.  
- **Outcomes**: 60% secure part‑time jobs.  
- **Impact**: Reduced local unemployment by 5%.  

### 4.4 Sample Program Narrative  

> *“Our Digital Futures program offers a 12‑week, hands‑on coding curriculum tailored to adults with limited prior exposure. Through interactive labs, mentorship, and job placement support, we aim to equip participants with marketable skills that translate into tangible employment opportunities.”*

## 5. Logic Models & Results Frameworks  

### 5.1 Why Logic Models Matter  
- **Visualization**: Connects resources to outcomes.  
- **Communication**: Helps stakeholders understand program flow.  
- **Evaluation**: Provides a basis for data collection.  

### 5.2 Building a Logic Model – Step‑by‑Step  
1. **Identifyाउने**: List all inputs.  
2. **Define Activities**: Map out what will happen.  
3. **Determine Outputs**: Immediate products or services.  
4. **Set Outcomes**: Short, medium, long‑term changes.  
5. **Articulate Impact**: Broader societal effect.  

### 5.3 Integrating Theory of Change  
- **Assumptions**: “Participants will attend all sessions.”  
- **Risks**: Transportation barriers, digital device availability.  
- **Mitigation Strategies**: Provide shuttle service, loaner laptops.

## 6. Budget Development  

### 6.1 Types of Budgets  
- **Capital Budget**: Infrastructure, equipment.  
- **Operational Budget**: Personnel, supplies, overhead.  
- **Program Budget**: Direct costs tied to program activities.  

### 6.2 Aligning Budget with Narrative  
- **Consistency**: Numbers must support claims.  
- **Zero‑Based**: Justify each line item.  

### 6.3 Salary & Fringe Calculations  
- **Gross Salary**: Base wage.  
- **Fringe**: Health, retirement, payroll taxes (typically 25–30% of gross).  

### 6.4 Indirect Cost (Overhead) Negotiation  
1. **Ask**: “What is your indirect cost rate?”  
2. **Negotiate**: Use comparable institutions or negotiate a fixed fee.  
3. **Document**: Include in the budget narrative.  

### 6.5 Budget Narrative Checklist  
- **Personnel**: Roles, hours, rates.  
- **Materials & Supplies**: Specific items, quantities, unit costs.  
- **Equipment**: Depreciationติด.  
- **Travel**: Distances, purpose, per diem.  
- **Indirect Costs**: Rate, calculation method.  
- **Contingency**: Usually 5–10% of total.

## 8. Editing & Proofreading  

### 8.1 Style & Tone Guidelines  
- **Voice**: Active, confident.  
- **Tone**: Professional yet accessible.  
- **Formatting**: Consistent font, margins, heading hierarchy.  

### 8.2 Peer Review Process  
1. **First Draft**: Write without editing.  
2. **Internal Review**: Team reads for content accuracy.  
3. **External Review χ**: Non‑staff reads for clarity.  

### 8.3 Common Grammatical Pitfalls  
- **Comma splices**.  
- **Misused “their” vs. “there”**.  
- **Passive voice**.  

### 8.4 Final Checklist  

1. **All sections present**.  
2. **Funder’s specifications met**.  
3. **Budget aligns with narrative**.  
4. **Proofreading completed**.  
5. **Submission format verified**.
