## 1. Introduction  

Grant writing is both an art and a science.  
Nonprofits that consistently win funding share a disciplined process: **research → relationship → narrative → budget → compliance → reporting**.  
This article walks you through each phase, provides concrete examples, and ends with printable checklists you can paste into your project management tool.

## 2. Understanding the Grant Landscape  

| Grant Type | Typical Funder | Average Award | Decision Cycle | Key Characteristics |
|------------|----------------|--------------|----------------|----------------------|
| Federal (e.g., NIH, NSF, HUD) | Government agencies | $100K–$5M+ | 6–12 months | Highly regulated, strict compliance, peer review |
| State/Local | State agencies, city councils | $10K–$500K | 3–6 months | Often tied to policy priorities, community impact |
| Private Foundations | Family, corporate, community foundations | $5K–$2M | 2–4 months | Mission‑driven, flexible, relationship‑focused |
| Corporate Giving / CSR | Businesses | $1K–$250K | 1–3 months | Brand alignment, employee engagement |
| Crowdfunding / Individual Donors | Public platforms | Variable | Ongoing | Story‑centric, low barrier, high volume |

**Takeaway:** Match your program’s scale, timeline, and mission to the right funder type before you invest hours in a proposal.

## 3. Researching Funders  

### 3.1 Core Research Steps  

1. **Define your funding need** – amount, purpose, geography, population.  
2. **Create a funder database** – spreadsheet or CRM (e.g., Airtable, Salesforce).  
3. **Use multiple sources** – Foundation Directory Online, GrantStation, GuideStar, IRS 990‑PF, funder websites, newsletters, LinkedIn.  
4. **Track key data points** (see checklist below).

| Funder Name | Identification |
| Mission Statement | Alignment test |
| Geographic Focus | Eligibility |

| Application Deadline | Timeline |
| Submission Format (online, PDF, portal) | Prep |
| Review Criteria (explicit or inferred) | Tailoring |
| Past Grantees (list 3–5) | Benchmark |
| Contact Person (Program Officer) | Relationship |
| Reporting Requirements | Post‑award load |
| Restrictions (no‑capital, no‑overhead, etc.) | Compliance |

## 4. Building Relationships with Funders  

### 4.1 Why Relationships Matter  

- **Insider insight** on unwritten priorities.  
- **Feedback loops** – you can ask “Would a pilot of X be competitive?”  
- **Higher success rates** – foundations often fund known partners.  

### 4.2 Relationship‑Building Tactics  

| Tactic | Frequency | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Introductory email (1‑page) | Once per funder | “Dear Maria, I admire HFF’s work on maternal health…” |
| Request a 15‑min “learning call” | 1–2 months before deadline | “Could we discuss your 2025 priorities?” |
| Attend funder webinars / conferences | As offered | “HFF’s Annual Grantee Convening – June 12.” |
| Share impact updates (even if unfunded) | Quarterly | “Our prenatal telehealth pilot reduced no‑show rates 22%.” |
| Invite to site visit / program demo | When feasible | “We’d love you to tour our new community kitchen.” |
| Thank‑you note after any interaction | Every time | Hand‑signed card or personalized email. |

### 4.3 Sample Introductory Email  

```
Subject: Exploring Alignment – Healthy Futures Foundation & [Org] Maternal Health Initiative

Dear Maria,

I’ve followed Healthy Futures Foundation’s recent grants to Midwest Moms Clinic and Urban Youth Wellness and am inspired by your focus on health equity for low‑income families.

[Org] runs a community‑based prenatal care coordination program serving 1,200 women annually in Cook County. Our 2023 outcomes: 15% increase in early‑prenatal visits, 22% reduction in preterm births among participants.

We are planning a 2‑year expansion to add telehealth navigation and a peer‑support curriculum, budgeted at $130,000. I would welcome a brief call to learn whether this aligns with HFF’s 2025 priorities.

Thank you for your consideration.

Warm regards,
[Name], Executive Director
[Phone] | [Email] | [Website]
```

### 4.4 Relationship Checklist  

- [ ] Identify 1–2 program officers per High‑Fit funder.  
- [ ] Send personalized intro email (template above).  
- [ ] Request a 15‑min learning call (schedule within 2 weeks).  
- [ ] Attend at least one funder event per quarter.  
- [ ] Send quarterly impact snapshot (1‑page PDF).  
- [ ] Log every interaction in CRM (date, notes, next step).  
- [ ] Send thank‑you note within 24 hrs of any meeting.

## 5. Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement  

### 5.1 Anatomy of a Needs Statement  

| Component | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Problem Definition** | Clear, data‑driven description of the issue. | “In Cook County, 38% of pregnant women living below 150% FPL receive no prenatal care in the first trimester (IL Dept. of Public Health, 2023).” |
| **Target Population** | Who is affected, with demographics. | “Low‑income, Medicaid‑eligible women ages 15‑44, predominantly Black and Latina.” |
| **Geographic Scope** | Specific neighborhoods, zip codes, or regions. | “Zip codes 60609, 60612, 60615 – areas with highest preterm birth rates.” |
| **Evidence of Gap** | What existing services miss. | “Only 2 community health centers offer evening prenatal visits; waitlists average 6 weeks.” |
| **Consequences** | Human, economic, systemic impacts if unaddressed. | “Preterm births cost $26K per infant in NICU care; long‑term developmental delays increase special‑education costs.” |
| **Urgency / Timeliness** | Why now? | “State Medicaid expansion ends Dec 2025; window to embed navigation before coverage shifts.” |
| **Alignment with Funder** | Explicit link to funder priorities. | “Directly addresses HFF’s maternal‑child health and health‑literacy priorities.” |

### 5.2 Writing Tips  

- **Lead with a striking statistic** (first sentence).  
- **Use “we” language** to show organizational ownership.  
- **Cite sources** in parentheses; keep a master reference list.  
- **Keep it ≤ 500 words** (most funders limit to 1–2 pages).  
- **Avoid jargon** – write for an informed layperson.  

### 5.3 Example Needs Statement (≈350 words)  

> **Need Statement – Prenatal Navigation Expansion**  
>   
> In Cook County, 38 % of pregnant women living below 150 % of the federal poverty level (FPL) receive no prenatal care in the first trimester (Illinois Department of Public Health, 2023). The disparity is starkest in zip codes 60609, 60612, and 60615, where preterm birth rates exceed 14 %—nearly double the national average. These communities are predominantly Black (62 %) and Latina (28 %), populations historically underserved by the health system.  
>   
> Existing safety‑net providers—two federally qualified health centers (FQHCs) and three hospital‑based clinics—offer limited evening and weekend appointments. Current waitlists for first‑trimester visits average six weeks, pushing many women into the second trimester before they see a provider. Moreover, only 12 % of eligible women are enrolled in the state’s Medicaid‑managed care prenatal navigation program, largely due to lack of awareness and language barriers.  
>   
> The consequences are profound. Each preterm birth incurs an average $26,000 in neonatal intensive care costs (March of Dimes, 2022) and raises the risk of lifelong developmental delays, increasing special‑education expenditures by an estimated $12,000 per child. Families experience lost wages, transportation hardship, and emotional stress.  
>   
> The urgency is heightened by the upcoming expiration of Illinois’ Medicaid expansion waiver on December 31, 2025. Without a robust navigation infrastructure now, thousands of women will lose the care coordination that bridges them to timely prenatal services.  
>   
> Our proposed **Prenatal Navigation Expansion** directly addresses Healthy Futures Foundation’s priorities of maternal‑child health, health literacy, and equity. By embedding community health workers (CHWs) into existing FQHCs and launching a multilingual telehealth platform, we will reduce first‑trimester access gaps by 30 % within two years, serving an additional 1,200 women annually.  

### 5.4 Needs Statement Checklist  

- [ ] Opening statistic (source cited).  
- [ ] Target population defined (demographics, geography).  
- [ ] Gap in current services described with data.  
- [ ] Consequences quantified (cost, health, social).  
- [ ] Urgency/timeliness explained.  
- [ ] Direct alignment to funder priorities stated.  
- [ ] Word count ≤ 500 (or funder limit).  
- [ ] All citations compiled in reference list.

## 6. Designing the Program – Logic Models & Theory of Change  

### 6.1 Theory of Change (ToC) vs. Logic Model  

| Aspect | Theory of Change | Logic Model |
|--------|------------------|------------|
| **Purpose** | Narrative explanation of *how* and *why* change happens. | Visual matrix linking resources → activities → outputs → outcomes → impact. |
| **Audience** | Internal strategy, funder narrative. | Grant applications, monitoring dashboards. |
| **Detail Level** | High‑level pathways, assumptions, context. | Specific indicators, targets, timelines. |

**Best practice:** Develop a ToC first (story), then translate into a logic model (table).  

### 6.2 Building a Theory of Change – Step‑by‑Step  

1. **Long‑Term Impact** – “Reduced preterm birth rate in target zip codes from 14 % to 10 % by 2028.”  
2. **Intermediate Outcomes** – “Increased first‑trimester prenatal visit adherence by 30 %.”  
3. **Short‑Term Outcomes** – “Improved health‑literacy scores among participants; increased CHW‑client contacts.”  
4. **Outputs** – “1,200 women enrolled; 2,400 CHW home visits; 1,200 telehealth sessions.”  
5. **Activities** – “Recruit/train 6 CHWs; develop multilingual curriculum; integrate EHR referral workflow.”  
6. **Inputs** – “$130K grant; 0.5 FTE program manager; 6 CHWs (0.75 FTE each); telehealth platform license.”  
7. **Assumptions** – “Medicaid reimbursement for CHW services remains stable; community trust in CHWs persists.”  
8. **External Factors** – “State policy changes; economic downturn affecting transportation.”  

### 6.3 Logic Model Template (One‑Page)  

| **Inputs** | **Activities** | **Outputs** | **Short‑Term Outcomes** | **Intermediate Outcomes** | **Long‑Term Impact** |
|------------|----------------|------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|----------------------|
| $130K grant | Recruit/train 6 CHWs | 6 CHWs certified | CHWs demonstrate competency (pre/post test ≥85 %) | 30 % increase in first‑trimester visits | Preterm birth rate ↓ 4 pp |
| 0.5 FTE PM | Develop curriculum (ENG/SPA/ARA) | 3 language modules | Participants score ≥80 % on health‑literacy quiz | 20 % reduction in missed appointments | NICU cost savings $3.1M |
| 6 CHWs (0.75 FTE) | Conduct home visits (2 per client) | 2,400 visits | Clients report ↑ confidence navigating system | 15 % ↑ Medicaid enrollment continuity | Health equity gap narrowed |
| Telehealth platform | Launch virtual navigation | 1,200 sessions | 90 % session completion rate | 25 % ↑ telehealth utilization | Sustainable model replicated |

### 6.4 Example: Translating ToC to Logic Model for the Prenatal Navigation Expansion  

| Inputs | Activities | Outputs | Short‑Term Outcomes | Intermediate Outcomes | Long‑Term Impact |
|--------|------------|---------|---------------------|-----------------------|------------------|
| $130,000 (2‑yr) | Hire & train 6 CHWs (40 hrs each) | 6 certified CHWs | CHW competency ≥85 % | 30 % more women attend 1st‑trimester visit | Preterm birth ↓ 4 pp |
| 0.5 FTE Program Manager | Develop 3‑language curriculum (ENG, SPA, AR) | 3 curriculum modules | 80 % participants pass literacy quiz | 20 % fewer missed appointments | NICU savings $3.1M |
| 6 CHWs (0.75 FTE) | Conduct 2 home visits per enrollee | 2,400 home visits | Client self‑efficacy ↑ (survey) | 15 % ↑ continuous Medicaid coverage | Equity gap ↓ 10 % |
| Telehealth license ($12K/yr) | Launch virtual navigation portal | 1,200 telehealth sessions | 90 % session completion | 25 % ↑ telehealth use | Model ready for scale |

### 6.5 Logic Model Checklist  

- [ ] Write a one‑paragraph Theory of Change.  
- [ ] Identify **Impact → Outcomes → Outputs → Activities → Inputs**.  
- [ ] List **Assumptions** and **External Factors** (separate column).  
- [ ] Assign **SMART indicators** to each outcome (see Section 7).  
- [ ] Validate with program staff & at least one external reviewer.  
- [ ] Export as a one‑page PDF for grant attachment.

## 7. Writing Clear Goals, Objectives, and Outcomes  

### 7.1 Definitions  

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Goal** | Broad, aspirational statement of ultimate change. | “Improve maternal‑child health equity in Cook County.” |
| **Objective** | Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time‑bound (SMART) step toward goal. | “Enroll 1,200 low‑income pregnant women in prenatal navigation by 12/31/2026.” |
| **Outcome** | Observable change in knowledge, behavior, condition, or status. | “First‑trimester prenatal visit rate increases from 62 % to 80 % among enrollees.” |
| **Indicator** | Quantifiable metric used to track an outcome. | “% of enrollees with a documented first‑trimester visit (EHR).” |
| **Target** | Numeric value you aim to reach by a date. | “80 % by 12/31/2026.” |

### 7.2 SMART Objective Template  

```
[Action verb] + [Target population] + [Specific change] + [Measurable indicator] + [Timeframe]
```

**Example:**  
“Recruit and train **six** community health workers **(CHWs)** to achieve **≥85 % competency** on the prenatal navigation curriculum **by 03/31/2025**.”

### 7.3 Sample Goal/Objective/Outcome Set  

| # | Goal | Objective (SMART) | Outcome | Indicator | Target |
|---|------|-------------------|---------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Reduce preterm births | Enroll 1,200 women in navigation by 12/31/2026 | ↑ First‑trimester visit adherence | % enrollees with 1st‑trimester visit (EHR) | 80 % |
| 2 | Increase health literacy | Deliver 3‑language curriculum to 1,200 participants by 06/30/2025 | ↑ Health‑literacy scores | Mean score on 20‑item quiz (pre/post) | ≥80 % post |
| 3 | Strengthen CHW workforce | Hire & certify 6 CHWs by 03/31/2025 | CHWs competent in navigation | % CHWs passing competency exam | ≥85 % |
| 4 | Leverage telehealth | Conduct 1,200 virtual navigation sessions by 12/31/2026 | ↑ Telehealth utilization | # completed sessions / # scheduled | 90 % completion |

### 7.4 Writing Tips  

- **Limit to 3–5 objectives** per grant (focus).  
- **Use active verbs**: *increase, reduce, achieve, deliver, establish*.  
- **Align each objective** to a logic‑model output/outcome.  
- **Include baseline data** (current %).  
- **Specify data source** (EHR, survey, admin records).  

### 7.5 Objectives Checklist  

- [ ] Each objective is SMART.  
- [ ] Objectives map 1‑to‑1 to logic‑model outcomes.  
- [ ] Baseline & target values are realistic (cite source).  
- [ ] Data source & collection method identified.  
- [ ] Responsible staff assigned.  
- [ ] Timeline with milestones (quarterly).

## 8. Developing an Evaluation Plan  

### 8.1 Evaluation Types  

| Type | Purpose | Typical Methods |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| **Formative (Process)** | Monitor implementation fidelity, reach, quality. | Checklists, attendance logs, focus groups, staff debriefs. |
| **Summative (Outcome/Impact)** | Determine if objectives were met. | Pre/post surveys, EHR extraction, comparative analysis. |
| **Developmental** | Support innovation in complex, evolving programs. | Real‑time dashboards, rapid‑cycle learning cycles. |

### 8.2 Evaluation Framework (CDC’s Framework)  

1. **Engage Stakeholders** – funder, staff, participants, community advisors.  
2. **Describe the Program** – logic model (Section 6).  
3. **Focus the Evaluation Design** – key questions, indicators.  
4. **Gather Credible Evidence** – data sources, instruments, sampling.  
5. **Justify Conclusions** – analysis plan, triangulation.  
6. **Ensure Use & Share Lessons** – reporting format, dissemination plan.  

### 8.3 Sample Evaluation Questions  

| Question | Type | Indicator | Data Source | Analysis |
|----------|------|-----------|-------------|----------|
| Did CHWs complete required training? | Process | % CHWs passing competency exam | Training records | Descriptive % |
| How many women enrolled? | Process | Enrollment count | Enrollment database | Cumulative count |
| Did first‑trimester visit rate increase? | Outcome | % enrollees with 1st‑trimester visit | EHR (claims) | Chi‑square vs. baseline |
| Did health‑literacy improve? | Outcome | Mean quiz score change | Pre/post survey | Paired t‑test |
| What barriers persisted? | Process/Qualitative | Themes from focus groups | Audio‑recorded FG | Thematic coding |

### 8.4 Data Collection Plan Template  

| Indicator | Tool | Frequency | Responsible | Storage | Quality Checks |
|-----------|------|-----------|-------------|---------|----------------|
| Enrollment count | CRM (Salesforce) | Weekly | Program Coordinator | Secure cloud | Duplicate check |
| First‑trimester visit | EHR extract | Monthly | Data Analyst | Encrypted server | Validation against claims |
| Health‑literacy score | 20‑item survey (Qualtrics) | Pre & post (baseline, 6 mo) | CHW | Survey platform | Missing‑data imputation |
| CHW competency | Written exam + observation | End of training | Training Lead | LMS | Inter‑rater reliability |

### 8.5 Evaluation Budget (Typical % of Total)  

| Category | % of Grant | Example ($130K) |
|----------|------------|-----------------|
| Personnel (eval lead, analyst) | 10‑15 % | $13K–$19.5K |
| Data collection tools (survey licenses, EHR fees) | 2‑4 % | $2.6K–$5.2K |
| Travel / focus groups | 1‑2 % | $1.3K–$2.6K |
| Reporting & dissemination | 1‑2 % | $1.3K–$2.6K |
| **Total** | **14‑23 %** | **$18K–$30K** |

### 8.6 Evaluation Checklist  

- [ ] Choose evaluation type(s) matching funder expectations.  
- [ ] Write 3–5 key evaluation questions.  
- [ ] Link each question to logic‑model outcome.  
- [ ] Define indicators, data sources, frequency.  
- [ ] Assign staff & budget for each data activity.  
- [ ] Develop analysis plan (statistical tests, qualitative coding).  
- [ ] Create a data‑management & privacy plan (HIPAA, IRB if needed).  
- [ ] Draft a reporting timeline (quarterly brief, final report).

## 9. Building a Realistic Budget & Budget Narrative

|----------|-------------------|------------|

| **Fringe Benefits** | Separate line or combined | Yes |
| **Travel** | Mileage, airfare, per diem (federal rates) | Yes – program‑related only |
| **Equipment** | Items >$5,000 useful life >1 yr | Yes – with prior approval |

### 9.3 Budget Narrative – What to Explain  

1. **Personnel** – % effort, justification for each role, salary source (grant vs. org).  
2. **Fringe** – rate used, components (health, retirement).  
3. **Travel** – purpose (home visits, training), rate basis (federal per diem).  
4. **Equipment** – why >$5K, depreciation schedule.  
5. **Supplies** – unit cost, quantity, program relevance.  
6. **Contractual** – scope of work, selection process, sub‑award compliance.  
7. **Other Direct** – rent allocation method (square‑footage), software license terms.  
8. **Indirect** – negotiated rate or de minimis, base (MTDC).  

### 9.4 Budget Narrative Example (Excerpt)  

> **Personnel – Program Manager (0.5 FTE, $55,000/yr)**  
> The Program Manager will oversee all grant activities, supervise CHWs, manage data collection, and serve as primary liaison to Healthy Futures Foundation. The 0.5 FTE allocation reflects the manager’s existing 0.5 FTE commitment to the organization’s maternal health portfolio; the grant covers the incremental effort required for the navigation expansion. Salary is based on the organization’s approved 2024 compensation scale (Step 3, $110,000 annual).  
>   
> **Fringe Benefits – 30 % of Personnel**  
> The organization’s fringe rate (30 %) includes FICA (7.65 %), health insurance (12 %), retirement contribution (5 %), workers’ comp (2 %), and unemployment insurance (3.35 %). This rate is documented in the organization’s federally negotiated indirect cost agreement (NICRA).  

### 9.5 Budget Checklist  

- [ ] All line items map to logic‑model activities.  
- [ ] Personnel effort percentages are realistic & documented.  
- [ ] Fringe rate matches organizational policy or NICRA.  
- [ ] Travel uses federal per‑diem/mileage rates.  
- [ ] Equipment >$5K justified with quotes.  
- [ ] Contractual agreements include scope, deliverables, budget.  
- [ ] Indirect cost base (MTDC) and rate are correct per funder.  
- [ ] Total request ≤ funder’s maximum award.  
- [ ] Narrative explains *why* each cost is necessary & allowable.  
- [ ] Budget spreadsheet balances (no rounding errors).

## 10. Writing the Narrative – Structure, Tone, and Persuasion  

### 10.1 Typical Grant Narrative Sections (Federal & Foundation)  

| Section | Typical Length | Core Content |
|---------|----------------|--------------|
| **Cover Letter / Executive Summary** | 1 page | Hook, request amount, fit, impact snapshot |
| **Statement of Need** | 1–2 pages | Data‑driven problem, gap, urgency |
| **Project Description / Program Design** | 3–5 pages | Logic model, activities, timeline, staffing |
| **Goals, Objectives, Outcomes** | 1–2 pages | SMART table (see Section 7) |
| **Evaluation Plan** | 1–2 pages | Questions, indicators, methods, analysis |
| **Sustainability / Future Funding** | 0.5–1 page | Diversification, earned income, policy advocacy |
| **Organizational Capacity** | 1 page | Track record, key staff bios, governance |
| **Budget & Budget Narrative** | 2–3 pages | Detailed spreadsheet + narrative |
| **Appendices** | As required | Letters of support, IRS 990, NICRA, CVs |

### 10.2 Writing Style Guidelines  

| Guideline | Why | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| **Active voice** | Clarity, ownership | “We will train six CHWs” vs. “Six CHWs will be trained.” |
| **Concrete verbs** | Measurability | “Increase,” “Reduce,” “Deliver,” “Establish.” |
| **Avoid jargon** | Accessibility | Use “community health worker” not “CHW” on first use. |
|
