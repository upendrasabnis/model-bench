**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our Community Resilience Grant is designed to support projects that deliver clear public benefit and demonstrate strong community involvement. Applicants may be either incorporated not‑for‑profit entities, registered charities, or unincorporated groups that are formally auspiced by a local government body. In your case, because the council holds the relevant insurances and can act as the fiscal sponsor, your group meets the eligibility criteria for the “unincorporated auspiced group” pathway. The council’s involvement also satisfies our requirement for a responsible contracting party, which simplifies the due‑diligence process for both parties.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The grant program does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, we evaluate proposals on their merit, community impact, and alignment with the program’s strategic priorities. Typical awards for projects of the scale you describe range from $15,000 to $75,000, but we have funded smaller pilot initiatives as well as larger, multi‑year infrastructure programs. The final amount will be determined by the scope of work, the proportion of eligible costs, and the overall available funding in the round.

**Eligibility of rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Infrastructure that directly enhances the sustainability, resilience, and operational capacity of community‑based projects is fully eligible. Rainwater tanks are considered “climate‑adaptation infrastructure” because they reduce reliance on municipal water supplies and help gardens remain productive during periods of heat stress. Similarly, a well‑designed shade structure that protects both crops and participants from extreme temperatures aligns with the program’s objective of building resilience to climate impacts. Both items will be evaluated as capital assets, provided they are justified in the project plan and accompanied by accurate cost breakdowns.

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following materials:

1. **Project Narrative** – A concise description (1–2 pages) that outlines the problem you are addressing, the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline with measurable milestones.  
2. **Budget and Financial Plan** – A detailed spreadsheet itemising all costs (equipment, installation, contingency, and any matching funds). Include quotations or provisional invoices for the rainwater tanks and shade structure.  
3. **Community Engagement Evidence** – Minutes or summaries from council meetings confirming the auspice relationship, letters of support from local residents, volunteer sign‑ups, and any existing membership data.  
4. **Insurance and Liability Documentation** – A copy of the council’s insurance certificate that confirms coverage for your activities, as well as a brief risk‑assessment plan.  
5. **Organizational Structure** – An org chart or brief description of your volunteer team, roles, and any governing document (e.g., a memorandum of understanding with the council).  
6. **Sustainability Plan** – A short section describing how the installed infrastructure will be maintained, operated, and monitored over the next five years.  
7. **Evaluation Framework** – Outline of how you will measure success (e.g., water savings, reduced heat stress for volunteers, increased produce yields) and any data collection tools you plan to use.
