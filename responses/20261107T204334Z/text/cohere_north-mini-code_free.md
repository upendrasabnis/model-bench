## Introduction  

In today’s competitive funding environment, organizations on both sides of the grant relationship—foundations, government agencies, corporate sponsors, and nonprofit grantees—face mounting pressure to do more with less. ## 3. Disbursement Management  

### 3. a. The Challenge  

- **Manual budget reconciliation** often leads to over‑ or under‑payments.  
- **Lack of real‑time tracking** makes it hard to know if funds are being used as intended.  
- **Compliance risk** when payments are not tied to documented milestones.  

### 3. b. How Grant Management Software Solves It  

#### Milestone‑Based Funding  

- **Milestone tracker** where grantees log completion of deliverables; automated triggers release the next payment tranche.  
- **Conditional logic** (e.g., “Release 30% upon receipt of signed contract”) reduces manual oversight.  

#### Integrated Budget Management  

- **Dynamic budget templates** aligned with the grant agreement, automatically updated as expenses are entered.  
- **Forecasts & alerts** warn funders when spending approaches limits, preventing overspend.  

#### Example: GrantManagement’s Disbursement Engine  

- **“Milestone Workbench”** lets grantees upload evidence (in‑kind receipts, invoices) and auto‑calculates eligible costs.  
- **“Payment Scheduler”** integrates with Treasury/FinOps systems to auto‑process ACH or wire transfers.  

### 3. c. Key Features to Look For  

| Feature | Why It Matters |
|---------|----------------|
| Milestone/phase‑based payment triggers | Aligns funding with progress, reduces risk |
| Real‑time budget vs. actual tracking | Provides transparency for both parties |
| Automated compliance checks (e.g., allowable costs) | Prevents disallowed expenditures |
| Multi‑currency & multi‑jurisdiction support | Essential for global funders |

## Best Practices for Implementing Grant Management Software  

### 8. a. Planning & Stakeholder Alignment  

1. **Map the End‑to‑End Workflow** – Document current processes, identify bottlenecks, and define desired future state.  
2. **Secure Executive Sponsorship** – Demonstrate ROI through pilot data; obtain budget approval early.  
3. **Form a Cross‑Functional Steering Committee** – Include IT, finance, program staff, and a grantee representative.  

### 8. b. Data Migration & Governance  

- **Audit Existing Grant Files** – Clean up duplicate records, standardize naming conventions, and validate data integrity.  
- **Define Data Ownership** – Clarify who controls applicant data, reporting metrics, and impact scores.  
- **Establish Retention Policies** – Align with legal requirements (e.g., grant agreements, audit trails).  

### 8. c. Customization vs. Configuration  

- **Prefer “Configure, Not Code”** – Most modern platforms allow workflow logic, scoring rubrics, and report templates to be built via UI, reducing reliance on custom development.  
- **Keep Defaults Where Possible** – Standard templates speed up implementation and ensure best practices are embedded.  

### 8. d. Change Management  

- **Create User Guides & Video Tutorials** – Tailored for reviewers, grantees, and finance teams.  
- **Run “Go‑Live” Training Webinars** – Include Q&A and live demo of key modules.  
- **Establish a Support Cadence** – Weekly office hours for the first month, then monthly check‑ins.  

### 8. e. Continuous Improvement  

- **Collect Usage Metrics** – Track login frequency, completion rates, and error logs.  
- **Schedule Quarterly Review Sessions** – Adjust scoring weights, add new impact KPIs, or refine reporting templates based on stakeholder feedback.  
- **Leverage Vendor Updates** – Stay current with platform releases; many vendors provide roadmap webinars and beta testing opportunities.
