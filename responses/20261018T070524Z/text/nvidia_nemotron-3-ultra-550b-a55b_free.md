NONPROFIT GRANT WRITING BEST PRACTICES  
A COMPREHENSIVE GUIDE FROM RESEARCH TO POST‑AWARD REPORTING  

INTRODUCTION  
GRANT WRITING IS A STRATEGIC PROCESS THAT COMBINES RESEARCH, STORYTELLING, AND FINANCIAL PLANNING.  
SUCCESSFUL PROPOSALS ALIGN THE NONPROFIT’S MISSION WITH THE FUNDER’S PRIORITIES.  
THIS GUIDE PROVIDES STEP‑BY‑STEP BEST PRACTICES, CONCRETE EXAMPLES, AND PRACTICAL CHECKLISTS.  

RESEARCHING FUNDERS  
IDENTIFY POTENTIAL FUNDERS THROUGH DATABASES SUCH AS FOUNDATION DIRECTORY ONLINE, GRANTS.GOV, AND LOCAL COMMUNITY FOUNDATIONS.  
USE KEYWORDS THAT MATCH YOUR PROGRAM AREA (E.G., “YOUTH MENTAL HEALTH”, “ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE”).  
CREATE A MASTER SPREADSHEET WITH COLUMNS: FUNDER NAME, FOCUS AREAS, GEOGRAPHIC LIMITS, AVERAGE GRANT SIZE, DEADLINE, CONTACT PERSON, NOTES.  
TRACK HISTORICAL GIVING PATTERNS BY REVIEWING 990 FORMS OR ANNUAL REPORTS.  
NOTE ANY RESTRICTIONS SUCH AS NO INDIRECT COSTS OR MATCHING REQUIREMENTS.  
PRIORITIZE FUNDERS WHO HAVE FUNDED SIMILAR ORGANIZATIONS IN THE PAST THREE YEARS.  

UNDERSTANDING FUNDER PRIORITIES  
READ THE FUNDER’S MISSION STATEMENT, STRATEGIC PLAN, AND RECENT GRANT ANNOUNCEMENTS.  
ATTEND INFORMATIONAL WEBINARS OR OFFICE HOURS WHEN AVAILABLE.  
MAP YOUR PROGRAM OUTCOMES TO THE FUNDER’S LOGIC MODEL OR THEORY OF CHANGE.  
IDENTIFY KEY METRICS THE FUNDER EMPHASIZES (E.G., NUMBER OF SERVED YOUTH, REDUCTION IN RECIDIVISM).  
DOCUMENT THESE PRIORITIES IN A ONE‑PAGE “FUNDER FIT” SHEET FOR QUICK REFERENCE.  

BUILDING A FUNDER DATABASE  
SET UP A CENTRALIZED CRM OR AIRBASE TO STORE ALL FUNDER INFORMATION.  
ASSIGN A STAFF MEMBER TO UPDATE THE DATABASE MONTHLY.  
TAG EACH FUNDER WITH STATUS: PROSPECT, ACTIVE APPLICATION, AWARDED, DECLINED.  
USE AUTOMATED REMINDERS FOR DEADLINES AND REPORTING DATES.  
EXPORT REPORTS FOR BOARD MEETINGS TO SHOW PIPELINE HEALTH.  

CRAFTING A COMPELLING NEEDS STATEMENT  
START WITH DATA: CITE LOCAL STATISTICS, CENSUS DATA, OR COMMUNITY HEALTH ASSESSMENTS.  
EXAMPLE: “IN ZIP CODE 90210, 27% OF CHILDREN LIVE BELOW THE POVERTY LINE, COMPARED TO 12% STATEWIDE.”  
CONNECT DATA TO HUMAN IMPACT THROUGH A SHORT STORY OR QUOTE FROM A BENEFICIARY.  
EXAMPLE: “MARIA, A 14‑YEAR‑OLD STUDENT, SAYS SHE CANNOT ACCESS AFTER‑SCHOOL TUTORING BECAUSE HER FAMILY CANNOT AFFORD TRANSPORT.”  
CLEARLY STATE THE GAP YOUR PROGRAM WILL FILL.  
AVOID JARGON; USE PLAIN LANGUAGE THAT A NON‑EXPERT READER CAN UNDERSTAND.  
LIMIT THE NEEDS STATEMENT TO ONE PAGE (APPROXIMATELY 350 WORDS).  

DEVELOPING GOALS AND OBJECTIVES  
WRITE ONE OVERARCHING GOAL THAT REFLECTS THE LONG‑TERM VISION.  
EXAMPLE: “GOAL: INCREASE HIGH SCHOOL GRADUATION RATES AMONG LOW‑INCOME YOUTH IN THE TARGET COMMUNITY BY 15% OVER FIVE YEARS.”  
BREAK THE GOAL INTO SMART OBJECTIVES (SPECIFIC, MEASURABLE, ACHIEVABLE, RELEVANT, TIME‑BOUND).  
OBJECTIVE 1: “ENROLL 120 YOUTH IN AFTER‑SCHOOL ACADEMIC SUPPORT BY END OF YEAR 1.”  
OBJECTIVE 2: “PROVIDE 300 HOURS OF MENTORING SESSIONS PER YEAR.”  
OBJECTIVE 3: “ACHIEVE A 90% RETENTION RATE OF PARTICIPANTS THROUGH YEAR 3.”  
ALIGN EACH OBJECTIVE WITH A SPECIFIC FUNDER METRIC IF POSSIBLE.  

LOGIC MODEL DEVELOPMENT  
CREATE A VISUAL LOGIC MODEL WITH FOUR COLUMNS: INPUTS, ACTIVITIES, OUTPUTS, OUTCOMES (SHORT‑TERM, INTERMEDIATE, LONG‑TERM).  
INPUTS EXAMPLE: “STAFF (2 FTE), VOLUNTEERS (10), CURRICULUM MATERIALS, TRANSPORTATION VOUCHERS.”  
ACTIVITIES EXAMPLE: “WEEKLY TUTORING SESSIONS, MONTHLY PARENT WORKSHOPS, QUARTERLY COLLEGE VISITS.”  
OUTPUTS EXAMPLE: “120 YOUTH SERVED, 300 TUTORING HOURS DELIVERED, 12 PARENT WORKSHOPS HELD.”  
SHORT‑TERM OUTCOMES EXAMPLE: “IMPROVED HOMEWORK COMPLETION RATES BY 20%.”  
INTERMEDIATE OUTCOMES EXAMPLE: “INCREASED SCHOOL ATTENDANCE BY 15%.”  
LONG‑TERM OUTCOMES EXAMPLE: “HIGHER GRADUATION RATES AND COLLEGE ENROLLMENT.”  
INCLUDE INDICATORS AND DATA SOURCES FOR EACH OUTCOME.  
USE THE LOGIC MODEL AS A ROADMAP FOR BOTH PROPOSAL NARRATIVE AND EVALUATION PLAN.  

PROGRAM DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION PLAN  
DETAIL THE PROGRAM TIMELINE WITH MILESTONES (QUARTERLY OR MONTHLY).  
ASSIGN RESPONSIBLE STAFF FOR EACH ACTIVITY.  
DESCRIBE RECRUITMENT, ENROLLMENT, AND RETENTION STRATEGIES.  
EXAMPLE: “RECRUIT THROUGH SCHOOL COUNSELORS, COMMUNITY EVENTS, AND SOCIAL MEDIA ADS TARGETING PARENTS.”  
EXPLAIN HOW YOU WILL ENSURE CULTURAL COMPETENCY AND ACCESSIBILITY.  
INCLUDE RISK MANAGEMENT: CONTINGENCY PLANS FOR STAFF TURNOVER OR FUNDING SHORTFALLS.  

BUDGET DEVELOPMENT  
USE THE FUNDER’S BUDGET TEMPLATE IF PROVIDED; OTHERWISE FOLLOW STANDARD CATEGORIES: PERSONNEL, FRINGE, TRAVEL, EQUIPMENT, SUPPLIES, CONTRACTUAL, INDIRECT COSTS.  
JUSTIFY EVERY LINE ITEM WITH A NARRATIVE NOTE.  
EXAMPLE: “PERSONNEL – PROGRAM DIRECTOR (0.5 FTE) $35,000 – OVERSEES CURRICULUM DEVELOPMENT AND STAFF SUPERVISION.”  
INCLUDE MATCHING FUNDS OR IN‑KIND CONTRIBUTIONS IF REQUIRED.  
CALCULATE INDIRECT COST RATE BASED ON YOUR ORGANIZATION’S NEGOTIATED RATE OR THE FUNDER’S CAP.  
PROVIDE A BUDGET NARRATIVE THAT EXPLAINS COST REASONABLENESS AND ALIGNMENT WITH ACTIVITIES.  
CHECK THAT TOTAL REQUESTED AMOUNT DOES NOT EXCEED FUNDER’S MAXIMUM AWARD.  

NARRATIVE WRITING BEST PRACTICES  
FOLLOW THE FUNDER’S SECTION ORDER EXACTLY.  
USE ACTIVE VOICE AND CONCRETE VERBS (“DELIVER”, “MEASURE”, “EVALUATE”).  
KEEP SENTENCES SHORT; AVERAGE 15‑20 WORDS.  
USE HEADINGS THAT MIRROR THE RFP (E.G., “NEEDS STATEMENT”, “PROGRAM DESIGN”, “EVALUATION”).  
INSERT DATA VISUALS (TABLES, CHARTS) ONLY IF ALLOWED; LABEL CLEARLY.  
PROOFREAD FOR GRAMMAR, CONSISTENCY, AND ADHERENCE TO WORD LIMITS.  
HAVE AT LEAST TWO INTERNAL REVIEWERS READ THE FULL PROPOSAL BEFORE SUBMISSION.  

LETTERS OF SUPPORT AND PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENTS  
REQUEST LETTERS FROM KEY PARTNERS (SCHOOLS, HEALTH CLINICS, COMMUNITY ORGS).  
PROVIDE A TEMPLATE THAT INCLUDES: PARTNER’S ROLE, COMMITMENT OF RESOURCES, CONTACT INFO.  
ENSURE LETTERS ARE ON PARTNER LETTERHEAD AND SIGNED BY AUTHORIZED REPRESENTATIVE.  
ATTACH MEMORANDA OF UNDERSTANDING (MOUs) FOR FORMAL COLLABORATIONS.  
LIMIT TO THREE TO FIVE HIGH‑QUALITY LETTERS UNLESS RFP SPECIFIES OTHERWISE.  

SUBMISSION CHECKLIST  
☐ CONFIRM ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA MET  
☐ VERIFY DEADLINE AND TIME ZONE  
☐ COMPLETE ALL REQUIRED FORMS (COVER SHEET, CERTIFICATIONS, BUDGET)  
☐ ATTACH ALL REQUIRED APPENDICES (LOGIC MODEL, RESUMES, LETTERS)  
☐ ENSURE FILE FORMATS MATCH REQUIREMENTS (PDF, WORD, EXCEL)  
☐ NAME FILES USING FUNDER’S NAMING CONVENTION (E.G., “ORGNAME_PROJECT_BUDGET.PDF”)  
☐ SUBMIT THROUGH DESIGNATED PORTAL OR EMAIL ADDRESS  
☐ SAVE CONFIRMATION RECEIPT AND SCREENSHOT OF SUBMISSION PAGE  
☐ LOG SUBMISSION IN FUNDER DATABASE WITH DATE AND TRACKING NUMBER  

POST‑AWARD MANAGEMENT  
SET UP A GRANT MANAGEMENT CALENDAR WITH ALL REPORTING DEADLINES.  
ASSIGN A GRANT MANAGER TO OVERSEE COMPLIANCE.  
ESTABLISH INTERNAL CONTROLS FOR EXPENDITURE TRACKING (SEPARATE BANK ACCOUNT OR COST CENTER).  
CONDUCT A KICKOFF MEETING WITH STAFF AND PARTNERS TO REVIEW SCOPE, TIMELINE, AND DELIVERABLES.  
DOCUMENT ANY SCOPE CHANGES AND SEEK PRIOR APPROVAL FROM FUNDER.  

REPORTING REQUIREMENTS  
REVIEW THE AWARD AGREEMENT FOR REPORTING FREQUENCY (QUARTERLY, SEMI‑ANNUAL, ANNUAL).  
PREPARE A REPORT TEMPLATE THAT INCLUDES: NARRATIVE PROGRESS, QUANTITATIVE METRICS, FINANCIAL EXPENDITURES, CHALLENGES, AND LESSONS LEARNED.  
USE THE LOGIC MODEL INDICATORS TO POPULATE OUTCOME DATA.  
PROVIDE VISUALS (BAR CHARTS, PROGRESS BARS) TO ILLUSTRATE ACHIEVEMENTS.  
SUBMIT REPORTS ON TIME; REQUEST EXTENSIONS ONLY WITH VALID REASONS.  

EVALUATION AND LEARNING  
DEVELOP AN EVALUATION PLAN AT PROPOSAL STAGE (FORMULATIVE AND SUMMATIVE).  
SELECT APPROPRIATE METHODS: SURVEYS, FOCUS GROUPS, ADMINISTRATIVE DATA, PRE‑POST TESTS.  
ASSIGN AN INTERNAL EVALUATOR OR CONTRACT EXTERNAL EVALUATOR IF BUDGET ALLOWS.  
COLLECT BASELINE DATA BEFORE PROGRAM LAUNCH.  
ANALYZE DATA REGULARLY TO INFORM PROGRAM ADJUSTMENTS (CONTINUOUS QUALITY IMPROVEMENT).  
SHARE FINDINGS WITH FUNDER, BOARD, AND COMMUNITY THROUGH BRIEFS OR PRESENTATIONS.  

SUSTAINABILITY PLANNING  
IDENTIFY DIVERSE REVENUE STREAMS: INDIVIDUAL DONORS, CORPORATE SPONSORSHIPS, GOVERNMENT CONTRACTS, EARNED INCOME.  
BUILD A CASE FOR SUPPORT THAT HIGHLIGHTS IMPACT DATA FROM THE GRANT.  
CREATE A MULTI‑YEAR FUNDING PLAN WITH TARGETS FOR EACH REVENUE SOURCE.  
ENGAGE BOARD MEMBERS IN FUNDRAISING ACTIVITIES (E.G., DONOR CULTIVATION EVENTS).  
DOCUMENT LESSONS LEARNED TO STRENGTHEN FUTURE PROPOSALS.  

COMMON PITFALLS AND HOW TO AVOID THEM  
PITFALL 1: GENERIC NEEDS STATEMENT – SOLUTION: USE LOCAL DATA AND PERSONAL STORIES.  
PITFALL 2: MISALIGNED BUDGET – SOLUTION: CROSS‑CHECK EVERY LINE ITEM AGAINST ACTIVITIES.  
PITFALL 3: MISSING DEADLINES – SOLUTION: AUTOMATE REMINDERS AND ASSIGN A DEADLINE OWNER.  
PITFALL 4: INSUFFICIENT EVALUATION – SOLUTION: INTEGRATE EVALUATION INTO LOGIC MODEL FROM DAY ONE.  
PITFALL 5: POOR COMMUNICATION WITH FUNDER – SOLUTION: SCHEDULE REGULAR CHECK‑INS AND SEND QUICK UPDATES.
