Subject: Re: Question about eligibility for the Community Resilience Grant  

Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the clear description of your garden’s objectives. I’m delighted to provide the following clarifications so that you can decide confidently whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups**  
Our grant program does accept applications from unincorporated community groups provided that the group operates under a formal “auspices” arrangement with a recognised local authority, such as your council. In practice, this means the council must act as the fiscal sponsor, holding any insurance, managing grant funds, and being accountable for the disbursement of resources on behalf of the group. You will need to include a written letter from the council confirming this auspice relationship, outlining the council’s responsibilities, and confirming that they are willing to sign any required funding agreements. If you are able to provide such documentation, unincorporated groups are fully eligible to apply.

**Grant amount – minimum and maximum**  
We do not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum grant size; awards are determined on the basis of project merit, cost‑effectiveness, and alignment with the programme’s strategic outcomes. In recent rounds, grants have ranged from roughly $5,000 to $75,000, with many successful projects receiving amounts in the $15,000‑$35,000 band. Your proposed infrastructure (rainwater tanks and a shade structure) falls squarely within the typical cost envelope for such initiatives, so a grant in the $20,000‑$40,000 range would be realistic, assuming the budget you submit is proportionate to the scope of work.

**Eligibility of the proposed infrastructure**  
Yes – rainwater tanks and shade structures are explicitly listed as eligible costs under the “Climate‑Resilient Community Assets” category of the Community Resilience Grant. Both elements directly support the program’s goal of enhancing community capacity to cope with extreme weather, reducing water consumption, and extending the operational season of vital community spaces. The key is to demonstrate how each component contributes to resilience (e.g., water harvesting for irrigation during drought, reduced heat stress for volunteers and visitors).

**Supporting documents we recommend**  
To strengthen your application, please prepare the following items:

1. **Auspice Letter** – a formal letter from your council confirming the sponsorship arrangement, insurance coverage, and willingness to act as the primary grantee.  
2. **Project Plan** – a concise narrative (max 2 pages) outlining the problem you are addressing, the proposed solution, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
3. **Detailed Budget** – a table breaking down all costs (materials, labour, installation, contingency) and indicating which items correspond to eligible grant categories.  
4. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small volunteer group, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and cash‑flow summary for the last fiscal year).  
5. **Insurance Certificate** – confirmation that the council’s insurance policy covers the activities you will be delivering.  
6. **Letters of Support** – at least two endorsements from local businesses, community organisations, or residents confirming the broader community benefit.  
7. **Site Plans / Sketches** – simple diagrams showing where the tanks and shade structure will be located.  

We strongly encourage you to review the full grant guidelines (available on our website) for any additional requirements, such as environmental impact assessments or procurement procedures. Should you decide to move forward, please submit your application through the online portal no later than the closing date.
