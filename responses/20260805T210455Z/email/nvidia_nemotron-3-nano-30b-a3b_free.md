**1. Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our funding guidelines allow organisations that are “auspiced” by a local government authority to apply, provided the auspicing body holds the necessary legal status and insurance on behalf of the project. Since your garden operates under the umbrella of the regional council and the council holds the required public liability and workers‑compensation coverage, you meet the eligibility criteria for this round of the Community Resilience Grant.  

**2. Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The current grant round offers funding between **$5,000 and $25,000**. Projects that demonstrate a clear resilience benefit and a realistic budget may be considered for any amount within this range. Your proposed investment in rainwater tanks and a shade structure falls comfortably within this scope, and we anticipate that a request for the full $25,000 would be competitive if supported by a robust cost‑benefit analysis.  

**3. Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure that enhances the long‑term sustainability of a community asset is explicitly eligible, provided it is directly related to climate‑resilience outcomes. Rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures are recognised eligible expenses because they reduce reliance on municipal water supplies and protect volunteers and produce from extreme heat.  

**4. Supporting documentation**  
To accompany your application, please prepare the following:  

- A brief project description (no more than 500 words) outlining objectives, anticipated outcomes, and the resilience benefits.  
- A detailed budget breakdown, including quotes or pro‑forma invoices for the rainwater tanks and shade structure.  
- Evidence of the council’s auspicing arrangement (e.g., a letter of support or a copy of the council’s insurance certificate).  
- Evidence of community need, such as participation statistics from your weekly produce stall or testimonials from local residents.  
- A risk‑management or mitigation plan that demonstrates how the project will address future climate‑related challenges.
