Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out before investing time in a full application, and I am pleased to address each of your questions in turn.

First, regarding your organisational structure: unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspicing arrangement with a local government authority are absolutely eligible to apply, provided the auspicing body (in this case, your local council) meets the program's eligibility criteria for administering organisations. The council must be willing to enter into a legally binding funding agreement on your behalf, assume full financial and legal accountability for the grant, and hold the required insurances—including public liability, professional indemnity, and workers' compensation where applicable. It is essential that you secure a written letter of support from the council confirming their willingness to act as auspice, along with a copy of their current insurance certificates of currency, to include with your application.

Second, the Community Resilience Grant offers funding between a minimum of $5,000 and a maximum of $50,000 per project. Your proposed budget for rainwater tanks and a shade structure falls well within this range. Capital infrastructure of this nature—specifically water security assets and climate adaptation structures that enable continued community use during extreme weather—is explicitly listed as an eligible cost category in the current guidelines. However, please note that site preparation, installation labour, and any required engineering certification are also eligible, whereas ongoing maintenance costs, operational salaries, and recurrent utility bills are not.

Third, with an annual turnover of approximately $40,000, your organisation sits comfortably within the "small community organisation" threshold (under $500,000), so you will not be required to provide audited financial statements. Instead, you should prepare: (1) a simple income and expenditure statement for the most recent full financial year, signed by your treasurer; (2) a current bank statement showing the account the grant would be paid into (which must be in the auspicing council's name); (3) a detailed project budget with at least two quotes for each major cost item; (4) a project plan outlining key milestones, risk management strategies, and how the infrastructure will be maintained post-completion; (5) evidence of land tenure or a letter from the landowner (likely the council) granting permission for the works; and (6) letters of support from at least two community stakeholders demonstrating the garden's broader social and environmental impact.
