Dear Jamie,

Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out before investing time in a full application, and I am happy to address each of your questions in turn.

First, regarding organisational structure: unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspicing arrangement with a local government authority are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing body (in this case, your local council) meets our standard eligibility criteria for lead applicants. The council must be willing to enter into a legally binding funding agreement on your behalf, assume full financial and legal responsibility for the grant, and provide evidence of appropriate insurance coverage—including public liability and workers’ compensation/volunteer insurance—that extends to the project activities. A signed auspicing agreement or letter of support from the council’s authorised delegate confirming these arrangements will be required at the application stage.

Second, the grant round offers a minimum request of $10,000 and a maximum of $100,000. Given your projected project scope, your budget appears to fall comfortably within this range. Please note that we cannot fund more than 80% of total eligible project costs; the remaining 20% must be contributed by the applicant (in-kind volunteer labour, materials, or cash are all acceptable).

Third, capital infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures is considered an eligible cost category under “Climate Adaptation Infrastructure,” provided the assets are fixed, serve a clear community resilience purpose, and remain accessible to the broader community beyond your membership base. Portable or temporary structures would not qualify.

Finally, to streamline your preparation, I recommend assembling the following supporting documents now:
1. Council auspicing agreement/letter on letterhead confirming insurance coverage and financial delegation.
2. Your group’s most recent annual financial statement (simple income/expenditure summary is sufficient for $40k turnover).
3. Quotes from at least two suppliers for each major infrastructure item.
4. A brief project plan including timeline, risk management, and community benefit statement.
5. Evidence of land tenure or council permission to install infrastructure on the site.
6. Letters of support from at least two community stakeholders (e.g., local school, neighbourhood house, CFA brigade).

The guidelines and application form are available on our website, and the portal opens for submissions on 1 May. Please do not hesitate to contact me if you require clarification on any of the above.
