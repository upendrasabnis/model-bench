Thank you for reaching out and for the clear description of your garden’s goals and operations. I’m happy to address each of the points you raised so that you can decide confidently whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our Community Resilience Grant programme welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. Unincorporated groups are eligible **provided they are formally auspiced or sponsored by a legal entity that can meet the programme’s fiduciary and insurance requirements**. In your case, the fact that your local council holds the relevant insurances on your behalf satisfies this condition. We typically require a brief auspice agreement (or a letter of support) from the council confirming that it will act as the primary point of contact, hold any necessary liabilities, and ensure compliance with grant conditions. If such a document does not yet exist, the council can draft a simple one; it should outline the council’s responsibilities, the scope of the auspice, and any reporting obligations.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The grant round does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, we assess each proposal against the overall programme budget and the merit of the project. Typical awards for infrastructure‑focused initiatives in your size range have ranged from **$15,000 to $45,000**, but we are prepared to consider requests outside this band if the justification is compelling and the requested amount aligns with the scope of work. The key is to demonstrate that the requested funds represent a proportionate contribution to the total project cost.

**Eligibility of rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes—capital infrastructure that enhances the sustainability and resilience of community assets is expressly covered under the “Environmental Resilience” component of the grant. Rainwater tanks, shade structures, and associated site‑work (e.g., foundations, plumbing, and safety fittings) are all considered eligible costs, provided they are directly linked to the project’s objective of maintaining garden operations during hot summers. We do require a clear breakdown of each item, including supply, delivery, installation, and any necessary permits.

**Supporting documentation you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documents (all to be submitted as part of the online portal):

1. **Auspice Agreement / Letter of Support** from the council confirming insurance coverage and financial oversight.  
2. **Proof of Identity** for the group’s primary contact (e.g., a copy of the coordinator’s ID).  
3. ** organisational Structure Document** – even for unincorporated groups, a brief description of roles, decision‑making processes, and any existing bylaws or constitution.  
4. **Financial Statements** for the most recent two years (including the $40,000 turnover figure you mentioned) – a profit‑and‑loss summary and a cash‑flow statement are sufficient.  
5. **Project Plan** outlining the objectives, timeline, deliverables, and expected outcomes, with particular emphasis on how the rainwater tanks and shade structure will achieve resilience.  
6. **Detailed Budget** itemising each capital item, unit costs, quantities, installation labour, and any contingency.  
7. **Letters of Support** from stakeholders (e.g., local schools, businesses, or community members) demonstrating broader community benefit.  
8. **Site Plans or Photographs** showing the current layout and where the new infrastructure will be placed.  
9. **Risk Assessment and Safety Plan** for the installation and ongoing use of the tanks and shade structure.  

If any of these documents are not yet available, please let us know; we can provide guidance on alternative evidence that may be acceptable (for example, a provisional council letter in lieu of a finalised auspice agreement). Our team is also happy to review a draft budget or project plan before you submit the formal application.
