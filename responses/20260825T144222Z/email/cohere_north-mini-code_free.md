Thank you for reaching out and for the opportunity to clarify the eligibility criteria for the upcoming Community Resilience Grant round. I’m delighted to hear about your garden’s initiative to enhance water security and climate‑resilience in your regional town; the project you describe aligns well with the grant’s objectives.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. Unincorporated groups that operate under a formal auspice arrangement with a local government entity—such as your council—are indeed eligible, provided the council can confirm that it will act as the responsible fiscal agent for any awarded funds. The council’s existing insurance coverage will satisfy our risk‑management requirements, and we will need a brief “Auspice Agreement” (or equivalent letter of support) outlining the council’s role and responsibilities. It is not a prerequisite to be a registered charity; the key is having a reliable legal entity that can administer the grant and report on its outcomes.

**Grant amount – minimum and maximum**  
We do not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum grant size. Awards are determined on a case‑by‑case basis, reflecting the scope of the proposed work, the overall budget, and the potential impact on community resilience. Past projects have ranged from $5,000 to $50,000, but applications with budgets outside this band are also considered if the justification is strong and the outcomes are clearly measurable.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Capital assets such as rainwater tanks and shade structures are fully eligible under the “Infrastructure and Equipment” cost category. These items must be essential to the project’s objectives, have a useful life of at least three years, and be directly attributable to the resilience outcomes you aim to achieve (e.g., reduced water consumption, increased crop yields during heat events). Please ensure that each item is clearly itemized in your budget, with unit costs and quantities.

**Supporting documentation we recommend**  
To streamline the review process, we suggest preparing the following documents:

1. **Auspice Agreement/Letter of Support** from your council confirming financial oversight and insurance coverage.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page description of the garden, its mission, volunteer base, and operating hours.  
3. **Financial Statements** for the most recent two years (or the latest audited accounts if available) to demonstrate the $40,000 turnover and stable funding sources.  
4. **Project Plan** – a detailed description of the rainwater‑tank and shade‑structure components, timelines, and implementation approach.  
5. **Budget Worksheet** breaking down capital costs, installation, and any ongoing maintenance expenses.  
6. **Letters of Support** from local businesses, community groups, or other stakeholders.  
7. **Risk Management Plan** (the council’s insurance certificate may suffice, but a brief outline of safety measures is helpful).  
8. **Outcomes and Evaluation Framework** outlining how you will measure the project’s impact (e.g., water saved, crop yield improvements, community usage metrics).

We strongly encourage you to begin drafting these materials now, as the application window closes in just a few weeks. Should you need any templates or further clarification on formatting, please do not hesitate to ask.
