Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the opportunity to clarify the eligibility criteria for our upcoming Community Resilience Grant round. I’m pleased to share that unincorporated community groups can indeed apply provided they are formally auspiced by a legal entity such as your local council. When a council serves as the auspice, it assumes responsibility for governance, financial oversight, and the required insurances, which satisfies the grant’s requirement for a responsible administering organisation. In practice, we have funded projects led by volunteer‑run gardens and similar initiatives that operate under municipal sponsorship, so your structure should be acceptable.

Our grant programme does not prescribe a minimum or maximum award amount; the funding level is determined by the scope, budget, and demonstrated community impact of each proposal. Typical awards for infrastructure projects in the $30,000–$80,000 range are common, but we encourage applicants to propose budgets that accurately reflect the cost of the proposed work while remaining realistic given the scale of the organisation.

Infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures is explicitly listed as an eligible cost under the “Environmental Sustainability and Climate Adaptation” component of the grant. These items are considered high‑impact, long‑life assets that directly support the resilience of community spaces, especially in the face of increasing summer heat. We have funded similar installations at community gardens and recreation centres, and they have consistently delivered measurable benefits in water conservation and user comfort.

To prepare a strong application, we recommend assembling the following supporting documents:

1. **Auspice Agreement** – a formal letter from the council confirming its support and confirming that it will hold insurance and liability coverage for the project.  
2. **Project Plan** – a concise description of the proposed installation, timelines, and implementation steps.  
3. **Budget Breakdown** – detailed cost estimates for the tanks, shade structure, installation, and any contingency funds, ideally with quotations from suppliers.  
4. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or a simple income‑statement/expense report if unaudited) to demonstrate financial management capacity.  
5. **Operational Documentation** – your group’s constitution or governing document, a list of volunteers, and proof of any existing permits or council approvals.  
6. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from local businesses, community members, or other stakeholders highlighting the project’s relevance.  
7. **Risk Assessment and Insurance Proof** – a summary of health and safety considerations, together with confirmation that the council’s insurance covers the work.

If you can provide these materials in a single PDF, we will be able to assess your proposal efficiently. Should you need any templates or further clarification on formatting, please let me know, and I will be happy to share them.
