## 1. Executive Summary: The Compliance Lifecycle

Grant compliance is not a administrative afterthought; it is the **legal and financial backbone of the funding relationship**. Most organizations treat the "Grant Agreement" as the finish line. In reality, the signature date is the starting gun for a complex regulatory marathon.

**The Compliance Lifecycle consists of four distinct phases:**
1.  **Pre-Award & Setup:** Chart of Accounts mapping, internal controls design, sub-recipient risk assessments.
2.  **Active Management (The "Burn Rate" Phase):** Monthly reconciliations, time & effort reporting, procurement compliance, prior approvals.
3.  **Acquittal & Reporting (The "True-Up"):** Financial reconciliation, narrative synthesis, asset disposition, certification.
4.  **Post-Award & Audit Defense:** Record retention, audit response, questioned cost resolution, close-out certification.

**The Core Thesis:** *Acquittal reporting is the formal, evidentiary process of proving that the funder’s investment yielded the contracted outputs/outcomes, was spent per the approved budget (and allowable cost principles), and that the organization retains the capacity to prove it years later.*

## 2. Deconstructing "Acquittal": Definitions, Purpose, and Legal Weight

### 2.1 What is an Acquittal?
**Acquittal** (often used interchangeably with *Financial Report*, *Final Report*, *Expenditure Report*, or *Close-out Package*) is the formal submission to a funder demonstrating that:
*   **Funds were expended:** Actual costs match the approved budget categories (or approved variances).
*   **Costs are Allowable:** Expenditures comply with the funder’s guidelines, the grant agreement, and applicable cost principles (e.g., 2 CFR 200 in the US, CGRGs in Australia, HM Treasury Green Book in UK).
*   **Activities were delivered:** The programmatic narrative aligns with the Logic Model/Theory of Change.
*   **Assets are accounted for:** Equipment/inventory purchased with grant funds is tracked, tagged, and dispositioned per policy.

### 2.2 The Legal Weight: It is a Certification, Not a Summary
When an Authorized Organizational Representative (AOR) signs an acquittal, they are making a **legal attestation** under penalty of law (e.g., False Claims Act in US, Crimes Act provisions in Commonwealth nations).
*   **Misrepresentation Risk:** Stating "Personnel costs = $100k" when timesheets only support $85k is not an "error"—it is a false certification.
*   **Clawback Trigger:** A failed acquittal is the primary trigger for **disallowance** (questioned costs) and **deobligation** (recovery of funds).

### 2.3 Acquittal vs. Progress Reporting
| Feature | Progress Report (Interim) | Acquittal / Final Report |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Quarterly / Semi-Annually | End of Period of Performance (PoP) + 90-120 days |
| **Basis** | Accrual / Estimates / Cash-out-the-door | **Final Accrual Basis** (All liabilities liquidated) |
| **Focus** | "Are we on track?" | "Did we do exactly what we said, legally?" |
| **Reversibility** | Errors correctable next quarter | **Final.** Errors require formal amendment/appeal. |
| **Audit Risk** | Low (sampling) | **High (100% population review)** |

## 3. The Funder Landscape: Government vs. Philanthropic Requirements

While the *principles* of accountability are universal, the *mechanics* differ vastly. A compliance officer must speak "Government" and "Philanthropy" fluently.

### 3.1 Government Funders (Federal/State/Provincial/Local)
**Governing Frameworks:**
*   **US:** 2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance) – The "Bible" for US federal funds.
*   **Australia:** Commonwealth Grants Rules and Guidelines (CGRGs) + RMG 411.
*   **UK:** HM Treasury Managing Public Money / Grant Standards.
*   **EU:** Financial Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2018/1046.

**Hallmarks of Government Compliance:**
1.  **Cost Principles are King:** "Allowable, Allocable, Reasonable, Consistent" (2 CFR 200.403). *Example: A $500 dinner for staff is "reasonable" for a conference but "unallowable" if alcohol is included per 2 CFR 200.423.*
2.  **Procurement Thresholds are Mandatory:** Micro-purchase (<$10k US), Simplified Acquisition ($10k-$250k), Formal Procurement (>$250k). **Sole source justification** requires rigorous documentation.
3.  **Indirect Costs (F&A/Overhead):** Negotiated Rate Agreement (NICRA) or De Minimis (10% MTDC). You cannot "make up" a rate.
4.  **Time & Effort Reporting:** **After-the-fact** certification (not budget estimates). Must reflect *actual* activity.
5.  **Property Standards:** Title vests in recipient *subject to* federal interest. Disposition requires federal approval if FMV > $5,000.
6.  **Single Audit Threshold:** $750k+ in federal expenditures triggers a Single Audit (formerly A-133).

### 3.2 Philanthropic & Corporate Funders
**Governing Frameworks:** Grant Agreement Contract Law + Funder-Specific Guidelines (e.g., Gates Foundation, Ford Foundation, Local Community Foundation).

**Hallmarks of Philanthropic Compliance:**
1.  **Flexibility within Guardrails:** Often allow "General Operating Support" (unrestricted) or flexible budget reallocation (±10-15% without prior approval).
2.  **Outcomes > Line Items:** Narrative reporting focuses on *impact metrics* (Logic Model outcomes) rather than general ledger mapping.
3.  **Restricted vs. Unrestricted:** Critical distinction. "Capacity Building Grant" ≠ "Program Grant." Misallocating unrestricted funds to cover restricted program deficits is a breach of trust (and sometimes law).
4.  **Intellectual Property (IP) & Data Rights:** Foundations often require open licensing (CC BY 4.0) for research outputs. Government usually retains a royalty-free license.
5.  **Lobbying/Advocacy Restrictions:** Private foundations (US 501(c)(3)) **cannot** fund lobbying. Grantees must certify no grant funds used for lobbying (IRS Form 5768 / 501(h) election nuances).
6.  **Reporting Cadence:** Often less frequent (Annual + Final), but narrative demands are higher (case studies, photos, beneficiary quotes).

### 3.3 Comparison Matrix: Key Requirement Differences

| Requirement | US Federal (2 CFR 200) | Australian Gov (CGRG) | Major US Foundation (e.g., Gates) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Budget Flexibility** | Prior approval >10% or scope change | Variations require Deed variation | Often ±15-20% without approval |
| **Indirect Costs** | NICRA or 10% De Minimis MTDC | Usually capped % of direct costs | Often capped at 10-15% direct; sometimes 0% |
| **Procurement** | Full FAR/2 CFR 200.317-.327 | Commonwealth Procurement Rules | "Best Value" / Conflict of Interest policy |
| **Time Tracking** | After-the-fact, per activity (PARs) | Timesheets linked to outputs | Often honor system / high-level % estimate |
| **Audit Trigger** | $750k Federal Expenditures | $500k+ (varies by state/agency) | Funder-initiated / Financial Statement Audit |
| **Asset Threshold** | $5,000 FMV / Useful life >1yr | $5,000 / $10,000 thresholds | Often $1,000 or $5,000 per agreement |
| **Lobbying Cert.** | SF-LLL / Byrd Amendment | Certification in Agreement | Explicit prohibition certification |

## 4. The Architecture of Record-Keeping: Building an Audit-Proof System

**"If it isn't documented, it didn't happen."** This is the auditor’s mantra. Record-keeping is not filing; it is **evidence generation**.

### 4.1 The "Golden Thread" of Documentation
Every dollar reported on the acquittal must trace a single, unbroken path:
`General Ledger (GL) Transaction` → `Source Document (Invoice/Receipt/Timesheet)` → `Approval Workflow` → `Budget Category Mapping` → `Acquittal Line Item`.

**Break the chain = Questioned Cost.**

### 4.2 Chart of Accounts (CoA) Design: The Foundation
**Failure Mode:** Using a single "Grant Expense" code.
**Best Practice:** **Segmented CoA (Multi-dimensional).**
*   *Segment 1:* Fund Source (Federal Grant #, Foundation Grant #, Unrestricted).
*   *Segment 2:* Program/Project Code.
*   *Segment 3:* Natural Account (Salaries, Fringe, Travel, Supplies, Equipment, Indirect).
*   *Segment 4:* Cost Share / Match Tracking (Separate segment or sub-code).
*   *Segment 5:* Activity/Task (for Time & Effort alignment).

*Example:* `2100-FED-NSF-12345-5000-01` = Fund 2100, Federal, NSF Grant 12345, Account 5000 (Salaries), Activity 01 (Field Research).

### 4.3 Source Document Hierarchy (Evidence Quality)
| Tier | Document Type | Evidentiary Strength | Retention Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1 (Highest)** | Original signed contracts, cancelled checks, bank statements, **original timesheets (wet ink or certified e-sig)**, paid invoices stamped "PAID". | **Primary Evidence** | 3-7 yrs post-closeout (Check funder regs) |
| **2** | Approved Purchase Orders, Travel Authorizations, Payroll Registers, Depreciation Schedules. | **Corroborating Evidence** | Same as Tier 1 |
| **3** | Internal memos, email approvals, budget spreadsheets, allocation methodologies. | **Supporting Context** | Same as Tier 1 |
| **4 (Weakest)** | Verbal attestations, recreated logs, estimates. | **Insufficient for Auditors** | N/A - Do not rely on these. |

### 4.4 Digital vs. Physical: The "Original" Problem
*   **Electronic Records:** Valid *if* system meets **integrity, authenticity, readability, and accessibility** standards (e.g., ISO 15489, IRS Rev. Proc. 97-22, ATO PS LA 2008/3).
*   **Scanning Protocol:** Scan at 300 DPI, PDF/A format, OCR enabled. **Destroy originals only after verified backup & QA.**
*   **System Certifications:** SOC 2 Type II reports for your Grant Management System (GMS) / ERP vendor are essential for audit defense.

### 4.5 Retention Schedules: The "Safe Harbor" Approach
*   **US Federal:** 3 years from submission of *final* expenditure report (2 CFR 200.334). *Exception:* Real property/equipment = 3 years after disposition. Litigation/Audit = until resolved.
*   **Australia:** 7 years generally (Corporations Act / Tax), but Grant Deeds often specify 7 years post-completion.
*   **Strategy:** Adopt a **"7 Years Post-Closeout"** organizational policy. It covers virtually all jurisdictions and audit cycles.

## 5. The Anatomy of an Acquittal Report: Financial & Narrative Components

An acquittal package is a **bound legal document**, not an email attachment. It typically comprises three pillars.

### 5.1 Pillar 1: The Financial Reconciliation (The "Numbers")
This is where the GL meets the Budget.

#### A. Statement of Expenditures (SOE) / Financial Status Report (FSR)
*   **Columns Required:** Approved Budget | Current Period Actuals | Cumulative Actuals | Variance ($) | Variance (%).
*   **Accrual Basis:** Must include **Accounts Payable** (goods received/services rendered by PoP end but unpaid) and **Accrued Payroll**.
*   **Liquidation Period:** Most gov grants allow 90 days post-PoP to pay invoices. The Final SOE *must* reflect $0 Accounts Payable for the grant.

#### B. The Budget-to-Actual Variance Analysis (The "Why")
**Do not just show numbers. Explain variances >10% (or funder threshold).**
*   *Bad:* "Travel underspent by 20%."
*   *Good:* "Travel underspent $12,000 (20%) due to COVID-19 travel restrictions preventing the Year 2 International Conference. Prior approval for reallocation to Supplies granted via Amendment #3 (dated 03/15/2023)."

#### C. Indirect Cost (F&A) Reconciliation
*   Show the **Base** (MTDC: Modified Total Direct Costs).
*   Show the **Rate** (Provisional / Predetermined / Fixed / 10% De Minimis).
*   Calculate: `MTDC Base x Rate = Indirect Charged`.
*   *Reconcile:* Does GL Indirect Expense = Calculated Indirect? If not, why? (e.g., "Excluded subaward >$25k from base per NICRA").

#### D. Cost Share / Match Certification
*   **Cash Match:** Bank statements showing org funds deposited/spent.
*   **In-Kind Match:** **Valuation methodology is critical.**
    *   *Volunteer Time:* Independent Sector rate *or* actual replacement cost (whichever funder allows).
    *   *Space:* Fair Market Rental appraisal (not "what we would charge").
    *   *Equipment Use:* Depreciation schedule, not purchase price.
*   *Audit Trap:* Claiming match on costs already charged to another federal grant (Double-dipping).

#### E. Program Income Accounting
*   Did the grant generate revenue? (e.g., Workshop fees, publication sales, patent royalties).
*   **Additive vs. Deductive vs. Cost Sharing:** The grant agreement dictates treatment. Default (US Fed) is **Additive** (Income adds to project budget). Must be reported and spent on allowable costs.

### 5.2 Pillar 2: The Narrative / Performance Report (The "Story")
This is not a marketing brochure. It is a **factual correlation of inputs to outputs.**

**Structure:**
1.  **Goals & Objectives Restatement:** Copy verbatim from Agreement.
2.  **Activities Completed vs. Milestones:** Table format. *Planned vs. Actual Dates.*
3.  **Outputs Delivered (Quantitative):** "Trained 120 nurses (Target: 100). Published 3 peer-reviewed articles (Target: 2)."
4.  **Outcomes / Impact (Qualitative/Quantitative):** "Clinic retention rates increased 15% post-training (Baseline 60% → 75%)."
5.  **Deviations & Corrective Actions:** "Objective 2 delayed 3 months due to IRB approval delays. Mitigation: Extended no-cost extension (NCE) approved."
6.  **Lessons Learned / Sustainability:** What happens after funding ends?
7.  **Publications / Products Inventory:** List all deliverables with DOIs/URLs.

### 5.3 Pillar 3: Certifications & Schedules (The "Legal")
1.  **Certification of Final Expenditures:** Signed by CFO/Finance Director + AOR.
2.  **Certification of Compliance:** Lobbying, Debarment, Drug-Free Workplace, Trafficking in Persons, FFATA/FSRS (US), Modern Slavery (UK/AU).
3.  **Property/Equipment Schedule:** List all assets > threshold. Serial #, Location, Condition, % Federal Participation, Disposition Plan.
4.  **Sub-recipient Monitoring Certification:** Confirm all sub-awards monitored, audits reviewed, management decisions issued.
5.  **Patent/Invention Reporting (iEdison / Bayh-Dole):** If applicable.
6.  **Indirect Cost Rate Proposal Submission Certification:** If using provisional rates.

## 6. The Audit Gauntlet: Preparing for Desk Reviews, Site Visits, and Forensic Audits

### 6.1 Types of Audits/Reviews
1.  **Desk Review (Remote):** Funder analyzes submitted reports, SOE, and Single Audit. *Low touch, high volume.*
2.  **Monitoring Visit / Site Visit (Programmatic + Financial):** Funder staff on-site (or Zoom) for 1-3 days. Reviews: Policies, Timesheets, Procurement files, Asset tags, Beneficiary interviews.
3.  **Single Audit / OMB A-133 (US) / Tier 2 Audit (AU):** Independent CPA firm tests **Compliance Requirements** (Activities Allowed, Allowable Costs, Eligibility, Matching, Reporting, Special Tests).
4.  **Forensic / Investigative Audit (OIG / ACIC / NAO):** Triggered by fraud allegation, whistleblower, or high-risk flags. **Presumption of adversarial intent.** Legal counsel required immediately.

### 6.2 The "Compliance Supplement" Logic (US Focus, Universal Principles)
Auditors test against **12 Compliance Requirements** (2 CFR 200 Appendix XI). Know these cold:
1.  **A. Activities Allowed/Unallowed**
2.  **B. Allowable Costs/Cost Principles**
3.  **C. Cash Management** (Drawdowns = Minimize time between receipt/disbursement).
4.  **D. Eligibility** (Beneficiaries/Sub-recipients).
5.  **E. Equipment/Real Property Management.**
6.  **F. Matching/Level of Effort/Earmarking.**
7.  **G. Period of Performance** (No pre-award/post-award costs without approval).
7.  **H. Procurement/Suspension & Debarment.**
8.  **I. Program Income.**
9.  **J. Reporting** (Timely, Accurate, Complete).
10. **K. Subrecipient Monitoring.**
11. **L. Special Tests & Provisions** (The "Unique" ones in your specific grant).

### 6.3 Preparing the "Audit Room" (Physical or Virtual Data Room)
**Pre-load these folders 48 hours before auditor arrival:**
*   `/01_Grant_Agreement_Amendments`
*   `/02_Org_Chart_Policies` (Procurement, Travel, HR, COI, Record Retention).
*   `/03_Financials` (GL Detail, Bank Recs, NICRA, Indirect Calc Workpapers).
*   `/04_Payroll` (Time & Effort Reports, Payroll Registers, Fringe Benefit Pool Calcs).
*   `/05_Procurement` (POs, Contracts, Sole Source Justifications, SAM.gov/Debarment checks).
*   `/06_Property` (Asset Register, Tags photos, Disposition forms).
*   `/07_Subrecipients` (Agreements, Risk Assessments, Monitoring Reports, Sub-Audit Reports).
*   `/08_Programmatic` (Logic Model, Raw Data, IRB Approvals, Beneficiary Lists).
*   `/09_Prior_Audits` (Previous findings, Corrective Action Plans, Closure letters).

### 6.4 Managing the Audit Interaction
*   **Single Point of Contact (SPOC):** One staff member coordinates all requests. Prevents conflicting answers.
*   **The "Parking Lot":** If auditor asks for something you can't find immediately, log it: "Item 4: Procurement file for Vendor X. Will provide by 3 PM tomorrow." **Meet the deadline.**
*   **Do Not Volunteer:** Answer the question asked. Do not speculate. "I don't know, I will get the exact document" > "I think it's because..."
*   **Exit Conference:** Attend. Take notes. Negotiate "Findings" vs. "Observations" vs. "Recommendations" *before* the report is finalized.

## 7. The "Dirty Dozen": The Most Frequent Compliance Failures & How to Avoid Them

Based on OIG reports, Single Audit findings, and funder feedback surveys.

### 1. Time & Effort Reporting Failures (The #1 Finding)
*   **Failure:** Using budget percentages (e.g., "50% Grant A / 50% Grant B") instead of actual hours. Missing signatures. Late certifications (>60-90 days post-period). No system for "after-the-fact" recording.
*   **Fix:** Implement **Positive Time Reporting** (daily timesheets) for all split-funded employees. Use electronic systems (e.g., Kronos, ADP, custom GMS) with **system-enforced routing**: Employee → Supervisor → Grant Manager → Payroll. *Zero tolerance for "I'll fill it out Friday."*

### 2. Procurement Violations: Sole Source Abuse & Missing Debarment Checks
*   **Failure:** Splitting purchases to stay under micro-purchase threshold ($10k US). Using "Sole Source" because "we like this vendor" without written justification (unique capability, emergency, public exigency). Failing to check **SAM.gov (US)** / **National Register (AU)** / **Consolidated List (EU)** for debarred/suspended vendors *before* award.
*   **Fix:** **Procurement Checklist** attached to every PO > $5k. Mandatory SAM.gov screenshot saved in procurement file. Annual "Splitting" audit by Finance.

### 3. Indirect Cost Rate Errors
*   **Failure:** Charging indirect costs on unallowable bases (e.g., equipment >$5k, participant support costs, subawards >$25k, tuition remission). Using an expired NICRA. Charging 10% De Minimis on Total Direct Costs instead of MTDC.
*   **Fix:** Build **MTDC Calculation Logic into ERP**. Automated exclusion rules for "Equipment," "Participant Support," "Subaward >25k." Quarterly reconciliation: `GL Indirect Expense` vs. `Calculated Indirect`.

### 4. Period of Performance (PoP) Violations
*   **Failure:** Pre-award costs without written approval. Charging costs *after* PoP end date (e.g., paying an invoice dated 09/30 for a grant ending 06/30 during the 90-day liquidation—**the liability must have been incurred by 06/30**). "Backdating" invoices.
*   **Fix:** **Hard Close Process.** 60 days pre-PoP: Freeze new obligations. 30 days pre-PoP: Accrual review. Day 1 Post-PoP: No new PO creation allowed in system for that grant code.

### 5. Cost Share / Match Shortfalls & Valuation Errors
*   **Failure:** Reporting match not yet incurred. Valuing volunteer time at "professional rate" instead of "replacement cost." Counting the same salary as match on two different grants.
*   **Fix:** **Match Tracker Dashboard.** Real-time tracking. Require *source documentation* for match *at the time of reporting* (not just at acquittal). Third-party in-kind requires donor letters *at time of donation*.

### 6. Subrecipient Monitoring Neglect (Pass-Through Risk)
*   **Failure:** Issuing subaward but not monitoring. Not obtaining/reviewing subrecipient Single Audit. Not issuing Management Decisions on sub-findings within 6 months. Flow-down clauses missing from sub-agreement.
*   **Fix:** **Risk-Based Monitoring Plan** at award. Tier 1 (High Risk): Site visit + Audit review. Tier 2: Desk review + Financial reports. Tier 3: Certification only. **Contractual right to audit** in every sub-agreement.

### 7. Equipment & Asset Management Gaps
*   **Failure:** No physical inventory in 2+ years. Missing tags. Assets disposed/sold without funder approval (FMV > $5k). Assets used for non-grant purposes. No depreciation schedule for cost-share valuation.
*   **Fix:** **Annual Physical Inventory** (mandatory). Barcode/QR tags linked to Asset Register. Disposition Request Form required *before* removal.

### 8. Program Income Mishandling
*   **Failure:** Depositing workshop fees into general operating account, not grant account. Not reporting it. Spending it on unallowable costs (alcohol, lobbying).
*   **Fix:** **Dedicated Program Income GL Code.** Policy: All income received → Recorded → Reported → Spent on Allowable Costs only.

### 9. Lobbying & Political Activity Violations
*   **Failure:** Using grant funds for "Grassroots Lobbying" (urging public to contact legislators) or Direct Lobbying. Charging salary of a lobbyist to the grant. Using foundation funds for lobbying (Private Foundation Tax Law).
*   **Fix:** **Lobbying Certification Training** for all PI/Key Personnel. Code lobbying time to Unrestricted/General Funds only. File SF-LLL (US) if *any* non-federal funds used for lobbying *related* to the grant.

### 10. Conflict of Interest (COI) & Nepotism
*   **Failure:** PI's spouse owns the consulting firm hired. Board member's company gets the contract. No COI disclosure forms collected. No mitigation plan.
*   **Fix:** **Annual COI Disclosure** (mandatory for all Key Personnel). **Organizational COI (OCI)** analysis for large contracts. Independent review panel for related-party transactions.

### 11. Cybersecurity & Data Privacy Non-Compliance (Emerging)
*   **Failure:** Storing PII/PHI on unencrypted laptops. No Data Use Agreement (DUA) with subrecipients. No Incident Response Plan. Non-compliance with NIST 800-171 (CUI) or GDPR/Privacy Act.
*   **Fix:** **Data Security Addendum** in all grants. Annual Pen Testing. Cyber Insurance. Breach notification clause (24-72 hrs).

### 12. Late / Incomplete Reporting
*   **Failure:** Missing deadlines. Submitting "Draft" as "Final." Narrative not matching financials.
*   **Fix:** **Automated Calendar Alerts** (90/60/30/7 days out). **Pre-Submission Checklist** (Finance signs off -> Program signs off -> AOR signs off).

## 8. Advanced Compliance: Sub-recipients, Multi-Year Grants, and Indirect Costs

### 8.1 Sub-recipient vs. Contractor Determination (The "Substance over Form" Test)
*Critical distinction: Sub-recipient = Compliance flow-down. Contractor = Procurement rules only.*
**Decision Matrix (2 CFR 200.331 / CGRG):**
| Characteristic | Sub-recipient | Contractor (Vendor) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Who determines eligibility?** | Sub-recipient determines who gets aid | Vendor provides goods/services to *you* |
| **Performance Measurement** | Measured against *program objectives* | Measured against *deliverables/specs* |
| **Decision Making** | Has programmatic discretion | Follows your specs |
| **Compliance Flow-down** | **YES** (All prime requirements apply) | NO (Only contract terms) |
| **Audit** | Subject to Single Audit / Tier 2 | Not audited for compliance (only financial stmt) |

*Real World Example:* Hiring a lab to run blood samples = **Contractor** (fee for service). Partnering with a clinic to recruit patients and deliver intervention = **Sub-recipient**.

### 8.2 Multi-Year Grants & "Carryover" Mechanics
*   **Automatic Carryover:** Some funders (NIH, NSF) allow automatic carryover of unobligated balances to next budget period (Year 2, 3) *without* prior approval, *if* spend rate is reasonable.
*   **Restricted Carryover:** Requires Prior Approval (e.g., >25% unobligated balance, or specific funder policy).
*   **Acquittal Nuance:** Interim acquittals (Annual Financial Reports) are "snapshots." Final Acquittal = Cumulative across all years.
*   **No-Cost Extensions (NCE):** Extends *time*, not *money*. Requires justification ("Scope not completed"). Usually limited to 12 months, one time only.

### 8.3 Indirect Costs: The "De Minimis" Trap
*   **10% De Minimis (2 CFR 200.414(f)):** Only for entities **never** having a negotiated rate.
*   **The Trap:** You accept a federal grant, use 10% De Minimis. Next year you get a second federal grant. You **must** use the same methodology (De Minimis) for *all* federal awards unless you negotiate a NICRA. You cannot pick and choose per grant.
*   **MTDC Base Exclusions (Memorize these):**
    1.  Equipment (Capital Expenditures >$5k).
    2.  Capital Expenditures (Buildings).
    3.  Participant Support Costs (Stipends, Travel for trainees).
    4.  Subaward amounts **exceeding $25,000** (per subaward).
    5.  Tuition Remission / Scholarships.
    6.  Rental Costs of Leased Equipment (sometimes).
    7.  Patient Care Costs.

## 9. Technology Stack & Workflow Automation for Modern Grant Management

Spreadsheets are a **control weakness**, not a tool. Modern compliance requires an integrated stack.

### 9.1 Core Systems Architecture
1.  **ERP / General Ledger:** (NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Microsoft Dynamics, Oracle, QuickBooks Enterprise). *Source of Truth for Financials.*
2.  **Grant Management System (GMS):** (Fluxx, Submittable, Foundant, Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud, AmpliFund, PeopleSoft Grants). *Source of Truth for Compliance Workflow, Reporting, Documents.*
3.  **HRIS / Timekeeping:** (ADP, Paycom, UKG, BambooHR, TSheets). *Source of Truth for Time & Effort.*
4.  **Procurement/AP Automation:** (Coupa, Bill.com, Tipalti, Concur). *Source of Truth for P2P Compliance.*
5.  **Document Management / Data Room:** (SharePoint, Box, Egnyte, Google Drive Enterprise - with Retention Policies).
