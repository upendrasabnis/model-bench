Thank you for reaching out and for the enthusiasm you have for enhancing the resilience of your community garden. I’m pleased to provide clarification on the points you raised so that you can determine whether our grant program aligns with your organization’s circumstances and project goals.  

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our Community Resilience Grant is open to a broad spectrum of applicants, including unincorporated community groups, provided that the group operates under a formal auspice arrangement with a legally‑registered entity. In your case, the fact that your garden functions under the auspices of the local council—and that the council holds the necessary insurances on your behalf—meets this requirement. To confirm eligibility, we will need a signed auspice agreement from the council that outlines the council’s role as fiscal sponsor, indemnifies the grant recipient for the duration of the project, and confirms that the council will be the conduit for any grant funds.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
We do not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum grant size; rather, awards are determined on the merit of the proposal, the overall budget, and the funder’s capacity for the round. Applicants typically receive between $5,000 and $50,000, but projects with a stronger case for community impact or those that align closely with our strategic priorities may receive larger awards. Our program is designed to be flexible, so the amount you request should reflect the actual costs of your project, with a clear justification for each line item.  

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs – rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Infrastructure of the type you describe is explicitly listed as an eligible cost under the “Climate‑Adaptation and Resilience” component of this grant round. Rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures that directly support year‑round garden operation are considered high‑value investments that reduce water consumption and mitigate heat‑stress impacts on both volunteers and produce. We do, however, require that any capital items be essential to the project’s objectives, be clearly itemized in the budget, and be supported by quotations or provisional quotes from reputable suppliers.  

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To assemble a competitive application, we recommend the following documentation:  

1. **Auspice Agreement** – A formal letter or memorandum from the council confirming its sponsorship and financial responsibility.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – A brief constitution or set of guiding principles, even if the group is unincorporated, to demonstrate its community basis.  
3. **Financial Statements** – Most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and cash‑flow summary) showing the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
4. **Project Plan** – A timeline, clear objectives, and description of how the rainwater tanks and shade structure will achieve resilience outcomes.  
5. **Detailed Budget** – Itemised costs for equipment, installation, permits, and any contingency funds, supported by supplier quotations.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Insurance** – Evidence of the council’s insurance coverage, plus any additional policies you may need for the project.  
7. **Letters of Support** – Endorsements from local businesses, community groups, or council members that attest to the project’s relevance and feasibility.  
8. **Monitoring and Evaluation Plan** – Outline of how you will measure success (e.g., water saved, increased produce yields, volunteer satisfaction).
