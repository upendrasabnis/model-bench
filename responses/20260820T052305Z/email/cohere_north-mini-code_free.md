**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines expressly permit applications from unincorporated community groups that operate under a formal auspice agreement with a local government body. The council’s role is to act as the legal sponsor, providing the necessary insurances, financial oversight, and accountability frameworks. To satisfy this requirement, you will need to submit a signed auspice agreement (or a letter of support) from your council confirming that it will be the contracting party for the grant, that it holds the required public liability and professional indemnity insurances, and that it will manage the disbursement of funds on your behalf. If such an agreement does not yet exist, we can work with you and the council to draft a concise memorandum of understanding that outlines responsibilities, reporting obligations, and the process for any required sign‑offs.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to fund projects ranging from modest pilot initiatives to larger, transformational infrastructure upgrades. Typically, grants awarded in recent rounds have fallen between **$5,000 and $75,000**, with the average award around **$30,000**. There is no strict minimum; however, projects that require only a small portion of the requested amount may be considered for partial funding. If your total project cost is lower than the usual range, we can discuss a scaled‑down application that focuses on the core components—rainwater harvesting and shade provision—while still delivering measurable resilience benefits.

**Eligibility of infrastructure (rainwater tanks and shade structures)**  
Both rainwater tanks and permanent shade structures are explicitly listed as eligible costs under the “Climate‑Adaptation and Infrastructure” category of our grant program. These items are viewed favourably because they directly increase the garden’s capacity to operate during extreme heat events, thereby enhancing community food security and reducing strain on municipal services. Eligible expenses include the purchase and installation of the tanks (including any necessary plumbing and overflow management), as well as durable shade structures such as pergolas, shade sails, or polytunnel frames. Please note that any costs associated with ancillary landscaping or non‑essential amenities may be deemed ineligible and would need to be excluded from the budget.

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To build a competitive application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:

1. **Project Proposal Form** – completed with all required fields, including a clear statement of the problem you are addressing (heat resilience), objectives, timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – a line‑item breakdown that separates eligible from ineligible costs, includes quotations or estimates for the tanks and shade structure, and shows how the grant funds will be utilized.  
3. **Auspice Agreement / Council Letter** – a formal letter from the council confirming sponsorship, insurance coverage, and the intention to act as the contracting entity.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – a brief description of your volunteer group, its mission, and how it engages the community, together with a copy of the council’s partnership agreement (if available).  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple cash‑flow summary) demonstrating an annual turnover of approximately $40,000.  
6. **Bank Account Details** – a copy of a resolution authorising the council to receive and disburse grant funds.  
7. **Letters of Support** – at least two endorsements from local stakeholders (e.g., neighbouring businesses, schools, or community groups) confirming the project’s relevance and community benefit.  
8. **Risk Management Plan** – an outline of how you will manage health and safety risks associated with the installation work, referencing the council’s insurance policy where appropriate.  
9. **Implementation Timeline** – a Gantt‑style chart or work‑plan spreadsheet that breaks the project into phases (design, procurement, installation, testing) with realistic milestones.
