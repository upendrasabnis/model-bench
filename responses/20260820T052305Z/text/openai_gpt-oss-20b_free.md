## 1.  What Is an Acquittal?

| Term | Definition | Who Uses It? |
|------|------------|--------------|
| **Acquittal** | A formal, written “report of compliance” in which a grantee demonstrates that it has spent grant funds in accordance with the funding agreement. | All federal, state, and local grant recipients; most major philanthropic foundations. |
| **Acquittal Report** | The document itself, often a combination of narrative statements, financial statements, and supporting documentation. | Grantees, auditors, funding agencies. |
| **Acquisition Statement** | A less formal, often internal statement that confirms receipt of funds and outlines initial budget. | Early project stages. |

> **Bottom line:** An acquittal is the final checkpoint that proves you followed the grant’s terms and used the money properly. Think of it as the “grand finale” of a grant cycle.

## 2.  Common Requirements Across Government and Philanthropic Funders

| Requirement | Government Grants | Philanthropic Foundations |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| **Narrative Summary** | Must describe program activities, outcomes, and lessons learned. | Same requirement, but often a more detailed “Impact Narrative.” |
| **Financial Statement** | Detailed line‑item budget vs. actuals, with explanations for variances. | Same, but many foundations also require a * deberán* (de‑brief financial) section. |
| **Timeline & Milestones** | Must show that project milestones were met on time. | Many foundations demand a “Key Milestone Summary.” |
| **Compliance with Laws** | Must demonstrate adherence to federal statutes (e.g., 42 CFR Part 2, Title IX). | Similar, plus “Ethical Conduct” statements. |
| **Audit & Review** | Some grants require a U.S. Government audit (e.g., OMB Uniform Guidance 2.0). | Foundations may require an external audit or a “Financial Integrity Statement.” |
| **Data & Outcomes** | Must submit quantitative and qualitative outcomes data. | Foundations often require a “Results & Impact” section. |
| **Signature & Certification** | Must be signed by the grantee’s executive officer. | Same, plus an “Acquittal Certification” from the CFO. |

## 3.  Record‑Keeping: The Core of Compliance

Effective record‑keeping is the backbone of a compliant acquittal. Below is a “checklist” that covers the most critical components.

### 3.1  Budget Tracking

| Component | What to Capture | Why It Matters |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Original, Approved Budget** | All line items, cost codes, and expected durations. | Provides the baseline for variance analysis. |
| **Actual Expenditures** | Dates, amounts, vendor names, and supporting receipts. |!!!!!

  *Shows that money was spent as planned.|
| **Unreimbursed vs. Reimbursable** | Distinguish between funds used for the grantee’s core operations vs. those that must be reported back to the funder. | Many funders disallow reimbursements for unrelated expenses. |
| **Adjustments & Variances** | Document and explain any changes to the budget. | Helps auditors understand why deviations occurred. |

### 3.2  Time‑Tracking & Personnel

| Role | Data Needed | Common Pitfalls |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Staff Hours** | Hours worked, task description, date, and budget line. | Under‑reporting or over‑reporting can trigger audit red flags. |
| **Consultants & Sub‑Contractors** | Contracts, invoices, and signed time sheets. | Missing contracts can lead to “unsubstantiated expenses.” |
| **Volunteer Hours** | Volunteer logs and how they contributed to program activities. | Volunteer time is usually not reimbursable, but you still need to document it. |

### 3.3  Physical & Digital Asset Tracking

| Asset | Tracking Tool | Why It Matters |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| **Equipment** | Asset register with serial numbers, purchase dates, and depreciation schedules. | Many grants have depreciation limits. |
| **Software Licenses** | License key inventory and renewal dates. | Unlicensed software can be a compliance violation. |
| **Facilities** | Lease agreements, maintenance logs, and cost allocations. | Some grants require that facility costs are “non‑allowable.” |

### 3.4  Data & Outcomes

| Data Type | Collection Method | Documentation |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Quantitative Metrics** | Surveys, test scores, or usage statistics. | Raw data files, aggregated reports. |
| **Qualitative Feedback** | Focus groups, interviews, or narrative case studies. | Audio recordings, transcripts, field notes. |
| **Impact Assessments** | Pre‑ and post‑intervention comparisons. | Statistical analyses, visual dashboards. |

> **Tip:** Store all data in a secure, backed‑up repository that follows the funder’s data‑security guidelines.

## 4.  Audits: What Auditors Look For

Auditors are the final gatekeepers ensuring that grant compliance is real, not just paperwork.

### 4.1  Types of Audits

| Audit Type | Who Conducts It | When It Happens? |
|------------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Internal Audit** | Organizational internal audit team or external CPA firm | Usually during the fiscal year, especially before the final acquittal. |
| **External Audit** | Independent auditor, often required by federal agencies | Usually at the end of the grant period. |
| **Programmatic Audit** | Agency audit specialists (e.g., DCAA for DoD) | As mandated by agency regulations. |

### 4.2  Common Audit Focus Areas

| Focus Area | What Auditors Check | Typical Findings |
|------------|---------------------|------------------|
| **Budget Compliance** | Are budget line items supported by receipts? | Unreimbursed expenses, phantom costs. |
| **Authority & Approvals** | Are all expenditures authorized by the correct signatory? | Unauthorized spending, lack of supporting approvals. |
| **Documentation of Outcomes** | Are outcome data linked to the budget? | Missing data, lack of data integrity. |
| **Internal Controls** | Are there segregation of duties, proper approvals, and audit trails? | Weak controls, single‑person approvals. |
| **Non‑Allowable Costs** | Are costs that the funder disallows (e.g., meals, entertainment) included? | Over‑expenditure on non‑allowable items. |

### 4.3  Preparing for an Audit

1. ** STD (Standard Operating Documents) Checklist**

2. **Walk‑through** – run through the entire grant process with the audit team.  
3. **Reconciliation** – reconcile financial statements with bank statements.  
4. **Corrective Action Plan** – prepare a plan to address any identified issues.

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **What is the difference between an “acquittal” and a “final report”?** | An acquittal is a compliance check; a final report focuses on outcomes and impact. Many funders merge the two. |
| **Do I need an external auditor for all grants?** | Not always. Federal grants often require it; many foundations allow an internal audit if it meets their standards. |
| **How long do I have to keep records?** | Federal law usually requires 3‑5 years; foundations may require up to 10 years. |
| **Can I use a cloud‑based accounting system?** | Yes, as long as it meets the security and audit trail requirements of the funder. |
| **What happens if I miss an acquittal deadline?** | Late filings can result in penalties, loss of future funding, or even legal action. |
