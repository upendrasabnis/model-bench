## 1. Introduction  

Grant writing is both an art and a science.  A winning proposal tells a **story** that is **data‑driven**, **mission‑aligned**, and **funder‑focused**.  This guide walks you through every phase of the grant lifecycle, offering concrete examples, ready‑to‑use templates, and practical checklists you can copy into your own workflow.  

> **Key Principle:** *Every section of a proposal must answer the funder’s “Why should I invest?” question.*

## 2. Understanding the Grant Landscape  

| Grant Type | Typical Size | Decision Timeline | Common Requirements |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Foundation (private)** | $5K‑$5M | 2‑6 months | Narrative, budget, letters of support |
| **Corporate Giving** | $1K‑$500K | 1‑3 months | Alignment with CSR goals, employee engagement |
| **Government (federal/state/local)** | $10K‑$10M+ | 3‑12 months | Strict compliance, SF‑424, cost‑share, audit |
| **Community Foundations** | $2K‑$250K | 1‑4 months | Local impact, community letters |
| **Crowdfunding / Individual Donors** | $100‑$50K | Ongoing | Storytelling, video, social proof |

**Action:** Map your organization’s funding mix (e.g., 40 % foundations, 30 % government, 20 % corporate, 10 % individual) and set annual targets for each.

## 5. Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement  

### 5.1 Anatomy of a Needs Statement  

| Component | What to Include | Example (Food Insecurity) |
|-----------|----------------|---------------------------|
| **Problem Definition** | Clear, concise description of the issue. | “In County X, 23 % of households experience food insecurity, double the state average.” |
| **Data & Evidence** | Recent statistics, citations, local sources. | “USDA 2023 Food Access Report; County Health Dept. 2022 survey.” |
| **Human Impact** | Stories or quotes that humanize the data. | “Maria, a single mother of three, skips meals to feed her children.” |
| **Gap Analysis** | What existing services miss. | “Only two food pantries serve the entire county; both operate < 20 hrs/week.” |
| **Urgency** | Why now? | “COVID‑19 increased unemployment 12 % in the last 12 months.” |
| **Alignment** | Tie to funder’s priority. | “The Foundation’s ‘Healthy Communities’ focus area explicitly targets food access.” |

### 5.2 Writing Tips  

- **Lead with the most compelling statistic.**  
- **Use “we” language for the organization, “they” for the community.**  
- **Limit to 1‑2 pages (≈ 500‑800 words).**  
- **Avoid jargon; define acronyms on first use.**  

### 5.3 Needs Statement Checklist  

- [ ] Problem stated in one sentence.  
- [ ] At least three recent, credible data points.  
- [ ] One personal story or quote.  
- [ ] Clear gap between need and current services.  
- [ ] Direct link to funder’s stated priorities.  
- [ ] No unsupported claims.

## 6. Designing a Logic Model / Theory of Change  

### 6.1 Logic Model Template  

| **Inputs** | **Activities** | **Outputs** | **Short‑Term Outcomes** | **Intermediate Outcomes** | **Long‑Term Impact** |
|------------|----------------|------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|----------------------|
| $150K grant, 2 FTE staff, volunteers | Weekly nutrition workshops, mobile pantry routes | 12 workshops, 500 food boxes distributed | ↑ Knowledge of healthy eating (pre/post survey) | ↑ Household food security (USDA 6‑item scale) | ↓ County food‑insecurity rate by 5 % in 3 yr |

### 6.2 Theory of Change Narrative (≈ 300 words)  

> *If we provide culturally relevant nutrition education (Activity) and increase physical access to fresh produce (Activity), then low‑income families will improve dietary knowledge (Short‑Term Outcome) and increase consumption of fruits/vegetables (Intermediate Outcome), ultimately reducing diet‑related chronic disease rates in the county (Long‑Term Impact).*

### 6.3 Logic Model Checklist  

- [ ] Every **input** has a cost line in the budget.  
- [ ] Activities are **specific, time‑bound, and assignable**.  
- [ ] Outputs are **countable** (e.g., “12 workshops”).  
- [ ] Outcomes are **measurable** with indicators & targets.  
- [ ] Arrows show **causal links** (no “magic” jumps).  
- [ ] Model fits on **one page** (landscape).

## 7. Writing Clear, Measurable Objectives and Outcomes  

### 7.1 SMART Objective Formula  

> **Specific** – What exactly?  
> **Measurable** – How many / how much?  
> **Achievable** – Realistic given resources.  
> **Relevant** – Tied to need & funder priority.  
> **Time‑bound** – By when?  

### 7.2 Example Objectives  

| # | Objective (SMART) | Indicator | Target | Data Source | Frequency |
|---|-------------------|-----------|--------|-------------|-----------|
| 1 | Increase nutrition knowledge among 200 participants by 30 % within 12 months. | Pre/post survey score | 30 % gain | SurveyMonkey | Baseline, 6 mo, 12 mo |
| 2 | Distribute 5,000 lbs of fresh produce to 250 households in Year 1. | Pounds delivered | 5,000 lbs | Delivery logs | Monthly |
| 3 | Reduce household food‑insecurity score (USDA 6‑item) by 1 point for 150 families in 24 months. | USDA score | −1 point | Annual household survey | Baseline, 12 mo, 24 mo |

### 7.3 Outcomes vs. Outputs  

| Output | Outcome |
|--------|---------|
| 12 workshops delivered | Participants report ↑ confidence cooking healthy meals |
| 500 food boxes packed | Households experience ↓ days without food |

**Tip:** Funders often ask for **both**; keep a separate “Outputs” table and an “Outcomes” table.  

### 7.4 Objective Checklist  

- [ ] Each objective follows SMART.  
- [ ] Indicator is **quantifiable** and **collectable**.  
- [ ] Target is **ambitious yet realistic** (benchmark against similar programs).  
- [ ] Data source & collection method are **named**.  
- [ ] Responsibility for data collection assigned.

## 8. Developing a Realistic Project Budget  

### 8.1 Budget Categories (Standard Federal & Foundation)  

| Category | Typical Line Items | % of Total (Rule of Thumb) |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| **Personnel** | Salaries, fringe, consultants | 40‑55 % |
| **Fringe Benefits** | Health, retirement, payroll tax | 15‑25 % of personnel |
| **Travel** | Mileage, airfare, per diem | 2‑5 % |
| **Equipment** | >$5K capital items | 1‑3 % |
| **Supplies** | Office, program materials | 3‑7 % |
| **Contractual** | Sub‑grants, evaluators | 5‑15 % |
| **Other Direct Costs** | Rent, utilities, insurance | 5‑10 % |
| **Indirect Costs (IDC)** | Overhead (if allowed) | 8‑15 % (negotiated) |

### 8.2 Sample Budget (12‑Month, $250K)  

| Line Item | Year 1 | Narrative Justification |
|-----------|--------|--------------------------|
| **Personnel** | | |
| Program Director (0.5 FTE) | $45,000 | Oversees all activities, reports to funder |
| Nutrition Educator (1 FTE) | $55,000 | Delivers workshops, develops curriculum |
| Data Analyst (0.25 FTE) | $18,000 | Manages surveys, outcome tracking |
| **Fringe (25 % of personnel)** | $29,500 | Health, retirement, payroll tax |
| **Travel** | $4,800 | Site visits to 4 partner pantries (12 trips) |
| **Supplies** | $7,200 | Workshop handouts, cooking kits, survey licenses |
| **Contractual – External Evaluator** | $22,000 | Independent outcome evaluation (Year 1) |
| **Equipment** | $0 | No capital purchases needed |
| **Other Direct – Rent/Utilities (shared)** | $12,000 | 20 % of office space allocated to program |
| **Indirect Costs (10 % MTDC)** | $19,500 | Approved rate per funder policy |
| **Total** | **$250,000** | |

### 8.3 Budget Narrative Tips  

- **Tie every line to a logic‑model activity.**  
- **Explain cost‑share** (e.g., “Organization contributes $30K in-kind volunteer hours”).  
- **Show sustainability** – list any **leveraged funds** or **future revenue**.  

### 8.4 Budget Checklist  

- [ ] All line items **match** activities in the logic model.  
- [ ] Personnel costs **include fringe** at the organization’s actual rate.  
- [ ] **Indirect cost rate** is either the federally negotiated rate or the funder’s cap.  
- [ ] **Cost‑share** documented with letters of commitment.  
- [ ] **No “miscellaneous”** line > 5 % of total.  
- [ ] Budget **balances** (total expenses = total revenue).  
- [ ] **Currency** and **fiscal year** clearly labeled.

## 9. Creating a Strong Narrative: Project Description & Methodology  

### 9.1 Structure (Typical 10‑12 pages)  

1. **Executive Summary** (1 page) – “Hook” + key numbers.  
2. **Organizational Background** (1‑2 pages) – Mission, track record, capacity.  
3. **Needs Statement** (1‑2 pages) – From Section 5.  
4. **Project Goals & Objectives** (1 page) – SMART table.  
5. **Logic Model / Theory of Change** (1 page graphic + ½ page narrative).  
6. **Methodology / Work Plan** (2‑3 pages) – Timeline, responsible staff, milestones.  
7. **Evaluation Plan** (1‑2 pages) – Indicators, data collection, analysis, reporting.  
8. **Sustainability / Future Funding** (½‑1 page).  
9. **Budget & Budget Narrative** (1‑2 pages).  
10. **Appendices** – Letters of support, bios, IRS determination, audit.  

### 9.2 Work Plan Example (Gantt‑style Table)  

| Month | Activity | Lead | Deliverable |
|-------|----------|------|-------------|
| 1‑2 | Staff onboarding, partner MOUs signed | Program Director | Signed MOUs, staff orientation checklist |
| 3‑4 | Curriculum finalization, pilot workshop | Nutrition Educator | Pilot workshop report (attendance, feedback) |
| 5‑12 | Monthly workshops (12 total) + mobile pantry runs | Nutrition Educator + Volunteers | Workshop logs, pantry distribution logs |
| 6, 12 | Mid‑year & year‑end outcome surveys | Data Analyst | Survey data set, preliminary analysis |
| 12 | Final evaluation report & sustainability plan | External Evaluator | Evaluation report, sustainability memo |

### 9.3 Writing Style Guidelines  

- **Active voice** (“We will deliver…”)  
- **Concrete verbs** (“conduct,” “measure,” “train”)  
- **Avoid “we hope to”** – use “we will achieve.”  
- **Use funder’s language** (mirror keywords from RFP).  
- **Keep paragraphs ≤ 6 sentences**; use sub‑headings every 2‑3 paragraphs.  

### 9.4 Narrative Checklist  

- [ ] Executive Summary stands alone (funder may read only this).  
- [ ] Organizational background includes **relevant past grants** (name, amount, outcomes).  
- [ ] Needs statement cites **local data** (not just national).  
- [ ] Objectives are **SMART** and **numbered**.  
- [ ] Logic model graphic is **high‑resolution** and **captioned**.  
- [ ] Work plan includes **milestones** and **responsible parties**.  
- [ ] Evaluation plan names **data collection tools**, **analysis methods**, **reporting schedule**.  
- [ ] Sustainability plan shows **diversified revenue** (e.g., earned income, membership).  
- [ ] Budget narrative references each **budget line**.  
- [ ] All appendices are **referenced** in the narrative.

## 10. Addressing Evaluation & Sustainability Plans  

### 10.1 Evaluation Framework (CDC’s Framework)  

| Step | Description | Example for Food Access Project |
|------|-------------|---------------------------------|
| **1. Engage Stakeholders** | Identify who cares about results. | Pantry staff, participants, county health dept. |
| **2. Describe the Program** | Logic model, activities, context. | See Section 6. |
| **3. Focus the Evaluation Design** | Choose **process**, **outcome**, **impact** questions. | Process: “Are workshops delivered on schedule?” Outcome: “Did knowledge increase?” |
| **4. Gather Credible Evidence** | Select indicators, tools, sampling. | Pre/post surveys (n=200), USDA food security module (n=150). |
| **5. Justify Conclusions** | Analyze, interpret, triangulate. | Paired‑t test for knowledge; regression for food security. |
| **6. Ensure Use & Share Lessons** | Dissemination plan. | Report to funder, community briefing, peer‑reviewed article. |

### 10.2 Sustainability Strategies  

| Strategy | Concrete Action | Timeline |
|----------|----------------|----------|
| **Diversify Revenue** | Launch a “Friends of the Pantry” monthly giving program (target $15K/yr). | Year 1‑2 |
| **Earned Income** | Sell branded reusable bags at farmers markets (net $5K/yr). | Year 2 |
| **Policy Advocacy** | Partner with county to adopt a “Food Access Ordinance” securing municipal funding. | Year 2‑3 |
| **Capacity Building** | Train 5 community volunteers as “Nutrition Ambassadors” (stipend‑free). | Year 1 |
| **In‑Kind Contributions** | Secure 2,000 lbs of produce annually from local farms (value $8K). | Ongoing |

### 10.3 Evaluation & Sustainability Checklist  

- [ ] Evaluation plan includes **process, outcome, and (if feasible) impact** components.  
- [ ] Indicators are **aligned** with SMART objectives.  
- [ ] Data collection tools are **validated** or **pilot‑tested**.  
- [ ] Analysis plan specifies **software** (e.g., SPSS, R) and **statistical tests**.  
- [ ] Reporting schedule matches funder’s requirements (quarterly, annual).  
- [ ] Sustainability plan lists **at least three distinct revenue streams**.  
- [ ] Letters of commitment for any **leveraged funds** are attached.

## 11. Polishing the Proposal: Formatting, Attachments, and Compliance  

### 11.1 Formatting Standards  

| Element | Requirement (Typical) |
|---------|-----------------------|
| **Font** | 12‑pt Times New Roman or Arial |
| **Margins** | 1‑inch all sides |
| **Line Spacing** | Single‑spaced narrative; double‑spaced tables |
| **Page Numbers** | Bottom center, “Page X of Y” |
| **Headers/Footers** | Organization name (left), Grant Title (right) |
| **File Format** | PDF/A‑1b (unless funder asks for Word) |
| **Naming Convention** | `OrgName_GrantProgram_FY2026_Proposal.pdf` |

### 11.2 Common Attachments  

| Attachment | When Required | Tips |
|------------|---------------|------|
| **IRS 501(c)(3) Determination Letter** | All foundations & government | Keep a scanned copy ready. |
| **Most Recent Audited Financial Statements** | Foundations >$100K, all government | Include management letter if any findings. |
| **Board List & Affiliations** | Most funders | Highlight any funder board members. |
| **Key Staff Bios (≤ 150 words each)** | All | Emphasize grant‑relevant experience. |
| **Letters of Support / MOUs** | When partners are essential | Use funder’s template if provided. |
| **Logic Model Graphic** | Often required | Export as high‑res PNG (300 dpi). |
| **Evaluation Instruments** | If requested | Attach blank survey, consent form. |
| **Cost‑Share Commitment Letters** | When cost‑share > 0 | Signed by authorized official. |

### 11.3 Compliance Checklist (Pre‑Submission)  

- [ ] **RFP/RFA** read in full; all mandatory sections addressed.  
- [ ] **Page limits** respected (including appendices).  
- [ ] **Font, margins, spacing** meet specs.  
- [ ] **Budget totals** match narrative and funder’s ceiling.  
- [ ] **Indirect cost rate** approved or justified.  
- [ ] **Certifications** (e.g., debarment, lobbying) signed.  
- [ ] **Electronic submission** tested (portal login, file size).  
- [ ] **Backup copy** saved in two locations (cloud + local).

## 12. Internal Review & Mock Review Process  

### 12.1 Review Roles  

| Role | Responsibility | Timing |
|------|----------------|--------|
| **Lead Writer** | Drafts narrative, integrates feedback. | Day 1‑10 |
| **Program Director** | Validates activities, outputs, outcomes. | Day 11‑12 |
| **Finance Officer** | Checks budget accuracy, fringe rates, cost‑share. | Day 11‑12 |
| **Evaluation Specialist** | Reviews evaluation plan, indicators. | Day 13 |
| **Executive Director / Board Chair** | Final sign‑off, ensures organizational alignment. | Day 14 |
| **External Peer Reviewer** (optional) | Fresh eyes, funder perspective. | Day 15‑16 |

### 12.2 Mock Review Checklist  

- [ ] **Clarity** – Can a non‑expert understand the problem and solution?  
- [ ] **Consistency** – Numbers in narrative = numbers in budget = numbers in logic model.  
- [ ] **Compliance** – All RFP requirements met.  
- [ ] **Persuasiveness** – Does the “why us?” story shine?  
- [ ] **Grammar/Style** – No typos, consistent terminology.  
- [ ] **Attachments** – All required docs present, correctly named.  

**Tip:** Use a **track‑changes** version in Word, then convert to PDF for final submission.

## 13. Submission Strategies & Deadlines Management  

### 13.1 Calendar Management  

| Tool | Use |
|------|-----|
| **Shared Google Calendar** | Color‑code: *LOI due*, *Full proposal due*, *Reporting due*. |
| **Project Management (Asana/Trello)** | Tasks: “Draft Needs Statement”, “Collect Letters”, “Final PDF”. |

### 13.2 Early Submission Advantages  

- **Technical issues** resolved before deadline.  
- **Funder goodwill** – some reviewers read early submissions first.  
- **Time for post‑submission follow‑up** (e.g., confirming receipt).  

### 13.3 Submission Checklist (Day‑Of)  

- [ ] Log into portal **30 min** before deadline.  
- [ ] Upload **PDF** + any required **Excel budget workbook**.  
- [ ] Verify **file size** < limit (often 25 MB).  
- [ ] Confirm **receipt email** and **reference number**.  
- [ ] Save **submission confirmation** in CRM.

## 14. Post‑Award Management: Reporting, Stewardship, and Renewal  

### 14.1 Reporting Calendar (Example)  

| Report | Due | Content | Format |
|--------|-----|---------|--------|
| **Quarterly Narrative** | 30 days after quarter end | Activities, outputs, challenges, stories | 2‑page PDF |
| **Quarterly Financial** | 30 days after quarter end | Expenses vs budget, cost‑share status | Excel template |
| **Annual Impact Report** | 90 days after grant year | Outcomes, evaluation findings, lessons learned | 5‑page PDF + infographics |
| **Final Report** | 60 days after grant close | Cumulative outcomes, sustainability plan, financial reconciliation | Full package per funder specs |

### 14.2 Stewardship Activities  

| Activity | Frequency | Owner |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| **Thank‑you call/email** | Within 48 h of award | Executive Director |
| **Site visit invitation** | Once per year | Program Director |
| **Impact newsletter** | Quarterly | Communications Manager |
| **Donor recognition event** | Annually | Development Team |
| **Mid‑grant check‑in** | 6 months | Grant Manager + Funder PO |

### 14.3 Renewal Strategy  

1. **Track “Renewal Indicators”** – e.g., % of outcomes met, funder engagement score.  
2. **Prepare a “Renewal Pitch”** 3 months before grant ends: updated logic model, new data, expanded scope.  
3. **Leverage Success Stories** – create a one‑pager with metrics + quotes for the funder’s board packet.  

### 14.4 Post‑Award Checklist  

- [ ] Set up **grant‑specific accounting code** in financial system.  
- [ ] Assign **reporting lead** and **data collector** for each indicator.  
- [ ] Schedule **quarterly internal review** of progress vs. work plan.  
- [ ] Store all **correspondence** (emails, call notes) in CRM.  
- [ ] Conduct **lessons‑learned debrief** within 30 days of final report.
