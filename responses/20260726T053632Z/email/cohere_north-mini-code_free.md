Thank you for reaching out and for the clear description of your garden’s objectives. I’m pleased to provide the following clarifications to help you decide whether to proceed with a full application for the upcoming Community Resilience Grant round.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program does accept applications from unincorporated community groups provided they are formally auspiced by a legal entity that can act as the primary applicant and assume financial responsibility. In practice, this means that if your local council holds the necessary insurances, tax registration and can sign the funding agreement on your behalf, your group qualifies. We recommend that the council’s relevant department submit the application, attaching a memorandum of understanding that outlines the scope of work your volunteer team will deliver and the mechanisms for ongoing oversight. If the council prefers to act only as a funding conduit rather than an applicant, you may need to consider registering as an incorporated association or a nonprofit entity before the submission deadline.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum award. Instead, we allocate funds based on the merit of the proposal, the overall budget required to achieve the stated outcomes, and the availability of program resources for the current round. Typical awards for infrastructure projects of the type you describe range from $15,000 to $75,000, but we have funded projects both above and below this band in previous cycles. Your budget should reflect the true cost of the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary works (e.g., site preparation). Providing a detailed line‑item budget will help the review panel assess the proportionality of the request.

**Eligibility of the proposed infrastructure**  
Both rainwater tanks and permanent shade structures are explicitly listed as eligible costs under the “Climate‑Adaptation and Resilience” component of the grant. The program is designed to support physical improvements that increase a community’s capacity to cope with extreme weather events, and your project directly addresses the impact of hotter summer temperatures on food production and community access. To strengthen your case, please quantify the expected water‑saving benefits (e.g., litres per hectare saved) and the projected reduction in heat exposure for users. Including any relevant certifications for the tank systems (e.g., Australian Standards compliance) will also be advantageous.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
While the full application pack outlines all required attachments, we find that the following documents significantly streamline the review process for groups like yours:

1. **Auspice Agreement / Letter of Support** – a formal statement from the council confirming its role as applicant, insurance coverage, and any conditions of use.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page overview of your volunteer team, its purpose, and how the project aligns with your community’s strategic plan.  
3. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for a small operation, a simple cash‑flow summary) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover and the sources of income.  
4. **Project Plan & Timeline** – a Gantt or work‑breakdown chart detailing each phase (design, procurement, installation, commissioning) with clear milestones and responsible parties.  
5. **Detailed Budget** – an Excel sheet or PDF break‑down of all capital and indirect costs, including contingency (typically 10 % of the total).  
6. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from local businesses, schools, or community organisations that will benefit from the project.  
7. **Risk Assessment & Safety Plan** – documentation confirming that the installation will meet occupational health and safety standards, leveraging the council’s existing insurance where appropriate.  
8. **Monitoring & Evaluation Framework** – a simple logic model outlining expected outputs, outcomes, and measurable indicators (e.g., water usage reduction, increased garden operating days).

We strongly advise that you begin drafting these documents now, as the application window closes next month and the review panel will expect a polished submission. If any of the above items are not yet available, please let us know, and we can provide guidance on acceptable alternatives (for example, a provisional budget or a draft auspice agreement).
