Subject: Re: Question about eligibility for the Community Resilience Grant

Dear Jamie,

Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out to confirm eligibility before investing time in a full application—this is exactly the right approach, and I am happy to address each of your questions in turn.

First, unincorporated groups that are auspiced by a local government authority are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing organisation (in your case, the council) is willing to enter into a legally binding funding agreement on your behalf, hold the grant funds, and assume financial and reporting accountability for the project. The council must also hold current public liability insurance that covers the proposed activities, which you have confirmed is already in place.

Second, the current grant round offers a minimum request of $5,000 and a maximum of $50,000 per project. Your proposed budget for rainwater tanks and a shade structure would fall well within this range, assuming the total project cost does not exceed $50,000. Please note that we require a minimum 20 per cent contribution from the applicant (which may be in-kind, such as volunteer labour or donated materials), so your budget should reflect this.

Third, infrastructure that directly supports community resilience—such as water security measures and heat-mitigation structures—is explicitly listed as an eligible cost category in the program guidelines. Capital items must be permanently affixed, comply with relevant Australian standards and local planning requirements, and be essential to the project’s stated outcomes. Portable or temporary structures are generally not funded unless a strong case for mobility is made.

Regarding supporting documentation, you should prepare the following: a signed letter from the council confirming their willingness to auspice the grant, including their ABN and insurance certificate of currency; your group’s most recent financial statement or treasurer’s report showing the $40,000 turnover; a detailed project budget with at least two quotes for each major cost item; a simple project plan with timeline, risk management, and volunteer coordination details; evidence of land tenure or a licence agreement for the garden site; and letters of support from at least two community stakeholders (e.g., local schools, neighbourhood houses, or health services) demonstrating broader community benefit.

I strongly recommend you download the full Program Guidelines and Application Form from our website, as they contain mandatory templates for the budget and project plan. The online application portal will open two weeks before the closing date, and I encourage you to start a draft early so we can provide feedback on any missing elements well before the deadline.

Please do not hesitate to contact me if you need further clarification on any of these points. We value the work your garden does for community wellbeing and food security, and we would welcome a strong application from your group.
