Thank you for your interest in our Community Resilience Grant and for taking the time to clarify the eligibility requirements you are seeking. I am pleased to provide the following information so you can make an informed decision about preparing a full application.

**1. Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme is designed to support projects that deliver clear community benefit, and it does not strictly require applicants to be registered charities. An unincorporated group may apply provided that it operates under a “responsible entity” that can assume legal and financial accountability for the grant. In practice, many successful applicants have a local council act as the auspicing body, holding insurance, managing contracts, and submitting the required documentation on behalf of the volunteer group. If your council is willing to serve as the lead applicant, your garden will meet this criterion. Should you decide to proceed, we will need a formal letter of endorsement from the council confirming that it will oversee the project, maintain the necessary insurances, and be accountable for any grant funds awarded.

**2. Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is tiered to accommodate a range of project scales. Applications are typically considered for amounts between **$5,000 and $75,000**. Smaller infrastructure projects such as rainwater tanks and shade structures often fall within the lower portion of this range, while larger, multi‑component initiatives may request up to the maximum. Funding decisions are based on the overall budget required to achieve the project outcomes, the demonstrated need, and the capacity of the applicant to match or leverage additional resources.

**3. Eligibility of infrastructure costs (rainwater tanks and shade structures)**  
Yes – capital infrastructure that directly supports the sustainability and operational resilience of community assets is an eligible cost. Rainwater tanks are specifically mentioned in our guidelines as eligible under the “Water‑saving and Climate‑Adaptation” category, while permanent shade structures (e.g., pergolas, shade sails, or covered walkways) qualify under “Community Space Improvement.” Both items must be essential to the project’s objectives, reasonably priced, and installed in accordance with local building and environmental regulations. Please ensure that any quotations you obtain break down the costs for equipment, installation, and any ancillary works (e.g., foundations, plumbing connections).

**4. Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:

- **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem you are addressing (e.g., heat stress on volunteers and visitors), the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline with clear milestones.  
- **Budget Table** – a detailed breakdown of all costs, including equipment quotations, installation, contingency, and any matching funds or in‑kind contributions.  
- **Letters of Support** – endorsements from the council (as the auspicing body), local businesses that may contribute materials or sponsorship, and community members who will benefit from the project.  
- **Organizational Profile** – a brief overview of your volunteer group, its mission, and how the project aligns with that mission. Include a copy of any governing rules or constitution, even if informal.  
- **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or a simple income‑statement/expenditure summary if you are not required to have audits). For a group with a $40,000 turnover, a one‑page summary of revenue sources and expenditures is usually sufficient.  
- **Insurance Certificate** – a copy of the council’s insurance policy confirming that it provides liability coverage for the project activities. If you have additional coverage (e.g., public liability), please attach that as well.  
- **Site Plans or Photos** – optional but helpful for demonstrating the current layout of the garden and the intended location of the tanks and shade structure.  
- **Risk Management Plan** – a simple outline of how you will manage safety, health, and environmental risks associated with installation and ongoing use of the infrastructure.
