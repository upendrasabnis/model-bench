## 1. Introduction: The Lifecycle of Trust

Grant funding is not a gift; it is a contract. Whether the source is a federal agency distributing taxpayer dollars, a state government administering a community infrastructure program, or a private foundation deploying endowed assets, every grant agreement carries an implicit and explicit bargain: *We provide resources; you provide verified results.*

The **acquittal** is the formal mechanism that closes this loop. It is the moment an organization stands before its funder and says, "We did what we said we would do, we spent the money exactly as budgeted, and here is the evidence."

Yet, for many non-profits, research institutions, and social enterprises, the acquittal is treated as a bureaucratic afterthought—a frantic scramble of receipts and narrative reports conducted in the two weeks before a deadline. This reactive posture is the single greatest predictor of compliance failure, reputational damage, and funding loss.

This article treats grant compliance not as a defensive chore, but as a core operational discipline. We will dissect the anatomy of the acquittal, contrast the divergent worlds of government and philanthropic reporting, build a record-keeping infrastructure that survives forensic scrutiny, and catalog the specific, recurring failures that trigger clawbacks, audit findings, and debarment. Through real-world scenarios—from a municipal arts grant gone wrong to a multi-million dollar federal research audit—we will illustrate how rigorous compliance transforms accountability from a liability into a competitive advantage.

## 2. Defining the Acquittal: More Than a Receipt

### 2.1 Etymology and Legal Basis
The term "acquit" derives from the Old French *aquiter* (to settle a claim) and Latin *quietare* (to set free, to put at rest). In a grant context, the acquittal **releases the grantee from the financial and performance obligations** of the agreement, provided the funder accepts the evidence.

Legally, the acquittal serves three distinct functions:
1.  **Financial Settlement:** Reconciling actual expenditure against the approved budget line items.
2.  **Performance Verification:** Confirming that Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), milestones, and deliverables were met.
3.  **Legal Discharge:** Formally closing the contract, limiting future liability (e.g., clawback claims), and often triggering the release of final retention payments (typically 10–15% of the grant value).

### 2.2 Types of Acquittals
*   **Progress/Interim Acquittal:** Required at set intervals (quarterly, bi-annually, or at milestones). Often a prerequisite for drawing down the next tranche of funding.
*   **Final Acquittal:** The comprehensive submission at project end. Usually due 30–90 days post-project completion.
*   **Special Purpose Acquittal:** Triggered by specific events—change of scope, extension of time, or early termination.

### 2.3 The "Acquittal Package"
A compliant acquittal is never a single document. It is a **package** typically comprising:
*   Signed Certification/Declaration by an Authorized Officer (CEO, CFO, Board Chair).
*   Financial Statement (Income & Expenditure vs. Budget).
*   General Ledger Extract / Transaction Listing.
*   Performance/Narrative Report.
*   Supporting Evidence (invoices, timesheets, photos, evaluation data, sub-contractor reports).
*   Asset Register (for capital grants).
*   Audit Report (if threshold triggered).

> **Real-World Context:** *A university research office receives a \$2M NIH grant. The "acquittal" for the final year isn't just the SF-425 Federal Financial Report. It includes the Final Invention Statement, the Final Progress Report (RPPR), property reports for equipment >\$5k, and the A-133 Single Audit submission. Missing *one* component (e.g., the invention report) places the entire \$2M award in "non-compliance" status, freezing future drawdowns across *all* NIH awards to that institution.*

## 3. The Regulatory Landscape: Government vs. Philanthropic Frameworks

Compliance requirements are not monolithic. The "Rules of the Road" differ radically between a Department of Health grant and a Family Foundation grant. Understanding the *philosophy* behind the rules dictates how you build your systems.

### 3.1 Government Funders: The Public Trust Doctrine
**Core Philosophy:** Stewardship of public funds. Transparency, probity, fairness, and adherence to legislation (e.g., *Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013* (AU), *2 CFR Part 200* (US), *Grant Regulations* (UK/EU)).

**Key Characteristics:**
*   **Prescriptive Budgets:** Line-item budgets are legally binding. Moving \$5,000 from "Travel" to "Supplies" often requires formal written approval (a "variation" or "budget modification").
*   **Procurement Rules:** Mandatory competitive tendering thresholds (e.g., 3 quotes >\$10k, open tender >\$250k). Sole-source justifications require rigorous documentation.
*   **Indirect Costs (Overheads):** Strictly defined rates (e.g., Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate Agreement - NICRA in US; capped at 10-15% in many AU/UK schemes). "De minimis" rates (10% MTDC in 2 CFR 200) are a safe harbor but often lower than actuals.
*   **Mandatory Audits:** Threshold-triggered (e.g., \$750k federal expenditure in US triggers Single Audit; \$500k in many AU state grants).
*   **Consequence of Failure:** **Debarment/Suspension** (exclusion from future funding), **Clawback** (repayment + interest), **Criminal Referral** (for fraud).

#### 3.1.1 The US Federal Framework (2 CFR Part 200 - Uniform Guidance)
This is the "Gold Standard" of complexity.
*   **Cost Principles (Subpart E):** Defines *Allowable*, *Allocable*, *Reasonable*, *Consistent*.
    *   *Allowable:* Is it legal? Does the award terms permit it? (e.g., lobbying, alcohol, entertainment are explicitly unallowable).
    *   *Allocable:* Does the project benefit proportionally? (Shared costs require a documented allocation methodology).
    *   *Reasonable:* Would a prudent person pay this price? (Market comparability).
*   **Procurement Standards (Subpart D):** Full and open competition; socioeconomic contracting (small business, minority-owned); domestic preference (Buy American Act).
*   **Subrecipient vs. Contractor Determination (Section 200.330):** Critical distinction. Subrecipients carry compliance flow-down; contractors do not. Misclassification is a top Single Audit finding.

#### 3.1.2 The Australian Commonwealth Framework (CGRGs / PGPA Act)
*   **Commonwealth Grants Rules and Guidelines (CGRGs):** Mandate value-for-money, probity, and proportionality.
*   **Grant Agreements:** Standardized templates (e.g., Simple Grant Agreement, Standard Grant Agreement).
*   **Acquittal Focus:** "Certified Financial Statements" often required for grants >\$100k–\$500k, signed by a qualified accountant (CPA/CA), not just the CEO.

### 3.2 Philanthropic Funders: The Impact & Relationship Model
**Core Philosophy:** Social Return on Investment (SROI), Trust-Based Philanthropy, Strategic Alignment.

**Key Characteristics:**
*   **Flexible Budgets:** Often "unrestricted" or "lightly restricted." Budget variations <10-20% usually require only notification, not permission.
*   **Narrative Heavy:** The "acquittal" is often a **Learning Report** or **Impact Story**. Quantitative metrics matter, but qualitative narrative (case studies, photos, beneficiary voice) carries equal weight.
*   **Overheads/Indirect Costs:** Increasingly moving toward **Full Cost Recovery** (paying true overheads) or **Unrestricted Funding** (no acquittal of specific line items, just general financial health).
*   **Audit Requirements:** Rarely mandatory for grants <\$100k–\$250k. May accept organizational audited financials instead of grant-specific audit.
*   **Consequence of Failure:** Relationship damage, non-renewal, reputational risk within the philanthropic ecosystem (funders talk). Rarely legal clawback unless fraud is evident.

#### 3.2.1 The Spectrum: Trust-Based Philanthropy vs. Traditional Restricted Grants
*   *Traditional:* "Here is \$50k for the Youth Mentorship Program. Report on hours mentored and submit receipts for snacks."
*   *Trust-Based (e.g., MacArthur Foundation, Ford Foundation, many Community Foundations):* "Here is \$200k General Operating Support over 3 years. Send us your Annual Report and Audited Financials. Call us if strategy shifts."

> **Strategic Implication:** An organization managing 20 grants (5 Federal, 5 State, 10 Philanthropic) needs a **compliance matrix** mapping every grant to its specific: *Reporting Frequency, Financial Thresholds, Approval Authorities, Evidence Standards, and Audit Triggers.* One size does not fit all.

## 4. Anatomy of an Acquittal Report: Standard Components

Regardless of funder, a "Perfect Acquittal Package" contains these discrete modules. Treat each as a work package with an owner and a deadline.

### 4.1 The Certification / Declaration (The "Sign-Off")
This is the legal instrument. It usually requires signature by:
*   **Chief Executive Officer / Executive Director:** Accountability for performance.
*   **Chief Financial Officer / Finance Manager:** Accountability for financial accuracy.
*   **Board Chair / Treasurer:** Governance oversight (common in government grants >\$500k).

**Wording Nuance:** "I certify that the expenditure reported has been incurred in accordance with the Grant Agreement..." vs. "To the best of my knowledge..." The former creates personal liability; the latter is a knowledge qualifier. **Know which you are signing.**

### 4.2 Statement of Receipts and Payments (Income & Expenditure)
*   **Format:** Budget vs. Actual vs. Variance (both \$ and %).
*   **Basis:** Cash vs. Accrual. *Government almost always demands Accrual basis (matching expenses to the period they relate to). Philanthropy often accepts Cash basis.*
*   **GST/VAT Treatment:** Report *net* of recoverable tax. If you claim \$110 including \$10 GST, but you claim the \$10 back from the Tax Office, the grant expenditure is \$100. **Double-dipping GST is a top audit finding.**

### 4.3 The General Ledger (GL) Reconciliation
The bridge between the Summary Report and the source data.
*   **Requirement:** A filtered GL extract showing *only* transactions coded to the Grant Cost Center/Project Code for the reporting period.
*   **Reconciliation Statement:** `GL Total = Acquittal Report Total`. Any difference must be explained (e.g., "Accrued salary \$5k not yet paid," "Deposit refund received -\$2k").

### 4.4 Performance / Narrative Report
*   **Logic Model Alignment:** Report against the *approved* Logic Model/Theory of Change (Inputs -> Activities -> Outputs -> Outcomes -> Impact).
*   **KPI Dashboard:** Target vs. Actual for every approved metric.
    *   *Quantitative:* "50 workshops delivered" (Target: 50). "1,200 participants" (Target: 1,000).
    *   *Qualitative:* "Participant satisfaction increased from 4.1 to 4.6/5."
*   **Variance Explanation:** *Why* did you miss the target? "COVID lockdowns prevented 10 workshops; pivot to online achieved 80% reach." Funders forgive variance *with evidence*; they punish silence.

### 4.5 Asset Register & Disposal Schedule (Capital Grants)
If the grant bought assets (vehicles, lab equipment, building fit-out):
*   **Register:** Asset ID, Description, Serial #, Purchase Date, Cost, Grant % Funding, Location, Condition.
*   **Disposal Plan:** Funders often retain an equity interest. You cannot sell the \$200k microscope bought 50% with grant funds without funder approval and sharing proceeds.

### 4.6 Sub-Contractor / Sub-Recipient Acquittals
If you on-granted or sub-contracted >\$10k/\$25k:
*   You are responsible for *their* compliance.
*   Must collect *their* acquittals, review them, and consolidate into yours.
*   **Flow-down clauses:** Your agreement with them must mandate the same record-keeping and audit access rights you have.

## 5. Financial Acquittal: The General Ledger as Evidence

The General Ledger (GL) is the "source of truth." The acquittal report is merely a *view* of the GL. If the GL is messy, the acquittal will be wrong.

### 5.1 Chart of Accounts (CoA) Design for Grants
**Failure Mode:** Using a single "Grant Expense" code for a \$5M project.
**Best Practice:** **Project/Dimension Coding.**
*   *Structure:* `Fund - Department - Project - Natural Account - Activity`
*   *Example:* `100-FED-NSF-GRANT-2024-6100-SALARIES-PI`
*   This allows a single SQL query to pull the exact trial balance for the acquittal.

### 5.2 Direct vs. Indirect Costs: The Allocation Battlefield
This is where 80% of financial audit findings live.

#### 5.2.1 Direct Costs
Specifically identifiable to the project.
*   *Salaries:* **Time & Effort Reporting** is mandatory (see Section 5.3).
*   *Materials:* Purchase Order must reference Grant Code. Invoice must be coded at line level.
*   *Travel:* Must follow funder travel policy (e.g., Fly America Act for US Federal; Economy class only for most AU/UK gov).

#### 5.2.2 Indirect Costs (Facilities & Administration - F&A / Overheads)
Costs shared across projects (Rent, Utilities, IT, HR, Finance, Audit Fees).
*   **Method 1: Negotiated Rate (NICRA / Predetermined Rate).** Apply % to Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC). *Easiest, safest.*
*   **Method 2: De Minimis (10% MTDC).** US Federal only. No negotiation needed, but you leave money on the table if real rate is 35%.
*   **Method 3: Direct Allocation (Cost Pool Method).** You calculate actual share. *High effort, high audit risk.* Requires a formal **Cost Allocation Plan (CAP)** approved by the cognizant agency.

> **Real-World Example: The "Rent Allocation" Trap.**
> *A non-profit occupies a building. 40% is program space (Grant A), 30% admin, 30% fundraising.*
> * **Correct:** Rent allocated 40% to Grant A (Direct or Indirect per policy), 60% to Unrestricted.
> * **Finding:** Auditor finds 100% of Rent charged to Grant A because "the program staff sit there." **Result:** \$120k disallowed cost. Clawback + interest.
> * **Fix:** Formal space study (square footage) updated annually. Signed by Facilities Manager and CFO.

### 5.3 Time & Effort Reporting (Personnel Costs)
**The #1 Audit Risk Area.** Salaries are usually 60-80% of grant budgets.

#### 5.3.1 Standards (2 CFR 200.430 / AU equivalent)
1.  **After-the-Fact (ATF):** Certification *after* work is done (not budget estimates).
2.  **Frequency:** At least monthly (semi-annual for some philanthropic).
3.  **Signatory:** Employee *and* Supervisor (Principal Investigator / Program Manager).
4.  **Detail:** Must reflect *actual* activity distribution (e.g., "Grant A: 60%, Grant B: 20%, Admin: 20%").

#### 5.3.2 Systems: Paper vs. Electronic
*   *Paper Timesheets:* High fraud risk (back-dating), lost forms, illegible.
*   *ERP/HRIS Integration (Workday, ADP, PeopleSoft, Xero Projects, GrantHub):* System-enforced rules (cannot submit >100%, must certify monthly, audit trail of changes).
*   **Best Practice:** **Positive Time Reporting** (Employee enters hours per project code daily/weekly). **Negative Reporting** (Assume 100% on grant unless exception noted) is **only** allowed for employees working 100% on a single award—and even then, risky.

#### 5.3.3 The "PI Summer Salary" Trap (Universities/Research)
*   Professor paid 9-month academic year by University.
*   Summer (3 months) charged 100% to Federal Grant.
*   **Requirement:** Must document *actual* research effort during summer. "I thought about the project" ≠ 100% effort. Effort must be measurable (lab supervision, manuscript prep, data analysis).

### 5.4 Procurement & Vendor Management
Every dollar spent on external vendors must withstand the "Competition Test."

| Threshold (Typical) | Requirement | Documentation Required |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Micro (< \$3k-\$10k)** | 1 Quote / Credit Card | Receipt + Business Justification |
| **Small (\$10k - \$50k/\$100k)** | 3 Written Quotes | Quote comparison matrix, Selection rationale |
| **Large (> \$100k-\$250k)** | Formal Tender / RFP | Public advertisement, Evaluation panel minutes, Conflict of Interest (COI) declarations for evaluators, Signed Contract |
| **Sole Source** | **Exception Only** | **Sole Source Justification Form:** "Only vendor with proprietary tech," "Emergency," "Continuity of care." Must be approved *before* purchase. |

**Conflict of Interest (COI):** If the Program Manager’s spouse owns the catering company, the COI declaration must exist *before* the quote is requested. Retrospective COI waivers are rarely accepted by government auditors.

### 5.5 Common Unallowable Costs (The "Blacklist")
Memorize these. Charging them = Automatic Finding.
1.  **Lobbying / Political Activity:** Direct/indirect influence on legislation/elections.
2.  **Entertainment / Alcohol:** Staff parties, gala dinners, donor cultivation events.
3.  **Fundraising:** Grant writing costs (for *other* grants), capital campaigns, donor management software (unless specifically for *this* grant's reporting).
4.  **Fines & Penalties:** Late tax lodgment, parking tickets, OSHA fines.
5.  **Bad Debts:** Writing off uncollectible receivables.
6.  **Contingency Reserves:** "Just in case" budget lines.
7.  **Excessive Travel:** First class, luxury hotels, per diem above federal/state rates.
8.  **Membership Dues:** Country clubs, social clubs, lobbying orgs (Chamber of Commerce often unallowable).

## 6. Performance Acquittal: Proving Impact, Not Just Activity

Financial acquittal proves you spent the money. Performance acquittal proves the money *mattered*.

### 6.1 The Logic Model as Contract
Your approved grant application contained a Logic Model. The acquittal **must** map back to it.
*   **Inputs** (Budget) -> Financial Acquittal.
*   **Activities** (What you did) -> Process Metrics.
*   **Outputs** (Immediate products) -> Counting Metrics.
*   **Outcomes** (Changes in knowledge/behavior/condition) -> Impact Metrics.
*   **Impact** (Long-term systemic change) -> Rarely required in acquittal, but expected in final evaluation.

### 6.2 Quantitative Reporting: Precision vs. Accuracy
*   **Output Example:** "Deliver 100 training sessions."
*   **Acquittal Data:** Session logs (Date, Topic, Trainer, Location, **Signed Attendance Sheets**).
*   **Common Failure:** Reporting "100 sessions delivered" but attendance sheets only exist for 85. The auditor counts *verifiable* sessions = 85. Finding: "Output not met."

### 6.3 Qualitative Evidence: The "So What?" Factor
Funders increasingly demand **Outcome Harvesting** or **Most Significant Change (MSC)** stories.
*   *Weak:* "Participants reported high satisfaction."
*   *Strong:* "Participant #402 (single mother, 34) reported: 'The financial literacy module allowed me to negotiate a repayment plan with my utility provider, avoiding disconnection. I have since opened a savings account.' (Consent form signed, Case Manager: J. Doe)."

### 6.4 Data Quality & Verification
*   **Source Documents:** Where does the number come from? (CRM export, SurveyMonkey raw data, Paper sign-in sheets scanned to PDF).
*   **De-duplication:** Did you count "Unique Individuals" or "Attendance Instances"? Funders usually want Unique Individuals. If John attended 5 workshops, he counts as **1** participant, **5** attendances. **Define your counting rules in the M&E Plan upfront.**

### 6.5 Handling Underperformance (The "Variance Narrative")
You missed the target (e.g., 60 workshops vs 100 target).
**Do not hide it.** Structure the variance narrative:
1.  **Acknowledge:** "Target missed by 40%."
2.  **Contextualize:** "COVID-19 restrictions Q2/Q3 prohibited in-person gatherings."
3.  **Mitigation:** "Pivoted to hybrid model in Q4; developed 20 online modules (unbudgeted output)."
4.  **Impact Assessment:** "Reach dropped 20%, but satisfaction scores rose 15% due to accessibility."
5.  **Lesson Learned:** "Future budgets will include 15% contingency for digital delivery capacity."

> **Real-World Example: The "Vanishing Participants" Case.**
> *A \$1.2M State Government "Youth Employment" grant. Target: 200 job placements.*
> *Acquittal claimed 195 placements. Auditor requested employer verification letters for a sample of 30.*
> *Result: 12/30 employers had no record of the participant. 5 participants were "placed" in the grantee's own admin roles (not "employment outcomes"). 3 participants were counted twice (two part-time jobs = 2 placements).*
> *Finding: **Material Non-Compliance.** Only 140 verified placements. Funder clawed back 30% of grant (\$360k). Organization placed on "High Risk" monitoring for 3 years.*

## 7. Record-Keeping Infrastructure: Building the Audit Trail

**"If it isn't documented, it didn't happen."** This is the auditor's mantra. Your record-keeping system must allow a stranger (auditor) to trace any dollar or metric in the acquittal back to a source document in **< 5 minutes**.

### 7.1 The Document Hierarchy
1.  **Source Documents (Primary Evidence):** Original invoices, receipts, timesheets (signed), contracts, bank statements, signed attendance sheets, raw survey data.
2.  **Processing Records (Secondary Evidence):** Purchase Orders, Payment Vouchers, Journal Entries, Payroll Registers, Bank Reconciliations.
3.  **Summary Reports (Tertiary Evidence):** GL Trial Balance, Acquittal Financial Statement, KPI Dashboard.

**Auditors test: Source -> Processing -> Summary.** If the Summary doesn't match Source, the system fails.

### 7.2 Digital vs. Physical: The Hybrid Reality
*   **Mandatory Digital:** GL, Payroll, Bank Feeds, Contracts, Final Acquittal Submission.
*   **Often Physical:** Petty cash receipts, Signed attendance sheets (ink signatures), Original signed contracts (wet ink sometimes required for legal), Asset tags on equipment.
*   **Best Practice:** **Scan at Source.** Reception scans invoice -> Email to `ap@org.org` -> OCR extracts data -> Attaches to ERP transaction. No paper filing cabinets.

### 7.3 Naming Conventions & Metadata
Chaos: `Invoice_Jan.pdf`, `Scan001.jpg`, `Grant_Report_Final_v2_REAL_Final.xlsx`.
Order: `YYYYMMDD_Funder_GrantID_DocType_Description_Version.ext`
*   Example: `20240315_DOH_GRANT-2023-045_Invoice_AcmeConsulting_MarFees_v1.pdf`
*   **Metadata Tags (in DMS/SharePoint/Google Drive):** Funder, GrantID, FY, DocType (Invoice, Timesheet, Report), Confidentiality (Public, Internal, Restricted).

### 7.4 Retention Schedules: The Legal Minimums
*   **US Federal (2 CFR 200.334):** **3 years** from submission of *final* expenditure report (not project end!). If litigation/audit starts, clock pauses.
*   **Australia (State/Commonwealth):** Generally **7 years** after final acquittal acceptance.
*   **Philanthropic:** Often **5-7 years** per internal policy.
*   **Property/Asset Records:** Retain **3-7 years after final disposition** of asset.
*   **Personnel/Time Records:** Often **7 years** (employment law overlap).

> **Critical Rule:** The retention period starts at **Final Acquittal Acceptance Date**, not Project End Date. A 3-year project with a 6-month acquittal period + 3-year retention = **6.5 years minimum** from project start.

## 8. The Audit Event: Preparation, Execution, and Response

An audit is not an investigation; it is a **verification engagement**. Treat it as a professional service you are facilitating, not an interrogation you are surviving.

### 8.1 Types of Audits
1.  **Financial Statement Audit (Org-wide):** Opinion on total financial statements. Grant is a line item. (Standard for all medium/large NFPs).
2.  **Grant-Specific / Project Audit:** Opinion *only* on the grant acquittal financial statement. Common for Govt capital grants >\$500k.
3.  **Compliance / Performance Audit:** Testing adherence to rules (procurement, eligibility, KPIs). Can be separate from financial audit.
4.  **Single Audit (US) / Tier 2 Audit (AU):** Threshold-triggered audit of *all* federal/state funds. High stakes (Major Programs).
5.  **Funder Site Visit / Monitoring Review:** Program officers checking "vibes," files, and physical assets. Less formal, but findings count.

### 8.2 The Pre-Audit Phase (The "PBC" List)
Auditors issue a **Prepared by Client (PBC) List** (usually 2-4 weeks before fieldwork).
*   **Action:** Assign every item to a staff member with a **deadline 3 days before auditor arrival**.
*   **Quality Control:** Finance Manager reviews *every* PBC item for completeness *before* uploading to portal.
*   **Common PBC Items:**
    *   Final Trial Balance (Grant Code filtered).
    *   Lead Schedule (Reconciliation: TB -> Acquittal Report).
    *   Sample selections: "Provide invoices for transactions #102, #455, #890, #1201."
    *   Payroll register for Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct.
    *   Procurement files for 3 largest vendors.
    *   Board minutes approving budget/variations.
    *   Insurance certificates (Workers Comp, Prof Indemnity, Public Liability).
    *   Conflict of Interest Register.

### 8.3 Fieldwork: The Dos and Don'ts
*   **DO:** Designate a single **Audit Liaison** (Finance Manager or Grant Accountant). All auditor requests go through them.
*   **DO:** Provide a quiet workspace, WiFi, printer access, coffee.
*   **DO:** Answer *only* the question asked. Do not volunteer unsubstantiated opinions.
*   **DON'T:** Say "I don't know, let me ask my boss" and disappear for 2 hours. Say "I will have that for you by 2 PM."
*   **DON'T:** Alter documents after fieldwork starts. (Version control is your witness).
*   **DON'T:** Argue findings in the moment. **Document the finding, ask for the criteria/standard referenced, agree on facts, dispute interpretation later in writing.**

### 8.4 The Management Letter / Findings Report
Auditors issue findings categorized by severity:
*   **Material Weakness:** Reasonable possibility of material misstatement not prevented/detected. (e.g., No segregation of duties; \$500k unallowable costs).
*   **Significant Deficiency:** Less severe but important. (e.g., Late bank recs; missing timesheets for 2 months).
*   **Non-Compliance / Other Matters:** Procedural gaps. (e.g., Missing COI form for one vendor; KPI target missed without variance note).

### 8.5 The Response: Corrective Action Plan (CAP)
You usually have 30 days to respond.
