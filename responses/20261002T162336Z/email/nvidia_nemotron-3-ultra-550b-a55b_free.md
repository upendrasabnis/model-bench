Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out before investing time in a full application, and I am happy to address each of your questions in turn.

First, regarding organisational eligibility: unincorporated groups that operate under a formal auspicing arrangement with an eligible legal entity—such as a local government authority—are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing body meets the program’s eligibility criteria (e.g., holds an ABN, maintains appropriate public liability insurance, and agrees to accept legal and financial responsibility for the grant). Your arrangement with the local council appears to satisfy this requirement. You will need to supply a signed auspicing agreement or letter from the council confirming their willingness to act as the legal applicant and administer the funds on your behalf.

Second, the current round offers grants between $5,000 and $50,000 (GST exclusive). There is no requirement to request the maximum amount; you should apply for the precise sum needed to deliver the proposed activities, supported by a detailed budget.

Third, infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience—such as rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures that enable continued operation during extreme heat—is considered an eligible capital cost. Eligible expenditure includes purchase, delivery, installation, and any necessary site preparation or permitting fees. Ongoing maintenance costs, however, are not funded under this program.

Finally, the key supporting documents you should prepare include:
1. The signed auspicing agreement from the council.
2. The council’s current Certificate of Currency for public liability insurance (minimum $20 million).
3. Your group’s most recent financial statement or treasurer’s report showing the ~$40,000 annual turnover.
4. Two written quotes for each major infrastructure item (tanks, shade structure, installation).
5. A simple project plan with timeline, risk assessment, and evidence of any required development approvals.
6. Letters of support from at least two community stakeholders demonstrating the garden’s broader resilience value.
