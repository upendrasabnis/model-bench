Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the thoughtful work you and your volunteers are doing to strengthen your community garden. I’m happy to provide the information you need to decide whether to proceed with an application for the Community Resilience Grant.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The grant guidelines expressly allow unincorporated, volunteer‑run organisations to apply provided they operate under a formal auspice arrangement with an eligible sponsor—in this case, your local council. The council must be able to demonstrate that it will assume legal and financial responsibility for the project, including holding the required public liability and volunteer insurance. A signed auspice agreement (or a letter of confirmation from the council’s grants or community‑development unit) that outlines the council’s role, the duration of the auspice, and the insurance coverage satisfies this requirement.

**Grant size**  
For the current funding round, the minimum award is **$5,000** and the maximum award is **$50,000** (GST exclusive). Applications requesting amounts outside this range will be deemed ineligible. Given your estimated turnover of approximately $40,000, a request in the mid‑range (e.g., $15,000–$25,000) would be well aligned with the program’s intent to support modest‑scale resilience projects without overburdening smaller groups.

**Eligibility of the proposed infrastructure**  
Capital works that enhance the ability of a community organisation to withstand climate‑related stresses are explicitly listed as eligible costs. Rainwater harvesting tanks and shade structures that improve water security and provide protection from extreme heat therefore qualify. The grant can cover the purchase and installation of the tanks, associated plumbing, foundations, and the shade‑structure framework, as well as any necessary engineering certification. Ongoing operational costs (e.g., maintenance, water‑use fees) are not eligible, but you may include a modest contingency (up to 10 % of the total project cost) for unforeseen installation expenses.

**Supporting documentation to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, please assemble the following items:

1. **Auspice confirmation** – a signed letter or agreement from the local council stating that it will auspice the project, hold the relevant insurances, and act as the legal entity for the grant.  
2. **Insurance certificates** – copies of the council’s public liability and volunteer insurance policies (or a certificate of currency) covering the garden’s activities and the construction works.  
3. **Financial information** – a recent profit‑and‑loss statement or bank‑statement summary showing the $40,000 annual turnover, and a simple budget outline for the project (including quotes for tanks, shade structure, labour, and any site works).  
4. **Project plan** – a brief description (no more than two pages) of how the rainwater tanks and shade structure will increase resilience, timelines for procurement and installation, and how the garden will continue to serve the community during hotter periods.  
5. **Quotations** – at least two itemised quotes for each major component (tanks and shade structure) to demonstrate cost‑effectiveness.  
6. **Letters of support** – optional but recommended: one or two letters from regular users of the garden, the council’s community‑development officer, or a local environmental group attesting to the project’s value.  
7. **Governance documents** – if you have any informal governing rules, meeting minutes, or a volunteer roster, include these to show organisational capacity.

Once you have compiled these materials, you can proceed to the online application portal. Should you encounter any difficulty uploading documents or need clarification on the budget template, please do not hesitate to contact me directly at grants@agency.gov.au or on 02 1234 5678.
