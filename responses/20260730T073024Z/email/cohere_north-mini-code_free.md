Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the clear description of your garden’s vision and operations. I’m pleased to provide the following clarification on the points you raised, and I hope it helps you to decide confidently whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines state that an applicant need not be a registered charity; any “legal entity” capable of entering into a binding grant agreement is acceptable. An unincorporated group that operates under the formal auspice of a local council—where the council holds the relevant insurances, holds any required permits, and acts as the fiscal sponsor—meets this requirement. In practice, we have funded projects led by similar community groups, provided the council supplies a written endorsement confirming its support and responsibility for any grant‑related obligations. Therefore, your arrangement should satisfy the eligibility criteria.

**Grant amount – minimum and maximum**  
We do not set a fixed minimum or maximum grant size for this round. The amount awarded is determined by the overall merit of the proposal, the total project cost, and the availability of funds within the grant pool. In recent cycles, successful projects have ranged from roughly $5,000 to $50,000, but applications outside this band are certainly considered if they demonstrate strong community impact and sound financial planning. You may propose a budget that reflects the full cost of the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary works; the panel will assess how much of that cost can be reasonably covered by grant funding.

**Eligibility of the proposed infrastructure**  
Yes—capital assets such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures are explicitly listed as eligible costs under the “Infrastructure and Equipment” category of the Community Resilience Grant. Both items must directly support the project’s resilience objectives, which yours do by reducing reliance on municipal water and mitigating heat stress during summer operations. We recommend that the budget line‑items be broken out clearly (e.g., “2 × 5 000‑litre rainwater tanks – $8,500”, “Permanent shade canopy – $12,000”, “Installation labour – $3,000”) and that any required certifications (e.g., plumbing compliance, structural engineering drawings) be attached to the application.

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your submission, we suggest assembling the following documentation:

1. **Auspice Confirmation Letter** – a formal letter from your local council confirming its role as fiscal sponsor, outlining the insurances it holds, and indicating willingness to sub‑grant funds if the application is successful.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – a brief overview of the garden’s history, volunteer composition, mission statement, and the number of beneficiaries (current members, produce stall customers, etc.).  
3. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and balance sheet) showing the $40,000 turnover you referenced, together with a cash‑flow projection for the upcoming year.  
4. **Bank Account Details** – a copy of the council‑held bank account or a separate trust account that will receive grant funds, confirming that the council is the designated signatory.  
5. **Project Plan & Timeline** – a Gantt‑style chart or milestone list detailing design, procurement, installation, and commissioning phases, with clear deliverables and target dates.  
6. **Detailed Budget** – an itemised spreadsheet (as mentioned above) that includes all capital costs, labour, contingency, and any in‑kind contributions.  
7. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from neighbouring community groups, local businesses, or user committees that attest to the need and anticipated impact of the project.  
8. **Risk Assessment & Insurance** – documentation confirming that the council’s insurance covers public liability, as well as any site‑specific risk mitigation plans.  
9. **Site Plans & Drawings** – scaled sketches or CAD drawings indicating where the tanks and shade structure will be placed, along with any necessary council permits.
