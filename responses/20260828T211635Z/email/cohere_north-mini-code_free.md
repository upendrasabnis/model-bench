**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. While we give priority to incorporated not‑for‑profit entities, unincorporated groups that operate under a formal auspice agreement with a local government are indeed eligible, provided the council can act as the responsible entity for the project. In practice this means the council must confirm that it will hold the grant funds, oversee compliance with any reporting requirements, and assume liability for the use of the awarded money. If you have a signed auspice agreement (or a letter of support) from your council that outlines these responsibilities, we will accept it as evidence of a responsible entity.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
We do not prescribe a fixed grant size; awards are determined on the basis of merit, community impact, and the proportion of the total project cost that can be reasonably funded. In recent rounds, grants have ranged from approximately $5,000 to $50,000, though larger requests are considered when justified by scale and outcomes. Your projected capital costs for rainwater tanks and a shade structure appear to fall comfortably within the typical range, and we would be happy to discuss the precise funding envelope once a full proposal is submitted.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes – capital infrastructure that directly enhances the resilience and sustainability of community operations is fully eligible. This includes, but is not limited to, water‑harvesting systems, shade structures, solar panels, and other climate‑adaptation features. The key is that the assets are essential to the project’s objectives, will remain the property of the community group, and will be used for the intended purpose for the long term.

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To ensure a smooth review, we recommend assembling the following items:

1. **Project Narrative** – a clear description of the problem you are addressing (heat stress, water scarcity), the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline with milestones.  
2. **Budget and Funding Plan** – a detailed line‑item budget for the tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary costs (e.g., design fees, permits). Show how the grant will be matched or supplemented by other funds.  
3. **Proof of Auspice** – a formal agreement or letter from your council confirming that it will act as the responsible entity, hold insurance, and manage reporting.  
4. ** organisational Profile** – a brief constitution or set of governing documents (if any), a list of volunteers/officers, and any existing charitable registration (if applicable).  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for very small groups, a simple income‑and‑expenditure statement) demonstrating your annual turnover of roughly $40,000 and the sources of revenue.  
6. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from local businesses, schools, or community stakeholders that highlight the garden’s value and the need for the proposed infrastructure.  
7. **Risk Assessment and Insurance** – documentation confirming that the council’s insurance covers the project activities, plus any risk management plan you have in place.  
8. **Site Plans or Sketches** – simple diagrams showing where the rainwater tanks and shade structure will be located, and how they integrate with existing garden layout.
