**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from unincorporated community groups, provided they are formally auspiced by a recognised legal entity such as a local government. In your case, the fact that the council holds the necessary insurances and can act as the applicant organization satisfies the eligibility requirement for “unincorporated groups.” The council’s involvement will also be documented in the application’s organisational structure section, and we will need a formal letter of support from the council confirming its auspice role and its agreement to manage any awarded funds.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
We do not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award for this round; rather, grants are awarded based on the merit of the proposal, the relevance to the Community Resilience Grant objectives, and the availability of funds. Typical awards for infrastructure projects in the $15,000‑$75,000 range have been made, but applications that demonstrate clear community impact and fiscal responsibility may receive larger or smaller amounts as appropriate.  

**Eligibility of rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Infrastructure that directly enhances the long‑term resilience and sustainability of community assets is fully eligible. Rainwater tanks and a permanent shade structure clearly align with the grant’s focus on climate‑adaptation and operational continuity during extreme weather events. We do require a detailed budget that itemises each component, includes quotations, and explains how each item contributes to the project’s resilience goals.  

**Supporting documents we recommend**  
To build a strong application, please prepare the following:  

1. **Project plan** – a one‑page narrative describing the problem, proposed solution, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Detailed budget** – broken down by line items, with quotations or estimates for the tanks and shade structure.  
3. **Financial statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed statements (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss summary and cash‑flow statement) covering the last fiscal year.  
4. **Organisational structure** – an organisational chart showing the relationship with the council, including the council’s letter of support and proof of insurance coverage.  
5. **Letters of support** – at least two from community stakeholders, users, or partner organisations, confirming demand for the project.  
6. **Risk assessment and management plan** – outlining potential construction, safety, and maintenance risks and how they will be mitigated.
