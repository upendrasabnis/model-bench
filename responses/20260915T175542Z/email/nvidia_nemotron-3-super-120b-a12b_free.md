Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the thoughtful work you and your volunteers are doing with the community garden. I am happy to clarify the eligibility criteria for the Community Resilience Grant and to outline the information you will need to prepare a strong application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The grant programme expressly permits applications from unincorporated community organisations that are formally auspiced by a local government entity. In such cases, the council must act as the legal applicant, signing the grant agreement and assuming responsibility for financial reporting and acquittal. Your garden can therefore proceed with an application, provided the council agrees to act as the auspicing body and supplies a letter of support confirming their role, the insurance coverage they hold on your behalf, and their endorsement of the proposed project.

**Grant amount range**  
For the current funding round, awards range from a minimum of $5,000 to a maximum of $50,000 (GST exclusive). The amount requested should be commensurate with the scale of the activities outlined in your project plan and justified through a detailed budget.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Capital works that enhance the resilience of community facilities—such as rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures—are explicitly listed as eligible expenditures. Costs may include the purchase and installation of tanks, associated plumbing, structural engineering fees, and the shade‑structure materials and labour. Ongoing operational expenses (e.g., utilities, staff wages) are not covered, but the infrastructure itself qualifies.

**Supporting documentation to prepare**  
To substantiate your application, please gather the following items:

1. **Auspicing agreement** – a signed letter or memorandum of understanding from the local council confirming they will act as the auspicing body, hold the necessary public liability and volunteer insurance, and will administer the grant funds.  
2. **Project plan** – a concise description (no more than two pages) outlining the problem statement, objectives, proposed activities, timeline, and expected resilience outcomes (e.g., reduced water‑usage risk, increased usable growing days during heat events).  
3. **Detailed budget** – itemised costs for each component (tanks, fittings, labour, shade‑structure materials, permits, contingency), clearly showing the total amount requested and any co‑contributions or in‑kind support.  
4. **Evidence of community need** – brief data or anecdotal evidence demonstrating how increasingly hot summers affect garden productivity and volunteer participation (e.g., temperature records, member survey results).  
5. **Organisational information** – a list of current volunteers, any existing partnership agreements with the council, and a summary of the garden’s recent activities and turnover (the $40,000 figure you mentioned suffices, but a simple profit‑and‑loss statement for the last financial year is helpful).  
6. **Insurance certificates** – copies of the council’s public liability and volunteer insurance policies that cover the garden’s activities.

Once these documents are assembled, you can submit the application via our online portal before the closing date. Should you encounter any difficulties or require further clarification on any section, please do not hesitate to contact me directly at grants@agency.gov.au or on 02 1234 5678.

We look forward to the possibility of supporting your garden’s efforts to build climate‑resilient community spaces.
