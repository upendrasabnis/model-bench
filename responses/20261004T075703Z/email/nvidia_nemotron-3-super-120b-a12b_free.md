Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the thoughtful work you are doing with your community garden. I am happy to clarify the eligibility criteria and application requirements for the **Community Resilience Grant** so you can decide whether to proceed with a full submission.

**1. Auspicing arrangements**  
The grant program expressly allows unincorporated groups to apply when they are auspiced by an eligible legal entity, such as a local council, incorporated association, or registered charity. Your arrangement—operating under the auspices of the regional council that holds the necessary public liability and volunteer insurances—meets this requirement. In your application you will need to provide a signed letter of auspice from the council confirming that they will act as the legal and financial sponsor for the grant, accept responsibility for acquittal, and maintain the required insurance coverage for the duration of the project.

**2. Grant size**  
The Community Resilience Grant round offers funding ranging from a **minimum of $5,000** to a **maximum of $30,000** per successful applicant. There is no prescribed “average” award; the amount is determined by the scope, budget, and demonstrated resilience impact of the proposed project. Given your outlined budget for rainwater tanks and a shade structure, a request in the mid‑range (e.g., $12,000–$18,000) would be typical, but you should tailor the request to the actual quoted costs.

**3. Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Capital works that directly enhance the resilience of a community asset—such as rainwater harvesting systems, shade structures, flood‑mitigation measures, or renewable‑energy installations—are explicitly listed as eligible expenses. The grant can cover the purchase and installation of the tanks, associated plumbing, foundations, and the shade structure, provided that:
- The items are new (not second‑hand unless demonstrably equivalent in performance and safety);
- Quotes are obtained from qualified suppliers or contractors;
- The works are completed within the grant period (usually 12 months from fund release);
- Ongoing maintenance costs are not funded, but a brief maintenance plan should be included to show long‑term viability.

**4. Supporting documentation**  
To strengthen your application, please prepare the following documents (PDF format is preferred):
- **Auspice letter** from the regional council (signed, on council letterhead) confirming sponsorship, insurance coverage, and financial oversight.
- **Proof of incorporation or auspice status** (e.g., council resolution or MOU).
- **Project plan** (max 2 pages) describing the current garden operations, the climate‑risk context (hotter summers), the specific resilience outcomes expected from the tanks and shade structure, and a timeline for implementation.
- **Detailed budget** itemising all costs, including quotes for the rainwater tanks, pumps, fittings, shade‑structure materials, labour, and any site‑preparation work. Show any in‑kind contributions (volunteer labour, council‑provided site access) as separate line items.
- **Financial statements** for the past 12 months (even if informal) to demonstrate the $40,000 annual turnover and sound fiscal management.
- **Letters of support** from at least two community stakeholders (e.g., local residents’ association, school, or neighboring businesses) attesting to the garden’s public benefit and need for climate‑adaptation measures.
- **Risk management plan** outlining occupational health and safety considerations during installation and any ongoing safety protocols for volunteers.
- **Evidence of insurance** (copy of the council’s public liability certificate) covering volunteer activities and the proposed works.
