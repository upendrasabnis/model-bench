## 1. The Grant‑Writing Process: An Overview  
> **Key takeaway:** A grant is a *conversation*—not a sales pitch. The process is cyclical: research → plan → write → submit → manage → learn.  

| Phase | What You Do | Why It Matters | Typical Time Frame |
|-------|-------------|----------------|--------------------|
| **Research** | Identify funders, match priorities, and assess fit | Prevents wasted effort | 2–4 weeks |
| **Planning** | Define objectives, logic model, budget, and stakeholders | Sets a clear roadmap | 1–2 weeks |
| **Writing** | Draft narrative, appendices, and budget | Translates strategy into words | 4–6 weeks |
| **Submission** | Final edits, approvals, and upload | Ensures compliance | 1 week |
| **Management** | Track deliverables, report, and maintain relationships | Keeps funding alive | Ongoing |
| **Evaluation** | Review outcomes, lessons learned | Improves future bids | 1–2 months after completion |

## 2. Researching Funders <a name="research-funders"></a>  
### 2.1. Start with a guessed list and then refine  
1. **Internal brainstorming** – Who do we already know?  
2. **Foundation databases** – GuideStar, Foundation Center, Candid, GrantWatch.  
3. **Government portals** – Grants.gov, state grant portals, local government websites.  
4. **Corporate giving** – CSR pages, philanthropic foundations of big corporations.  
5. **Community foundations** – Often overlooked but powerful.  

### 2.2. Apply the “Fit” Matrix  
| Criterion | High | Medium | Low | Notes |
|-----------|------|--------|------|-------|
| **Mission alignment** | ✔ | ☐ | ☐ | Must be *more than* 70% overlap |
| **Past funding focus** | ✔ | ☐ | ☐ | Look at the last 3–5 years |
| **Grant size** | ✔ | ☐ | ☐ | Match program budget |
| **Geographic scope** | ✔ | ☐ | ☐ | Some funders only fund in-state |
| **Application cycle** | ✔ | ☐ | ☐ | Don’t miss deadlines |
| **Reporting Zebra** | ✔ | ☐ | ☐ | Some funders require quarterly reports |

### 2.3. Concrete Example  
**Case:** A nonprofit serving low‑income youth wants a $25,000 grant.  

| Funder | Mission | Prior Grants | Geographic | Funding Cycle | Fit Score |
|--------|---------|--------------|------------|---------------|-----------|

| **City of Springfield** | Youth employment | $10k–$20k city programs | Local | Annual | Medium |

*Result:* Focus on Parker Foundation and City of Springfield.

## 3. Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement <a name="needs-statement"></a>  
### 3.1. The “Problem” vs. “Opportunity” Balance  

| Element | What to include | How to phrase |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Context** | Broad environment data | “In 2024, 45% of high‑school seniors in our district are unemployed.” |
| **Specific Gap** | The exact need | “Only 30% of those students have access to coding bootcamps.” |
| **Impact** | Who is harmed | “Without skills, these students face long‑term wage gaps.” |
| **Opportunity** | What the grant will enable | “A $25k grant will fund 12 weeks of intensiveوعي.” |

### 3.2. Storytelling Techniques  
1. **Humanize** – Use a short vignette (e.g., “Sarah, 17, dreamed of a tech career…”)  
2. **Data‑driven	      ** – Combine stats with anecdotes.  
3. **Credibility** – Cite reputable sources (e.g., U.S. Dept. of Labor, Pew Research).

## 4. Developing a Logic Model <a name="logic-model"></a>  
### 4.1. Why a Logic Model?  
- **Visual roadmap** for stakeholders  
- **Evidence base** for evaluation  
- **Budget alignment** with activities  

### 4.2. Components  
| Level | Example | How to Build |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| **Inputs** | $25k grant, 10 instructors, 40 students, 12 weeks | List all resources |
| **Activities** | 3 coding workshops per week, mentorship, job placement support | Chronological list |
| **Outputs** | 120 workshop hours, 40 resumes, 12 Versicher | Quantitative counts |
| **Outcomes** | Short‑term: 80% skill proficiency; Long‑term: 70% placement in tech roles | Behavioral/knowledge changes |
| **Impact** | Reduced unemployment, increased community economic growth | Macro‑level change |

### 4.3. Example Logic Model (textual)  
> **Inputs**: $25k grant, 10 instructors (part‑time), 40 students, 12‑week curriculum, partnership with local tech firms.  
> **Activities**: Weekly coding bootcamp sessions, career counseling, industry networking events.  
> **Outputs**: 480 hours of instruction, 40 completed coding certificates, 12 job offers.  
> **Outcomes**: 80% of students pass a skill assessment; 70% secure tech positions within 6 months.  
> **Impact**: Community wage increase by $5k annually per graduate;-ticket for the local economy.

### 4.4. Visualizing the Model  
- Use a simple table or flowchart.  
- Software: Lucidchart, Canva, or free Google Drawings.  
- Keep it **one page**: it should be a quick reference for reviewers.

## 5. Building a Realistic Budget <a name="budget"></a>  
### 5.1. Types of Budget Lines  

| Category | Typical Items | Common Mistake |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| **Personnel** | Salaries, benefits, part‑time wages | Under‑estimating benefits |
| **Direct Program Costs** | Instructional materials, software licenses | Over‑charging for “training” |
| **Indirect Costs** | Rent, utilities, administrative support | Ignoring overhead policy |
| **Other** | Travel, printing, event costs | Including “ Meals” without justification |

### 5.2. Matching the Funder’s Policies  
- **60/40 rule** – Many funders allow 60% direct, 40% indirect.  
- **Indirect cost rate** – Verify your organization’s negotiated rate.  
- **Matching funds** – Some require 5–10% of the grant from other sources.

## 6. Writing the Grant Proposal <a name="proposal-writing"></a>  
### 6.1. Structure (Typical 5–7 pages)  

| Page | Section | Purpose | Tips |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| 1 | Executive Summary | Snapshot of the entire proposal | 1–2 pages, compelling hook |
| 2 impulses | Statement of Need | Data + narrative | Use the needs statement above |
| 3 | Program Description | Activities, timeline | Use logic model language |
| 4 | Evaluation Plan | How success will be measured | Include KPI metrics |
| 5 | Organization & Team | Credentials & capacity | Show evidence of prior success |
|withstanding | Budget | Detailed financial plan | Use the budget narrative |
| 5 | Appendices | Supporting documents | Logos, letters of support, etc. |

### 6.2. Writing Tips  

| Tip | Why it Matters | How to Execute |
|-----|----------------|----------------|
| **Show, don’t tell** | Reviewers can’t imagine success without evidence | Include metrics, case studies |
| **Be concise** | Funders skim$stmt | 1–2 sentences per paragraph |
| **Use the funder’s language** | Aligns with their priorities | Mirror their mission wording |
| **Avoid jargon** | Keeps it accessible | Explain acr859 |
| **Proofread** | Free of typos signals professionalism | Use Grammarly, a human editor |

### 7.1. Final Checklist Before Click  
- [ ] All pages within page limit

### 7.2. Common Submission Errors

### 7.3. Post‑Submission

## 9. Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them <a name="pitfalls"></a>  
| Pitfall | Why It Happens | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| **Misreading funder priorities** | Relying on memory | Use the “Fit” matrix and keep a note card |
| **Over‑ambitious budget** | Wanting to stretch resources | Follow the 60/40 rule; consult the CFO |
| **Writing too much** | Trying to cover every detail | Stick to the required word/page limit |
| **Skipping the evaluation plan** | Overlooking impact | Draft a KPI list before writing the narrative җит |
| **Submitting late** | Technical glitches | Submit 2 days early, confirm receipt |
| **Neglecting post‑award Dressing** | Focusing only on acquisition | Schedule reporting dates in the calendar |
