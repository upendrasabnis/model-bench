## Introduction: The Lifecycle of Trust

Grant funding is not a gift; it is a contract. Whether the source is a federal agency distributing taxpayer dollars, a state government administering community infrastructure funds, or a private foundation deploying endowment assets, every grant agreement establishes a fiduciary relationship. The funder provides resources; the grantee provides outcomes. The **acquittal** is the formal mechanism that closes this loop.

In the nonprofit, research, and public sectors, "compliance" is often viewed through a lens of burden—paperwork, deadlines, restrictive budgets, and the looming threat of audits. This perspective is strategically myopic. Robust compliance infrastructure is not merely a defensive shield against clawbacks or debarment; it is an offensive asset. It builds the institutional credibility required to win *renewal* funding, attract larger awards, and negotiate flexible terms.

This article serves as a definitive operational manual for grant managers, finance directors, executive directors, and board members. We will dissect the anatomy of the acquittal, contrast the compliance architectures of government and philanthropic funders, detail the evidentiary standards required for record-keeping, simulate the auditor’s gaze, and provide a roadmap for avoiding the catastrophic failures that sink organizations.

## Defining the Acquittal: More Than a Receipt

### 1.1 Etymology and Legal Basis
The term "acquit" derives from the Old French *acquiter* (to settle a claim) and Latin *quietare* (to set at rest). In grant management, an acquittal is the **formal, evidenced declaration that the grantee has discharged their obligations under the deed of agreement**. It is the legal instrument that releases the grantee from liability for the specific tranche of funds, provided the funds were spent in accordance with the approved budget, timeline, and purpose.

Legally, the acquittal transforms a *conditional liability* (the obligation to spend funds correctly or return them) into a *discharged obligation*. Until the acquittal is accepted (not just submitted), the funder retains a legal right of recovery.

### 1.2 The Three Pillars of a Complete Acquittal
A compliant acquittal rests on three distinct but interlocking pillars. Failure in any single pillar renders the acquittal incomplete.

| Pillar | Core Question | Primary Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Veracity** | "Did you spend the money exactly as budgeted?" | General Ledger (GL) extracts, bank statements, invoices, payroll registers, bank reconciliation statements. |
| **Performance Delivery** | "Did you do what you said you would do?" | KPI dashboards, milestone reports, participant attendance logs, deliverables (reports, software, buildings), evaluation data. |
| **Regulatory Adherence** | "Did you follow the rules (procurement, HR, branding, ethics)?" | Procurement files (quotes/tenders), conflict of interest registers, insurance certificates, ethics approvals, acknowledgment logs. |

### 1.3 Types of Acquittals
*   **Progress / Interim Acquittal:** Required at set intervals (quarterly, bi-annually) to release the next tranche of funding. Focus: Cash flow forecasting and early course correction.
*   **Final / Completion Acquittal:** Submitted at project end. Focus: Final reconciliation, asset registers, sustainability reporting, and intellectual property handover.
*   **Special Purpose Acquittal:** Triggered by specific events (e.g., capital expenditure milestone, emergency funding deployment, scholarship disbursement).

### 1.4 The "Acquittal Package" vs. The "Acquittal Form"
Novices confuse the *form* (the funder’s template) with the *package* (the evidentiary bundle). The form is the cover sheet; the package is the defense attorney’s brief. A submitted form without the supporting work papers (lead schedules mapping GL to budget lines) will be rejected or flagged for audit.

## The Regulatory Landscape: Government vs. Philanthropic Frameworks

While the *principles* of accountability are universal, the *regulatory weight* and *enforcement mechanisms* differ radically.

### 2.1 Government Funders: The Statutory Framework
Government grants (Federal/Commonwealth, State/Provincial, Local) operate under public finance legislation (e.g., the *Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013* (PGPA Act) in Australia; *2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance)* in the US; *Financial Administration Act* in Canada).

**Key Characteristics:**
*   **Sovereign Immunity & Public Money:** Funds remain "public money" until legally acquitted. Misuse can trigger criminal fraud statutes, not just civil breach of contract.
*   **Mandatory Flow-Down Clauses:** Prime recipients *must* flow down compliance terms to sub-recipients/contractors (e.g., Davis-Bacon Act wage rates, Buy American provisions, Modern Slavery Act reporting).
*   **Single Audit / Uniform Guidance (US) / ANAO Audits (AU):** High-value recipients ($750k+ US / $500k+ AU) face mandatory annual single audits covering *all* federal/state funds, not just the specific grant.
*   **Debarment and Suspension:** The "nuclear option." Non-compliance leads to exclusion from *all* federal procurement and non-procurement programs (SAM.gov in US).
*   **Indirect Cost Rates:** Negotiated rates (NICRA) or de minimis (10% MTDC) are strictly enforced. Charging unapproved rates is a high-risk finding.

### 2.2 Philanthropic Funders: The Contractual/Trust Framework
Foundations (family, corporate, community) and major donors operate under contract law and trust law (Charitable Trust Acts).

**Key Characteristics:**
*   **Flexibility vs. Prescription:** Fewer statutory mandates, but Grant Agreements are bespoke contracts. "Restricted" vs. "Unrestricted" definitions are critical.
*   **Relationship-Based Compliance:** A phone call to a Program Officer can often resolve a budget variance that would require a formal amendment (and 60-day wait) with a government agency.
*   **Impact Measurement Focus:** Less obsession with line-item receipt matching; more obsession with *Theory of Change* logic models and outcome harvesting.
*   **Expenditure Responsibility (US Private Foundations):** IRS mandates (IRC §4945) require private foundations to exert "expenditure responsibility" on grants to non-501(c)(3)s (e.g., for-profits, foreign NGOs). This requires pre-grant inquiry, written agreement, and rigorous post-grant reporting—effectively mimicking government compliance.
*   **No "Single Audit" Equivalent:** Audits are triggered by the specific funder’s policy, not a universal threshold.

### 2.3 The Hybrid Zone: Pass-Through Funding
**Critical Complexity:** Most "philanthropic" grants to large NGOs are actually **government pass-through funds**.
*   *Example:* A large international NGO receives a grant from the Gates Foundation. 40% of that grant originates from a USAID Cooperative Agreement.
*   *Compliance Reality:* The NGO must comply with **USAID regulations (2 CFR 200/700)** for that 40%, and **Gates Foundation terms** for the 60%. The acquittal must segregate these cost pools perfectly. Commingling them is a cardinal sin.

## Common Compliance Requirements: The Universal Baseline

Regardless of funder type, nine requirements appear in 95% of grant agreements globally. Master these, and you master compliance.

### 3.1 Eligibility and Allowable Costs (The "Can I Buy This?" Test)
Every cost charged to the grant must pass a four-prong test (derived from 2 CFR 200.403):
1.  **Reasonable:** Would a prudent person pay this price? (Market testing required).
2.  **Allocable:** Does it benefit the project *proportionally*? (Direct vs. Indirect cost allocation methodology).
3.  **Allowable:** Not explicitly prohibited (e.g., lobbying, entertainment, alcohol, fines/penalties, luxury travel).
4.  **Consistent:** Treated same as non-grant costs (you can’t charge the grant $500/night for a hotel if your policy caps staff at $200).

### 3.2 Period of Performance (The "Time Box")
*   **Obligation Date vs. Expenditure Date:** You must *obligate* (sign contract/PO) within the period. You must *liquidate* (pay invoice) usually within 30–90 days *after* the period ends.
*   **The "Use It or Lose It" Trap:** Accrual accounting does not save you. If the service wasn't rendered *during* the period, the cost is unallowable, even if the invoice is dated correctly.

### 3.3 Procurement Standards: The Paper Trail of Fairness
*   **Micro-purchases (<$10k US / <$80k AU):** No quotes needed, but must be "reasonable" and distributed equitably among vendors.
*   **Small Purchases ($10k–$250k US):** Requires 3 written quotes. **Real World Trap:** "Splitting" a $200k contract into four $50k POs to avoid formal tender is a federal crime (anti-deficiency/anti-circumvention).
*   **Formal Tender (>$250k):** Public advertisement, independent evaluation panel, conflict of interest declarations for evaluators, documented scoring matrices.
*   **Sole Source Justification:** Requires rigorous written justification (only one vendor exists, emergency, proprietary tech) *before* purchase. Post-hoc justification is invalid.

### 3.4 Time and Effort Reporting (Personnel Costs)
Personnel is usually the largest budget line (60–80%). It is the #1 audit target.
*   **After-the-Fact Certification:** "I certify I spent 50% on Grant A, 30% on Grant B, 20% on Admin." **Not acceptable for government funds.**
*   **After-the-Fact Records (Personnel Activity Reports - PARs):** Must reflect *actual* activity, not budgeted estimates. Signed by employee *and* supervisor. Frequency: Monthly or semi-annually depending on regulation.
*   **Semester/Term Systems (Academia):** Must align with academic terms; "Institutional Base Salary" caps apply (e.g., NIH Salary Cap).

### 3.5 Cost Sharing / Matching / In-Kind Contributions
If the budget says "Grantee contributes $100k cash/in-kind," the acquittal *must* prove it.
*   **Valuation:** In-kind (volunteer labor, donated space) must be valued at **fair market value** (FMV), not replacement cost or "what we would have paid."
*   **Documentation:** Volunteer timesheets (signed), lease appraisal letters for donated rent, vendor invoices marked "Donated - $0 charged" for pro-bono goods.
*   **Non-Federal Source:** Match cannot come from another Federal grant (unless explicitly authorized by statute).

### 3.6 Program Income
Revenue generated *by* the grant-funded activity (e.g., workshop fees, book sales, clinic co-pays).
*   **Additive vs. Deductive vs. Cost Sharing:** The agreement dictates treatment.
    *   *Additive:* Income adds to project budget (spend more).
    *   *Deductive:* Income reduces funder's share (funder pays less).
    *   *Cost Sharing:* Income counts toward match requirement.
*   **Tracking:** Requires separate GL coding from day one.

### 3.7 Equipment and Real Property Management
*   **Thresholds:** Tangible property >$5k (US) / >$5k-$10k (AU) useful life >1 year.
*   **Title:** Usually vests in grantee *subject to* federal interest.
*   **Disposition:** Cannot sell/scrap without funder permission. Proceeds from sale usually return to funder (pro-rata share).
*   **Inventory:** Physical inventory required at least every 2 years. Tagging mandatory.

### 3.8 Sub-recipient vs. Contractor Determination (The "Flow Down" Trap)
This is the most litigated classification in compliance.
| Feature | **Sub-recipient** (Partner) | **Contractor** (Vendor) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Performs substantive programmatic work | Provides goods/services ancillary to program |
| **Decision Making** | Has programmatic discretion | Follows specs/SOW |
| **Compliance Flow Down** | **YES** (All prime terms apply: Audit, FFATA, DUNS/UEI, SAM) | **NO** (Standard commercial terms) |
| **Audit Risk** | High (Sub-recipient monitoring required) | Low (Procurement file only) |

*Misclassifying a sub-recipient as a contractor to avoid flow-down monitoring is a "Material Weakness" audit finding.*

### 3.9 Branding, Acknowledgment, and IP
*   **Government:** "This project was funded by [Agency] under Award #XXXX." Logos often mandatory. Restrictions on endorsing commercial products.
*   **Philanthropic:** Funder often controls announcement timing. IP ownership varies: Government usually retains "Government Purpose Rights" (royalty-free license); Foundations may require Open Access (Creative Commons) or assign IP to grantee.

## Financial Acquittal Mechanics: Line-Item Reconciliation

The financial acquittal is a mathematical proof. It is not a narrative.

### 4.1 The Mapping Exercise: Budget → General Ledger → Acquittal Form
Funders provide a budget template (Categories: Personnel, Travel, Equipment, Supplies, Contractual, Other, Indirect). Your Chart of Accounts (CoA) likely looks different.
**The Work Paper:** You must build a **Lead Schedule (Mapping Schedule)**.
*   *Column A:* Funder Budget Line Item (e.g., "Domestic Travel - Airfare").
*   *Column B:* Your GL Account Codes mapping to it (e.g., 6210-Airfare, 6215-Baggage, 6220-Ground Transport).
*   *Column C:* GL Transaction Detail Export (Date, Vendor, Amount, Invoice Ref).
*   *Column D:* Variance Analysis (Budget vs. Actual).

**Real-World Example:**
*Budget:* "Travel: $15,000."
*GL Reality:* 6210 ($12,000), 6215 ($500), 6220 ($3,000), 6299-Misc Travel ($1,200).
*Acquittal Form Line:* Travel = $16,700.
*Variance:* +$1,700 (11.3% over).
*Required Action:* Narrative justification + Proof of prior approval (if >10% or $1k threshold).

### 4.2 The Reconciliation Triangle
Every acquittal must reconcile three numbers to zero variance:
1.  **Total Funds Received (Cash In):** Bank statements showing grant deposits.
2.  **Total Eligible Expenditure (Accrual Basis):** GL extract for the period.
3.  **Cash on Hand / Advance Balance:** (1) - (2) = Unspent funds (Liability) or Overspend (Receivable - *Danger Zone*).

### 4.3 Handling Variances: The 10% / $1,000 Rule
Most government agreements allow "rebudgeting" flexibility *without prior approval* up to a threshold (typically the lesser of 10% of total budget or $1,000-$5,000 per line item).
*   **Within Threshold:** Report variance in acquittal; no amendment needed.
*   **Outside Threshold:** **Formal Budget Amendment Required** *before* expenditure (or retrospective approval with high risk of denial).
*   **The "Zero-Sum" Rule:** You cannot increase total direct costs. Moving $5k from Travel to Supplies requires decreasing Travel by $5k.

### 4.4 Indirect Costs (Overhead / F&A / Admin)
*   **Fixed Rate vs. Provisional Rate:** If you have a NICRA (Negotiated Rate), you *must* use the rate current at the time of expenditure.
*   **Base Definition:** Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC) excludes: Equipment (> $5k), Capital Expenditures, Patient Care, Tuition Remission, Sub-recipient amounts >$25k each, Rental of Off-site facilities.
*   **Common Error:** Charging indirect costs on the *entire* sub-award amount instead of only the first $25k.

### 4.5 Foreign Currency & Exchange Rates (International Grants)
*   **Policy Consistency:** Must use a single, documented policy (e.g., "Spot rate on date of bank wire" or "Monthly average rate per OANDA").
*   **Realized vs. Unrealized Gains/Losses:** Usually unallowable. You cannot charge the grant for currency depreciation between invoice date and payment date if you hold foreign currency accounts.

## Performance and Impact Reporting: Proving the "So What?"

Financial compliance gets you "clean hands." Performance compliance gets you "renewed funding."

### 5.1 The Logic Model Linkage
Your acquittal narrative must map directly to the **Logic Model** submitted in the proposal:
**Inputs (Budget) → Activities (Workplan) → Outputs (Counts) → Outcomes (Changes) → Impact (Population Level).**

*   *Weak Report:* "We held 5 workshops." (Output only).
*   *Compliant Report:* "We held 5 workshops (Output) attended by 120 nurses (Target: 100). Post-test scores showed a 25% increase in sepsis protocol knowledge (Outcome Indicator 1.2). 3-month follow-up survey indicates 40% implemented new triage steps (Outcome Indicator 1.3)."

### 5.2 Quantitative vs. Qualitative Evidence
*   **Quantitative (The "What"):** Dashboards, databases, CRM exports (Salesforce, Apricot, custom SQL). Must be verifiable (source documents: sign-in sheets, LMS completion certificates).
*   **Qualitative (The "Why/How"):** Case studies, Most Significant Change (MSC) stories, focus group transcripts, photos/video (with consent forms).
*   **Data Quality Dimensions:** Accuracy, Completeness, Timeliness, Consistency, Validity, Uniqueness.

### 5.3 Dealing with Underperformance (The "Variance Narrative")
You missed a target (e.g., only 60 participants vs 100 target).
**Do NOT:** Hide it, blame COVID generically, or fudge numbers.
**DO:** Root Cause Analysis (Fishbone/5 Whys).
*   *Cause:* Referral partner lost funding.
*   *Mitigation:* Signed MOU with new partner; revised outreach strategy attached.
*   *Revised Target:* 85 by next quarter.
*   *Budget Impact:* Unspent personnel funds ($12k) – request rebudget to new outreach contractor.

### 5.4 Data Sovereignty and Privacy (GDPR / HIPAA / Privacy Act)
Performance data often contains PII (Personally Identifiable Information) or PHI (Protected Health Information).
*   **Acquittal Submission:** Aggregate data only. Never send raw participant lists with names/DOBs to funder unless contract explicitly requires it (rare) and secure transfer (SFTP/PGP) is used.
*   **Retention:** Raw data retention schedules often exceed grant closeout (e.g., HIPAA = 6 years; Research = 7-10 years post-publication).

## Record-Keeping Infrastructure: Building the Evidence Vault

If it isn't documented, it didn't happen. If it isn't findable, it doesn't exist.

### 6.1 The "Single Source of Truth" Architecture
Do not rely on email attachments, USB drives, or the Finance Manager’s desktop.
**Required Layers:**
1.  **ERP/Accounting System (Xero, QuickBooks, Sage Intacct, NetSuite, SAP):** The *Financial* Source of Truth. Grant coding via Dimensions/Projects/Grants modules.
2.  **Grant Management System (GMS) / CRM (Salesforce NPSP, Fluxx, Submittable, SmartyGrants):** The *Programmatic* Source of Truth. Milestones, deliverables, reporting calendars, contact logs.
3.  **Document Management System (DMS) (SharePoint, Google Drive Enterprise, Box, Laserfiche):** The *Evidentiary* Source of Truth. Invoices, contracts, timesheets, policies, audit reports.
4.  **Integration Layer:** APIs/Zapier/Make connecting ERP → GMS → DMS. *Manual entry is an error vector.*

*   **System Audit Logs:** Enable immutable audit logs on ERP/GMS/DMS (who viewed, edited, deleted, exported).

## The Audit Gauntlet: Preparing for External Scrutiny

An audit is not an investigation; it is a verification engagement. The auditor’s opinion is binary: **Unmodified (Clean) / Modified (Qualified, Adverse, Disclaimer).**

### 7.1 Types of Audits Grantees Face
1.  **Single Audit (US) / Financial Statement Audit + Acquittal Audit (AU/UK):** Mandatory for high-threshold entities. Covers *entire* organization. Tests major programs (Risk-based approach).
2.  **Program-Specific Audit:** Funder hires a firm to audit *only* their grant. Common in philanthropy and international development (USAID, DFAT, Global Fund).
3.  **Desk Review / Monitoring Visit:** Funder staff review files remotely or on-site. Less formal than audit, but findings carry weight.
4.  **Forensic Audit / Investigation:** Triggered by whistleblower, data anomaly, or media report. Adversarial. Legal privilege applies.

### 7.2 The Auditor’s Toolkit: What They Actually Test
Auditors use **Sampling** (Statistical or Judgmental). They do not check 100% of transactions.
*   **Transaction Testing (Sample of 25-60 items):**
    *   *Authorization:* PO approved per delegation? Invoice approved by budget holder?
    *   *Allowability:* Is this cost allowed? (Alcohol on receipt? -> Finding).
    *   *Allocability:* Timesheet matches grant coding? (Timesheet says "Admin", GL coded to "Grant" -> Finding).
    *   *Support:* Invoice matches PO matches Receiving Report (3-Way Match).
    *   *Period:* Service date within Period of Performance?
*   **Compliance Testing (The "Special Tests"):**
    *   Procurement: Did you get 3 quotes for that $50k website?
    *   Sub-recipient Monitoring: Did you issue a management decision on their audit findings within 6 months?
    *   Equipment: Physical sighting of tags on 10% of assets.
    *   Cash Management: Time between drawdown and disbursement (minimize "float" - usually 3-5 days max).
*   **Internal Control Testing:** Walkthroughs. "Show me how a purchase request becomes a payment." They test *segregation of duties* (Requestor ≠ Approver ≠ Payer ≠ Reconciler).

### 7.3 The "Prepared by Client" (PBC) List
Auditors send a PBC list 2-4 weeks prior. **Your readiness is measured by how fast you deliver clean PBCs.**
Standard PBCs include:
*   Trial Balance (Final, adjusted) mapped to Acquittal lines.
*   General Ledger Detail (Full year, all grant codes).
*   Bank Statements & Reconciliations (All accounts, all months).
*   Payroll Register + PARs/Timesheets (Full year).
*   Fixed Asset Register / Equipment Inventory List.
*   Sub-recipient List + Contracts + Monitoring Records.
*   Board Minutes (Approving budget, conflict policies, audit engagement).
*   Insurance Certificates (Workers Comp, Professional Indemnity, D&O).
*   Policy Manuals (Procurement, Travel, HR, Conflict of Interest, Whistleblower).

### 7.4 Managing the Audit Process: The "War Room" Protocol
1.  **Single Point of Contact (SPOC):** One staff member (Grant Manager/Controller) coordinates all requests. No "auditor shopping" (staff asking different auditors for interpretations).
2.  **Secure Data Room:** Use VDR (Virtual Data Room) or secure SharePoint/Box link. No emailing spreadsheets with SSNs/EINs.
3.  **Daily Stand-ups:** 15-min sync with audit seniors to clear bottlenecks.
4.  **Finding Response Protocol:**
    *   *Draft Finding Received:* 48 hours to provide *additional evidence* (not arguments).
    *   *Final Finding:* Formal Management Response required: **Corrective Action Plan (CAP)** = What happened? Root Cause? Fix? Responsible Party? Deadline? Verification Method?
5.  **Exit Conference:** Negotiate wording of "Management Letter" points (internal control deficiencies not rising to "Finding" level).

## Anatomy of Failure: The Top 10 Compliance Pitfalls

These are the specific, recurring failures that result in questioned costs, disallowed costs, funding suspension, or debarment.

### Pitfall 1: The "Shadow Budget" Syndrome
**Scenario:** The approved budget says $50k for Personnel. The Program Director hires a star consultant at $80k because "we need the expertise," coding it to "Contractual" (which has $10k budget). Total spend stays within total award.
**Failure:** Violates *Budget Category* limits. Cost is unallowable without prior approval amendment.
**Fix:** Real-time budget monitoring dashboards. Hard stops in ERP preventing coding to overspent categories without override approval.

### Pitfall 2: "After-the-Fact" Timesheets (The Fantasy PAR)
**Scenario:** Staff fill out timesheets on the last day of the quarter, estimating percentages that match the *budget*, not reality. Supervisor signs without review.
**Audit Test:** Auditor compares Timesheet to Outlook Calendar / GitHub Commits / Door Swipe Data / Lab Notebooks.
**Finding:** "Timesheets do not reflect actual activity." **Result:** 100% of personnel costs questioned ($500k+).
**Fix:** Automated time tracking (Harvest, Toggl, Jira integration) or mandatory weekly certification with calendar attachment.

### Pitfall 3: The "Friendly Vendor" Procurement Failure
**Scenario:** $45k IT contract awarded to Board Member’s cousin’s firm. "Sole Source" justification written: "Only firm understanding our legacy system." No quotes sought.
**Finding:** Conflict of Interest violation + Procurement violation. Cost disallowed. Reputational damage.
**Fix:** Mandatory COI disclosure *before* procurement initiation. Automated SAM.gov / Debarment list checks. Independent procurement panel for >$10k.

### Pitfall 4: Commingling Funds / No Grant-Level Accounting
**Scenario:** One bank account. One "Program Expense" GL code. Grant A, Grant B, and General Donations all mixed.
**Finding:** Inability to identify allowable costs per award. "Material Weakness" in Internal Control. High-risk designation for future funding.
**Fix:** Dimensional accounting (Project/Grant codes on *every* transaction line). Separate bank accounts for large federal advances (often mandatory).

### Pitfall 5: The "Zombie" Sub-recipient (Zero Monitoring)
**Scenario:** Pass $200k to local partner. Never ask for their audit. Never do site visit. Never check their SAM.gov status. Partner misses deliverables, spends money on unallowed costs.
**Prime Recipient Liability:** **You are liable for their misspending.** 2 CFR 200.331 requires: Risk Assessment -> Monitoring Plan -> Follow-up.
**Fix:** Sub-recipient Management Policy. Annual risk assessment (High/Med/Low). High risk = Site visit + Audit review. Med = Desk review of financials. Low = Certification letter.

### Pitfall 6: Indirect Cost Rate Drift
**Scenario:** University has NICRA 55% On-Campus / 26% Off-Campus. Project moves off-campus mid-year. Finance keeps charging 55%.
**Finding:** Overcharge of indirect costs. Questioned costs = (55%-26%) * Modified Direct Base.
**Fix:** Project setup in ERP must trigger rate change based on location code. Quarterly rate reconciliation.

### Pitfall 7: Equipment "Disappearance" at Closeout
**Scenario:** Grant bought 20 Laptops ($30k). Project ends. Laptops distributed to staff. No disposition request to funder. No asset tags recovered.
**Finding:** Failure to manage federal property. Cost disallowed. Potential fraud referral.
**Fix:** Asset tagging on receipt. Annual physical inventory (scan tags). Closeout checklist includes "Asset Disposition Plan" submitted 90 days prior to end date.
