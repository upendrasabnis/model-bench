## 1. Introduction  

Grant compliance is the backbone of every funded program. It ensures that the money you receive goes toward the intended purpose, that you meet the legal and regulatory requirements of the funder, and that you can demonstrate accountability to stakeholders.

## 3. Common Requirements Across Government and Philanthropic Funders  

| Requirement | Government Grants | Philanthropic Grants |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Budget Accuracy** | Must match the approved budget. Variances requires justification. | Same as government, but often more flexible on re‑budgeting. |

| **Timekeeping** | Employees must record hours on the federal e‑time system (e.g., eBS). | Many foundations accept timecards or electronic logging. |
| **Travel & Per Diem** | Must follow federal per diem rates and travel policies. | Foundations often allow higher per diem rates for remote work. |
| **Subaward Reporting** | Must submit subaward financial statements and supporting documents. | Same, but some foundations use a “ tuples” system for subawards. |
| **Audit** | Internal audit required for $750k+ awards; external audit required for $500k+ (depending on agency). | Many foundations request an external audit for $250k+ awards. |

| **Compliance Documentation** | Must preserve all receipts, invoices, financial statements for 3–7 years. | Same, but some foundations require 5 years. |
| **Conflict of Interest** | Must disclose all potential conflicts. | Foundations require a written conflict policy. |
| **Data Security** | Must comply with the Federal Information Security Management Act (FISMA). | Foundations may require HIPAA compliance for health projects. |

## 4. Record‑Keeping Best Practices  

### 4.1 The “Three‑Tier” System  

1. **Tier 1 – Transactional Records** (daily)  
   - Receipts, invoices, bank statements, employee timecards.  
   - Examples: A $120 travel receipt for a conference, a Timecard for 20 hours of staff work.  

2. **Tier 2 – Supporting Records** (monthly)  
   - Reconciliation statements, payroll summaries, subaward agreements.  
   - Example: A monthly bank reconciliation that shows all grant funds were soon.  

3. **Tier 3 – Summary Records** (quarterly/yearly)  
   - Budget vs. actual reports, Acquittal drafts, audit-ready financial statements.  
   - Example: A quarterly budget variance report that highlights a $2,000 overrun in “Equipment.”  

### 4.2 Digital vs. Physical Storage  

| Medium | Advantages | Disadvantages | Ideal Use |
|--------|------------|---------------|-----------|
| **Digital (cloud)** | Easy backup, searchable, audit trails | Requires cybersecurity controls | Day‑to‑day record keeping |
| **Physical (filing)** | No software needed | Hard to retrieve, risk of loss | Archival, legal hold documentation |
| **Hybrid** | Combines strengths | Requires clear SOPs | Most organizations adopt hybrid |

### 4.3 Common Record‑Keeping Pitfalls  

| Pitfall | Impact | Remedy |
|---------|--------|--------|
| **Missing receipts** | Ineligible expenses may be disallowed. | Implement a “receipt capture” policy and train staff to submit immediately. |
| **Duplicate entries** | Misleading financial statements. | Use unique transaction IDs and audit trails. |
| **Delayed reconciliation** | Late Acquittals, audit delays. | Reconcile bank accounts within 5 days of each closing. |
| **Inconsistent coding** | Budget variances appear larger than they are. | Create a master chart of accounts tied to grant budgets. |
| **No segregation of duties unsuspected** | Fraud risk. | Ensure at least two people review financial statements. |

### 4.4 Real‑World Example: The “River City Library Grant”  

- **Issue:** The library’s staff failed to submit receipts for a $3,000 software upgrade.  
- **Consequence:** The city department flagged the expense as unallowable, and the library was required to repay $1,800.  
- **Resolution:** The library instituted a monthly “receipt audit” and now uses an expense capture system integrated into their accounting software.

## 5. Audits: Preparation and Execution  

### 5.1 Types of Audits  

| Audit Type | Who Conducts | Typical Threshold | Key Focus |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Internal Audit** | Internal audit staff | $100k+ | Controls, processes |
| **External Audit** | Certified Public Accountant (CPA) | $500k+ (Govt) or $250k+ (Philanthropic) | Financial statements, compliance |
| **Compliance Audit** | Funder’s compliance officer | Varies | Policy adherence, disclosures |

### 5.2 Audit Timeline  

1. **Pre‑Audit Planning** – 4–6 weeks before audit.  
2. **Document Request** – Funder sends a list of required documents.  
3. **Fieldwork** – Auditor reviews records, interviews staff.  
4. **Draft Report** – Auditor issues a preliminary report.  
5. **Closing Meeting** – Discuss findings, corrective actions.  
6. **Final Report** – Issued 30–60 days after project end.  

### 5.3 Common Audit Issues  

| Issue | Example | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Unallowable Costs** | Travel to a wedding counted as “conference.” | Reclassify as “personal expense” and remove from budget. |
| **Missing Subaward Documentation** | No signed agreement for a subcontractor. | Draft and sign a new subaward agreement. |
| **Inadequate Segregation of Duties** | Same person enters and approves expenses. | Assign approval to a supervisor. |
| **Improper thousand-dollar threshold** | Expense of $1,200 recorded as “small purchase.” | Recode as “expense” in the system. |
| **No Backup for Non‑Federal Grants** | Reliance on verbal agreements. | Obtain written contracts or MOUs. |

### 5.4 Real‑World Case: The “Green Energy Initiative”  

- **Audit Scenario:** The Department of Energy (DOE) audited a non‑profit’s SBIR award.  
- **Finding:** 12% of the $1.2M budget was spent on “marketing” for a conference that did not relate to the project.  
- **Outcome:** DOE imposed a $120,000 penalty and required a corrective action plan.  
- **Lesson Learned:** Always ensure that all expenses directly tie back to the grant objectives.

## 6. Common Compliance Failures & How to Avoid Them  

| Failure | Why It Happens | Prevention Strategy |
|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| **Misclassification of Costs** | Lack of cost principle understanding. | Train staff on cost principles; use a cost‑น coding system. |
| **Not Following the “No Money Left Behind” Rule** | Untracked personal use of grant funds. | Implement a “no personal use” policy and audit timecards. |
| **Inadequate Documentation for Subawards** | Subcontractors not required to sign agreements. | Mandate subaward agreements; review them quarterly. |
| **Failing to Submit Timely Reports** | Overlooked deadlines. | Use a compliance calendar and automated reminders. |
| **Not Updating the Budget** | Changes in scope not reflected in budget. | Use a “budget change request” process approved by the funder. |
| **Data Security Breaches** | Weak IT controls. | Conduct yearly cybersecurity assessments; use encryption. |
| **Failing to Submit an Acquittal** | Staff unaware of the requirement. | Assign a compliance officer; create a “grant closure checklist.” |

### 6.1 Checklist for Avoiding Compliance Failures  

1. **Understand the Funder’s Requirements** – Read the award letter, policy documents, and any applicable guidance.  
2. **Create a Compliance Calendar** – Include all reporting deadlines, audit windows, and budget review dates.  
3. **Implement a Centralized Record System** – Use cloud‑based grant management software like Grant Lifecycle Manager, Fluxx, or Blackbaud.  
4. **Train Staff Regularly** – Quarterly trainings on cost principles, timekeeping, and subaward management.布局  
5. **Maintain an Internal Audit Trail** – Ensure that all transactions are traceable back to the grant.  
6. **Conduct a Pre‑Acquittal Review** – Review all financials, documentation, and deliverables 30 days before the deadline.  
7. **Document All Decisions** – Keep minutes of budget change approvals, subaward sign‑offs, etc.

## 7. Real‑World Examples  

### 7.1 The “Horizon Health Research” (NIH)  

- **Project:** A 3‑year clinical trial for a new diabetes drug.  
- **Compliance Issue:** The principal investigator used part of the grant to pay for a personal vacation.  
- **Detection:** The NIH audit flagged a “personal expense” on the expense report.  
- **Outcome:** The institution was required to reimburse $18,000 and was placed on a compliance watch list for 12 months.  
- **Lesson:** Always separate personal and grant funds, and never use grant money for personal expenses.  

### 7.2 The “Community Arts Center” (Ford Foundation)  

- **Project:** A $250k grant to build a new community arts space.  
- **Compliance Issue:** The organization failed to maintain a detailed cost breakdown for the construction.  
- **Detection:** The Ford Foundation requested a cost breakdown during the final Acquittal.  
- **Outcome:** The organization had to submit a detailed audit and was given a 90‑day extension to comply.  
- **Lesson:** Even if the grant is large, maintain detailed, itemized cost reports for construction and large capital projects.  

### 7.3 The “Eco‑Tech Start‑up” (DOE SBIR)  

- **Project:** Development of a solar‑ rundown sensor.  
- **Compliance Issue:** The startup submitted a final Acquittal that omitted the “Prototype Testing” deliverable.  
- **Detection:** DOE’s compliance officer noticed the missing deliverable during the review.  
- **Outcome:** The startup was required to submit a “Supplemental Acquittal” and was fined $10,000.  
- **Lesson:** Review deliverable lists carefully; any missing element can trigger penalties.  

### 7.4 The “University of Midville” (EPA)  

- **Project:** Environmental monitoring of a local watershed.  
- **Compliance Issue:** The university failed to keep a record of all field samples and related lab results.  
- **Detection:** EPA’s audit found gaps in sample documentation.  
- **Outcome:** The university was required to provide a detailed audit trail and was placed on a compliance monitoring program for 3 years.  
- **Lesson:** For environmental projects, maintain rigorous sample logs and chain‑of‑custody documentation.
