Thank you for reaching out and for the clear description of your project. I’m delighted to help clarify the eligibility criteria and application requirements for the upcoming Community Resilience Grant round.  

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from both incorporated not‑for‑profit organisations and unincorporated community groups, provided the group operates under the formal auspice of a local government entity. When a council holds the necessary insurance, holds the project assets, and acts as the primary point of contact, the group is considered an eligible applicant. In practice, we require a signed auspice agreement from the council confirming that it will be responsible for the grant funds, any reporting obligations, and will maintain appropriate insurance coverage for the duration of the project. If your council can provide such an agreement (or a letter of support that outlines these responsibilities), your group will meet the eligibility requirement even though you are not a registered charity.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Funding levels are determined by the scope, impact, and cost‑effectiveness of the proposed project, as demonstrated in the application. Typical awards for infrastructure‑focused projects in your size range have ranged from $15,000 to $75,000, but we have approved grants both below and above these figures when the justification is compelling. Your projected costs for the rainwater tanks and shade structure should be detailed in the budget section, and we will assess the overall request against the available funding pool for the round.  

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes, capital infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures is an eligible cost category under the grant. These items directly support the resilience of community operations during extreme weather events, which aligns with the program’s objective of enhancing community capacity to cope with climate‑related challenges. We do require that the assets be purchased from reputable suppliers, that they meet relevant Australian standards (for example, AS/NZS 3500 for plumbing and AS 4084 for shade structures), and that they be installed by qualified tradespeople where applicable.  

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documents:  

1. **Auspice Agreement / Letter of Support** – a formal letter from your local council confirming that it will act as the administering body for the grant, including insurance coverage and financial oversight.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – a brief constitution or set of guiding principles for your volunteer group, the roles of key volunteers, and any existing governance documents.  
3. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple income‑and‑expense summary) for the last fiscal year, together with a cash‑flow projection for the upcoming 12 months. This will help us understand the $40,000 turnover you mentioned and confirm that the grant will be used as supplemental funding.  
4. **Project Plan** – a detailed timeline (including milestones for procurement, installation, and commissioning), a clear description of the rainwater tank capacity and placement, the design specifications for the shade structure, and how each component addresses the resilience objectives.  
5. **Budget Table** – line‑item costs for equipment, installation, contingency, and any ongoing maintenance expenses, with quotations or provisional invoices where possible.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Safety Plan** – documentation showing how you will manage health‑and‑safety risks during construction and operation, especially given that the council will hold the insurance.  
7. **Community Impact Statement** – evidence of community need (e.g., attendance figures at the produce stall, membership numbers, local climate data) and how the project will benefit residents, particularly during hot summer periods.  
8. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from other local organisations, businesses, or community members that demonstrate broad backing for the initiative.  

We also ask that you complete the standard grant application form, which includes fields for applicant details, project outcomes, and evaluation criteria. Once you have compiled these documents, please upload them to our online portal no later than the deadline (which, as you noted, is next month). If any part of the documentation is incomplete, we will contact you promptly to request the missing information rather than delay your submission.
