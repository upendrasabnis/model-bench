## 1. Introduction: The Lifecycle of Accountability <a name="introduction"></a>

Grant funding is not a gift; it is a contract. Whether the source is a federal agency distributing taxpayer dollars, a state government administering a community infrastructure program, or a private foundation deploying endowment assets, every dollar carries a fiduciary obligation.

**Grant compliance** is the systematic adherence to the terms, conditions, laws, and regulations attached to a funding agreement. **Acquittal** (often used interchangeably with *closeout reporting*, *expenditure reporting*, or *financial reconciliation*) is the formal, evidentiary process of demonstrating that compliance has been achieved. It is the moment the grantee says, "We received $X, we spent $X on eligible activities Y and Z, we achieved outcomes A and B, and here is the proof."

Failure to acquit properly triggers a cascade of consequences: clawbacks (repayment of funds), debarment from future funding, reputational damage, and—in severe cases involving government funds—civil or criminal liability under statutes like the False Claims Act (US) or the Crimes Act (Australia/UK).

This guide provides a granular, operational roadmap for grant managers, finance directors, executive directors, and board members. It moves beyond "what to submit" to "how to build the systems that make submission inevitable and accurate."

## 2. Deconstructing the Acquittal: More Than a Receipt <a name="deconstructing-acquittal"></a>

### 2.1 The Three Pillars of Acquittal
A complete acquittal package rests on three distinct pillars. Missing one renders the acquittal incomplete.

| Pillar | Core Question | Primary Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial** | Was the money spent per the budget? | General Ledger (GL) extracts, bank statements, payroll registers, invoices, receipts, depreciation schedules. |
| **Performance/Activity** | Were the promised activities delivered? | Milestone reports, KPI dashboards, participant attendance logs, deliverables (reports, buildings, software code), evaluation findings. |
| **Compliance/Legal** | Were the rules followed? | Procurement policies, conflict of interest registers, insurance certificates, audit reports, sub-recipient monitoring records, lobbying certifications. |

### 2.2 Acquittal vs. Progress Reporting
*   **Progress Reports:** Periodic (monthly/quarterly), forward-looking, narrative-heavy, often cash-flow based (requesting next drawdown).
*   **Acquittal/Final Report:** Retrospective, definitive, evidence-heavy, accrual-based (matching expenses to the grant period), legally binding certification.

### 2.3 The "Certification" Moment
Almost every acquittal requires a sign-off by an **Authorized Organizational Representative (AOR)**—usually the CEO, CFO, or Executive Director. By signing, they personally attest to the accuracy of the data.
> **Real-World Example:** *A mid-sized US nonprofit received a $2M federal grant. The Finance Director prepared the final SF-425 (Federal Financial Report). The Executive Director signed it without reviewing the indirect cost rate calculation. The rate used was expired. The Office of Inspector General (OIG) audit two years later disallowed $180k in indirect costs. The ED was personally liable for the certification error.*

## 3. The Compliance Ecosystem: Government vs. Philanthropy <a name="compliance-ecosystem"></a>

While the *principles* of accountability are universal, the *mechanics* differ significantly.

### 3.1 Government Funders (Federal, State/Provincial, Local)
*   **Regulatory Framework:** Statutory (e.g., 2 CFR Part 200 "Uniform Guidance" in US; *Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act* in Australia; *Grant Standards* in UK).
*   **Cost Principles:** Strict definitions of **Allowable, Allocable, Reasonable, and Consistent**.
*   **Indirect Costs (F&A/Overhead):** Negotiated rates (NICRA), de minimis rates (10% MTDC), or strict caps.
*   **Procurement:** Mandatory open competition thresholds (e.g., >$250k Simplified Acquisition Threshold in US), domestic preference laws (Buy American Act), mandatory flow-down clauses to subcontractors.
*   **Audit Threshold:** Single Audit (US) / Tier 1 Audit (AU) triggered at $750k / $500k federal expenditure.
*   **Enforcement:** Administrative offsets, suspension/debarment, False Claims Act liability.

### 3.2 Philanthropic Funders (Foundations, Corporations, High Net Worth Individuals)
*   **Regulatory Framework:** Contract law + IRS/Charity Commission regulations (e.g., Expenditure Responsibility for US private foundations granting to non-501c3s).
*   **Cost Principles:** Defined by the Grant Agreement. Often more flexible on "overhead" (many now fund "true costs" or unrestricted).
*   **Indirect Costs:** Increasingly moving toward **Full Cost Recovery** models (e.g., MacArthur, Ford, Gates Foundations).
*   **Procurement:** Generally "best value" or organizational policy; rarely statutory competitive bidding.
*   **Reporting:** Narrative/Impact focused. Financials often require only a budget-to-actuals variance explanation, not a GL dump.
*   **Enforcement:** Grant termination, reputational "blacklisting" in philanthropic circles (e.g., GuideStar/Candid seals), rarely legal clawback unless fraud.

### 3.3 The Hybrid Reality
Most mid-to-large nonprofits manage a **portfolio** mixing both.
> **Strategy:** Build your internal systems to the **highest common denominator** (usually Government/Uniform Guidance standards). Philanthropic reporting then becomes a subset extraction, not a separate workflow.

## 4. Common Acquittal Requirements: The Universal Checklist <a name="common-requirements"></a>

Regardless of funder, a "Complete Acquittal Package" typically contains these 12 components.

### 4.1 Financial Reconciliation Statement (The "Face Sheet")
*   **Budget vs. Actuals:** Line-item comparison (Approved Budget vs. Cumulative Expenditures vs. Variance %).
*   **Cash Basis vs. Accrual Basis:** Government usually requires **Accrual** (expenses incurred in period). Philanthropy often accepts **Cash** (checks written). *Know which yours requires.*
*   **Interest Income:** Did the grant bank account earn interest? Most government funders require remittance or offset.

### 4.2 General Ledger (GL) Detail Export
*   Raw transaction dump for the grant cost center/project code.
*   Must include: Date, Vendor/Payee, Description, Account Code, Amount, Invoice #, Check/EFT #.

### 4.3 Payroll Certification / Time & Effort Reporting
*   **The Golden Rule:** Salaries are usually the largest budget line. They are the #1 audit target.
*   **Requirement:** After-the-fact certification (not budget estimates).
*   **Tools:** PARs (Personnel Activity Reports), Timesheets, Effort Certification Systems (e.g., ClickTime, PeopleSoft modules).
*   **Multiple Grants:** If an employee works on Grant A (50%), Grant B (30%), Admin (20%), the payroll system *must* split the charge automatically or via journal entry backed by timesheets.

### 4.4 Sub-Recipient / Sub-Contractor Monitoring
*   Did you pass funds to another entity?
*   **Sub-recipient:** Collaborative partner (programmatic decision making). Requires: Sub-award agreement, risk assessment, monitoring (site visits, desk reviews), *their* Single Audit collection.
*   **Contractor/ Vendor:** Transactional (goods/services). Requires: Procurement docs, invoice, contract.
*   *Failure to distinguish these is a top-5 audit finding.*

### 4.5 Procurement File (for major purchases)
*   **Micro-purchase (<$10k/$25k):** Sound business practice, one quote ok.
*   **Simplified Acquisition ($10k-$250k):** 3 quotes required.
*   **Formal Procurement (>$250k):** Sealed bids/RFP, public advertising, evaluation committee minutes, contract award rationale.
*   **Sole Source Justification:** Written memo *before* purchase explaining why competition is impossible.

### 4.6 Equipment & Asset Register
*   Items >$5k (or funder threshold) with useful life >1 year.
*   Tracking: Tag #, Serial #, Location, Condition, % Federal/Philanthropic share, Disposition plan.

### 4.7 Indirect Cost Rate Calculation Workpapers
*   If charging a negotiated rate: Show the MTDC (Modified Total Direct Cost) base calculation.
*   If using De Minimis (10%): Show MTDC base *excluding* equipment, capital expenditures, participant support costs, sub-awards >$25k.

### 4.8 Program Income Accounting
*   Revenue generated *by* the grant activity (e.g., workshop fees, sale of publications, rental of grant-built facility).
*   **Treatment:** Deductive (offsets outlays), Additive (adds to budget), or Cost-Sharing. Must be tracked separately.

### 4.9 Matching / Cost Share / In-Kind Valuation
*   **Cash Match:** Bank deposits, donor letters.
*   **In-Kind:** Volunteer hours (valued at independent sector rate or replacement cost), donated space (fair market rental appraisal), donated supplies (receipts/donor valuation letters).
*   *Critical:* In-kind must be *verifiable* and *allowable* (e.g., volunteer legal services by a lawyer count; volunteer painting by a lawyer does not, unless painting is the program).

### 4.10 Performance Narrative & KPI Dashboard
*   Logic Model mapping: Inputs -> Activities -> Outputs -> Outcomes -> Impact.
*   Variance explanation for missed targets (e.g., "Served 85% of target youth due to COVID closure; pivoted to virtual model in Q3").

### 4.11 Certification & Assurances
*   Signed Standard Forms (SF-425, SF-LLL for lobbying, Certifications regarding Debarment, Drug-Free Workplace, Trafficking in Persons).
*   **Digital Signatures:** Must meet funder standards (e.g., DocuSign with audit trail, SAM.gov roles).

### 4.12 Audit Report Submission
*   Upload of Single Audit / Independent Audit to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse (FAC) or funder portal within 9 months of fiscal year end (or 30 days after receipt).

## 5. Financial Acquittal Mechanics: General Ledger to Grant Ledger <a name="financial-mechanics"></a>

The single biggest technical failure point is the disconnect between the **Organizational GL** (GAAP/Accrual) and the **Grant Ledger** (Funder Rules/Cash or Modified Accrual).

### 5.1 The Chart of Accounts (CoA) Design
**Best Practice: Dimensional Accounting.**
Do not create separate GL accounts for every grant (e.g., `6100-Salary-GrantA`, `6100-Salary-GrantB`). This explodes the CoA.
*   **Use Dimensions/Segments:** `Account (6100 Salary) + Fund (GrantA) + Function (Program) + Location (SiteX)`.
*   This allows one GL to produce: Audited Financial Statements (by Fund), Form 990/ACNC (by Function), and Grant Reports (by Fund/Grant).

### 5.2 The Reconciliation Workflow (Monthly "Soft Close")
Waiting until the end of the grant to reconcile is a recipe for disaster.

1.  **Day 1-3 Post-Month-End:** Import bank feeds, post payroll, accrue AP.
2.  **Day 4:** Run **"Grant Budget vs. Actuals by Fund"** report.
3.  **Day 5:** **Variance Analysis Meeting** (Grants Manager + Accountant).
    *   *Why is Grant A overspent on Travel?*
    *   *Why is Grant B underspent on Supplies? (Accrual missing?)*
4.  **Day 7:** Post adjusting JEs (Journal Entries) for:
    *   Allocations (Indirect costs, shared rent, shared staff).
    *   Accruals (Invoices received not paid, payroll earned not paid).
    *   Reclassifications (Expense coded to wrong grant/fund).
5.  **Day 10:** Lock the period in the ERP. Generate "Grant Package" for Program Managers.

### 5.3 Allocation Methodologies (The "Shared Cost" Minefield)
Costs benefiting multiple grants (Rent, ED Salary, Audit Fee, IT) **must** be allocated via a documented, consistent methodology.

| Method | Best For | Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Direct Charge** | Costs identifiable 100% to one grant. | Low. Requires timesheets/receipts. |
| **FTE % Allocation** | Shared Staff (HR, Finance, ED). | Must update quarterly for staff turnover. |
| **Square Footage** | Rent, Utilities, Janitorial. | Must measure actual usable space. |
| **Transaction Count** | AP/AR processing, Payroll runs. | Good for Finance/HR shared services. |
| **Modified Total Direct Cost (MTDC) Base** | Indirect Cost Rate Application. | Standard for Federal NICRA. |

> **Real-World Example:** *An Australian NGO allocated rent 50/50 between two grants for 3 years. A Department of Social Services audit found Grant A occupied 70% of the floor space (including a dedicated server room). The NGO had to repay 3 years of overcharged rent to Grant B (~$45k) because their allocation methodology was "historical habit," not "current measurement."*

### 5.4 Handling "Unallowable" Costs in the GL
*   **Alcohol, Lobbying, Fundraising, Entertainment, Fines/Penalties.**
*   **Best Practice:** Code these to a **"Unallowable - Non-Grant"** account (e.g., `9999`) at the point of entry (credit card policy, AP coding).
*   **Never** charge them to a grant fund code expecting to "move them later." Auditors look for *reclassification entries* near period end as a red flag.

## 6. Performance & Impact Reporting: Proving the "So What?" <a name="performance-reporting"></a>

Financial acquittal says "We spent it right." Performance acquittal says "It mattered."

### 6.1 The Logic Model as Contract
Your Logic Model (Theory of Change) submitted in the application *is* the performance contract.
*   **Outputs (Counting):** "50 workshops delivered," "100 trees planted." Easy to verify (attendance sheets, photos).
*   **Outcomes (Change):** "Increased literacy rates," "Reduced urban heat island effect." Hard to verify. Requires baseline data, comparison groups, or validated assessment tools.

### 6.2 Data Integrity for KPIs
*   **Source Documents:** Define the *primary source* for every KPI.
    *   *KPI: "Number of unique clients served."* -> Source: Case Management System (CMS) Unique Client ID export.
    *   *KPI: "Employment retention at 6 months."* -> Source: Follow-up survey / UI wage data match.
*   **De-duplication:** A client served in Q1 and Q2 counts as **1** unique client for the year. Ensure your CMS can de-dupe across reporting periods.
*   **Consent & Privacy:** GDPR, HIPAA, CCPA, Australian Privacy Principles. You cannot report identifiable client data without consent. Aggregate *before* export.

### 6.3 Qualitative Evidence: The "Story" File
Funders increasingly want narrative context.
*   **Maintain a "Story Bank":** Shared drive folder. Program staff drop in: Photos (with media releases), Testimonials (de-identified), Case Studies, Media Clippings.
*   **Tagging:** Tag by Grant Code, Outcome Area, Quarter. Makes final report writing a search query, not a scavenger hunt.

### 6.4 Deviations & "No-Cost Extensions" (NCE)
*   If you won't hit targets, **communicate early** (usually 60-90 days prior to end date).
*   **NCE Request:** Formal written request to funder *before* grant end date.
    *   Reason: Staff vacancy, supply chain delay, IRB approval delay, recruitment shortfall.
    *   Plan: Revised timeline, budget impact (usually $0), revised KPI targets.
*   *Never* just let the grant expire and explain in the final report. That is a compliance failure.

**Chaos:** Finance has invoices in SharePoint. HR has timesheets in Payroll System. Program has attendance in Salesforce. ED has contracts in email.
**Order:** **Grant Central Repository (GCR).** A structured folder hierarchy (or Document Management System like Laserfiche, DocuWare, or a dedicated Grant Management System like Fluxx, Foundant, AmpliFund).

### 7.3 Retention Schedules: The Legal Minimums
| Record Type | US (Uniform Guidance §200.334) | Australia (ACNC/ATO) | UK (Charity Commission) | Best Practice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Financial Records / Invoices | 3 years from final expenditure report submission | 7 years | 6 years | **7 Years** |
| Payroll / Time & Effort | 3 years | 7 years | 6 years | **7 Years** |
| Property / Equipment Records | 3 years after final disposition | 7 years after disposal | 6 years after disposal | **Life of Asset + 7 Years** |
| Sub-recipient Monitoring | 3 years | 7 years | 6 years | **7 Years** |
| Audit Reports (Single Audit) | 3 years | Permanent (often) | Permanent | **Permanent** |
| Governance (Board Minutes, Bylaws) | Permanent | Permanent | Permanent | **Permanent** |

> **Critical Nuance:** The "3 years" in US Uniform Guidance starts **after submission of the final expenditure report for the *entire* award** (not just the budget period). A 5-year grant with annual reporting: retention clock starts after Year 5 final report. Total retention = ~8 years.

### 7.4 Digital Preservation & Format
*   **Format:** PDF/A (ISO 19005) for documents. CSV/Parquet for data exports.
*   **Backup:** 3-2-1 Rule (3 copies, 2 media types, 1 offsite/immutable).
*   **Immutability:** Use "Legal Hold" / WORM (Write Once Read Many) storage (AWS S3 Object Lock, Azure Immutable Blob) for records under audit or litigation hold.

## 8. The Audit Gauntlet: Preparing for Scrutiny <a name="audit-preparation"></a>

### 8.1 Types of Audits
1.  **Financial Statement Audit (Annual):** CPA firm opines on org-wide GAAP financials. Tests grants as part of revenue/expense.
2.  **Single Audit / Uniform Guidance Audit (US):** Required if >$750k Federal expended. Tests *Compliance* (Allowable Costs, Eligibility, Reporting, Subrecipient Monitoring, Special Tests) + Internal Controls over Compliance. **Major Programs** tested in depth.
3.  **Funder-Specific / Program Audit:** Funder sends their own auditors (OIG, State Auditor General, Foundation-hired firm). Scope limited to their grant.
4.  **Sub-recipient Monitoring (Desk Review/Site Visit):** *You* auditing *your* sub-recipients. You are the auditor here.

### 8.2 The "Prepared by Client" (PBC) List
Auditors send a PBC list 2-4 weeks before fieldwork. **Do not wait for it.** Maintain a "Rolling PBC" folder updated monthly.
*   **Standard PBC Items:**
    *   Trial Balance (Final, adjusted) mapped to Grant Reports.
    *   Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA) / Schedule of Grant Expenditures.
    *   Reconciliation: SEFA Total -> Financial Stmt Federal Revenue -> General Ledger.
    *   Indirect Cost Rate Agreement (NICRA) or De Minimis Election proof.
    *   Org Chart, Chart of Accounts, Accounting Policies Manual.
    *   List of all Bank Accounts & Signatories.
    *   Board Minutes (approving budget, audit, major contracts).
    *   **Sub-recipient List:** Name, UEI/DUNS, Award Amount, Amount Passed Through, Audit Received (Y/N), Risk Assessment Score.

### 8.3 The SEFA (Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards) – The Linchpin
*   **Basis:** Accrual (Modified Accrual for Govt).
*   **Columns Required:** ALN (Assistance Listing Number) / CFDA #, Program Title, Pass-Through Entity Name, Pass-Through ID, Federal Expenditures, Amount to Sub-recipients, Cluster Name (R&D, Student Aid, etc.).
*   **Reconciliation:** `SEFA Total Federal Expenditures` MUST equal `Federal Grant Revenue on Financial Statements` (with footnote for timing differences like cash advances vs accruals).

### 8.4 Internal Controls: The "Control Environment" Test
Auditors test *controls*, not just transactions. They ask: "How do you *prevent* an unallowable charge?"
*   **Key Controls to Document (Narrative + Evidence):**
    *   **Budget Control:** System blocks posting if line item > budget (Hard Stop) or requires approval (Soft Stop).
    *   **Segregation of Duties:** Person entering invoice  Person approving payment  Person reconciling bank.
    *   **Timekeeping Approval:** Supervisor approves timesheet *before* payroll runs.
    *   **Procurement Thresholds:** System workflow enforces 3 quotes > $10k.
    *   **Access Controls:** IT removes terminated employee access within 24 hrs.

### 8.5 Common Audit Findings & Remediation
| Finding Type | Example | Corrective Action Plan (CAP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Unallowable Cost** | $12k Alcohol charged to Grant. | 1. Refund $12k to Grant. 2. Update Credit Card Policy. 3. Train Staff. 4. Implement Block on MCC codes 5813 (Bars) in Expensify. |
| **Time & Effort** | 3 of 10 files missing certifications. | 1. Obtain retro certs (if allowed). 2. Implement automated reminder in Payroll System. 3. Quarterly internal audit of 10% sample. |
| **Procurement** | Sole source used without justification. | 1. Document justification post-hoc (weak). 2. Update Procurement Policy requiring pre-approval form. 3. Train Program Managers. |
| **Subrecipient Monitoring** | No risk assessment done; Audit not collected. | 1. Collect missing audits immediately. 2. Issue Management Decision for findings. 3. Implement Subrecipient Tracker spreadsheet/System. |
| **Reporting** | Final FFR submitted 45 days late. | 1. Calendarize all due dates in Grant Calendar. 2. Assign "Report Owner" & "Backup Owner". 3. Pre-draft templates 30 days out. |

## 9. The "Dirty Dozen": Most Frequent Compliance Failures & Mitigations <a name="dirty-dozen"></a>

Based on OIG reports, Single Audit clearinghouse data, and funder feedback.

### 1. The "Budget Drift" (Failure to Request Prior Approval)
*   **Failure:** Spending 15% more on Personnel, 20% less on Equipment without asking. Federal threshold: **10% or $25k** (whichever is less) variance on a line item requires prior approval (2 CFR 200.308).
*   **Fix:** Monthly variance report flags >5% drift. Auto-email to Grants Manager: "Prior Approval Required Check."

### 2. "Zombie" Effort Certifications
*   **Failure:** Certifications signed 6 months late, by someone who didn't supervise the work, or using % estimates ("I think 50%") rather than actuals.
*   **Fix:** System-enforced certification workflow. **After-the-fact.** Quarterly frequency. Supervisor *must* have first-hand knowledge.

### 3. The "Subrecipient vs. Contractor" Misclassification
*   **Failure:** Treating a collaborative partner (subrecipient) as a vendor to avoid monitoring requirements. Or vice versa.
*   **Fix:** Use the **Substance over Form** checklist (2 CFR 200.331):
    *   Determines *who* is eligible? -> Subrecipient.
    *   Has compliance requirements? -> Subrecipient.
    *   Uses funds for program? -> Subrecipient.
    *   Provides goods/services *ancillary* to program? -> Contractor.

### 4. Indirect Cost Rate "Double Dipping" or "Wrong Base"
*   **Failure:** Charging indirect costs on Equipment (> $5k), Capital Expenditures, Participant Support Costs, or Subawards >$25k (these are excluded from MTDC base). Or charging a 40% rate on a grant capped at 15%.
*   **Fix:** ERP configuration: **Exclusion Accounts** mapped in the Indirect Cost Calculation engine. Hard-code the Grant Cap % in the Grant Master Record.

### 5. Procurement "Splitting" to Avoid Thresholds
*   **Failure:** Buying $24k of laptops via three $8k purchase orders to avoid the $25k/3-quote rule.
*   **Fix:** System control: Aggregate spend by Vendor/Category per Fiscal Year. Alert if aggregate > Simplified Acquisition Threshold.

### 6. Lobbying Cost Charges (The "SF-LLL" Trap)
*   **Failure:** Using grant funds for "Grassroots Lobbying" (urging public to contact legislators) or paying a lobbyist. Charging "Association Dues" where % is lobbying (must allocate out).
*   **Fix:** Annual Lobbying Certification. Chart of Accounts: `Lobbying Expense (Unallowable)`. Review Association Invoices for lobbying % allocation.

### 7. Program Income "Lost" or Misclassified
*   **Failure:** Workshop fees deposited into General Operating account, not tracked as Grant Program Income. Results in over-drawing federal cash.
*   **Fix:** Separate Bank Account or GL Revenue Code: `Program Income - Grant [ID]`. Cash Drawdown formula: `Total Expenses - Program Income Received = Federal Draw`.

### 8. Equipment "Ghost Assets"
*   **Failure:** Laptop bought on Grant A in 2018. Not on Asset Register. Staff took it home in 2021. No disposition record.
*   **Fix:** Physical Inventory **Annually** (or biennially per policy). Barcode tags. Disposition Form required for *any* removal (Transfer, Sale, Trash, Theft - police report).

### 9. Conflict of Interest (COI) "File and Forget"
*   **Failure:** Board member owns the cleaning company hired. COI form signed but not *acted upon* (recusal documented).
*   **Fix:** COI Review Committee meets quarterly. **Mitigation Plan** documented in minutes for every disclosed conflict. Procurement file includes COI clearance memo.

### 10. Cybersecurity / Data Privacy Negligence
*   **Failure:** PII (Personally Identifiable Information) of grant clients emailed in unencrypted Excel sheets. No Written Information Security Plan (WISP).
*   **Fix:** NIST 800-171 / CMMC (for DoD) or State Privacy Laws (NY SHIELD, CCPA). Encrypted email (Virtru/Zix). Secure File Transfer (SFTP/ShareFile) for data. Annual Pen Test.

### 11. The "Silo" Syndrome (Program vs. Finance)
*   **Failure:** Program Manager promises deliverables in Year 2 that Finance knows are unfunded. Finance cuts travel budget not knowing Site Visits are mandatory for compliance.
*   **Fix:** **Monthly Grant Huddle (30 mins).** Standing agenda: Budget vs Actual, Upcoming Deliverables, Procurement Pipeline, Compliance Alerts, Staffing Changes.

### 12. Late/Incomplete Closeout
*   **Failure:** Final report submitted day of deadline. Missing property disposition. Missing final invention report (Bayh-Dole). Funder closes grant in "Non-Compliant" status in SAM.gov/Grants.gov.
*   **Fix:** **Closeout Checklist initiated at T-90 Days.**
    *   T-90: Confirm all expenses posted. Request final invoices from vendors.
    *   T-60: Draft Final Financial Report. Reconcile Cash Draws.
    *   T-45: Draft Final Performance Report. Get Program Sign-off.
    *   T-30: Internal Review (Finance + Compliance + ED).
    *   T-15: Submit.
    *   T-0: Confirm Funder Acceptance. Archive.
