**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme welcomes applications from both incorporated not‑for‑profit organisations and unincorporated groups, provided that the applicant can demonstrate a stable governance structure and reliable financial oversight. When an unincorporated group operates under the auspices of a local council—such that the council holds legal responsibility for insurances, contracts and any required licences—the group is considered an eligible applicant. In practice, we require a formal “auspices agreement” or memorandum of understanding between your garden and the council, confirming that the council will act as the fiscal host for the grant (if awarded) and will manage any insurance or liability matters on your behalf. This arrangement satisfies our requirement for a responsible signatory and ensures that funds are handled in accordance with public‑sector accountability standards.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum award. Instead, we evaluate proposals against the programme’s strategic objectives and the total cost of the proposed project. Typical awards for infrastructure‑focused initiatives range from $15,000 to $75,000, but we are prepared to consider applications that fall outside this band if the justification and cost‑benefit analysis are compelling. Your projected budget for rainwater tanks and a shade structure should be detailed in the financial section of the application so we can assess proportionality.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes, capital‑intensive infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures is fully eligible under the “Resilient Community Assets” category of the grant. Both items must directly support the project’s resilience objective—in your case, enhancing water security and reducing heat stress during summer months. We do require evidence that the installations will be permanently affixed to the land, have appropriate building or engineering approvals (if required), and will be maintained for at least five years post‑installation.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:

1. **Auspices Agreement / MOU** – a concise document signed by senior council representatives confirming the council’s role in insurance, governance and financial oversight.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page summary of your garden’s mission, volunteer base, operating hours and community impact.  
3. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for an unincorporated group, a set of internally prepared financial statements) showing the $40,000 turnover and the sources of income (produce stall, membership fees, etc.).  
4. **Project Budget and Narrative** – a detailed breakdown of all costs (including installation, permits, contingency) and a clear description of how each component addresses resilience.  
5. **Site Plans and Drawings** – scaled sketches or CAD drawings indicating where the tanks and shade structure will be located, along with any necessary site‑specific considerations (e.g., accessibility, drainage).  
6. **Insurance Certificate** – a copy of the council’s insurance policy confirming coverage for the proposed works, or a letter of assurance that coverage will be extended to include the new assets.  
7. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from local businesses, schools or community groups that demonstrate broad backing for the project.  
8. **Risk Assessment and Maintenance Plan** – a brief outline of how you will manage health‑ and safety‑related risks and a five‑year maintenance schedule for the installed infrastructure.
