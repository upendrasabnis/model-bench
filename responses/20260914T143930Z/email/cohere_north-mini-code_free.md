Thank you for reaching out and for the opportunity to clarify the eligibility criteria for our Community Resilience Grant. I appreciate your proactive approach in seeking confirmation before investing time in a full application, and I am happy to address each of the points you raised.

**Eligibility for unincorporated groups under council auspice**  
Our grant programme is designed to support projects that deliver measurable community benefit, and we do accept applications from unincorporated groups provided they operate under a formal auspice arrangement with an eligible organisation. In your case, having the local council act as the auspice—where the council holds the necessary insurances and provides governance—generally satisfies our requirement for a responsible lead entity. However, we do request a written agreement (or a letter of support) from the council confirming that they will act as the fiscal sponsor for the project, including responsibility for any grant funds that may be awarded. This documentation helps us verify that the grant will be administered in compliance with our financial‑oversight standards.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed grant size; awards are determined on the basis of project merit, budget justification, and available funding for the round. Historically, grants have ranged from a minimum of approximately $5,000 to a maximum of around $150,000, though projects with exceptionally strong community impact may be considered for larger amounts. Your projected costs for rainwater tanks and a shade structure fall well within this typical range, and we would be happy to discuss any specific budget figures you have prepared.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Capital infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures is expressly permitted under the “Resilience‑Enhancing Infrastructure” component of the grant. These items must be directly linked to the project’s resilience objectives—in your case, improving water security and heat‑stress mitigation for the garden’s operations. We do require that the infrastructure be purchased new (or, where appropriate, refurbished to a like‑new condition) and that the purchase includes warranties or guarantees where applicable.

**Supporting documentation to prepare**  
To ensure a smooth review process, we recommend compiling the following items:

1. **Project Narrative** – a clear description of the problem you are addressing, the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline with milestones.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – broken down by line item (including equipment, installation, contingency, and any council‑related fees), with quotations or invoices where possible.  
3. **Letters of Support** – at least two from community stakeholders, local businesses, or the council confirming their endorsement of the project.  
4. **Proof of Insurance and Liability Coverage** – a copy of the council’s insurance policy that names your garden as an additional insured, or a certificate confirming coverage for the project duration.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for a small volunteer group, a simple statement of financial position and cash flow) demonstrating your $40,000 annual turnover.  
6. **Organizational Structure Document** – a brief charter or memorandum of understanding that outlines the roles of the council as auspice and any governing committee within your garden.  
7. **Risk Assessment and Health & Safety Plan** – particularly relevant for the installation of rainwater tanks and a shade structure.  

We encourage you to submit these documents in a single PDF folder, clearly labelled with your organisation’s name and the grant round reference. If any of the above items are not yet available, a brief explanatory note will be helpful; we are often able to work with draft versions and can provide guidance on how to obtain missing information.
