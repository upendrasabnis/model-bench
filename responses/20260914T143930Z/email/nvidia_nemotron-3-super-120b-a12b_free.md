Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the thoughtful work you are doing with your community garden. I’m pleased to provide the clarification you requested regarding the Community Resilience Grant (CRG) round that closes next month.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The CRG expressly permits applications from not‑for‑profit entities that are either incorporated associations, registered charities, or unincorporated groups operating under an auspicing arrangement with a local government body. Because your garden is auspiced by the regional council—which maintains the necessary public liability and volunteer insurances—you meet the eligibility criterion. You will need to include a signed auspice agreement (or a letter of support) from the council confirming that they will act as the legal and financial sponsor for the grant, should your application be successful.

**Grant amount range**  
For this funding round, the minimum award is $5,000 and the maximum is $50,000 (GST exclusive). Applications are assessed on a competitive basis, and the amount awarded will reflect the scale, impact, and budgetary justification of the proposed project. Your outlined budget for rainwater tanks and a shade structure should fall comfortably within this range, provided you can demonstrate cost‑effectiveness and clear community benefit.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Capital infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems, shade structures, and related fittings are considered eligible expenses under the CRG, provided they directly contribute to enhancing community resilience to climate‑related stresses (e.g., extreme heat, water scarcity). Ongoing operational costs (e.g., utilities, staff salaries) are not fundable, but the installation, materials, and any necessary engineering or design consultancy fees are permissible.

**Supporting documentation to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, please assemble the following items:

1. **Auspice agreement or letter of support** from the regional council, detailing their role as sponsor and confirming insurance coverage.  
2. **Project plan** (max. two pages) outlining the objectives, anticipated outcomes, timeline, and how the rainwater tanks and shade structure will improve resilience for garden users and the broader community.  
3. **Detailed budget** (itemised) showing quoted costs for tanks, pumps, fittings, shade structure materials, labour, and any contingency (typically 10 %). Include at least two written quotes for major items where possible.  
4. **Evidence of community need** – e.g., recent temperature records for your region, photos of heat‑stressed plants, or survey results from garden members indicating demand for improved water and shade infrastructure.  
5. **Governance documents** – a copy of your garden’s constitution or terms of reference, and a list of current office‑bearers or volunteer coordinators.  
6. **Financial statements** – a simple profit‑and‑loss statement showing the $40,000 annual turnover and a breakdown of revenue sources (produce stall, membership fees).  
7. **Risk management plan** – brief notes on safety considerations during installation and ongoing use of the infrastructure.

Once these documents are compiled, you can submit your application via the online grants portal before the closing date (please verify the exact deadline on the grant guidelines page). Should you encounter any technical difficulties or require further clarification on specific budget lines, feel free to contact me directly at grants@agency.gov.au or on 02 1234 5678.
