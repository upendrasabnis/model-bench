## 1.  Introduction  

Grant compliance is the set of rules, processes, and documentation that ensure a grantee uses federal, state, local, or philanthropic funds as intended.  
An *acquittal* (U.S. federal terminology) or a *final report* (philanthropic language) is the formal statement in which the grantee confirms that the grant was spent in accordance with the award terms.  

Why does this matter?  
* **Legal obligation** – Failing to file an acquittal can result in penalties, loss of future funding, or even criminal charges.  
* **Reputation** – Strong compliance builds trust with funders and the public.  
* **Operational health** – Audits and final reports surface systemic issues that can be corrected early.  

This article walks through what acquittal reporting is, the common requirements across government and philanthropic funders, record‑keeping best practices, audit mechanics, and practical advice to avoid the most frequent compliance failures.

## 5.  Audits: The Final Checkpoint  

### 5.1  Types of Audits  

| Audit Type | Who Conducts | Typical Threshold | What It Looks At |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Financial Audit** | External CPA | $750k (federal) or $500k (philanthropic) | Ledger, invoices, payroll |
| **Program Audit** | Internal or external | >$250k | Program outcomes, compliance with objectives |
| **Compliance Audit** | OIG, Internal Office | Any | Adherence to cost principles, restrictions |
| **Data Quality Audit** | Internal | Any | Data sets, integrity, security |

### 5.2  Common Audit Triggers  

1. **Large variances** between budgeted and actual spend.  
2. **Unreconciled bank statements**.  
3. **Mis‑classification of costs** (e.g., indirect costs on a direct‑cost grant).  
4. **Missing documentation** for a substantial portion of expenditures.  
5. **Non‑compliance with cost στιγ** (e.g., spending on prohibited activities).  

### 5.3  How Audits Work  

1. **Pre‑audit Preparation** – Internal review, self‑audit, risk assessment.  
2. **Audit Request** – Funder sends a formal request.  
3. **Field Work** – Auditor examines documents, interviews staff.  
4. **Audit Report** – Findings, recommendations, corrective actions.  
5. **Close‑out** – Grantee signs off, resolves discrepancies.

## 6.  The Most Frequent Compliance Failures & How to Avoid Them  

| Failure | Example | Root Cause | Prevention |
|---------|---------|------------|-----------|
| **Misallocated Indirect Costs** | NIH grant used indirect costs on a research subaward. | Lack of understanding of cost‑sharing rules. | Train staff on OMB Circulars; double‑check budget codes. |
| **Unapproved Travel** | Travel to a restricted country without permission. | Incomplete travel policy. | Central travel approvals; pre‑travel authorization. |
| **Unrecorded Time** | Staff logged hours on unrelated systems. | No time‑tracking policy. | Mandatory timesheet entry; audit trails. |
| **Missing Receipts** | $10,000 expense without a receipt. | Inadequate receipt capture. | Capture receipts via mobile app; require scan before approval. |
| **Data Sharing Violations** | Release of patient data without IRB clearance. | Confusion over data‑sharing policy. | IRB training; data‑use agreements. |
| **Failure to Submit Narrative Early** | Final report due 30 days after grant close but submitted 60 days late. | Poor project scheduling. | Project calendar with key compliance dates; automated reminders. |

### 6.1  High‑Impact Remediation Strategies  

1. **Risk‑Based Auditing** – Focus on high‑variance areas.  
2. **Continuous Monitoring** – Use dashboards that flag thresholds.  
3. **Automated Workflows** – Approvals, receipts, time‑cards.  
4. **Clear SOPs** – Document every step of the compliance process.  
5. **Regular Training欢乐** – Quarterly refresher courses for finance, project managers, and staff.

*Outcome*: The foundation required a data‑use agreement and re‑submittal of the data.

- Train researchers on data privacy.
- Review data-sharing agreements before upload.

*Challenge*: Unreconciled bank statements in the final report.  
*Outcome*: The foundation withheld the final payment until discrepancies were resolved.  
*Lessons Learned*:
- Conduct monthly reconciliations between grant bank accounts and general ledger.
