**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme is open to both incorporated non‑profit entities and unincorporated community groups, provided that the group operates under a formal auspice arrangement with a municipal or regional council. In your case, the fact that the council holds the relevant insurances on your behalf satisfies the “responsible entity” requirement that we typically require for unincorporated applicants. However, we do ask that the council sign a brief endorsement letter confirming that it will act as the fiscal sponsor for any awarded funds and that it will be responsible for compliance reporting. This arrangement ensures that the grant funds are managed in accordance with our financial‑administration standards while still allowing the garden to retain operational autonomy.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award amount. Instead, we evaluate proposals on the basis of need, community impact, and the proportion of project costs that the applicant can realistically fund. Typical awards for infrastructure‑focused projects in your size range have ranged from $15,000 to $75,000, but we are prepared to consider requests outside this band if the justification is compelling. The key is to demonstrate that the requested amount aligns with the scope of the work and the funder’s expectations for measurable outcomes.  

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, capital infrastructure that directly supports the sustainability and operational continuity of community‑based projects is an eligible cost category under the grant. Rainwater tanks, shade structures, and associated installation work are expressly listed as permissible expenditures because they address climate‑resilience objectives and reduce ongoing operational costs (e.g., water usage and heat‑related staff welfare). We do require that each item be justified with a clear link to the project’s resilience goals, and that the budget includes contingency provisions for unforeseen installation challenges.  

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documents:  

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem you are addressing, the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline with key milestones.  
2. **Budget and Financial Plan** – a detailed breakdown of all costs, including the infrastructure items, any matching contribution you will provide, and how the council’s auspice arrangement will manage funds.  
3. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from the council (the auspice letter), local businesses, or partner organisations that demonstrate community backing.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – a brief overview of your group’s volunteer composition, governance structure (even if informal), and how the project aligns with your broader mission.  
5. **Insurance and Risk Management Documentation** – the council’s certificate of insurance confirming coverage for public liability and any works‑related risks.  
6. **Site Plans and Technical Specifications** – drawings or sketches of the proposed rainwater tank placement, shade structure design, and any site constraints.  
7. **Evidence of Funding** – proof of your current annual turnover (e.g., recent audited financial statements or tax returns) and any other sources of revenue that demonstrate financial viability.  

We also advise that you review the full grant guidelines on our website, as they contain specific formatting requirements for each of the above documents and a checklist of any additional items we may request during the assessment phase.
