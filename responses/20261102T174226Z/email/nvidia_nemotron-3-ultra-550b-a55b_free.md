Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out to clarify eligibility before investing time in a full application—this is exactly the kind of due diligence that strengthens submissions. I am pleased to address each of your questions in turn.

**Auspicing Arrangements:** Unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspicing agreement with a local government authority are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing body (in your case, the council) is a legal entity that can enter into a funding agreement on your behalf. The council must hold appropriate public liability insurance and agree to accept legal and financial responsibility for the grant. You will need to provide a signed letter of support from the council confirming the auspicing arrangement, their ABN, and evidence of current insurance coverage.

**Grant Amounts:** This round offers grants between $5,000 and $50,000 (GST exclusive). There is no minimum project size, but the requested amount must be proportionate to the scope of work and your group’s capacity to deliver. Given your annual turnover of approximately $40,000, a request within the lower-to-mid range would be viewed favourably, provided the budget is realistic and well justified.

**Eligible Costs:** Infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience—such as rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures to mitigate heat impacts—is explicitly listed as an eligible capital expense. Eligible costs include purchase, delivery, installation, and any necessary site preparation or permitting fees. Ongoing maintenance costs, however, are not covered.

**Supporting Documents to Prepare:**
1. Auspicing agreement letter from the council (on letterhead, signed by an authorised officer).
2. Council’s current Certificate of Currency for public liability insurance ($20M minimum).
3. Your group’s most recent financial statement or treasurer’s report (showing the $40,000 turnover).
4. Two written quotes for each major infrastructure item (tanks, shade structure, installation).
5. A simple project plan with timeline, risk assessment, and volunteer capacity statement.
6. Evidence of land tenure or council permission for the installation site.
7. Letters of community support (2–3) demonstrating broader benefit.
