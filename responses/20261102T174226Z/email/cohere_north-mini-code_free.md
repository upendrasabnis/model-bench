Subject: Re: Question about eligibility for the Community Resilience Grant  

Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the clear, concise outline of your project. I’m pleased to confirm that we have received many applications from community‑based groups that operate under the auspices of a local council, and such arrangements are fully acceptable for the Community Resilience Grant. The key eligibility requirement is that the applicant be a legally‑recognised entity capable of entering into a grant agreement and being accountable for the use of funds. An unincorporated group that is formally **auspiced** (i.e., sponsored or supported) by a council can satisfy this requirement provided the council signs a **memorandum of understanding** or **auspices agreement** that outlines its oversight responsibilities, insurance coverage, and financial accountability. In practice, we have seen successful applications where the council acts as the primary contracting party while the community group delivers the activities on the ground.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, we evaluate each proposal against the program’s strategic objectives and the total cost of the project. Typical awards for infrastructure‑focused initiatives range from $15,000 to $75,000, but we have approved grants well beyond that ceiling for larger, multi‑year undertakings. Your projected budget for rainwater tanks and a shade structure appears to fall comfortably within the normal range, and we would be happy to discuss any additional funding needs during the full‑application stage.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes—capital assets such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures are expressly listed as eligible expenses under the “Climate‑Resilient Infrastructure” category of the grant. Both items must demonstrably contribute to the project’s resilience objectives (e.g., reducing water consumption, mitigating heat stress, and ensuring continuity of garden operations during extreme weather). We recommend attaching clear specifications, cost breakdowns, and justification statements that link each item to the anticipated outcomes.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
When you move to the full‑application phase, we suggest assembling the following documentation:

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem, goals, implementation plan, and expected impacts.  
2. **Budget Worksheet** – itemised costs with unit prices, quantities, and any contingency amounts.  
3. **Design/Technical Plans** – site plans, tank specifications, and shade‑structure drawings (or quotes from reputable suppliers).  
4. **Auspices Agreement** – a formal letter or agreement from the council confirming its support, insurance coverage, and willingness to act as the contracting entity.  
5. **Organizational Profile** – a brief description of your group’s composition, volunteer base, and any governing documents (e.g., constitution) even if you are unincorporated.  
6. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or a simple cash‑flow summary if none exist) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
7. **Risk Assessment & Insurance Certificate** – confirmation that the council’s insurance policy extends to the proposed works and that you have any additional coverage required for construction activities.  
8. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from local businesses, community organisations, or council representatives that validate the community need and feasibility.  

We also recommend reviewing the full grant guidelines (available on our website) for any specific formatting requirements, such as the maximum length of the narrative or the acceptable file types for attachments. If you have any further questions about the documentation or would like a pre‑submission review of a draft application, please feel free to forward it to us and we will provide feedback within three business days.
