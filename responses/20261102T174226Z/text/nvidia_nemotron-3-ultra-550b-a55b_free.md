## Why a Systematic Approach Matters  

- **Consistency** – A repeatable process reduces last‑minute scrambling.  
- **Quality** – Each component (needs, logic model, budget) gets the attention it deserves.  
- **Scalability** – The same framework works for a $5 K seed grant or a $5 M multi‑year award.  
- **Accountability** – Clear documentation makes post‑award reporting straightforward.  

> **Rule of thumb:** Treat every proposal as a *project* with its own work plan, milestones, and deliverables.

## Understanding the Grant Landscape  

| Grant Type | Typical Size | Decision Cycle | Key Decision Makers | Common Requirements |
|------------|--------------|----------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Foundation (private)** | $5 K–$500 K | 1–6 months | Program officers, board | Narrative, budget, logic model |
| **Corporate Giving** | $1 K–$250 K | 1–3 months | CSR manager, exec sponsor | Alignment with brand, employee engagement |
| **Government (federal, state, local)** | $10 K–$10 M+ | 3–12 months | Grants officers, review panels | Strict compliance, cost‑share, reporting |
| **Community Foundations** | $2 K–$100 K | 1–4 months | Grant committee | Local impact, community letters |

**Takeaway:** Map each funder to the *type* above; the workflow (research → relationship → proposal → reporting) stays the same, but the *emphasis* shifts.

- [ ] **Deadlines & Cycles** – Annual, rolling, multi‑year?  
- [ ] **Reporting Expectations** – Quarterly narrative, financials, outcomes metrics?  
- [ ] **Contact Preferences** – Email, phone, in‑person meeting?

## Building Relationships Before You Write  

| Action | Timing | Tools | Example |
|--------|--------|-------|---------|
| **Introductory Email** | 3–6 months before deadline | Personalized email, 1‑page impact snapshot | “Dear Maria, I’ve followed Greenfield’s support of youth STEM…” |
| **Informational Interview** | 2–4 months | 30‑min Zoom, prepared questions | Ask about “what makes a proposal stand out this cycle?” |
| **Site Visit / Program Tour** | 1–2 months | Invite funder to see program in action | Show a live after‑school coding club. |
| **Share Success Stories** | Ongoing | Quarterly impact brief (PDF) | “Our 2023 cohort increased coding proficiency 42%.” |
| **Acknowledge Past Funding** | Immediately after award | Thank‑you letter, public acknowledgment (with permission) | “Thanks to Greenfield, 120 students accessed laptops.” |

**Relationship‑Building Checklist**  

- [ ] Identify 1–2 key contacts per funder.  
- [ ] Schedule at least one touchpoint *before* the LOI.  
- [ ] Document every interaction in your CRM (date, notes, next steps).  
- [ ] Send a *value‑add* (research brief, data snapshot) at least once per quarter.  
- [ ] Keep a “funder memory” sheet: preferences, pet peeves, personal details (e.g., “Maria loves bird‑watching”).

## Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement  

### 1. Anatomy of a Winning Needs Statement  

| Component | Purpose | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Problem Definition** | Clear, data‑driven description of the issue | “In 2023, 38 % of 8th‑graders in District 12 scored below proficiency in math (state assessment).” |
| **Scope & Scale** | Quantify who is affected, where, and how many | “That equals 1,240 students across 7 middle schools.” |
| **Root Causes** | Show you understand *why* the problem persists | “Limited access to qualified STEM teachers, outdated curricula, and lack of after‑school enrichment.” |
| **Consequences** | Human and societal impact if unaddressed | “Students who fall behind in math are 3× more likely to drop out of high school.” |
| **Urgency** | Why *now*? | “District’s new STEM graduation requirement takes effect 2025; we have a 2‑year window.” |
| **Evidence Base** | Cite peer‑reviewed studies, local reports, or your own pilot data | “A 2022 RAND study found after‑school STEM programs raise math scores 0.35 SD.” |
| **Alignment with Funder** | Explicitly connect to funder’s priorities | “Greenfield’s focus on ‘equitable STEM access for underserved youth’ matches this need.” |

### 2. Writing Tips  

- **Lead with a powerful statistic** (the “hook”).  
- **Use the “Problem‑Solution‑Impact” triangle** – each paragraph moves the reader from need → your approach → projected change.  
- **Avoid jargon** – write for an intelligent layperson.  
- **Quote a stakeholder** (student, teacher, parent) for authenticity.  
- **Keep it to 1–2 pages** (≈ 500–800 words).  

### 3. Needs‑Statement Checklist  

- [ ] Opening hook (statistic or story).  
- [ ] Clear problem definition with data sources cited.  
- [ ] Geographic & demographic scope.  
- [ ] Root‑cause analysis (≥ 3 factors).  
- [ ] Consequences if unmet.  
- [ ] Urgency rationale.  
- [ ] Evidence base (≥ 2 citations).  
- [ ] Direct link to funder’s stated priorities.  
- [ ] No more than 2 pages, 12‑pt font, 1‑inch margins.

## Defining Goals, Objectives, and Outcomes  

| Level | Definition | Example (SMART) |
|-------|------------|-----------------|
| **Goal** | Broad, long‑term change | “Increase STEM career pathways for low‑income middle‑school students in District 12.” |
| **Objective** | Specific, measurable, time‑bound step toward goal | “By June 2026, enroll 200 students in a year‑long after‑school coding curriculum.” |
| **Outcome** | Observable change in knowledge, behavior, or condition | “80 % of participants will improve math proficiency by ≥ 15 % (pre/post test).” |
| **Indicator** | Metric used to track outcome | “Pre‑/post‑test score change; attendance ≥ 90 %.” |
| **Target** | Numeric benchmark | “150 students complete the full curriculum.” |

### 1. Writing SMART Objectives – Template  

```
[Who] will [action verb] [what] by [date] as measured by [indicator] reaching [target].
```

**Filled‑in Example:**  

> *The program coordinator will recruit 200 eligible 7th‑grade students by 30 Sept 2025 as measured by enrollment forms reaching a target of 200 completed forms.*  

### 2. Objective‑Setting Checklist  

- [ ] Each objective is **Specific** (who, what, where).  
- [ ] **Measurable** – includes a quantifiable indicator.  
- [ ] **Achievable** – realistic given resources & timeline.  
- [ ] **Relevant** – directly serves the goal & funder’s priorities.  
- [ ] **Time‑bound** – clear deadline.  
- [ ] **Aligned** – each objective maps to at least one outcome indicator.  
- [ ] **Limited** – 3–5 objectives per proposal (focus).

## Logic Models & Theory of Change  

### 1. Logic Model Core Components  

| Column | Content | Example (STEM After‑School) |
|--------|---------|------------------------------|
| **Inputs** | Resources invested | $150 K grant, 2 staff, 10 laptops, curriculum licenses |
| **Activities** | What you do | Recruit students, deliver 2‑hr weekly coding sessions, host quarterly showcase |
| **Outputs** | Direct products/services | 200 students served, 80 sessions delivered, 4 showcase events |
| **Short‑Term Outcomes** | Immediate changes | ↑ coding confidence (self‑efficacy survey), ↑ attendance |
| **Intermediate Outcomes** | Behavioral/knowledge shifts | ↑ math test scores, ↑ enrollment in high‑school CS courses |
| **Long‑Term Impact** | Systemic change | ↑ STEM degree attainment, reduced income disparity |

### 2. Theory of Change Narrative (≈ 300 words)  

> *If we provide low‑income middle‑school students with consistent, high‑quality coding experiences (Activities), then they will develop computational thinking skills and a sense of belonging in STEM (Short‑Term Outcomes). Research shows that early exposure predicts high‑school CS enrollment (Intermediate Outcome). Over time, a larger, more diverse pipeline of students will enter STEM majors and careers, narrowing the equity gap in the regional tech workforce (Long‑Term Impact).*

### 3. Building the Model – Step‑by‑Step  

1. **Gather the team** (program staff, evaluator, finance).  
2. **List all inputs** – include in‑kind contributions.  
3. **Map activities to outputs** – one‑to‑many is okay.  
4. **Define outcomes** – start with *short‑term* (knowledge/attitude), then *intermediate* (behavior), then *long‑term* (systemic).  
5. **Identify assumptions** – “School partners will provide classroom space.”  
6. **Specify external factors** – “State policy on STEM graduation requirements.”  
7. **Validate with stakeholders** – run a 30‑min review session.  

### 4. Logic‑Model Checklist  

- [ ] All inputs accounted for (cash, in‑kind, volunteer hours).  
- [ ] Activities are *action‑oriented* verbs.  
- [ ] Outputs are *countable* (numbers, not “improved”).  
- [ ] Outcomes are *change statements* (increase, decrease, improve).  
- [ ] Each outcome has at least one **indicator** and **data source**.  
- [ ] Assumptions & external factors explicitly listed.  
- [ ] Visual diagram (table or flowchart) included in proposal appendix.

## Designing the Program Narrative  

### 1. Structure of a Winning Narrative  

| Section | Approx. Length | Key Elements |
|---------|----------------|--------------|
| **Executive Summary** | ½ page | Problem, solution, ask, impact |
| **Organizational Capacity** | 1 page | Mission, track record, staff bios, governance |
| **Needs Statement** | 1–2 pages | (see above) |
| **Program Description** | 2–3 pages | Activities, timeline, staffing, partners |
| **Logic Model / Theory of Change** | 1 page + diagram | (see above) |
| **Goals, Objectives, Outcomes** | 1 page | SMART table |
| **Evaluation Plan** | 1–2 pages | Design, tools, data collection, analysis |
| **Sustainability Plan** | ½–1 page | Diversified funding, earned income, policy advocacy |
| **Budget Narrative** | 1 page | Line‑item justification |
| **Appendices** | As needed | Logic model graphic, letters of support, IRS 501(c)(3) letter |

### 2. Writing Style Guidelines  

- **Active voice** – “We will deliver…”, not “The program will be delivered…”.  
- **Concrete verbs** – *design, pilot, scale, evaluate*.  
- **Data‑rich** – embed numbers in sentences (“served 200 students”).  
- **Storytelling** – one brief anecdote per major section.  
- **Funder‑centric language** – mirror the funder’s own keywords (e.g., “equitable access,” “community‑driven”).  

### 3. Narrative Checklist  

- [ ] Executive summary stands alone (decision‑maker reads only this).  
- [ ] Organizational capacity demonstrates *relevant* experience.  
- [ ] Needs statement cites *local* data, not just national stats.  
- [ ] Program description includes a **timeline** (Gantt or table).  
- [ ] Logic model diagram is **legible** (minimum 300 dpi).  
- [ ] Objectives are **SMART** and *numbered* for easy reference.  
- [ ] Evaluation plan names **specific tools** (e.g., “pre/post survey using the STEM Attitude Scale”).  
- [ ] Sustainability plan shows *at least two* non‑grant revenue streams.  
- [ ] Budget narrative explains *every* line > $5 K.  
- [ ] All appendices are **referenced** in the narrative.

## Developing a Realistic, Transparent Budget  

### 1. Budget Categories (Standard Federal & Foundation)  

| Category | Typical Line Items | % of Total (Rule of Thumb) |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| **Personnel** | Salaries, fringe, consultants | 40–55 % |
| **Fringe Benefits** | Health, retirement, payroll tax | 15–25 % of personnel |
| **Travel** | Local mileage, conference travel | 2–5 % |
| **Equipment** | Laptops, lab gear (≥ $5 K) | 1–3 % |
| **Supplies** | Curriculum kits, consumables | 3–7 % |
| **Contractual** | Sub‑grants, evaluation firm | 5–15 % |
| **Other Direct Costs** | Space rental, utilities, insurance | 2–5 % |
| **Indirect Costs (IDC)** | Overhead (if allowed) | 10–15 % of MTDC* |

*MTDC = Modified Total Direct Costs (excludes equipment, tuition, sub‑awards > $25 K).  

### 2. Building the Budget – Step‑by‑Step  

1. **List every activity** from the logic model.  
2. **Assign staff time** (FTE %) to each activity.  
3. **Calculate salary + fringe** per FTE.  
4. **Add non‑personnel costs** per activity (materials, travel).  
5. **Apply IDC rate** (use funder’s approved rate or your negotiated rate).  
6. **Cross‑check** – total must match the *requested amount* exactly.  
7. **Create a Budget Narrative** – one paragraph per major line item.  

### 3. Sample Budget Table (Excerpt)  

| Line Item | Description | Qty | Unit Cost | Total | % of Grant |
|-----------|-------------|-----|-----------|-------|------------|
| **Personnel** | Program Director (0.5 FTE) | 12 mo | $5,000/mo | $60,000 | 30 % |
| | Curriculum Specialist (0.3 FTE) | 12 mo | $4,200/mo | $45,360 | 22.7 % |
| **Fringe** | 25 % of personnel | — | — | $26,340 | 13.2 % |
| **Travel** | Staff to 2 regional conferences | 2 | $1,200 | $2,400 | 1.2 % |
| **Equipment** | 10 laptops (student use) | 10 | $800 | $8,000 | 4 % |
| **Supplies** | Coding kits, licences | 200 | $45 | $9,000 | 4.5 % |
| **Contractual** | External evaluator (0.2 FTE) | 12 mo | $3,500/mo | $42,000 | 21 % |
| **Other Direct** | Classroom rental (10 mo) | 10 | $500 | $5,000 | 2.5 % |
| **Indirect (10 % MTDC)** | — | — | — | $19,810 | 9.9 % |
| **Total Requested** | — | — | — | **$218,910** | **100 %** |

### 4. Budget‑Development Checklist  

- [ ] Every line item ties to a specific activity in the logic model.  
- [ ] Personnel costs reflect *actual* salaries (not “market rate” unless justified).  
- [ ] Fringe rate matches your organization’s approved rate.  
- [ ] Equipment > $5 K is listed separately (often requires justification).  
- [ ] Sub‑awards > $25 K are broken out with their own mini‑budget.  
- [ ] IDC rate is *allowable* per funder guidelines.  
- [ ] No “miscellaneous” line > 5 % of total.  
- [ ] Budget narrative explains *why* each cost is necessary and *how* it was calculated.  
- [ ] All numbers **reconcile** (sum of line items = total request).

## Writing the Evaluation & Sustainability Plans  

### 1. Evaluation Plan Framework  

| Element | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Evaluation Questions** | What you want to learn | “Does the after‑school coding program increase math proficiency?” |
| **Design** | Experimental, quasi‑experimental, pre/post, mixed‑methods | “Pre/post test with matched comparison group (n=100).” |
| **Indicators** | Quantitative & qualitative metrics | Math score change, attendance, student self‑efficacy survey |
| **Data Sources** | Where data comes from | School district test data, program attendance logs, survey platform |
| **Data Collection Timeline** | When & how often | Baseline (Sept), Midpoint (Jan), Post (June) |
| **Analysis Plan** | Statistical tests, qualitative coding | Paired t‑test, thematic analysis of focus groups |
| **Reporting** | Deliverables to funder & internal | Quarterly dashboards, final evaluation report |
| **IRB / Ethics** | Human subjects protection | Exempt status obtained from university IRB |

### 2. Sustainability Plan – 4 Pillars  

| Pillar | Strategies | Concrete Actions |
|--------|------------|------------------|
| **Diversified Funding** | Grants, individual giving, corporate sponsorship | Apply to 3 new foundations Year 2; launch “Code for Kids” annual gala. |
| **Earned Income** | Fee‑for‑service, curriculum licensing | Sell curriculum package to neighboring districts ($15 K/yr). |
| **Policy & Advocacy** | Leverage outcomes for public funding | Present results to state legislature; seek line‑item appropriation. |
| **Organizational Capacity** | Staff development, board engagement | Train 2 staff in grant writing; recruit board member with tech industry ties. |

### 3. Evaluation & Sustainability Checklist  

- [ ] At least **two** evaluation questions (process + outcome).  
- [ ] Design matches **funder’s evidence expectations** (e.g., “rigorous” vs. “formative”).  
- [ ] Indicators are **SMART** and linked to logic model outcomes.  
- [ ] Data collection tools are **validated** or pilot‑tested.  
- [ ] Timeline aligns with grant period (baseline before activities start).  
- [ ] Analysis plan includes **statistical power** justification (if quantitative).  
- [ ] Sustainability plan names **specific** future funders or revenue streams.  
- [ ] Includes **board‑approved** sustainability resolution (if required).

## Polishing the Proposal – Style, Formatting, and Compliance  

### 1. Formatting Standards (Most Funders)  

| Requirement | Typical Spec |
|-------------|--------------|
| **Font** | 12‑pt Times New Roman or Arial |
| **Margins** | 1‑inch all sides |
| **Line Spacing** | Double‑spaced narrative; single‑spaced tables |
| **Page Limits** | Narrative ≤ 15 pages (excluding budget, appendices) |
| **File Format** | PDF (searchable) + Excel budget |
| **Naming Convention** | `OrgName_GrantProgram_FY2026_Proposal.pdf` |
| **Appendix Order** | Logic model → Letters of support → IRS determination → Staff bios |

### 2. Language & Tone  

- **Confident but humble** – “We will achieve…”, not “We hope to…”.  
- **Evidence‑based** – Cite sources in parentheses; include a reference list if > 5 citations.  
- **Equity‑focused** – Explicitly address DEI (demographics, accessibility).  
- **Avoid acronyms** on first use; provide a glossary if > 10.  

### 3. Compliance Checklist (Pre‑Submission)  

- [ ] **Eligibility** – 501(c)(3) status, geographic, organizational size.  
- [ ] **Required Forms** – SF‑424, SF‑424A, certifications, lobbying disclosure.  
- [ ] **Certifications** – Debarment, drug‑free workplace, anti‑discrimination.  
- [ ] **Budget** – Matches narrative, includes IDC rate, no unallowable costs.  
- [ ] **Letters of Support** – Signed, on letterhead, dated within 6 months.  
- [ ] **Data Management Plan** – If required (e.g., NSF, NIH).  
- [ ] **Conflict‑of‑Interest** – Disclosed for key personnel.  
- [ ] **Page Count** – Within limits (including appendices if counted).  
- [ ] **File Size** – < 25 MB (or funder’s limit).  
- [ ] **Submission Portal** – Test login, upload a dummy file 48 hrs before deadline.

## Post‑Award Management – Reporting, Compliance, and Stewardship  

### 1. Immediate Post‑Award Steps (First 30 Days)  

| Task | Owner | Deadline |
|------|-------|----------|
| **Award Acceptance** | Executive Director | Day 1 |
| **Set Up Grant Accounting** | Finance Manager | Day 5 |
| **Kick‑off Meeting** | Program Director + Evaluator | Day 10 |
| **Create Reporting Calendar** | Grants Manager | Day 15 |
| **Distribute Award Letter & Terms** | Grants Manager | Day 5 |
| **Update CRM** | Grants Manager | Day 5 |

### 2. Ongoing Financial Management  

- **Monthly** – Reconcile grant ledger to bank statements.  
- **Quarterly** – Prepare *Financial Status Report* (FSR) per funder template.  
- **Annually** – Conduct *Single Audit* (if > $750 K federal).  

**Key Controls**  

- Separate **grant‑specific chart of accounts**.  
- **Time‑tracking** for all personnel (e.g., Toggl, Harvest).  
- **Approval workflow** for any budget amendment > 10 % of line item.  

### 3. Programmatic Reporting  

| Report Type | Frequency | Core Content | Example Template |
|-------------|-----------|--------------|------------------|
| **Progress Narrative** | Quarterly | Activities completed, outputs, deviations, success stories | Funder’s “Quarterly Narrative” form |
| **Performance Metrics** | Quarterly | Indicator values vs. targets (traffic light) | Dashboard (Excel/PowerBI) |
| **Evaluation Interim** | Mid‑term | Preliminary findings, data quality notes | Evaluator’s brief |
| **Final Report** | End of grant | Full outcomes, lessons learned, sustainability update | Funder’s “Final Report” template |

**Reporting Checklist (Quarterly)**  

- [ ] Narrative ≤ 2 pages, aligned with logic model activities.  
- [ ] All indicators updated; note *why* any target missed.  
- [ ] Financials match accounting system (no rounding errors).  
- [ ] Attach supporting docs (photos, press clippings, participant quotes).  
- [ ] Submit *before* funder deadline; keep copy in grant file.  

### 4. Compliance & Audit Readiness  

- **Document Retention** – Keep all receipts, timesheets, contracts for **3 years** after final report (or per funder).  
- **Allowable Cost Review** – Quarterly cross‑check against OMB Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200) or funder‑specific cost principles.  
- **Sub‑recipient Monitoring** – If you sub‑grant, collect their A‑133 audit or financial statements annually.  

### 5. Stewardship & Relationship Deepening  

| Activity | Timing | Owner | Example |
|----------|--------|-------|---------|
| **Thank‑You Call** | Within 1 week of award | ED / Program Director | “We’re thrilled to partner….” |
| **Site Visit Invitation** | Quarter 1 & Quarter 3 | Program Director | “Join us for a coding showcase.” |
| **Impact Brief** | Semi‑annual | Grants Manager | 1‑page PDF with photos & metrics. |
| **Annual Donor Report** | Year‑end | Development | Include grant outcomes in org‑wide report. |
| **Recognition** | As appropriate | Communications | Social media shout‑out (with permission). |

## Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them  

| Pitfall | Why It Hurts | Prevention |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| **“Copy‑Paste” Proposals** | Funders spot generic language; low fit score. | Tailor each narrative; use a *master template* but customize every section. |
| **Over‑Promising Outcomes** | Credibility loss; risk of non‑compliance. | Ground targets in pilot data or literature; use conservative estimates. |
| **Budget‑Narrative Mismatch** | Reviewers flag inconsistencies → rejection. | Cross‑walk every budget line to a narrative paragraph. |
| **Ignoring Funder’s Formatting Rules** | Automatic disqualification. | Create a *style sheet* per funder; run a pre‑flight checklist. |
| **Weak Evaluation Plan** | Funders increasingly demand evidence. | Involve an evaluator *early*; use a logic model to drive questions. |
| **Late Submission** | No exceptions. | Set internal deadline **48 hrs** before external deadline. |
| **No Post‑Award Plan** | Missed reporting → future funding jeopardized. | Build reporting calendar at award acceptance. |
| **Insufficient Documentation** | Audit findings, cost disallowances. | Implement a *document control SOP* (naming, storage, retention). |
| **Neglecting Equity Lens** | Many funders require DEI analysis. | Include demographic breakdown of participants; address barriers. |
| **Single‑Point‑of‑Contact Risk** | Staff turnover loses institutional knowledge. | Document everything in shared drive; cross‑train at least two staff. |
