**Auspicing arrangements**  
The grant program does accept applications from unincorporated groups that are auspiced by an eligible legal entity, such as a local council. In order to qualify, you will need to provide a formal letter of auspice from the council confirming that they will act as the legal and financial sponsor for the grant, that they hold the necessary public liability and volunteer insurance on your behalf, and that they will be responsible for acquitting any funds awarded. This letter should be on council letterhead, signed by an authorised officer, and include the council’s ABN.

**Grant size**  
The Community Resilience Grant operates within a range of **$5,000 to $50,000** per successful applicant. There is no prescribed minimum turnover for organisations; however, the assessment panel does consider the scale of the proposed activity relative to the organisation’s capacity and the requested amount. Your reported annual turnover of approximately $40,000 falls well within the range of groups that have previously been funded, provided the budget you submit is realistic and justified.

**Eligible costs**  
Capital infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience to climate‑related stresses is an eligible cost category. Rainwater harvesting tanks and shade structures that enable continued operation during extreme heat are expressly listed as permissible expenditures under the “infrastructure adaptation” line item. You will need to obtain itemised quotes (or a detailed cost estimate) from reputable suppliers, demonstrate that the proposed solution meets relevant Australian standards (e.g., AS/NZS 3500 for plumbing, AS 1170 for wind loads), and show how the assets will be maintained and used by the garden volunteers over the grant period.

**Supporting documentation**  
To strengthen your application, please prepare the following items:

1. **Auspice letter** from the local council (as described above).  
2. **Project plan** (max 2 pages) outlining the problem statement, objectives, activities, timeline, and expected resilience outcomes.  
3. **Detailed budget** (including quotes for tanks, shade structure, installation, any ancillary works, and a contingency line).  
4. **Proof of insurance** – a certificate of currency showing the council’s public liability and volunteer coverage.  
5. **Financial summary** – a recent statement of income and expenditure (or the council’s financial statement if you are relying on theirs) to illustrate your $40,000 turnover.  
6. **Letters of support** (optional but helpful) from garden members, local residents, or partner organisations demonstrating community backing.  
7. **Risk management plan** – brief notes on safety during installation, ongoing maintenance responsibilities, and how you will monitor the effectiveness of the infrastructure.
