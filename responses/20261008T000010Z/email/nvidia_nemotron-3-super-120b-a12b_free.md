**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The CRG program expressly allows applications from not‑for‑profit organisations that are either incorporated associations, registered charities, or unincorporated groups operating under an auspicing arrangement with a eligible host organisation. A local government body such as your council qualifies as an eligible auspice, provided it can confirm in writing that it will assume legal and financial responsibility for the grant funds and any associated reporting obligations. Therefore, your garden’s current arrangement—operating under the council’s auspice while the council holds the necessary insurances—meets the program’s eligibility criteria.

**Grant size**  
For the current funding round, the CRG offers a minimum award of $5,000 and a maximum award of $50,000 (GST inclusive). Applications are assessed on a competitive basis, and the amount awarded is proportionate to the demonstrated need, scope, and anticipated impact of the proposed project. Given your estimated annual turnover of approximately $40,000, a request within the mid‑range of the available spectrum (e.g., $15,000–$25,000) would be typical for a capital‑works project of the scale you describe, though you are free to request any amount within the stated limits that reflects your actual budget.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Capital works that enhance the resilience of community facilities—such as the installation of rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures—are explicitly listed as eligible expenses under the “Infrastructure Adaptation” category of the CRG. Both rainwater tanks (including associated plumbing, pumps, and filtration) and permanent or semi‑permanent shade structures (e.g., shade sails, pergolas, or solid‑roof covers) qualify, provided they are directly linked to the goal of maintaining operational capacity during extreme heat or water‑scarcity events. Ongoing maintenance costs, however, are not covered; only the capital outlay for purchase and installation is eligible.

**Supporting documentation to prepare**  
To substantiate your application, please compile the following items:

1. **Auspice agreement** – A signed letter from the council confirming its role as auspice, outlining responsibilities for fund management, reporting, and insurance coverage.  
2. **Proof of not‑for‑profit status** – While you are not a registered charity, a copy of the council’s endorsement or a memorandum of understanding that recognises your group as a community‑based, not‑for‑profit initiative will suffice.  
3. **Financial information** – A recent statement of income and expenditure (covering the last 12 months) showing the $40,000 turnover, plus a breakdown of revenue sources (produce stall, membership fees, any other income).  
4. **Project plan** – A concise description of the proposed works, including site plans, timelines, and how the rainwater tanks and shade structure will improve resilience (e.g., expected water savings, temperature reduction, extended operating hours).  
5. **Budget and quotes** – Itemised budget that matches the requested grant amount, accompanied by at least two written quotes for each major component (tanks, plumbing, shade structure).  
6. **Letters of support** – Optional but valuable: statements from the council, local residents, or partner organisations attesting to the garden’s community value and the need for the proposed upgrades.  
7. **Insurance verification** – Evidence that the council’s existing public liability and property insurance extend to the proposed works during installation and thereafter.

Once you have assembled these documents, you can proceed to the online application portal, where you will be prompted to upload each item and to complete the narrative sections addressing selection criteria such as community need, project feasibility, and expected outcomes.
