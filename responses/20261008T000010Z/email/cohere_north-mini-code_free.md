Thank you for reaching out and for the clear description of your garden’s vision and activities. I’m pleased to provide the following clarification on the points you raised, and I hope it will help you decide whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑focused entities. Unincorporated groups are indeed eligible, provided they operate under a formal auspice agreement with a recognised legal entity—such as your local council—that can assume legal responsibility for the use of grant funds, meet any insurance or risk‑management requirements, and act as the primary point of contact for reporting purposes. If your council holds the relevant insurances on your behalf, that arrangement should satisfy the auspice criteria. We will require a signed auspice agreement (or a letter of support from the council) confirming that the council will administer the grant, manage expenditures, and provide any necessary indemnities.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to support projects ranging from modest pilot initiatives to larger, transformative installations. The minimum award we typically consider is **$5,000**, while the maximum is **$50,000** per fiscal year. Individual projects may receive funding that represents a proportionate share of the total budget—generally between 30 % and 70 % of the total project cost—so applicants should be prepared to demonstrate how the requested amount aligns with the overall scope and sustainability plan.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, capital infrastructure that enhances the resilience and operational capacity of community spaces is an eligible cost. Rainwater tanks, shade structures, and associated installation work are specifically listed under the “Climate‑Adaptation and Sustainability” category of our guidelines. We do require that the items are directly linked to the project’s stated outcomes (e.g., reducing water consumption, extending the growing season, protecting participants from extreme heat) and that they are procured in line with best‑practice standards for durability and environmental impact.

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:

1. **Auspice Agreement/Letter of Support** – a formal letter from your council confirming the partnership, insurance coverage, and financial oversight.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – a brief constitution or operational handbook outlining your group’s purpose, volunteer structure, and governance.  
3. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or a set of cash‑flow records if you are unincorporated) for the last two fiscal years, together with a simple budget forecast for the upcoming year.  
4. **Project Plan** – a clear description of the rainwater tank and shade structure components, timelines, and implementation steps, including any required permits or council approvals.  
5. **Budget Breakdown** – a line‑item budget that itemises equipment, installation, labour (if applicable), and any contingency funds, linked to the grant amount requested.  
6. **Community Impact Statement** – evidence of community need (e.g., attendance figures, survey results) and how the project will address the challenges of hotter summers.  
7. **Risk Management and Insurance Documentation** – copies of the council’s insurance certificates and any additional safety plans you will implement for the installation.  
8. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from local businesses, schools, or other stakeholder groups that demonstrate broader community backing.

We strongly advise that you review the full grant guidelines on our website (particularly the “Eligibility” and “Eligible Costs” sections) before submitting your application.
