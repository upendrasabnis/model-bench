**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from both incorporated not‑for‑profit entities and unincorporated groups, provided that the applicant can demonstrate a clear governing body and reliable financial oversight. In cases where an unincorporated group operates under the auspices of a local council—such as yours—we accept the council’s endorsement as evidence of administrative capacity and risk management. The council’s responsibility for insurance, procurement, and compliance is regarded favourably, and we typically require a formal letter of support or auspice agreement from the council confirming that they will act as the fiscal host for any awarded funds. If you have not yet secured such a letter, I recommend obtaining one from your council’s community services or parks department before you begin the full application.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not impose a fixed minimum or maximum award. Instead, we evaluate each proposal on its merit, relevance to the program’s objectives, and the proportion of eligible costs that the grant would cover. Historically, awards have ranged from approximately $5,000 to $75,000, but we are prepared to consider projects of any scale provided the budget is realistic and well‑justified. For a project focused on rainwater tanks and a shade structure, a grant in the $20,000–$40,000 range would typically be appropriate, assuming the overall project cost aligns with the community’s capacity to contribute matching funds or in‑kind support.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure items such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures are explicitly listed as eligible expenditures under the “Environmental Sustainability and Climate Adaptation” category of this grant round. Both items must be directly related to enhancing the resilience of community operations during extreme weather events, which your project clearly does. We do require that all purchased equipment meets relevant Australian standards (e.g., AS/NZS 3500 for plumbing, AS 4084 for shade structures) and that installation complies with local building codes and council regulations. Detailed quotations, product specifications, and compliance certificates will be needed as part of the cost breakdown.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem, objectives, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Budget and Narrative Budget Table** – itemised costs for tanks, shade structure, installation, contingency, and any matching contributions.  
3. **Council Auspice Letter** – a formal statement from your local council confirming their support, insurance coverage, and willingness to act as fiscal host.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – even for unincorporated groups, include a brief description of the garden’s history, volunteer base, and governance structure.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or a simple profit‑and‑loss summary if none are available) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
6. **Insurance Certificate** – a copy of the council‑held public liability and professional indemnity policies, noting that they extend to your activities.  
7. **Quotes and Technical Specifications** – at least three quotations for the tanks and shade structure, along with installation diagrams and compliance certifications.  
8. **Community Support** – letters of support from local businesses, schools, or community groups, and evidence of public engagement (e.g., petition signatures, social media reach).  
9. **Risk Assessment and Management Plan** – outline of potential environmental, safety, and operational risks, together with mitigation strategies.  
10. **Monitoring and Evaluation Framework** – clear indicators, data collection methods, and reporting milestones for measuring the project’s impact.
