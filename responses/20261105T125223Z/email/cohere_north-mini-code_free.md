Thank you for reaching out and for the enthusiasm you and your volunteers are bringing to the community‑garden project. I’m pleased to provide the following clarification on the points you raised, which should help you decide whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme welcomes applications from both incorporated not‑for‑profit organisations and unincorporated community groups, provided the group operates under the formal auspices of a local government entity that can demonstrate legal responsibility for the project. In your case, the council holding the required insurances and acting as the primary contracting body satisfies this criterion. Therefore, an unincorporated group such as yours is eligible to apply, although the application must be signed by an authorised council representative confirming that the council will be the grantee for reporting and audit purposes.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum award. Instead, funding levels are determined on a case‑by‑case basis, reflecting the scope of the proposed activities, the demonstrated community benefit, and the proportion of the total project cost that can realistically be covered by other sources. Past applications of a similar scale (project budgets ranging from $30,000 to $120,000) have received awards between $15,000 and $60,000, but we assess each proposal on its own merits. If you anticipate a total project cost of roughly $40,000–$50,000, a grant in the $25,000–$35,000 range would be a realistic target.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes—capital assets that directly support the resilience and sustainability of community initiatives are fully eligible. Rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures clearly align with the grant’s objective of enhancing operational continuity during extreme weather events. The key is to demonstrate how each item will reduce water consumption, mitigate heat stress, or otherwise improve the long‑term viability of the garden. Detailed specifications, cost breakdowns, and evidence of need (e.g., historical water usage data or heat‑impact reports) strengthen the case.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
When you move to the full‑application stage, we recommend assembling the following materials:

1. **Project Narrative** – a clear description of the problem, objectives, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Budget and Funding Plan** – line‑item costs for the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any complementary expenses, together with a statement of other funding sources.  
3. ** organisational Profile** – a short overview of your volunteer team, the garden’s history, and its community impact.  
4. **Council Auspices Agreement** – a formal letter from the council confirming its role as the contracting body, outlining insurance coverage, and indicating willingness to sub‑grant funds if the application is successful.  
5. **Site Plans and Drawings** – sketches or CAD drawings showing where the tanks and shade structure will be placed.  
6. **Supporting Evidence** – utility bills or water‑usage logs demonstrating the need for water‑saving infrastructure, and any local climate data highlighting increasing summer temperatures.  
7. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from neighbouring community groups, schools, or local businesses.  
8. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and cash‑flow summary) to verify the $40,000 turnover figure.  
9. **Risk Management Plan** – an outline of how you will manage health and safety risks associated with the installation and use of the new infrastructure.
