**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. Unincorporated groups that operate under the auspices of a local government entity are indeed eligible, provided that the council can demonstrate its endorsement of the project and assume financial responsibility for any liabilities that may arise. In practice, this means that the council should sign a supporting letter confirming that it will act as the fiscal host for the grant, hold any required insurance, and oversee the implementation of the works on your behalf. If your council has not yet issued such a letter, we can include a short “auspice” clause in the application form that allows you to attach the council’s endorsement later in the process.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum award. Instead, we allocate funds based on the overall merit of the proposal, the demonstrated community benefit, and the proportion of the total project cost that can be covered by other sources. In recent cycles, awards have ranged from approximately $5,000 to $75,000, but we are happy to consider applications that fall outside these parameters as long as the budget justification is robust and aligns with the grant’s objectives.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes—capital infrastructure that enhances the long‑term resilience and sustainability of community assets is fully eligible. Rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures clearly support the goal of enabling year‑round operation during extreme weather events, a core pillar of the Community Resilience Grant. We do, however, expect the budget breakdown to be detailed, with line items for equipment, installation, any necessary civil works, and a contingency factor (typically 10 % of the total cost). Photometric or structural engineering reports are not required for modest installations, but any site‑specific assessments (e.g., soil testing, drainage plans) should be included if they affect the installation process.

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend compiling the following documents:

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem, objectives, implementation timeline, and anticipated outcomes.  
2. **Budget Table** – a clear spreadsheet showing all costs, including equipment, labour (if any), installation, and a 10 % contingency.  
3. **Council Endorsement Letter** – an official letter from your local council confirming its auspice role, insurance coverage, and support for the project.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – a short overview of your garden’s history, volunteer base, and current operations (including the $40,000 turnover figure).  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small unincorporated group, a simple income‑expenditure summary) to demonstrate fiscal responsibility.  
6. **Community Impact Statement** – evidence of community need, such as letters from local residents, usage statistics, or a brief survey showing the anticipated benefit of reliable water supply and shade.  
7. **Risk Assessment & Safety Plan** – an outline of how you will manage installation risks, especially given that the council holds the insurance.  
8. **Partner Contributions** – any in‑kind or cash contributions from other stakeholders (e.g., volunteer labour, material donations).

Please note that the application portal will also require a completed “Grant Application Form” and a “Compliance Checklist.” All documents should be uploaded in PDF format and named consistently (e.g., “Budget_Jamie_Garden.pdf”).

Finally, we encourage you to review the full grant guidelines on our website, paying particular attention to the evaluation criteria and any reporting obligations that will follow an award.
