Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the thoughtful work you are doing with your community garden. I’m happy to clarify the eligibility criteria for the Community Resilience Grant and to outline the documentation you will need to prepare a strong application.

**Auspiced groups**  
The grant programme does accept applications from unincorporated groups that are auspiced by a recognised legal entity, such as a local council. In these cases the council must be the nominated applicant and will hold the grant funds on behalf of the garden. You will need to provide a formal letter of auspice from the council confirming:

* that the council agrees to act as the legal applicant and financial manager of the grant,  
* that the council’s public liability and volunteer insurance cover the garden’s activities, and  
* the council’s ABN/ACN and contact details for the grant officer who will oversee the funds.

**Grant size**  
The Community Resilience Grant has a minimum award of **AU $5,000** and a maximum of **AU $50,000** per project. Requests outside this range will not be considered. Given your estimated turnover of $40,000, a request in the mid‑range (e.g., $15,000–$25,000) would be well‑aligned with the programme’s intent to support scalable resilience measures.

**Eligible costs**  
Capital works that directly enhance climate resilience—such as the installation of rainwater harvesting tanks and shade structures—are explicitly listed as eligible expenditures, provided they are:

* essential to the project’s resilience objective,  
* supported by itemised quotes from qualified suppliers, and  
* not already funded through another source (unless the grant is clearly supplementing, not duplicating, existing funding).

You should therefore include detailed specifications, cost breakdowns, and a brief justification of how each item will mitigate heat‑related risks and improve water security for the garden.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
1. **Auspice letter** from the local council (signed by an authorised officer).  
2. **Council’s insurance certificate** showing coverage for volunteer activities and public liability.  
3. **Financial summary** of the garden (e.g., recent bank statements or a simple profit‑and‑loss showing the $40,000 turnover).  
4. **Project plan** (one‑ to two‑page) outlining the problem, objectives, activities, timeline, and expected outcomes.  
5. **Itemised quotes** for the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any associated works (e.g., footings, plumbing).  
6. **Letters of support** (optional but helpful) from community members, the council’s sustainability officer, or local schools that benefit from the garden.  
7. **Risk management plan** addressing safety during installation and ongoing maintenance.  
8. **Evidence of any existing contributions** (e.g., volunteer labour, in‑kind donations) to demonstrate co‑funding or community buy‑in.
