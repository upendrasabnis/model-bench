I’m happy to clarify the eligibility criteria and outline the supporting documentation you’ll need for a successful application.

**1. Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The Community Resilience Grant program expressly welcomes applications from groups that are *auspiced* by a local government authority, provided the auspice arrangement meets the following conditions:  

- The council must hold a current, written agreement that authorises the group to act on its behalf for the purposes of the grant.  
- The group must be able to demonstrate that it has a distinct governing body (e.g., a volunteer committee) that oversees project implementation and reporting.  
- All financial and contractual obligations must be traceable to the council’s insurances and account‑keeping systems.  

If your council’s agreement satisfies these points, you are eligible to apply as an unincorporated entity.

**2. Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The grant round offers awards ranging from **$5,000 up to $25,000**. Projects that request less than $5,000 may still be considered, but they must clearly demonstrate how the requested amount aligns with the project’s scope and outcomes. Conversely, applications exceeding $25,000 will be ineligible for this particular funding stream.

**3. Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure that directly contributes to climate‑ready resilience is an eligible expense. The following items are typically fundable:  

- Rainwater harvesting systems (including tanks, pumps, and associated plumbing).  
- Shade structures, pergolas, or other passive cooling installations that reduce heat stress on volunteers and plants.  
- Supporting works such as site preparation, foundations, and utility connections that are integral to the above infrastructure.  

Please note that routine maintenance or purely aesthetic improvements are not considered eligible costs.

**4. Supporting documents you should prepare**  

1. **Letter of auspice** – A signed letter from the council confirming its role as the supervising authority and outlining the terms of the arrangement.  
2. **Financial information** – Recent financial statements or a budget summary showing the $40,000 annual turnover, with a clear breakdown of revenue sources (e.g., produce stall, membership fees).  
3. **Project proposal** – A concise narrative (1–2 pages) describing the objectives, expected outcomes, and how the rainwater tanks and shade structure will improve community resilience.  
4. **Detailed budget** – Itemised costs for the infrastructure, including quotes or pro‑forma invoices from suppliers, and a justification for each line item.  
5. **Risk assessment and mitigation plan** – An outline of potential risks (e.g., construction delays, maintenance challenges) and how you will address them.  
6. **Evidence of governance** – Minutes from committee meetings or a constitution that demonstrate decision‑making processes within the volunteer group.  

Submitting these documents together with your application will enable the assessment panel to evaluate eligibility, financial viability, and the overall merit of the project efficiently.
