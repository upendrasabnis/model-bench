Subject: Re: Question about eligibility for the Community Resilience Grant

Dear Jamie,

Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out before investing time in a full application, and I am pleased to provide clarity on each of the points you have raised.

**Eligibility of Auspiced Groups**
Unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspicing arrangement with an eligible legal entity—such as a local council—are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing organisation meets the program’s eligibility criteria (e.g., incorporated not-for-profit, local government authority, or registered charity) and agrees to accept legal and financial responsibility for the grant. As your council already holds the relevant insurances, you are well positioned. You will need to submit a signed Auspice Agreement (template available on our website) and a letter from the council confirming their willingness to act as auspice, including their ABN and insurance details.

**Grant Amount Limits**
For this round, the minimum grant request is $5,000 and the maximum is $50,000. Your proposed project scope appears to fall comfortably within this range. Please note that we cannot fund more than 80% of total eligible project costs; the remaining 20% may be met through cash or in-kind contributions (volunteer labour valued at $30/hr, donated materials, etc.).

**Eligible Infrastructure Costs**
Capital infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience is an eligible cost category. Rainwater tanks and a permanent shade structure qualify, provided they are fixed assets that remain with the garden site. Eligible expenses include purchase, delivery, installation, and any necessary site preparation (e.g., earthworks, plumbing, engineering certification). Ongoing maintenance costs are not eligible.

**Supporting Documents to Prepare**
In addition to the Auspice Agreement and council letter, please have ready:
1. A simple project plan with timeline, risk register, and budget (use the provided Excel template).
2. Evidence of community need (e.g., letters of support, photos of heat-affected areas, climate data for your region).
3. Your group’s most recent financial statement (even if unaudited) showing the $40,000 turnover.
4. Quotes from at least two suppliers for each major cost item.
5. Proof of land tenure or a long-term licence/lease for the garden site.
6. A sustainability plan outlining how the infrastructure will be maintained post-grant.

The application portal opens on 1 June and closes 30 June. I recommend drafting your responses in the offline template first, then copying them into the online form. If you would like a brief pre-submission review, please email a draft to grants@communityresilience.gov.au at least five business days before the deadline.

We wish you the very best with your project—it sounds like a valuable contribution to local food security and climate adaptation.
