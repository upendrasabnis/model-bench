<a name="introduction"></a>
## 1. Introduction  

Grant writing is both an art and a science.  Successful proposals result from a **systematic process** that begins long before the application deadline and continues long after the first dollar is spent.  This article walks you through **best‑practice strategies** for each phase of grant writing, offering concrete examples, practical checklists, and actionable tips you can apply to any nonprofit context.  

> **Why this matters:**  According to the Foundation Center, nonprofits that follow a disciplined grant‑writing workflow increase their funding success rate by 30‑40 % and improve program sustainability by building stronger relationships with funders.  

The guide is organized into eight phases, each with clear headings, real‑world examples, and checklists you can copy into your project management tool (e.g., Trello, Asana, or a simple spreadsheet).  Whether you are a solo executive director or part of a large operations team, the same principles apply: **research, clarity, alignment, compliance, and continuous learning**.

## 2. Phase 1 – Researching Funders  

### 2.1 Types of Funders & Their Priorities  

| Funder Type | Typical Funding Focus | Typical Grant Size | Reporting Frequency | Key Language to Use |
|-------------|----------------------|-------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Private Foundations** (e.g., Ford, Gates) | Systemic change, advocacy, research | $250k‑$10M | Annual + final report | “Our work aligns with the foundation’s strategic priorities…” |
| **Corporate Foundations** (e.g., Coca‑Cola HBC) | Community development, employee volunteerism | $50k‑$500k | Quarterly progress updates | “Leveraging corporate partnerships to expand reach…” |
| **Federal Agencies** (e.g., NIH, DOE) | Scientific research, public policy | $100k‑$5M | Quarterly + final | “Meets the agency’s mission to advance…” |
| **State/Local Governments** | Neighborhood services, education | $20k‑$200k | Semi‑annual | “Supports city‑wide initiatives for…” |
| **Individual Donors / Endowments** | Small‑scale programs, scholarships | $5k‑$50k | Annual | “Your investment will directly fund…” |

Understanding the funder’s **mission language** helps you mirror their terminology in your proposal, a subtle but powerful way to signal alignment.

### 2.2 Systematic Research Strategies  

| Step | Action | Tools & Resources | Tips |
|------|--------|-------------------|------|
| **1. Identify Likely Partners** | Use the **Foundation Directory Online (FDO)**, **Guidestar**, **Candid**, and **IRS Form 990** searches. | FDO, Candid, IRS e-file | Filter by grant size, geographic focus, and program area. |
| **2. Review Past Grants** | Pull the organization’s **grant history** (via grant databases, annual reports, or the nonprofit’s own “Grants Awarded” page). | GrantStation, Instrumentl, internal CRM | Look for patterns: recurring funders, successful program types. |
| **3. Map Funding Cycles** | Record each funder’s **deadline, award season, and reporting schedule** in a calendar. | Google Calendar, Excel, or specialized tools like **Grant Management Software** (e.g., Submittable). | Set calendar alerts 3‑4 months before deadline to allow time for research. |
| **4. Analyze Application Requirements** | Download the most recent RFP or grant guidelines; note required attachments, format, and evaluation criteria. | PDF copies, funder websites | Highlight any “must‑have” sections (e.g., logic model, budget narrative). |
| **5. Conduct “Funder Deep‑Dives”** | For each top candidate, read at least 2‑3 recent grant reports or press releases to understand their current priorities. | Funders’ annual reports, news releases, social media | Note any recent strategic shifts or new focus areas. |

Create a **centralized spreadsheet** (or use a CRM) with columns such as:

**Tip:** Use conditional formatting to flag upcoming deadlines (e.g., red for <30 days, yellow for 31‑90 days).

### 2.4 Checklist: Funder Research  

- [ ] Identify at least **5–10** potential funders for each proposal cycle.  
- [ ] Verify **eligibility** (geographic, sectoral, organizational type).  
- [ ] Record **funding priorities** and **strategic themes** from the latest funder reports.  
- [ ] Capture **application requirements** (forms, attachments, word limits).  
- [ ] Note **contact information** for program officers (name, email, phone).  
- [ ] Update the **funder database** with a “research date” and “next steps”.  
- [ ] Set **calendar reminders** for each deadline and for follow‑up communications.

## 3. Phase 2 – Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement  

### 3.1 Core Elements of a Needs Statement  

A strong needs statement does three things: **demonstrates relevance, quantifies impact, and creates urgency**. Include the following components:

| Component | What to Include | Example Phrase |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| **Context & Data** | Local statistics, demographic trends, existing service gaps. | “In 2023, 1 in 4 families in our county lived below the federal poverty line, a 12 % increase since 2019.” |
| **Impact on Community** | Human, economic, health, or social consequences. | “Children in these households experience a 30 % higher rate of chronic absenteeism, affecting long‑term academic outcomes.” |
| **Gap Analysis** | What currently exists vs. what is needed. | “While three after‑school programs serve 120 children, there is a documented need for an additional 80 slots for children ages 6‑12.” |
| **Urgency** | Timeline, worsening conditions, or time‑sensitive opportunities. | “The pending closure of the city’s only free tutoring center in June will leave 200 students without critical support.” |
| **Call to Action** | Implicit or explicit request for funding to address the need. | “Your grant will fund the creation of a new after‑school enrichment program that will serve 80 at‑risk youth.” |

### 3.2 Example: From Data to Narrative  

**Raw Data:**  
- 2022 Census: 18 % of families in XYZ County earn < $30k/year.  
- Local school district reports a 15 % dropout rate among students receiving free lunch.  
- Existing community centers provide after‑school programs for only 200 of the 500 eligible children.

**Drafted Needs Statement (≈150 words):**  

> In XYZ County, **18 % of families live below the federal poverty line**, a figure that has risen by 5 % over the past five years.  This economic strain directly correlates with a **15 % dropout rate** among students qualifying for free lunch, jeopardizing future workforce readiness.  While three community centers currently offer after‑school programs, **only 200 of the 500 eligible children** are served, leaving a critical gap in academic support and safe learning environments.  The **pending loss of a key funding source** for the county’s only free tutoring hub will further exacerbate this gap, affecting more than 200 students by the start of the 2025 school year.  **Your investment will create a new, comprehensive after‑school program that will serve 300 additional children, providing homework assistance, mentorship, and enrichment activities proven to reduce dropout rates by 10 % within two years.**  

### 3.3 Checklist: Needs Statement Development  

- [ ] **Collect recent data** (census, health department, school district) that quantifies the problem.  
- [ ] **Identify the target population** (age, geography, income level).  
- [ ] **Calculate the size of the gap** (e.g., number of unmet slots, dollars needed).  
- [ ] **Explain the consequences** (educational, health, economic) of the gap.  
- [ ] **Establish urgency** (timeline, pending cuts, seasonal factors).  
- [ ] **Align language** with funder’s mission terminology (use keywords from funder’s RFP).  
- [ ] **Revise for clarity** – aim for 150‑300 words; ensure it reads like a story, not a bullet list.  
- [ ] **Obtain internal review** from program staff, finance, and senior leadership.

## 4. Phase 3 – Developing the Program Narrative & Logic Model  

### 4.1 What Is a Logic Model?  

A **logic model** is a visual roadmap that links **inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes** (both short‑ and long‑term).  It helps reviewers understand *how* your program will achieve impact and provides a basis for monitoring and evaluation.

**Key Components**  

| Component | Description | Example (Youth Workforce Program) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Inputs** | Resources you bring to the table (staff, money, partners). | $150k grant, 2 full‑time program managers, partnership with local tech bootcamp. |
| **Activities** | Concrete tasks you will do. | 12‑week paid internship, weekly soft‑skill workshops, job‑search coaching. |
| **Outputs** | Immediate, measurable deliverables. | 30 participants placed in internships, 5 workshop sessions completed, 10 job‑ready portfolios created. |
| **Outcomes** | Short‑term (e.g., confidence) and long‑term (e.g., employment). | ↑ Participants’ self‑efficacy score (average +1.5 on 5‑point scale), ↑ 70 % employment rate 6 months post‑program. |
| **Impact** | Broader community or systemic changes. | Reduction in local youth unemployment rate by 2 % within two years. |

### 4.2 Steps to Build a Logic Model  

1. **Start with the “Theory of Change”** – Ask: *If we do X, why will Y happen?*  
2. **Map Inputs** – List all resources (funding, staff, facilities).  
3. **Define Activities** – Break the program into discrete actions (workshops, training modules, outreach).  
4. **Identify Outputs** – Quantify what you will produce (number of participants, reports, materials).  
5. **Select Outcomes** – Choose measurable changes (knowledge gain, employment, attitudes).  
6. **Choose Indicators** – Determine how you’ll measure each outcome (surveys, employment records, performance metrics).  
7. **Create the Visual** – Use a simple table or flow chart (many funders provide templates).  
8. **Review with Stakeholders** – Validate with program staff, board, and external partners.  

### 4.3 Example Logic Model (Youth Workforce Program)  

| Inputs | Activities | Outputs | Outcomes | Impact |
|--------|-----------|---------|----------|--------|
| $150k grant; 2 program managers; partnership with TechHub | • 12‑week paid internship placement<br>• Weekly professional‑development workshops<br>• One‑on‑one career coaching | • 30 youth placed in internships<br>• 5 workshop sessions completed<br>• 10 polished job portfolios created | **Short‑term**: ↑ Confidence (average +1.5/5)<br>**Medium‑term**: ↑ Job‑search skills (90 % rate)<br>**Long‑term**: 70 % employed 6 months later | Local youth unemployment rate drops from 12 % to 10 % in two years. |

### 4.4 Checklist: Logic Model Creation  

- [ ] **Inputs**: List all financial, human, and in‑kind resources.  
- [ ] **Activities**: Ensure each activity is **SMART** (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time‑bound).  
- [ ] **Outputs**: Quantify with numbers (e.g., “X participants,” “Y workshops”).  
- [ ] **Outcomes**: Distinguish short, medium, and long‑term impacts.  
- [ ] **Indicators**: Choose reliable data sources (pre/post surveys, administrative records).  
- [ ] **Visual**: Use a clean table or flowchart; keep it **one page** for reviewers.  
- [ ] **Stakeholder sign‑off**: Get approval from program staff and board.  
- [ ] **Alignment**: Verify that each component directly addresses the needs statement.

## 5. Phase 4 – Designing a Credible Budget  

### 5.1 Direct vs. Indirect Costs  

| Cost Type | Definition | Typical Examples | Notes for Grant Budgets |
|-----------|------------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **Direct Costs** | Expenses that can be traced directly to a specific activity. | Salaries of program staff, supplies, travel for outreach. | Itemize clearly; include fringe benefits if applicable. |
| **Indirect Costs** | Organizational overhead that supports multiple programs. | Rent, utilities, administrative salaries, IT infrastructure. | Many funders cap indirect rates (e.g., 10 % of direct costs). Use **modified indirect cost** if required. |
| **In‑Kind Contributions** | Non‑cash resources contributed by the nonprofit. | Volunteer hours, donated office space, pro‑bono legal services. | Must be valued and documented (e.g., $5k market value for donated venue). |

### 5.2 Budget Format & Templates  

- **Standard Format**: One‑page table with **Line Item**, **Quantity**, **Unit Cost**, **Total**, **Notes**.  
- **Narrative Budget**: For each major line item, a brief justification (1‑2 sentences).  
- **Budget Narrative**: Explain any **unusual expenses** (e.g., travel, equipment) and how they align with program goals.  

**Template (Excel/Google Sheets)**  

| Line Item | Category | Quantity | Unit Cost | Total | Justification |
|-----------|----------|----------|-----------|-------|---------------|
| Program Manager Salary | Personnel | 1 | $55,000 | $55,000 | 0.5 FTE for 12‑month program |
| Administrative Assistant | Personnel | 0.5 | $30,000 | $15,000 | Supports program reporting |
| Rent – Community Center | Facilities | 12 | $800 | $9,600 | Monthly space rental |
| Supplies & Materials | Direct Costs | – | $2,500 | $2,500 | workbooks, art supplies |
| Travel & Mileage | Direct Costs | 200 mi | $0.65/mi | $130 | Outreach to partner schools |
| Evaluation Consultant | Professional Services | 1 | $7,000 | $7,000 | External impact assessment |
| **Total Direct Costs** | | | | **$89,230** | |
| **Indirect (10 % of Direct)** | Overhead | | | **$8,923** | |
| **Grand Total** | | | | **$98,153** | |

### 5.3 Example Budget Narrative (Excerpt)  

> **Personnel – Program Manager**: The program manager will dedicate 0.5 FTE to oversee daily operations, participant recruitment, and partner coordination. This role is essential to ensure the 12‑week internship timeline is met and that participants receive consistent mentorship.  

> **Facilities – Community Center**: Rental costs cover a dedicated classroom space for workshops and a quiet study area for participants. The location was selected for its accessibility via public transit and its proximity to the target neighborhoods.  

> **Evaluation – External Consultant**: An independent evaluator will conduct pre‑ and post‑program surveys, track employment outcomes, and produce a comprehensive impact report for the funder’s use. This ensures rigorous, unbiased measurement of program effectiveness.  

### 5.4 Checklist: Budget Development  

- [ ] **Separate Direct & Indirect** – Use a consistent overhead rate (if required).  
- [ ] **Itemize Salaries** – Include benefits, taxes, and any fringe benefits.  
- [ ] **Add a 10‑15 % contingency** for unexpected expenses (if funder permits).  
- [ ] **Document In‑Kind** – Provide market‑value estimates and letters of donation.  
- [ ] **Justify Each Line Item** – One‑sentence rationale linking to program activities.  
- [ ] **Ensure Totals Align** – Grand total matches the requested amount in the cover letter.  
- [ ] **Run a “Zero‑Based” Review** – Ask: *If I had $0, could I still deliver the program?*  
- [ ] **Obtain Finance Sign‑off** – Have your accountant or fiscal sponsor verify calculations.

## 6. Phase 5 – Preparing Supporting Documents  

### 6.1 Letters of Support  

**Purpose:** Demonstrate community backing and partnership credibility.  

**Key Elements**  

1. **Header** – Organization’s letterhead, date, and recipient’s name/title.  
2. **Opening** – State who you are and the purpose of the letter.  
3. **Specific Support** – Quote the grant proposal (e.g., “We endorse the ‘Youth Workforce Program’ as outlined in Section 3.”).  
4. **Evidence of Involvement** – Mention past collaborations, board membership, or service agreements.  
5. **Contact Information** – Letter writer’s name, title, phone, email.  

**Example (Partner School Letter):**  

> Dear Jane Smith, Program Officer, XYZ Foundation,  
>   
> This letter confirms that **River Valley High School** supports the proposed after‑school enrichment program described in your request for $75,000.  Our school has partnered with your organization since 2021 for the annual science fair, and we are confident that your program will enhance student engagement and academic outcomes.  We are prepared to provide up to **200 volunteer hours** and access to our campus facilities at no cost.  
>   
> Sincerely,  
> **Maria Lopez, Principal, River Valley High School**  

### 6.2 Organizational Profile & CVs  

- **Organizational Profile**: One‑page overview of mission, history, governance, staff, and past achievements.  
- **Key Staff CVs**: Focus on relevant experience (e.g., 5 years of youth program management, grant writing, evaluation).  

### 6.3 Proof of In‑Kind Contributions  

- **Valuation**: Use market rates or reputable sources (e.g., IRS Publication 561).  
- **Documentation**: Receipts, letters, or signed agreements from donors.  

### 6.4 Checklist: Supporting Docs  

- [ ] **Letters of Support**: Minimum 3–5 letters from diverse stakeholders (partners, community leaders, board members).  
- [ ] **Organizational Profile**: Updated within 30 days of submission; include latest audited financial statements.  
- [ ] **Staff CVs**: Tailored to highlight grant‑relevant skills.  
- [ ] **In‑Kind Documentation**: Include valuation sheet and donor acknowledgement letters.  
- [ ] **All Documents**: Proofread for formatting consistency (font, spacing).  
- [ ] **Digital Copies**: Store in a secure, version‑controlled folder (e.g., Google Drive).

<a name="phase-6-submitting-a-winning-application"></a>
## 7. Phase 6 – Submitting a Winning Application  

### 7.1 Final Review & Formatting  

| Review Item | What to Check | Tool/Tip |
|-------------|---------------|----------|
| **Narrative Flow** | Does the needs statement → program description → logic model → budget narrative tell a cohesive story? | Read aloud; note any “jumps.” |
| **Compliance** | All required attachments present, formatting matches RFP specs (page limits, font, margins). | Use a checklist template. |
| **Spelling/Grammar** | Professional polish; no typos. | Grammarly or manual peer review. |
| **Budget Alignment** | Each budget line ties to an activity in the logic model. | Cross‑reference tables. |
| **Legal/Regulatory** | 501(c)(3) status, conflict‑of‑interest disclosures (if required). | Check funder’s “legal requirements” PDF. |
| **Signature** | Authorized signatory (Executive Director or Board Chair) signs cover letter. | Keep a PDF copy of signed letter. |

### 7.2 Deadline Management  

- **Create a Master Calendar** – Include “Internal Draft Due,” “Final Proofreading,” “Submit by,” and “Follow‑up Date.”  
- **Set Up Automated Reminders** – Use Outlook/Google Calendar alerts 7 days, 48 hours, and 2 hours before deadline.  
- **Track Submission Status** – Record the date you click “Submit”; note any confirmation emails.  

### 7.3 Submission Confirmation  

- **Save Confirmation Page** – Screenshot or PDF of the funder’s submission receipt.  
- **Log in Funder Database** – Record “Submitted – Pending Review” in your CRM.  
- **Notify Stakeholders** – Email program staff, board, and finance that the application is live.  

### 7.4 Checklist: Application Submission  

- [ ] All required sections completed and formatted per RFP.  
- [ ] Supporting documents attached and named exactly as required.  
- [ ] Budget narrative linked to logic model activities.  
- [ ] Letters of support uploaded with proper filenames.  
- [ ] Cover letter signed by authorized person.  
- [ ] Internal approvals (Finance, Legal, Board) obtained and documented.  
- [ ] Submission confirmation saved and logged.  
- [ ] Follow‑up plan (e.g., thank‑you email) scheduled for 24‑48 hours post‑submission.

## 8. Phase 7 – Post‑Award Management & Reporting  

### 8.1 Understanding the Grant Agreement  

- **Award Letter**: Contains funding amount, period of performance, reporting requirements, and any deliverables.  
- **Term Sheet**: May outline milestones, evaluation schedule, and compliance obligations.  
- **Legal Attachments**: Confidentiality, data privacy, and intellectual property clauses (if applicable).  

**Action**: Store the full agreement in a secure folder, and create a **Grant Task List** (see Appendix A).  

### 8.2 Financial Reporting  

| Report Type | Frequency | Content | Example Timeline |
|-------------|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Quarterly Financial Report** | Every 3 months | Budget to actual spending, encumbrances, bank reconciliations. | Q2 – 6/30/2025 |
| **Expenditure Schedule** | With each financial report | Detailed line‑item breakdown of expenditures. | Q2 – 6/30/2025 |
| **Final Financial Closeout** | At project end | Year‑end audit, unspent funds disposition. | 12/31/2026 |

**Best Practices**  

- Use **accounting software** (e.g., QuickBooks, NetSuite) that can generate exportable reports.  
- Keep **receipts** for all expenditures > $100.  
- Reconcile bank statements **within 15 days** of each month‑end.  

### 8.3 Progress / Narrative Reporting  

- **Template**: Include headings: Project Overview, Activities Completed, Challenges & Solutions, Next Steps, Indicators Update.  
- **Evidence**: Include photos, participant testimonials, survey excerpts.  
- **Metrics**: Reference the logic model’s indicators; show pre/post data where possible.  

**Example Progress Report Excerpt (6 months)**  

> **Project Overview** – Implemented the “After‑School Enrichment” program serving 120 students across three schools.  
>   
> **Activities Completed** – Conducted 12 workshops, trained 3 volunteer mentors, and launched a digital learning portal.  
>   
> **Challenges & Solutions** – Unexpected school closure due to weather forced us to shift two sessions online; we secured Google Classroom licenses and delivered virtual sessions without loss of engagement.  
>   
> **Next Steps** – Finalize participant assessments, begin dissemination of best‑practice guide to partner organizations.  
>   
> **Indicators Update** – Pre‑program survey showed an average self‑efficacy score of 2.8/5; post‑program survey (n=115) shows 4.3/5 (p < 0.01), indicating a significant gain in confidence.  

### 8.4 Monitoring, Evaluation & Learning (MEL)  

1. **Create a MEL Plan** early (often required in the grant agreement).  
2. **Define Data Sources** – Surveys, focus groups, administrative records, third‑party audits.  
3. **Set Frequency** – Weekly activity logs, monthly progress updates, quarterly evaluation reviews.  
4. **Establish a Review Cycle** – Quarterly board MEL subcommittee meeting to assess against indicators.  
5. **Document Lessons Learned** – Capture what worked, what didn’t, and why.  

### 8.5 Checklist: Post‑Award Compliance  

- [ ] **Grant Agreement Review** – All terms understood; share with finance and program team.  
- [ ] **Budget Management** – Quarterly financial reports filed on time; variance analysis completed.  
- [ ] **Expenditure Documentation** – All receipts filed; supporting docs attached to reports.  
- [ ] **Progress Reports** – Submitted per schedule; include evidence and indicator updates.  
- [ ] **MEL Activities** – Data collection tools deployed; baseline and endline data collected.  
- [ ] **Compliance Audits** – Internal or external audit completed; findings addressed.  
- [ ] **Stakeholder Communication** – Quarterly newsletters or updates sent to funders and partners.  
- [ ] **Unspent Funds** – Disbursed or returned per funder instructions.  
- [ ] **Future Funding** – Begin preliminary research for renewal or new grants while current project is active.

## 9. Phase 8 – Continuous Improvement & Future Funding  

### 9.1 De‑brief After Each Grant  

| De‑brief Element | How to Capture | Tool |
|------------------|---------------|------|
| **What Worked** | List activities, partnerships, or funding decisions that exceeded expectations. | One‑page “Success Log” (Google Doc) |
| **What Didn’t** | Document barriers (e.g., low enrollment, unforeseen regulatory delays). | Same doc, “Challenges” column |
| **Lessons Learned** | Summarize best practices to apply to next proposal (e.g., timeline buffers, stronger needs statement). | “Action Items” column with owners and due dates |
| **Financial Insights** | Identify cost overruns, under‑budgeted items, or unexpected in‑kind value. | Spreadsheet with variance % |
| **Stakeholder Feedback** | Note funder comments (e.g., “budget narrative could be tighter”) for future alignment. | Email thread archive |

**Tip:** Conduct a **de‑brief meeting** with program staff, finance, and senior leadership within 30 days of final report submission.  

### 9.2 Updating Your Grant Calendar  

- **Template**: Columns – Funder, Deadline, Submission Date, Status (Draft, Submitted, Closed), Next Action (e.g., “Follow‑up”).  
- **Automation**: Use Google Sheets conditional formatting to highlight upcoming deadlines (red <30 days, yellow 31‑90 days).  
- **Version Control**: Keep a **master archive** of all submitted proposals (PDFs, emails, confirmations).  

### 9.3 Leveraging Success for New Proposals  

1. **Showcase Success Stories** – Use participant testimonials, media coverage, and funder‑provided press releases.  
2. **Data‑Driven Expansion** – If a pilot program succeeded, use the logic model to propose scaling to new geographies.  
3. **Network Building** – Attend funder’s grantee conferences; ask for introductions to peer organizations with similar missions.  
4. **Renewal Strategies** – Prepare a **renewal proposal** 3‑4 months before expiration, highlighting measurable outcomes and lessons learned.

### 10.3 Glossary of Common Grant Terms  

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Indirect Cost** | Overhead expenses that support multiple programs; often expressed as a % of direct costs. |
| **In‑Kind Contribution** | Non‑cash resources (volunteered services, donated goods) valued at fair market price. |
| **Logic Model** | Visual framework linking resources to activities, outputs, and outcomes. |
| **Milestones** | Specific, measurable achievements set to track progress (e.g., “Recruit 30 participants”). |
| **Narrative** | The written story that explains the problem, solution, and impact. |
| **Reporting Period** | The interval (monthly, quarterly, annually) in which progress reports are due. |
| **RFP (Return on Funding Partnership)** | Funders’ internal metric for how effectively their dollars generate measurable change. |
| **Subgrant** | Transfer of a portion of award to subgrantees or partner organizations. |
| **Term** | Length of time for which the grant is awarded (e.g., 12 months). |
| **Unspent Funds** | Money not used by project end; must be returned or re‑allocated per funder policy. |
