SECTION 1: RESEARCHING FUNDERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1.1 DEFINE YOUR ORGANIZATION’S FUNDING PRIORITIES
- List the program areas you need support for (e.g., youth mentorship, environmental conservation, health equity).
- Rank each area by strategic importance and funding gap.
- Note any geographic restrictions (local, regional, national, international).

STEP 1.2 BUILD A FUNDER DATABASE
- Use Foundation Directory Online, GrantStation, Guidestar, and state grant portals.
- Include columns: Funder Name, Mission Alignment, Giving History, Average Grant Size, Application Deadline, Contact Person, Preferred Format (LOI, full proposal, online portal).
- Update the database quarterly.

STEP 1.3 ANALYZE GIVING PATTERNS
- Pull the last 5‑10 years of grants for each target funder.
- Identify trends: focus areas, types of organizations funded, typical grant duration, reporting requirements.
- Flag funders that have funded similar organizations or projects.

STEP 1.4 MATCH FUNDER PRIORITIES TO YOUR NEEDS
- Create a “Fit Score” matrix (1‑5) for each funder across: Mission Alignment, Geographic Fit, Funding Capacity, Application Complexity, Relationship Potential.
- Prioritize funders with a total score ≥ 18 out of 25.

STEP 1.5 ESTABLISH RELATIONSHIPS BEFORE APPLYING
- Attend funder webinars, site visits, and networking events.
- Schedule a brief introductory call with program officers.
- Send a concise one‑page “impact snapshot” highlighting recent outcomes.

SECTION 2: CRAFTING A COMPELLING NEEDS STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 2.1 START WITH DATA, NOT ANECDOTE
- Cite recent statistics (e.g., “In 2023, 22% of children in County X lived below the poverty line, up 3% from 2021”).
- Use reputable sources: Census, CDC, peer‑reviewed journals, local government reports.

PRINCIPLE 2.2 CONNECT DATA TO YOUR COMMUNITY
- Translate national numbers to local impact (“That equals 1,850 children in our service area lacking access to after‑school programs”).
- Include a brief story or quote from a beneficiary to humanize the data.

PRINCIPLE 2.3 SHOW THE GAP
- Explain what currently exists (e.g., “Two after‑school sites serve 300 youth; waitlist exceeds 400”).
- Quantify unmet need (“Additional 200 slots required to meet demand”).

PRINCIPLE 2.4 ARTICULATE URGENCY
- Reference time‑sensitive factors: policy changes, funding cuts, emerging crises.
- Example: “State budget cuts will eliminate the only transportation voucher program for youth by July 2025.”

PRINCIPLE 2.5 ALIGN WITH FUNDER’S LANGUAGE
- Mirror key phrases from the funder’s guidelines (e.g., “equitable access,” “capacity building,” “systemic change”).
- Avoid jargon that the funder does not use.

EXAMPLE NEEDS STATEMENT (150 WORDS)
“In the Riverbend School District, 22% of students (1,850 children) live in households earning less than $25,000 annually (U.S. Census, 2023). Only two community centers provide after‑school programming, serving 300 youth. A waitlist of 420 children demonstrates a 140% unmet demand. Recent state legislation eliminates the transportation voucher that enabled 60% of low‑income families to reach these centers, effective July 2025. Without immediate investment, an additional 250 children will lose access to safe, enriching environments, increasing risk for academic failure and juvenile justice involvement. Our proposed Expanded Horizons Program will add three mobile units, delivering STEM, arts, and mentorship directly to neighborhoods, closing the access gap for 500 children in the first year.”

CHECKLIST: NEEDS STATEMENT
[ ] Data sources cited and current (≤ 2 years old)
[ ] Localized statistics presented
[ ] Gap quantified with numbers
[ ] Urgency factor identified
[ ] Language aligned with funder’s priorities
[ ] Length ≤ 250 words (or funder limit)

SECTION 3: DEVELOPING A LOGIC MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------
COMPONENT 3.1 INPUTS
- Staff (FTE), volunteers, facilities, equipment, partnerships, cash and in‑kind contributions.
- Example: 2.5 FTE program coordinators, 1 van, $15,000 in donated curriculum kits.

COMPONENT 3.2 ACTIVITIES
- Specific actions: curriculum delivery, mentor recruitment, family workshops, transportation.
- Example: “Weekly 2‑hour STEM labs at 3 mobile sites; monthly mentor‑matching events.”

COMPONENT 3.3 OUTPUTS
- Countable products: number of sessions held, youth served, materials distributed.
- Example: “120 STEM sessions; 500 youth enrolled; 300 family workshop attendees.”

COMPONENT 3.4 OUTCOMES (SHORT‑TERM)
- Changes in knowledge, attitudes, skills.
- Example: “80% of participants demonstrate increased STEM interest (pre/post survey).”

COMPONENT 3.5 OUTCOMES (INTERMEDIATE)
- Behavioral or condition changes.
- Example: “30% reduction in school disciplinary referrals among participants after 12 months.”

COMPONENT 3.6 IMPACT (LONG‑TERM)
- Systemic or population‑level change.
- Example: “Increase high‑school graduation rate in target zip codes by 5% within 5 years.”

VISUAL TEMPLATE (TEXT‑BASED)
INPUTS → ACTIVITIES → OUTPUTS → SHORT‑TERM OUTCOMES → INTERMEDIATE OUTCOMES → IMPACT

CHECKLIST: LOGIC MODEL
[ ] All six components completed
[ ] Each link supported by evidence or theory of change
[ ] Indicators defined for every output and outcome
[ ] Model fits on one page (or funder’s required format)
[ ] Reviewed by program staff and evaluator

SECTION 4: WRITING THE PROJECT NARRATIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
STRUCTURE 4.1 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (≤ 1 PAGE)
- One‑sentence problem statement.
- One‑sentence solution.
- Target population, geographic area.
- Total request amount and project period.
- Key outcomes and sustainability hint.

STRUCTURE 4.2 ORGANIZATIONAL CAPACITY
- Mission, history, governance, staff expertise.
- Highlight past grant performance (on‑time reports, outcomes met).
- Include letters of support or MOUs.

STRUCTURE 4.3 PROGRAM DESIGN
- Detailed description of activities, timeline (Gantt style), staffing plan.
- Reference logic model components.
- Address cultural competence and accessibility.

STRUCTURE 4.4 EVALUATION PLAN
- Formative (process) and summative (outcome) methods.
- Data sources, collection tools, analysis plan.
- Role of external evaluator if applicable.

STRUCTURE 4.5 SUSTAINABILITY
- Diversified funding strategy (earned income, individual donors, corporate sponsors).
- Plan for integrating successful elements into core operations.
- Policy or systems change leverage.

WRITING TIPS
- Use active voice (“We will deliver” not “Delivery will be made”).
- Keep sentences ≤ 20 words.
- Avoid acronyms unless defined first.
- Align each paragraph with a specific review criterion.

CHECKLIST: PROJECT NARRATIVE
[ ] Executive summary hits all required elements
[ ] Capacity section includes ≥ 3 concrete achievements
[ ] Activities mapped to logic model outputs
[ ] Evaluation includes both quantitative and qualitative measures
[ ] Sustainability plan names at least two future funding sources
[ ] Word count within funder limits

SECTION 5: BUDGET DEVELOPMENT AND JUSTIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY 5.1 PERSONNEL
- List each position, % FTE, annual salary, fringe rate, grant‑funded portion.
- Example: Program Director 0.5 FTE @ $70,000 = $35,000; Fringe 28% = $9,800.

CATEGORY 5.2 FRINGE BENEFITS
- Health, retirement, payroll taxes.
- Use organization’s actual rates; note if any costs are cost‑shared.

CATEGORY 5.3 TRAVEL
- Local mileage (rate per IRS), lodging, per diem for conferences.
- Justify each trip (e.g., “Site visits to 3 mobile units quarterly”).

CATEGORY 5.4 EQUIPMENT
- Items > $5,000 unit cost, useful life > 1 year.
- Provide quotes or vendor estimates.

CATEGORY 5.5 SUPPLIES
- Consumables: curriculum kits, snacks, printing.
- Unit cost × quantity.

CATEGORY 5.6 CONTRACTUAL
- Sub‑contractors, consultants, evaluation firm.
- Include scope of work and rate.

CATEGORY 5.7 OTHER DIRECT COSTS
- Rent, utilities, insurance, communications, indirect costs (if allowed).

CATEGORY 5.8 INDIRECT COSTS
- Apply negotiated rate (e.g., 15% MTDC) or funder’s capped rate.
- Show calculation base.

BUDGET NARRATIVE (JUSTIFICATION)
- One paragraph per line item explaining necessity, cost reasonableness, and allocation method.
- Reference logic model activities.

EXAMPLE BUDGET NARRATIVE EXCERPT
“Personnel: The 0.5 FTE Program Director oversees curriculum development, staff supervision, and funder reporting. Salary reflects the organization’s approved pay scale for a director‑level position. Fringe benefits calculated at the organization’s 28% rate, covering health insurance, retirement contribution, and payroll taxes.”

CHECKLIST: BUDGET
[ ] All line items match narrative activities
[ ] Personnel costs broken down by FTE and fringe
[ ] Travel rates follow federal or organizational policy
[ ] Equipment quotes attached
[ ] Indirect cost rate approved or funder‑capped
[ ] Total request equals sum of line items
[ ] Budget narrative provided for every line item
[ ] No unallowable costs (e.g., lobbying, entertainment)

SECTION 6: PREPARING SUPPORTING DOCUMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
DOCUMENT 6.1 LETTERS OF SUPPORT / MEMORANDA OF UNDERSTANDING
- Minimum three: one from a community partner, one from a government agency, one from a beneficiary group.
- Each letter on partner letterhead, signed, dated, referencing the specific project.

DOCUMENT 6.2 ORGANIZATIONAL CHART
- Show governance (board), executive leadership, program staff, and grant‑funded positions highlighted.

DOCUMENT 6.3 AUDITED FINANCIAL STATEMENTS (LAST 2 YEARS)
- Include management letter if any findings.

DOCUMENT 6.4 IRS 990 (MOST RECENT)
- Highlight program service revenue and net assets.

DOCUMENT 6.5 KEY STAFF RESUMES / BIOS
- One‑page each, emphasizing relevant experience and grant management.

DOCUMENT 6.6 EVALUATION TOOLS (SURVEYS, INTERVIEW PROTOCOLS)
- Attach draft instruments; note IRB status if applicable.

SECTION 7: SUBMISSION PROCESS AND TIMELINE MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 7.1 CREATE A MASTER CALENDAR
- Mark funder deadlines, internal review dates, board approval dates, and buffer days (minimum 5 business days before deadline).
- Use a shared project management tool (Asana, Trello, Google Sheet).

PHASE 7.2 ASSIGN RESPONSIBILITIES
- Lead writer, budget officer, evaluator, executive director, board liaison.
- Define deliverable dates for each section.

PHASE 7.3 INTERNAL REVIEW CYCLES
- First draft → program staff review (content accuracy).
- Second draft → finance review (budget alignment).
- Third draft → executive director sign‑off.
- Final proofread → compliance officer (format, page limits, required attachments).

PHASE 7.4 SUBMISSION METHOD
- Online portal: create account early, test upload of a dummy PDF.
- Email: confirm attachment size limits, use PDF/A format.
- Hard copy: use certified mail with tracking; include cover letter.

PHASE 7.5 POST‑SUBMISSION TRACKING
- Log submission confirmation number.
- Set reminder for funder’s anticipated decision date.
- Prepare a “debrief” template for any feedback received.

CHECKLIST: SUBMISSION
[ ] Master calendar populated with all deadlines
[ ] Roles and internal deadlines assigned
[ ] Three review cycles completed
[ ] Final PDF meets all formatting specs
[ ] Submission confirmed (receipt or tracking)
[ ] Decision‑date reminder set

SECTION 8: POST‑AWARD MANAGEMENT AND REPORTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 8.1 AWARD ACCEPTANCE
- Sign grant agreement, note any special conditions.
- Set up a dedicated grant account in accounting software.

STEP 8.2 KICKOFF MEETING
- Invite program staff, finance, evaluator, funder program officer (if possible).
- Review timeline, deliverables, reporting schedule, communication protocol.

STEP 8.3 FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT
- Track expenditures against budget line items monthly.
- Use a grant‑specific budget vs. actual report.
- Request prior approval for any budget revisions > 10% of a line item.

STEP 8.4 PROGRAMMATIC MONITORING
- Collect output data per logic model (attendance sheets, session logs).
- Conduct quarterly process evaluations (staff debriefs, participant feedback).

STEP 8.5 REPORTING REQUIREMENTS
- Interim narrative report (usually 6 months): progress toward outcomes, challenges, adjustments.
- Financial report: actuals vs. budget, explanation of variances > 5%.
- Final report: outcome data, impact stories, lessons learned, sustainability update.
- Adhere to funder’s template, page limits, and submission portal.

STEP 8.6 DATA MANAGEMENT & PRIVACY
- Store participant data in encrypted, access‑controlled system.
- Follow HIPAA/FERPA as applicable.
- Document data retention schedule.

STEP 8.7 CLOSEOUT
- Submit final financial reconciliation (including any unspent funds).
- Return or reallocate equipment per funder policy.
- Archive all grant files (digital and physical) for minimum 3 years post‑closeout.

CHECKLIST: POST‑AWARD
[ ] Grant agreement signed and filed
[ ] Dedicated accounting code created
[ ] Kickoff meeting held within 30 days
[ ] Monthly budget vs. actual reports produced
[ ] Quarterly programmatic data uploaded to funder portal
[ ] Interim narrative and financial reports submitted on time
[ ] Final report includes outcome metrics and sustainability plan
[ ] All records archived per retention policy

SECTION 9: CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT AND LEARNING
--------------------------------------------------------------------
ACTION 9.1 POST‑GRANT DEBRIEF
- Convene staff and evaluator within 60 days of closeout.
- Review what worked, what didn’t, and why.
- Capture lessons in a “Grant Learning Log” accessible to all proposal writers.

ACTION 9.2 UPDATE FUNDER DATABASE
- Record funder feedback, reporting experience, and likelihood of future funding.
- Adjust fit scores for future cycles.

ACTION 9.3 REFINE TEMPLATES
- Incorporate successful language, budget formats, and evaluation tools into master templates.
- Version‑control templates (e.g., v2.3 2025‑07).

ACTION 9.4 STAFF DEVELOPMENT
- Schedule annual grant‑writing workshops.
- Encourage certification (e.g., GPC, CFRE) for key personnel.

ACTION 9.5 BENCHMARKING
- Compare success rates (applications submitted vs. awarded) against sector averages.
- Set improvement targets (e.g., increase award rate from 22% to 30% in 2 years).

CHECKLIST: CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

[ ] Funder database updated with outcomes
[ ] Templates revised and versioned
