**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of applicants, including unincorporated community groups, provided that the group has a reputable “auspicing” organization that can act as the fiscal and legal conduit for the grant. In practice, many successful applicants rely on a local council to serve this role because the council already holds the necessary insurances, holds assets, and can enter into contracts on behalf of the group. If your council is willing to act as the implementing partner—sign­ing the grant agreement, managing disbursements, and reporting on outcomes—your garden will meet the eligibility criteria. If the council prefers a more hands‑off approach, you may also be able to apply directly, but you would then need to secure equivalent insurance coverage and demonstrate that you can meet all fiduciary requirements. In either case, a formal letter of support from the council confirming its willingness to auspice the project is a standard requirement.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to fund projects that deliver measurable resilience benefits, and the award size reflects the scale and impact of the proposed work. Typically, grants range from **$15,000 to $150,000**, although the exact amount is determined during the review stage based on the project’s budget, cost‑benefit analysis, and alignment with program priorities. There is no fixed minimum; even smaller‑scale infrastructure upgrades can be funded if they demonstrate strong community benefit and feasibility. Conversely, there is no upper cap beyond the total budget allocated for the round, so larger, multi‑component initiatives are also possible provided they meet all other eligibility criteria.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rain‑water tanks and shade structures**  
Yes—capital assets that directly enhance a community’s ability to withstand climate‑related stresses are expressly eligible. Rain‑water harvesting systems and durable shade structures clearly fall under the “climate adaptation” and “essential services” categories outlined in our guidelines. The key is that the costs must be **directly attributable to the project**, include appropriate specifications, and be justified by the anticipated resilience outcomes (e.g., reduced water consumption, lower heat stress for volunteers and visitors, and continued operation during drought or extreme heat events). We recommend that the budget line items for these items be broken down with unit costs, quantities, and any installation fees to facilitate assessment.

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we suggest assembling the following documentation:

1. **Project Narrative** – A concise description of the problem, objectives, implementation timeline, and expected resilience outcomes.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – Itemized costs for tanks, shade structures, installation, contingencies, and any other consumables (e.g., water‑filtration filters).  
3. **Organizational Profile** – A brief overview of your garden’s mission, volunteer base, and community reach, plus the council’s auspice letter.  
4. **Financial Statements** – Most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and cash flow summary) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you referenced.  
5. **Insurance Certificate** – Confirmation that the council’s insurance covers the project activities, or, if you apply independently, a copy of your own liability and property insurance.  
6. **Letters of Support** – At least two endorsements from local stakeholders (e.g., neighboring businesses, schools, or community groups) affirming the project’s value.  
7. **Site Plans / Drawings** – Sketches or architectural plans showing where the tanks and shade structure will be placed.  
8. **Monitoring and Evaluation Plan** – Outline of how you will track usage, water savings, temperature reduction, and community satisfaction post‑implementation.  

We also recommend reviewing the full grant guidelines (available on our website) for any specific formatting requirements, such as the maximum length of the project narrative or the acceptable file types for supporting documents.
