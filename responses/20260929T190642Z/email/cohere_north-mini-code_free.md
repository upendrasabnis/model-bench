I’m pleased to share that the program is designed to support a broad spectrum of community‑based initiatives, and many of the features you have described align well with our objectives.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The grant guidelines expressly permit unincorporated community groups that operate under the auspices of a local government body, provided the council holds the necessary legal responsibilities (e.g., insurance, governance, and financial accountability) on the group’s behalf. In such cases, the council is considered the “lead applicant” for the purposes of the application process, and the community group’s project proposal is submitted through the council’s administrative channel. This arrangement is common for volunteer‑run gardens, playgrounds, and similar facilities that benefit from the council’s existing risk‑management framework.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
Our Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, the funding level is determined by the overall merit of the proposal, the total project cost, and the availability of funds within the current round. Typical awards for infrastructure projects of the scale you describe (rainwater harvesting and shade structures) range from $15,000 to $75,000, but we have funded smaller pilot installations as well as larger, multi‑year upgrades. The key is to demonstrate clear outcomes, cost‑effectiveness, and alignment with the grant’s resilience objectives.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes—capital infrastructure such as rainwater tanks, associated plumbing, and permanent shade structures are explicitly listed as eligible expenditures. The program seeks projects that enhance environmental sustainability, reduce resource consumption, and improve the ability of community spaces to function under climate stress. In addition to the tanks and shade, you may also include site preparation, landscaping, and basic monitoring equipment (e.g., water level sensors) as allowable costs.

**Supporting documentation you should prepare**  
To build a compelling application, we recommend compiling the following documents:

1. **Project Proposal Brief** – a concise narrative (maximum 2 pages) that outlines the problem, proposed solution, expected benefits, implementation timeline, and budget breakdown.  
2. **Budget Table** – a line‑item spreadsheet showing all capital and operational costs, including contingency (typically 10 % of total project cost).  
3. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from the local council (confirming auspice status and insurance coverage) and, if relevant, neighboring community members or stakeholder groups.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page description of your volunteer team, governance structure, and any past projects (even informal ones).  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small group, a simple cash‑flow summary) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover figure and how the project will be funded.  
6. **Risk Management Plan** – an outline of safety measures, maintenance responsibilities, and how the council’s insurance will cover the new infrastructure.  
7. **Sustainability Plan** – a brief outline of ongoing maintenance, water‑usage monitoring, and community engagement activities to ensure long‑term operation.

We also advise that you verify the exact application deadline (you mentioned “next month”) and confirm whether the council will be acting as the primary applicant. If the council will submit the application, you may need to provide them with a draft proposal for internal review before the formal submission date.

Finally, should you decide to proceed, our grants team is happy to provide a technical review of your draft proposal at no cost. Please forward any early drafts to grants@communityresilience.org, and we will acknowledge receipt within 48 hours and provide feedback within five business days.
