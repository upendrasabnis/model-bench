**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. Unincorporated groups that are formally auspiced—or “hosted”—by a local government authority are indeed eligible, provided that the council can confirm it will act as the fiscal sponsor for any awarded funds and will assume responsibility for the required insurances, accounting and reporting obligations. In practice this means the council’s endorsement should be documented in a short letter of support, and the council should be prepared to sign a grant agreement on your behalf. If you are able to obtain such a letter (or a memorandum of understanding) from your council, we will treat the group as a “hosted entity” for the purposes of the application.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
We do not prescribe a fixed grant size; awards are determined on the basis of project merit, community impact, and the proportion of total eligible costs that the project requires. Typical awards for infrastructure projects in the community‑garden sector range from approximately $5,000 to $75,000, but larger amounts may be considered for more extensive works or for projects that demonstrate strong partnership and leverage. Your projected cost for two rainwater tanks and a shade structure will likely fall within the mid‑range of this band, though the final amount will depend on the detailed budget you provide.

**Eligibility of the proposed infrastructure**  
Yes—capital assets such as rainwater tanks and permanent shade structures are expressly listed as eligible costs under the “Climate‑Resilient Community Infrastructure” category of this grant round. Both items must directly support the ongoing operation of the garden during hot‑weather periods, and they must be purchased and installed in accordance with relevant building, plumbing and environmental regulations. Please ensure that any tank you select complies with the Australian Standards for rainwater harvesting and that the shade structure is designed to meet local wind‑load requirements.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To complete a competitive application we recommend assembling the following documents (all to be submitted via the online portal):

1. **Project Narrative** – a clear description of the problem you are addressing (increasing summer heat), the proposed solution, and the expected outcomes for garden users and the wider community.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – line‑item costs for tanks, shade structure, installation, any ancillary materials, and a breakdown of your organisation’s contribution (in‑kind labour, volunteer hours, etc.).  
3. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for a small group, a simple statement of income and expenses) demonstrating an annual turnover of roughly $40,000.  
4. **Council Auspice Documentation** – a formal letter from your local council confirming that it will act as fiscal sponsor, hold insurance, and sign the grant agreement.  
5. **Insurance Confirmation** – a copy of the council’s insurance certificate (or a letter confirming coverage) that names the garden as an insured activity.  
6. **Letters of Support** – at least two letters from community stakeholders (e.g., neighbouring schools, local businesses, or other volunteer groups) endorsing the project.  
7. **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page overview of your group’s mission, volunteer composition, and any previous community projects.  
8. **Risk Management Plan** – a brief outline of how you will manage installation safety, maintenance, and ongoing operational risks.  

If you are able to provide these documents in a single PDF package, the application will be processed more efficiently.
