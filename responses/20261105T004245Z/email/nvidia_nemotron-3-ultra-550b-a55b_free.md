Subject: Re: Question about eligibility for the Community Resilience Grant

Dear Jamie,

Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. It is encouraging to hear about the valuable work your community garden is undertaking, and I am pleased to provide detailed guidance on each of your questions to assist with your planning.

First, regarding organisational eligibility: unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspice arrangement with an eligible legal entity—such as your local council—are absolutely eligible to apply. The auspicing organisation must be a legal entity (e.g., an incorporated association, company limited by guarantee, or local government authority) that holds appropriate public liability insurance and agrees to accept legal and financial responsibility for the grant. As your council already holds the relevant insurances and provides governance oversight, this arrangement meets our requirements. You will need to provide a signed auspice agreement or letter of support from the council confirming their willingness to act in this capacity, along with the council’s ABN and proof of current public liability insurance.

Second, the Community Resilience Grant offers funding between $5,000 and $50,000 per project. There is no minimum project budget, but the grant amount requested must fall within this range. Given your annual turnover of approximately $40,000, your project budget should demonstrate that the grant request is proportionate and that your group has the capacity to manage the funds and deliver the project.

Third, infrastructure costs such as rainwater tanks and shade structures are considered eligible capital expenditure, provided they are directly linked to the project’s resilience outcomes—specifically, enabling continued operation during extreme heat and water scarcity. Eligible costs include purchase, delivery, installation, and any necessary site preparation or plumbing. Ineligible costs include ongoing maintenance, operational salaries, or retrospective funding for works already completed.

Finally, to streamline your application, please prepare the following supporting documents: (1) a detailed project plan with timelines and risk management; (2) a budget breakdown with quotes for all major items; (3) the council’s auspice agreement and insurance certificate; (4) evidence of community support (e.g., letters from members, partner organisations, or the council); (5) your group’s most recent financial statement or treasurer’s report; and (6) a brief governance statement outlining how decisions are made within the volunteer group.

We also recommend attending our optional online applicant workshop on 14 May, which covers assessment criteria and common pitfalls. Applications close at 5:00 pm AEST on 30 June, and late submissions cannot be accepted.

Please do not hesitate to contact me if you require further clarification or wish to discuss your project concept before applying. We are committed to supporting grassroots initiatives like yours and look forward to potentially receiving your application.
