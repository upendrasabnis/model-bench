Thank you for reaching out and for the clear description of your project – it sounds like a very worthwhile initiative that aligns well with the goals of the Community Resilience Grant program. I’m pleased to provide the following clarification on the points you raised.  

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines expressly permit applications from unincorporated community groups that are formally auspiced (or “hosted”) by a local government entity. The council’s involvement is viewed favourably because it provides a layer of oversight, ensures that the group has access to necessary insurance coverage, and helps to guarantee that the project will be integrated into broader municipal planning. To satisfy the eligibility criteria, we will need to see a formal letter of auspice from your council confirming that they will act as the fiscal host for any grant funds received, that they will maintain the required insurances, and that they consent to managing the grant on your behalf.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award; rather, the funding level is determined by the scope, cost‑effectiveness, and overall impact of the proposed project. Typical awards for infrastructure‑focused initiatives range from $15,000 to $75,000, but applications that clearly demonstrate strong community benefit and fiscal responsibility may receive funding above or below this band. We encourage you to draft a budget that reflects the actual costs of the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary expenses, and to be prepared to justify any amount you request.  

**Eligibility of rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes – both rainwater harvesting equipment and permanent shade structures are considered eligible capital costs under the “Climate‑Resilient Infrastructure” category of the grant. The program specifically supports projects that reduce water consumption, mitigate heat‑island effects, and enhance the ability of community spaces to operate under extreme weather conditions. We do require that the items be new, purpose‑built for the garden’s needs, and installed in accordance with all relevant building codes and council regulations.  

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To complete a strong application, we recommend assembling the following documents:  

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem you are addressing, the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline for implementation.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – itemised costs for tanks, shade structure, installation, contingency, and any staff or volunteer labour.  
3. **Letter of Auspice** – from your local council confirming the hosting arrangement, insurance coverage, and consent to manage grant funds.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page overview of your garden, its history, volunteer base, and how the project aligns with your mission.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent two years of revenue and expenditure statements (or a simple cash‑flow summary if you lack formal audited statements).  
6. **Risk Assessment & Safety Plan** – outlining how the installation will be managed safely, including any required permits or council approvals.  
7. **Community Support** – letters of endorsement from local businesses, resident groups, or other stakeholders, demonstrating broad backing for the initiative.  

If you can provide the letter of auspice and a preliminary budget, we will be able to give you a more precise assessment of the likely funding range for your project. Should you decide to proceed with a full application, we ask that you submit the above documents together with the online application form no later than the deadline next month.
