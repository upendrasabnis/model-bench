First, regarding your organisational structure: unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspicing arrangement with a local council are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing body (in this case, your council) is a legal entity that can enter into a funding agreement on your behalf. The council must hold appropriate public liability insurance and agree to accept legal and financial responsibility for the grant. You will need to provide a signed letter from an authorised council officer confirming the auspicing arrangement, the council’s ABN, and evidence of current insurance coverage.

Second, the grant round offers a minimum request of $5,000 and a maximum of $50,000 per project. With an annual turnover of approximately $40,000, your organisation falls well within the program’s target cohort of small-to-medium community groups. There is no requirement for matched funding, although any in-kind contributions (such as volunteer labour for installation) should be documented in your budget as they strengthen the value-for-money assessment.

Third, the infrastructure you describe—rainwater tanks and a permanent shade structure—is considered eligible capital expenditure under the “Community Infrastructure” stream, provided the assets are fixed, directly support the garden’s ongoing operation, and comply with relevant Australian standards and local planning regulations. Portable or temporary structures (e.g., pop-up gazebos) would not qualify, but engineered shade sails or steel-framed shelters do. Please include itemised quotes from at least two suppliers for each major component.

Finally, the key supporting documents you should prepare are:
1. Auspicing agreement letter from the council (as above).
2. Your group’s most recent financial statement or treasurer’s report showing the $40,000 turnover.
3. A project plan with timeline, risk management, and maintenance strategy for the new infrastructure.
4. Two written quotes per capital item.
5. Evidence of land tenure or a letter of support from the landowner (if the garden site is not council-owned).
6. A brief governance statement outlining how decisions are made within the volunteer group.
