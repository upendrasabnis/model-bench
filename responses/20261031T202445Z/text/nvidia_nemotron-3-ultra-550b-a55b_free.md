## 1. Executive Summary: The Compliance Imperative

Grant compliance is not merely administrative hygiene; it is the **fiduciary backbone of the social sector**. It is the mechanism by which trust is converted into impact. An "acquittal" (often used interchangeably with *financial report*, *expenditure report*, or *close-out package*) is the formal, evidentiary demonstration that a recipient organization has spent funds in strict accordance with the legal contract (Grant Agreement) and the approved budget.

**Why this matters now:**
*   **Regulatory Convergence:** Governments (e.g., US Uniform Guidance 2 CFR 200, UK Grant Management Framework, Australian CGRGs) are harmonizing standards toward risk-based assurance.
*   **Philanthropic Professionalization:** Large foundations (Gates, Ford, MacArthur) and intermediaries (GlobalGiving, CAF) now demand government-grade rigor.
*   **Reputational Capital:** A single failed acquittal triggers "debarment" risk, freezing future funding streams across *all* funders who share due diligence databases (e.g., SAM.gov, GuideStar, ACNC registers).

This article provides the architectural blueprint for a compliance function that survives audits, satisfies funders, and protects organizational mission.

## 2. Deconstructing the Acquittal: More Than a Receipt

### 2.1 Definition & Legal Standing
An **Acquittal Report** is a legally binding declaration submitted by the grantee to the grantor certifying that:
1.  **Expenditure Eligibility:** Every dollar claimed was spent on *allowable* activities per the Agreement.
2.  **Budget Adherence:** Spending aligns with approved line items (or approved variances).
3.  **Time-Boundarity:** Costs were incurred within the *Period of Performance* (PoP).
4.  **Completeness:** All matching funds, in-kind contributions, and program income are declared.

**Legal Consequence:** Signing the acquittal (often by the CEO, CFO, or Authorized Organizational Representative) constitutes a **certification under penalty of law** (e.g., US False Claims Act, UK Fraud Act 2006, Australian Crimes Act 1914). Misrepresentation constitutes fraud, not just a breach of contract.

### 2.2 The Three Pillars of an Acquittal Package
A complete acquittal is a "triangulation" of three distinct evidence streams:

| Pillar | Content | Evidentiary Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Financial Narrative (The "What")** | Statement of Receipts & Expenditure (SRE); Budget vs. Actual variance analysis; General Ledger (GL) reconciliation. | Must tie *exactly* to audited trial balance / GL. Zero unreconciled differences. |
| **2. Performance Narrative (The "So What")** | Milestone achievement report; KPI/Output/Outcome data; Deviation explanations. | Must correlate financially: "We spent $X on Activity Y and achieved Z Outcome." |
| **3. Source Evidence (The "Prove It")** | Sampled invoices, timesheets, procurement records, bank statements, asset registers. | **Originals** (or certified true copies) with clear audit trail: PO → Goods Receipt → Invoice → Payment → GL Coding. |

### 2.3 Types of Acquittals
*   **Progress/Interim Acquittal:** Quarterly or semi-annual. Triggers next tranche payment. Focus: Cash flow forecasting & burn rate.
*   **Final Acquittal:** Submitted 30–90 days post-PoP. Focus: Final reconciliation, unspent funds return, asset disposal, intellectual property handover.
*   **Special Purpose Acquittal:** Triggered by specific events (e.g., Capital Works completion, Research Clinical Trial phase end, Emergency Response phase gate).

## 3. The Funder Landscape: Government vs. Philanthropic Requirements

While the *principles* are universal (allowability, allocability, reasonableness), the *mechanics* differ significantly.

### 3.1 Government Funders (The "Compliance Heavy" Model)
**Regulatory Frameworks:**
*   **US Federal:** 2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance) – The "Gold Standard." Covers Cost Principles (Subpart E), Audit Requirements (Subpart F - Single Audit), and Administrative Requirements.
*   **UK:** HM Treasury *Managing Public Money* / *Grant Standard Terms*; Cabinet Office *Grants Management Function* standards.
*   **Australia:** Commonwealth Grants Rules and Guidelines (CGRGs); *Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013* (PGPA Act).
*   **EU:** Financial Regulation (EU) 2018/1046; Grant Agreements under Horizon Europe / Erasmus+.

**Common Government Requirements:**
| Requirement | Detail | Real-World Trap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Indirect Cost Rates** | Negotiated NICRA (US) / Full Economic Costing (UK/EU). Fixed % vs. Actuals. | Using an expired provisional rate; failing to propose a final rate within 6 months of FYE. |
| **Procurement Thresholds** | Micro-purchase (<$10k US), Simplified Acquisition (<$250k US), Full Open Competition (>$250k). | "Splitting" purchases to stay under thresholds (fraud); insufficient documentation for sole source justification. |
| **Time & Effort Reporting** | **After-the-fact** certification (US) vs. **Real-time** timesheets (EU Horizon). | Estimating percentages ("I spent 50% on this grant") instead of actual hours recorded daily. |
| **Equipment/Asset Thresholds** | Title vesting rules. US: >$5k useful life >1yr. EU: >€5k. | Disposing of assets without funder permission; failing to track asset location/condition. |
| **Sub-recipient vs. Contractor** | **Substance over form** determination (2 CFR 200.331). | Misclassifying a partner as a "vendor" to avoid flow-down compliance (flow-down clauses, monitoring, audit requirements). |
| **Single Audit / A-133** | Mandatory if >$750k (US) / £500k (UK) federal expenditure. | Auditor issues "Findings" (Material Weakness/Significant Deficiency) -> Resolution tracking required. |

### 3.2 Philanthropic & Corporate Funders (The "Relationship & Impact" Model)
**Characteristics:** Fewer statutory mandates, higher reliance on Grant Agreements as contract law. Focus on *Impact Measurement* and *Narrative*.

**Common Requirements:**
*   **Restricted vs. Unrestricted:** Philanthropy often gives "General Operating Support" (unrestricted). Acquittal here is an **Annual Report + Audited Financials**, not line-item receipts.
*   **Program-Related Investments (PRIs) / Mission-Related Investments (MRIs):** Acquittal = Financial Return reporting + Social Impact metrics (IRIS+ standards).
*   **Multi-Year Grants:** "Continued Funding" logic. Year 2 payment contingent on Year 1 *Impact* report, not just financial burn.
*   **Funder Collaboratives (e.g., Co-Impact, Audacious Project):** Harmonized reporting templates (often aligned with **IATI** - International Aid Transparency Initiative standards).

**The "Hidden" Philanthropic Requirement: Relationship Capital.**
*   *Example:* A foundation Program Officer (PO) may waive a minor budget variance *if* you called them 3 months prior. Government officers rarely have this discretion.
*   *Strategy:* Treat the PO as a compliance partner. "No surprises" communication is the #1 unwritten requirement.

### 3.3 Cross-Border / Multi-Donor Complexity
**Scenario:** An NGO receives US AID (2 CFR 200), UK FCDO (Grant Conditions), and Gates Foundation funding for one program.
**Solution:** **Harmonized Chart of Accounts (CoA).** Map every transaction to the *strictest* common denominator (usually US Federal cost principles).
*   *Tool:* Use "Fund-Donor-Activity-Location" dimensional coding in ERP (NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Unit4, Xero with tracking categories).

## 4. The Compliance Lifecycle: From Award to Final Report

Compliance is not an end-of-grant event. It is a **continuous control environment**.

### Phase 1: Pre-Award & Kickoff (Days 1–30) — *The Setup Determines the Outcome*
1.  **Regulatory Mapping:** Read the *entire* Agreement + Referenced Documents (General Terms, Specific Terms, Funder Guidelines). Create a **Compliance Obligation Register (COR)**.
    *   *COR Columns:* Clause Ref | Requirement | Frequency | Owner | Evidence Required | Tool/System | Status.
2.  **Budget Translation:** Convert Funder Budget Template → Internal Chart of Accounts. **Lock the mapping.** No "manual journal adjustments" at year-end.
3.  **Policy Alignment Check:** Does your Travel Policy allow "Business Class for flights >8hrs" but Funder caps at "Economy"? **Adopt the stricter policy for the grant.**
4.  **Sub-recipient Onboarding:** Flow-down clauses *must* be in sub-agreements *before* first invoice. Conduct **Pre-Award Risk Assessment** (Financial capacity, prior audit history, internal controls).
5.  **Bank Account Setup:** Separate bank account? (Often mandatory for >$500k gov grants). If pooled, strict sub-ledger segregation is non-negotiable.

### Phase 2: Operational Execution (Monthly/Quarterly) — *The Rhythm*
*   **Monthly "Soft Close" for Grants:** Reconcile grant sub-ledger to GL *before* organizational month-end close.
*   **Variance Thresholds:** Set automated alerts: >10% variance on any line item triggers Program Manager justification *immediately*, not at reporting deadline.
*   **Timesheet Lock:** Hard close on timesheets by 5th business day of following month. No back-dating.
*   **Procurement Log Maintenance:** Live register of all commitments >$X. Tracks: PO #, Vendor, Amount, Competitive Method, Status (Open/Received/Invoiced/Paid).

### Phase 3: Interim Reporting (The "Health Check")
*   **Cash Reconciliation:** `Cash Drawndown - Reported Expenditure = Cash on Hand`. Must match bank statement.
*   **Burn Rate Analysis:** `(Total Spend to Date / Total Budget) vs (Time Elapsed / Total Period)`. Flag if >110% or <80%.
*   **Narrative Alignment:** Program team writes narrative; Finance validates numbers. **Single Source of Truth** meeting before submission.

### Phase 4: Year-End / Period-End Hard Close
1.  **Accruals:** Identify all goods/services received but not invoiced (GRNI - Goods Received Not Invoiced). Accrue strictly per **Cut-off Procedures**.
2.  **Indirect Cost True-Up:** Calculate actual indirect rate vs. provisional billing rate. Prepare carry-forward adjustment.
3.  **Asset Verification:** Physical inventory count of grant-funded assets.
4.  **Unliquidated Obligations (ULOs):** List all open POs/Commitments. Decide: Cancel? Pay? Carry forward? (Requires funder approval usually).

### Phase 5: Final Acquittal & Close-Out (The "Exam")
*   **Final Financial Report (FFR):** SF-425 (US) or equivalent.
*   **Refund Calculation:** `Total Drawndowns - Total Allowable Expenditure = Refund Due`. Wire transfer *with* final report.
*   **Asset Disposition Report:** Request disposition instructions for residual inventory/equipment >$5k.
*   **Record Retention Certification:** Confirm records retained for required period (usually 3 years from final report submission; longer for real estate/patents).
*   **Audit Submission:** Upload Single Audit / Independent Audit to clearinghouse (FAC - Federal Audit Clearinghouse) or funder portal.

### 5.2 Metadata Tags (Enable Search/Filter)
Every file *must* have metadata:
*   `Grant_ID`
*   `Transaction_Type` (Payroll, Procurement, Travel, Indirect, Subrecipient)
*   `Period` (YYYY-MM)
*   `Document_Class` (Source, Approval, Payment, Correspondence)
*   `Confidentiality` (Public, Internal, Restricted-PII)

### 5.3 Retention Schedules (Legal Minimums)
| Record Type | US Federal (2 CFR 200.334) | UK / EU / AU Standard | Best Practice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Financial Records / Invoices | 3 yrs from Final Report submission | 6-7 yrs (Tax/Companies Act) | **7 Years** |
| Real Property / Equipment Records | 3 yrs after final disposition | Life of asset + 6 yrs | **Life of Asset + 7 Years** |
| Personnel / Timesheets | 3 yrs | 6 yrs (Payroll tax) | **7 Years** |
| Audit Reports / Findings | 3 yrs | 6 yrs | **Permanent** |
| Grant Agreements / Amendments | 3 yrs | 6 yrs post-expiry | **Permanent** |
| *Note:* If litigation, audit finding, or claim arises -> **Freeze destruction** until resolved. |

### 5.4 The "Originals" Rule
*   **Digital Originals:** PDF/A format (ISO 19005) with digital signatures (DocuSign, Adobe Sign) are legally original in most jurisdictions (ESIGN Act US, eIDAS EU, Electronic Transactions Acts AU/UK).
*   **Scanning Standards:** 300 DPI, OCR enabled, grayscale/color. Shred physical originals *only* after verified scan + QC check (unless law requires wet ink, e.g., some deeds, notarized docs).

## 6. The Audit Event: Surviving Scrutiny

### 6.1 Types of Audits You Will Face
1.  **Single Audit (US) / Statutory Audit (UK/AU):** Org-wide. Tests *Compliance* (Major Programs) + *Internal Controls* + *Financial Statements*.
2.  **Funder-Specific Monitoring Review:** Programmatic + Financial. Usually on-site or "desk review." High frequency for high-risk grantees.
3.  **Sub-recipient Monitoring (You auditing your subs):** **You are the auditor here.** 2 CFR 200.332 requires *you* to issue a management decision on their findings within 6 months.
4.  **Forensic / Fraud Investigation:** Triggered by whistleblower, data anomaly, or referral. Legal privilege applies; engage counsel immediately.

### 6.2 The Auditor’s "Request List" (PBC - Prepared by Client)
Standard PBC list for a Grant Compliance Audit:
1.  Grant Agreement & Amendments.
2.  Org Chart & Key Personnel CVs (verify "Key Personnel" clause compliance).
3.  **Reconciliation:** GL Trial Balance → Grant Expenditure Report → Funder Portal Submission.
4.  **Sample Selection:** Auditors use **Statistical Sampling** (e.g., Monetary Unit Sampling) or **Judgmental Sampling** (High risk: Travel, Sole Source, Subrecipients, Year-end cut-off).
    *   *Expect:* 25–60 transactions tested per major grant.
5.  **Payroll Testing:** Selection of 10–25 employees charged to grant. Request: Timesheets, After-the-fact Certifications, Fringe Rate Calculation, Effort % verification.
6.  **Procurement Files:** Complete file for 5–10 procurements > Simplified Acquisition Threshold.
7.  **Indirect Cost Rate Workpapers:** Pool costs, Base costs, Allocation methodology, Reconciliation to Audited Financials.
8.  **Subrecipient Files:** Risk assessments, Monitoring reports, Audit report collection (FAC check), Management Decisions issued.
9.  **Cash Management:** Drawdown schedule vs. Expenditure timeline (Minimize time between draw and disbursement - **3-day rule** US / **Just-in-Time** EU).
10. **Prior Audit Findings:** Status of Corrective Action Plans (CAPs).

### 6.3 Managing the Audit: The "War Room" Protocol
*   **Single Point of Contact (SPOC):** One person (Grants Manager/Controller) coordinates all document pulls. **No direct auditor-staff contact without SPOC.**
*   **Secure Data Room:** Use VDR (Virtual Data Room) or secure SharePoint link with expiration. **Never email spreadsheets with PII/SSNs.**
*   **Daily Stand-ups:** 15 mins with audit team to track open items.
*   **Finding Response Protocol:**
    1.  **Fact Check:** "We agree/disagree with the *facts*."
    2.  **Root Cause:** "Why did the control fail?" (5 Whys).
    3.  **Corrective Action:** Specific, Measurable, Assigned, Dated.
    4.  **Systemic Fix:** Policy change, System config, Training.
*   **Exit Conference:** Negotiate "Findings" vs. "Management Letter Comments." A **Finding** requires a formal CAP; a **Comment** is advisory. Fight to downgrade Findings to Comments if remediation is already in progress.

## 7. The "Dirty Dozen": Most Frequent Compliance Failures & Mitigation Strategies

Based on analysis of **US OIG Audit Reports, UK NAO Reports, ACNC Investigations, and Foundation Feedback Loops**.

### Failure 1: "The Phantom Budget" (Budget vs. Reality Drift)
*   **Symptom:** Spending $120k on "Personnel" when budget was $100k; $80k on "Travel" budgeted at $100k. Net zero, but line items violated.
*   **Root Cause:** Program staff spend to need; Finance reports to budget. No monthly variance review.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Hard Control:** ERP Budget Control = "Control at Line Item Level" (Hard Stop or Warning + Justification Mandatory).
    *   **Process:** Monthly "Budget Owner Sign-off" on variance report.
    *   **Funder Rule:** Know the **Prior Approval Threshold** (usually 10-25% or $25k). Submit *before* spending.

### Failure 2: "The Timesheet Fiction" (Effort Reporting Fraud/Error)
*   **Symptom:** Timesheets show 100% grant effort for 12 months; Outlook calendar shows 50% meetings on other projects. Or: Timesheets signed 6 months late by PI who "estimates."
*   **Root Cause:** Culture of "compliance as paperwork"; lack of integrated HR/Timesheet system.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **System:** Timesheet system *forces* daily entry; locks after period close; routes to Supervisor *then* PI for certification.
    *   **Policy:** "If it's not in the timesheet by Day 5, it's Unallowable." No retroactive corrections without Dean/VP approval + documentation.
    *   **After-the-Fact Certification (US):** Must be signed by *employee* (or direct supervisor with first-hand knowledge), not just Dept Admin.

### Failure 3: "The Sole Source Habit" (Procurement Violations)
*   **Symptom:** 80% of procurements >$10k are "Sole Source – Only Vendor Available." Justification: "They know our system."
*   **Root Cause:** Poor planning; vendor lock-in; laziness.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Procurement Calendar:** Map all known needs 12 months out at Kickoff.
    *   **Sole Source Form:** Requires: Technical Specs, Market Research (3 failed searches), Price Reasonableness Analysis (Historical / Parametric / Independent Estimate), Conflict of Interest Cert.
    *   **Threshold:** Any Sole Source >$25k (US) / €15k (EU) requires **Funder Prior Written Approval**.

### Failure 4: "The Indirect Cost Black Hole"
*   **Symptom:** Charging "De Minimis 10% MTDC" (US) when organization has a Negotiated Rate (NICRA). Or: Including *unallowable costs* (alcohol, lobbying, entertainment, fundraising) in the Indirect Pool.
*   **Root Cause:** Finance doesn't understand Cost Principles (Subpart E); uses "General Admin" bucket for everything.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Annual Rate Proposal Discipline:** Dedicated staff or consultant for NICRA/ICR proposal.
    *   **Unallowable Cost Segregation:** Chart of Accounts *must* have natural accounts for Unallowables (e.g., 6500-Lobbying, 6510-Alcohol, 6520-Fundraising). System auto-excludes from Indirect Pool.

### Failure 5: "Subrecipient Blindness" (Pass-Through Risk)
*   **Symptom:** Prime pays sub-invoice without reviewing sub's financial report; never collects sub's Single Audit; no site visits.
*   **Root Cause:** "They are the experts, we trust them." Misunderstanding **Prime Liability**. The Prime is liable to the Funder for *every dollar* the Sub spends.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Risk-Based Monitoring Plan:** High Risk (New sub, >$250k, prior findings) = Site Visit + Desk Review Quarterly. Low Risk = Annual Desk Review.
    *   **Flow-Down Checklist:** Verify Sub Agreement has *all* required clauses (Audit, Records, Termination, FFATA, Trafficking, etc.).
    *   **FFATA/FSRS Reporting (US):** Report sub-awards >$30k in FSRS by end of month following obligation. **Late filing = Finding.**

### Failure 6: "The Cash Management Gap" (Interest Income / Excess Cash)
*   **Symptom:** Drawing down $500k on Day 1 of quarter; spending it over 90 days. Holding $200k avg daily balance. Funder demands interest earned.
*   **Root Cause:** Fear of cash flow crunch; poor forecasting.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Cash Flow Forecast:** Rolling 13-week forecast per grant.
    *   **Drawdown Policy:** "Draw only what you need for next 5-7 business days."
    *   **Interest Tracking:** Separate GL account for Federal Interest Income. Remit annually (US) / Quarterly (EU).

### Failure 7: "Capital Asset Amnesia" (Equipment/Real Property)
*   **Symptom:** Laptop bought on Grant A in 2018. Found in staff locker in 2024. No tag, no inventory, disposed via e-waste without permission.
*   **Root Cause:** Asset tracking owned by IT/Ops, not Grants Finance. No tagging at receipt.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Tag at Receiving Dock:** Barcode/QR code applied *before* asset leaves loading dock.
    *   **Asset Register Fields:** Grant ID, % Federal Share, Acquisition Cost, Location, Custodian, Condition, Disposition Date/Method/Proceeds.
    *   **Annual Physical Inventory:** Mandatory. Variance >0 = Investigation.

### Failure 8: "Period of Performance Violations" (Cut-off Errors)
*   **Symptom:** Invoice dated June 30 (Grant End) paid July 15. Charged to Grant. *Or* Salary for July 1-5 charged to Grant ending June 30.
*   **Root Cause:** Accrual confusion; "Use it or lose it" mentality.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Hard Close Calendar:** "No new obligations last 30 days." "All services must be *rendered* by PoP End Date."
    *   **Accrual Rules:** Accrue only for *binding obligations* (PO issued, service rendered) existing *on* PoP end date.
    *   **Payroll:** Prorate final pay period daily. No "charging forward."

### Failure 9: "Cost Sharing / Matching Shortfalls"
*   **Symptom:** Grant requires $1M Cost Share. Org claims $1M via "Volunteer Hours" valued at $50/hr (market rate $15/hr) or "Donated Rent" without appraisal.
*   **Root Cause:** Desperation to meet match; weak valuation policies.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Valuation Policy:** Written policy *pre-award*. Volunteer rates = **Actual** replacement cost (not "value to program"). Third-party in-kind = Fair Market Value (FMV) documentation (appraisal, comparable ads).
    *   **Tracking:** Cost Share tracked in GL *real-time* (Project Code = Grant_ID_MATCH). Not calculated at year-end.

### Failure 10: "Program Income Diversion"
*   **Symptom:** Grant funds a training program. Org charges fees ($50k revenue). Revenue deposited to General Fund, not reported/deducted from grant spend.
*   **Root Cause:** Program staff don't know revenue is "Program Income" (2 CFR 200.307).
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Revenue Classification:** Any income *generated by* grant-supported activity = Program Income.
    *   **Default Method:** **Deduction Alternative** (Income reduces allowable costs). Must be tracked in GL separately.

### Failure 11: "Lobbying & Advocacy Cross-Contamination"
*   **Symptom:** Grant-funded Policy Director spends 20% time meeting legislators. Charged 100% to Grant.
*   **Root Cause:** Blurry line between "Education" (Allowable) and "Lobbying" (Unallowable - 2 CFR 200.450 / 18 USC 1913).
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Activity Coding:** Timesheet *Activity Codes*: `GRANT_RESEARCH`, `GRANT_EDUCATION`, `LOBBYING_FEDERAL`, `LOBBYING_STATE`, `FUNDRAISING`.
    *   **Legal Review:** Annual review of "Advocacy Plan" by General Counsel.

### Failure 12: "The Silent Treatment" (Communication Failures)
*   **Symptom:** Key Personnel change (PI leaves). Funder finds out at Final Report. Scope change (site closure). Funder finds out at Site Visit.
*   **Root Cause:** Fear of "bothering" funder; no change management process.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Change Control Log:** Mandatory for: Key Personnel, Scope, Budget Rebudgeting > Threshold, No-Cost Extension, Foreign Travel.
    *   **Rule:** "Bad news early is management. Bad news late is a finding." Notify Funder *immediately* (usually 10-30 days written notice).

## 8. Case Studies in Failure and Success

### Case Study A: The "University Research Empire" Collapse (Failure)
**Context:** Major R1 University, $50M/yr Federal Research Portfolio.
**The Failure:**
*   **Decentralized Chaos:** 50 Depts managed grants independently. No central CoA mapping.
*   **Effort Reporting:** Paper timesheets, signed annually by Dept Admin. PIs never saw them.
*   **Subrecipients:** 200+ subs. Zero monitoring. One sub embezzled $2M over 5 years.
*   **Audit Result (OIG):** **Material Weakness** in Internal Controls. **$12M Questioned Costs**. **Suspension** from new awards for 18 months. Reputational damage: #1 ranking dropped to #15.
**The Fix (Post-Crisis):**
1.  Centralized **Office of Research Administration (ORA)** with authority.
2.  Implemented **Enterprise Research Admin System** (Huron/Kuali/Workday Grants).
3.  Mandatory **Effort Certification System** (ECS) with PI login.
4.  **Subrecipient Monitoring Unit** (3 FTEs) + Risk-based sampling.
5.  **Result:** Clean Single Audit 2 years later. Suspension lifted.
