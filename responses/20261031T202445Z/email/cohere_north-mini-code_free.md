**Eligibility – Unincorporated Groups Auspiced by a Council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. While we give priority to registered charities, we also accept applications from unincorporated groups that are formally auspiced—or “hosted”—by a local government body. In your case, the fact that your council holds the requisite insurances on your behalf satisfies the primary requirement for organisational capacity. To demonstrate that the council’s involvement is substantive, you will need to provide a brief letter of support from the council confirming (a) that it will act as the fiscal host for any grant funds, (b) that it will maintain the required public liability and professional indemnity insurance for the duration of the project, and (c) that it will be responsible for any reporting or audit requirements that arise. If you can supply such a letter, you should be considered eligible under the “community group” category.

**Minimum and Maximum Grant Amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to fund projects that deliver measurable community benefit. The typical award range is **$5,000 – $50,000**, although we have approved projects for as little as $2,500 in cases where the scope is modest and the community impact is clear. There is no formal upper ceiling; larger requests are evaluated on their merit, sustainability plan, and alignment with grant objectives. Your proposed infrastructure (rainwater tanks and a shade structure) is estimated to fall comfortably within this range, but please feel free to share a detailed budget if you anticipate exceeding the upper end.

**Eligibility of Infrastructure Costs**  
Yes—capital infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures is explicitly listed as an eligible cost under the “Environmental Resilience and Climate Adaptation” component of the grant. The program recognises that such assets enhance the long‑term viability of community spaces, particularly in the face of increasing summer temperatures. Eligible expenses include the purchase and installation of tanks, associated plumbing, structural frameworks, anchoring work, and any necessary site preparation. Please note that routine operating expenses (e.g., maintenance contracts beyond the warranty period) are not covered.

**Supporting Documents – What to Prepare**  
To present a competitive application, we recommend compiling the following documents:

1. **Project Proposal Form** – completed with all mandatory fields, including a clear statement of the problem you are addressing, your objectives, and measurable outcomes.  
2. **Budget Table** – a line‑item breakdown that distinguishes capital costs (equipment, installation, contractor fees) from any indirect costs, with quotations or provisional invoices where possible.  
3. **Letter of Support from the Council** – as described above, confirming auspice status, insurance coverage, and fiscal responsibility.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page description of your group’s composition, volunteer base, and any previous community projects you have delivered.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed statements (or, for small organisations, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and balance sheet) demonstrating your $40,000 annual turnover.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Safety Plan** – outlining how the installation will be managed, including any necessary permits, site‑specific hazards, and mitigation strategies.  
7. **Community Engagement Evidence** – minutes or letters from local stakeholders, usage plans, or sign‑ups demonstrating demand and support for the project.  
8. **Sustainability Plan** – a brief outline of how you will maintain the infrastructure after installation, including any training for volunteers or allocation of a maintenance budget.
