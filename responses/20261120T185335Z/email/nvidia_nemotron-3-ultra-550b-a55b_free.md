Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out before investing time in a full application, and I am pleased to address each of your questions in turn.

First, regarding organisational structure: unincorporated groups operating under the auspices of a local government authority are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing body (in this case, your local council) meets the program’s eligibility criteria for lead applicants. The council must be a legal entity with an ABN, hold the required public liability insurance (minimum $20 million), and be willing to enter into a legally binding funding agreement on your group’s behalf. You will need to provide a formal letter of support from the council confirming their auspicing arrangement, their acceptance of financial and reporting responsibility, and evidence of their current insurance certificates.

Second, the grant round offers funding between $5,000 and $50,000 per project. There is no minimum total project cost, but the grant cannot exceed 80% of eligible project expenditure; the remaining 20% (or more) must be covered by cash or in-kind contributions from your group or other partners. Given your described project scope, it would be prudent to obtain at least two quotes for the tanks and shade structure to demonstrate value for money and to confirm the total budget falls within the program limits.

Third, infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience—such as rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures that enable continued operation during extreme heat—is explicitly listed as an eligible capital cost in the program guidelines. Eligible expenses include purchase, delivery, installation, and any necessary site preparation or plumbing work. Ongoing maintenance costs, however, are not eligible.

Finally, for supporting documents you should prepare: the council’s auspicing letter and insurance certificates; your group’s most recent financial statement (or a simple income and expenditure summary for the last 12 months); evidence of land tenure or a licence to occupy the garden site; two written quotes for each major infrastructure item; a brief project plan outlining timelines, risk management, and community benefit; and letters of support from at least two community stakeholders (e.g., local schools, neighbourhood houses, or health services) demonstrating broader resilience outcomes.
