**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. While we give priority to incorporated non‑profit entities, unincorporated groups that operate under the formal auspices of a local government are indeed eligible, provided that the council can confirm it will act as the fiscal sponsor for any awarded funds. In practice this means the council must be able to hold the grant money, enter into a grant agreement, and be responsible for any reporting or audit requirements on behalf of the group. If your council is willing to act in this capacity, we can proceed with the application using the council’s legal identity.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to fund projects that deliver measurable community benefit, and we do not impose a rigid floor or ceiling on individual awards. Typical grants in this round range from $5,000 to $50,000, reflecting the scale and scope of the proposed work. However, we assess each proposal on its own merits, so a project costing less than $5,000 can still be funded if the budget aligns with the impact you expect to achieve. Conversely, larger projects may be considered up to $100,000, especially when they incorporate multi‑partner collaboration or demonstrate strong sustainability outcomes.  

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting tanks and shade structures is fully eligible under the “Environmental Sustainability” and “Climate‑Resilient Infrastructure” categories of our grant. Both items must be directly related to the project’s core objectives, demonstrably improve the garden’s capacity to operate during extreme heat, and be justified with clear specifications, cost estimates, and maintenance plans. We encourage you to include detailed technical drawings, supplier quotations, and any necessary planning approvals from the council.  

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
1. **Project Narrative** – A concise description of the problem, objectives, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Budget and Financial Projections** – Itemised costs (including tanks, shade structure, installation, and any contingency), plus a simple cash‑flow statement showing your current turnover and how the grant will be integrated.  
3. **Organisational Profile** – Even if unincorporated, provide a letter of support from your council confirming its auspice role, a copy of any memorandum of understanding, and a brief description of your volunteer team and governance structure.  
4. **Proof of Insurance** – Since the council holds the insurance policy, a certification from the council confirming coverage for the proposed works is sufficient.  
5. **Site Plans and Drawings** – Scaled plans showing where the tanks and shade structure will be located, along with any required council permits.  
6. **Letters of Support** – At least two letters from community stakeholders (e.g., local schools, businesses, or user groups) affirming the project’s value.  
7. **Evaluation Framework** – Outline how you will measure success (e.g., water usage reduction, increased garden usage during heat events) and any monitoring mechanisms.
