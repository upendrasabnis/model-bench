Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out to clarify eligibility before investing time in an application, and I am happy to address each of your questions in turn.

**Eligibility of Auspiced Groups:** Yes, unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspice arrangement with an eligible legal entity—such as a local council—are eligible to apply. The auspicing organisation must be listed as the legal applicant on the grant agreement, hold the required insurances (public liability, workers’ compensation, etc.), and provide a signed letter of support confirming their willingness to accept legal and financial responsibility for the project. Your council’s existing insurance coverage for the garden satisfies this requirement.

**Grant Amounts:** The Community Resilience Grant offers a minimum request of $5,000 and a maximum of $50,000 per project. With an annual turnover of approximately $40,000, your organisation falls well within the program’s financial viability thresholds. Please note that the assessment panel will consider the scale of the request relative to your operational capacity and the demonstrated community benefit.

**Eligible Costs – Infrastructure:** Capital infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience is an eligible cost category. Rainwater tanks and a permanent shade structure, as described, qualify provided they are fixed assets that remain with the garden and are not portable equipment. Installation costs, engineering certification (if required), and necessary permits are also claimable. Ongoing maintenance costs, however, are not eligible.

**Supporting Documents to Prepare:** To streamline your application, please gather the following:
1. A signed auspice agreement or letter from the council confirming the arrangement and insurance coverage.
2. Your most recent annual financial statement or a simple income-and-expenditure summary for the last financial year.
3. Quotes from at least two suppliers for the tanks and shade structure (or one quote with a justification for sole sourcing).
4. A project plan outlining timelines, risk management, and how the infrastructure will extend growing seasons and community access during extreme heat.
5. Evidence of community support (letters from members, partner organisations, or the council).
6. Proof of land tenure or a licence agreement for the garden site.
