**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines state that applicants may be either incorporated not‑for‑profit entities or unincorporated groups that are formally auspiced (i.e., sponsored or supported) by a public‑sector body such as a local council. In your case, because the council holds the relevant insurances and can act as the responsible entity for the project, your group would satisfy the “auspiced” requirement. It is important, however, that the council provides a formal letter of support confirming that it will oversee the grant funds, manage reporting, and assume liability for any project‑related risks. If such a letter is not already in place, we recommend drafting one that includes the council’s official logo, the date, and a clear statement that the council endorses the project and will act as the fiscal agent.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to fund projects ranging from modest improvements to larger‑scale infrastructure initiatives. The typical award size is between **AU$5,000 and AU$250,000**, although there is no fixed maximum; larger requests are considered on a case‑by‑case basis and are expected to demonstrate proportional community impact and sustainability. Your proposed installation of rainwater tanks and a shade structure would likely fall within the mid‑range of this band, given the total cost estimate you provided.  

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, capital infrastructure that directly enhances the resilience and operational capacity of community‑based projects is an eligible expense. Specifically, the grant’s “Climate‑Adaptation and Resource‑Efficiency” objective covers items such as:  

* Water‑harvesting equipment (rainwater tanks, gutters, storage containers)  
* Shade structures that reduce heat exposure for users and protect produce  
* Associated installation labour (provided the work is performed by qualified tradespeople)  

We do require that each purchased item be clearly linked to the project’s resilience goals, and that you include itemized quotes or quotations in your budget documentation.  

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  

1. **Project Narrative** – A concise description of the problem you are addressing (increasing heat stress on garden operations), the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and how the project aligns with the grant’s resilience objectives.  
2. **Budget Table** – Detailed line‑item costs for the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary expenses (e.g., permits, contingency). Include quotes or invoices where possible.  
3. **Financial Statements** – For the most recent fiscal year (or the period covered by your $40,000 turnover). If you are an unincorporated group, these may be prepared by the auspicing council.  
4. **Proof of Insurance** – A copy of the council’s insurance certificate confirming that it provides liability coverage for the project activities.  
5. **Letters of Support** – At least two community endorsements (e.g., from local businesses, schools, or community groups) describing the garden’s impact and the need for the proposed infrastructure.  
6. **Site Plans / Drawings** – Simple sketches or CAD drawings showing where the tanks and shade structure will be located, together with a timeline for installation.  
7. **Governance Document** – If your group has a constitution or operating agreement, include it; if not, the council’s auspice letter should outline the decision‑making authority for the project.
