**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines state that applicant organizations must be either incorporated not‑for‑profit entities or unincorporated groups that are formally auspiced by a public‑sector body such as a local council. An auspice arrangement means that the council assumes legal responsibility for the group’s activities, holds any necessary insurances, and can act as the primary point of contact for grant purposes. Provided that your garden operates under a written auspice agreement with the council (or that the council can demonstrate it holds the relevant insurances and liabilities on your behalf), unincorporated groups like yours are eligible to apply.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to fund projects of varying scale, and we do not set a minimum grant amount. However, the maximum grant we can award for any single application is $150,000. This ceiling reflects the overall budget allocated to the grant round and the expectation that larger projects will require complementary funding sources. Most successful applicants request amounts in the $30,000‑$80,000 range, but we assess each proposal individually based on its merit, community impact, and alignment with grant objectives.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, infrastructure that directly enhances the resilience and sustainability of community‑focused initiatives is an eligible cost. Specifically, rainwater harvesting equipment (including tanks, filtration systems, and plumbing) and permanent shade structures that protect participants and produce are considered permissible expenditures. Both items must be directly linked to the project’s objective—namely, ensuring the garden can operate during hotter periods. We also accept ancillary costs such as project management, site preparation, and minor electrical or plumbing work that are essential to the installation.

1. **Auspice confirmation** – a letter from the council confirming that it holds insurances and acts as the formal sponsor of your group.  
2. **Organizational profile** – a brief overview of your mission, the services you provide, and the target beneficiary groups.  
3. polycyclic‑**Financial statements** – audited or reviewed accounts for the most recent fiscal year (or, if you are not required to have audited accounts, a set of internally prepared financial statements with supporting notes).  
4. **Budget and cash‑flow projection** – a detailed breakdown of all costs, including the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary expenses.  
5. **Project plan** – a timeline, milestones, and deliverables, together with a description of the expected outcomes and how they align with the grant’s resilience objectives.  
6. **Community impact statement** – evidence of community need (e.g., recent weather impact reports, local survey data, or letters of support from residents or neighboring organizations).  
7. **Risk assessment and mitigation plan** – an outline of potential project risks and how you propose to manage them.  
8. **Letters of support** – endorsements from the council, local businesses, or community groups that confirm the relevance and feasibility of the project.
