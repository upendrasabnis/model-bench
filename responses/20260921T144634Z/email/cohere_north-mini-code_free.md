**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our Community Resilience Grant is open to a broad range of applicants, including unincorporated community groups, provided that the group operates under a formal auspicing arrangement with a public‑sector body such as your local council. In practice, the council’s role as fiscal sponsor satisfies the requirement for a legally‑recognized entity to hold the grant and be accountable for reporting. If your council can confirm in writing that it will act as the fiscal agent for the grant funds, your group will meet the eligibility criteria. Should you wish to become a registered charity in the longer term, that is certainly beneficial, but it is not a prerequisite for this round.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
There is no fixed minimum or maximum grant size for the Community Resilience Grant. Awards are determined on the basis of the project’s relevance to the grant’s objectives, the quality of the budget, and the capacity of the applicant to deliver the outcomes. Typical awards for community‑infrastructure projects range from $5,000 to $50,000, though larger or smaller amounts can be approved if the case is compelling. We encourage you to draft a realistic budget that reflects the true cost of the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary works.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes—capital infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures is expressly listed as an eligible cost under the “Climate‑Resilient Community Assets” category. These items must be directly tied to the project’s goal of enhancing operational resilience during hot summers, and they should be justified with clear specifications, cost estimates, and a timeline for installation.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
When you move to the full‑application stage, we recommend assembling the following documents:

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem, objectives, and expected outcomes, linking the infrastructure to climate‑resilience.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – line‑item costs (equipment, installation, contingency), plus a breakdown of how the $40,000 annual turnover will be allocated.  
3. **Proof of Auspice** – a letter from your council confirming its willingness to act as fiscal sponsor and its responsibility for insurance and risk management.  
4. **Insurance Documentation** – a copy of the council’s insurance certificate that names the garden as a covered activity, or a letter confirming that the council will maintain coverage for the project.  
5. **Organizational Structure** – a brief chart showing volunteers, roles, and any governing committee, together with any relevant minutes or a constitution.  
6. **Financial Statements** – the most recent set of accounts or a simple cash‑flow summary for the past fiscal year.  
7. **Site Plans or Sketches** – visual representation of where the tanks and shade structure will be placed.  
8. **Evaluation Plan** – outline how you will measure success (e.g., water‑usage data, user satisfaction surveys) and report back to the grant program.  

We also advise that you keep a copy of the grant guidelines and this correspondence for reference while you draft your application. If any of the above documents are not yet available, a provisional explanation of why they are pending (e.g., “waiting for council’s formal auspice letter”) is acceptable, provided you commit to delivering the final versions before the submission deadline.
