## 1. Executive Summary: The Compliance Lifecycle

Grant compliance is not a post-award administrative burden; it is a **continuous lifecycle** beginning at the pre-proposal stage and extending years beyond the final acquittal. Most organizations treat compliance as a "reporting event"—the frantic two weeks before a deadline. High-performing organizations treat it as a **system of internal controls** that generates the report as a byproduct of daily operations.

**The Acquittal** is the formal, legal conclusion of the grant contract. It is the moment the funder verifies that:
1.  **Financial Probity:** Funds were spent per the approved budget (or approved variations).
2.  **Performance Delivery:** Outputs/outcomes defined in the Grant Agreement were achieved.
3.  **Legal/Regulatory Adherence:** Procurement, employment, WHS, and specific legislative requirements (e.g., Modern Slavery, Indigenous Procurement Policy) were met.

Failure to acquit correctly triggers **clawback provisions**, reputational damage, blacklisting from future rounds, and in severe cases (government fraud), criminal prosecution. This guide provides the architectural blueprint for a compliance function that survives scrutiny.

## 2. Defining Acquittal Reporting: More Than a Receipt

### 2.1 The Legal Definition
An **Acquittal Report** (variously termed *Financial Acquittal*, *Final Report*, *Statement of Expenditure*, or *Certification*) is a legally binding declaration signed by an authorized officer (CEO, CFO, Board Chair) attesting to the truthfulness of the financial and performance data.

> **Key Distinction:** A *Progress Report* updates the funder on trajectory. An *Acquittal* closes the contract. It converts "grant funds received" into "expenditure verified." Until acquittal is accepted, the liability remains on the organization’s balance sheet as *Unearned Revenue* or *Deferred Income*, not recognized revenue.

### 2.2 The Three Pillars of an Acquittal Package
A complete acquittal package is a **triangulation of evidence**:

| Pillar | Content | Evidentiary Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Statement** | Income/Expenditure Statement vs. Approved Budget; General Ledger (GL) extract; Bank Reconciliation. | **Source Document Verification:** Every line item traceable to an invoice/receipt/payroll record. |
| **Performance Narrative** | Achievement of KPIs, Outputs, Outcomes; Variance explanation (quantitative & qualitative). | **Evidence-Based Claims:** "We held 10 workshops" $\rightarrow$ Attendance sheets, photos, feedback forms. |
| **Certification/Declaration** | Signed statutory declaration or specific funder form by Authorized Signatory. | **Legal Weight:** False declaration = Fraud (Crimes Act / False Statements Act equivalents). |

### 2.3 Types of Acquittals
*   **Interim/Progress Acquittal:** Required for multi-year grants (e.g., annual certification for a 3-year NHMRC grant). Releases next tranche of funding.
*   **Final Acquittal:** Comprehensive closure. Usually due 30–90 days post-project end date.
*   **Special Purpose Acquittal:** Triggered by specific events (e.g., Capital Works completion, Scholarship acquittal, Emergency funding spend).

## 3. The Regulatory Landscape: Government vs. Philanthropic Frameworks

While the *principles* are universal (probity, transparency, value-for-money), the *enforcement mechanisms* differ radically.

### 3.1 Government Funders (Federal, State, Local)
**Governing Instruments:**
*   **Commonwealth:** *Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013 (PGPA Act)*, Commonwealth Grants Rules and Guidelines (CGRGs), Specific Grant Opportunity Guidelines.
*   **States:** e.g., NSW *Government Sector Finance Act 2018*, VIC *Financial Management Act 1994*, QLD *Financial Accountability Act 2009*.
*   **Audit Oversight:** Auditor-General (Performance Audits), Internal Audit functions, GrantConnect/State reporting portals.

**Characteristics:**
*   **Statutory Authority:** Non-compliance is a breach of legislation, not just contract.
*   **Probity Focus:** Extreme emphasis on procurement (open tender thresholds, conflict of interest registers), probity advisors for high-value grants.
*   **Standardized Templates:** Mandatory use of specific acquittal forms (e.g., Commonwealth "Grant Acquittal Report" template).
*   **Clawback Certainty:** Automated debt recovery via government accounting systems (e.g., GovTEAMS, SAP).

### 3.2 Philanthropic & Corporate Funders (Foundations, Trusts, CSR)
**Governing Instruments:**
*   **Contract Law:** The Grant Agreement (Deed or Agreement) is the supreme document.
*   **ACNC Governance Standards** (for registered charities receiving funds).
*   **Self-Regulation:** *Australian Philanthropic Services (APS) Guidelines*, *Philanthropy Australia* best practice.

**Characteristics:**
*   **Relationship-Based:** Often flexible on *budget variations* (e.g., moving 15% between line items without approval) if outcomes are met.
*   **Outcome/Impact Focus:** Less obsessed with "receipt matching," more obsessed with "Theory of Change" evidence.
*   **Reporting Fatigue Risk:** High-volume grantees face 20+ different report formats.
*   **No Statutory Audit Right:** Usually rely on "right to audit" clauses in the deed. Rarely exercise it unless fraud suspected.

### 3.3 The "Pass-Through" Complexity
**Scenario:** Federal Govt $\rightarrow$ State Govt (Intermediary) $\rightarrow$ NGO (Service Provider).
*   **Flow-Down Clauses:** The NGO must comply with *Federal* PGPA Act *and* State Treasury directions *and* the Intermediary's specific contract terms.
*   **Risk:** The Intermediary often imposes stricter reporting (monthly vs quarterly) to manage their own risk. You must comply with the *strictest* standard in the chain.

## 4. Common Requirements Across Funders: The Universal Baseline

Regardless of source, every acquittal demands proof of these **Five Pillars**. Build your systems to produce these automatically.

### 4.1 Eligibility of Expenditure (The "Allowable Cost" Test)
*   **Incurred within Project Dates:** Invoice date *and* service delivery date must fall within the Grant Activity Period. *Pre-paid expenses for post-period services are usually unallowable.*
*   **Directly Attributable:** Costs must be identifiable *specifically* to the grant.
    *   *Direct Costs:* Project Officer salary (100%), project-specific travel, materials.
    *   *Indirect/Overhead Costs:* Rent, insurance, IT, Finance salaries. **Critical:** Most government grants cap overheads (e.g., 10–15% of direct costs) or use a specific methodology (e.g., Activity Based Costing). Philanthropy often allows "Full Cost Recovery" (FCR) but requires a methodology.
*   **Reasonable & Market Rate:** Did you pay $500/hr for consulting when market is $200? Auditors will benchmark.

### 4.2 Procurement & Probity (The "Value for Money" Test)
*   **Thresholds:**
    *   <$10k: One quote (usually).
    *   $10k–$80k (Govt often $80k): 3 written quotes.
    *   >$80k–$200k: Formal RFQ/RFT process, evaluation panel, conflict declarations.
    *   >$200k (Commonwealth): Open market approach (AusTender), mandatory evaluation criteria weighting.
*   **Conflict of Interest (COI):** **Mandatory** written declaration for *every* procurement decision >$5k (varies). Must be signed *before* engagement.
*   **Sole Source Justification:** If only one supplier exists, a detailed written justification signed by CEO/Delegate is required *before* purchase.

### 4.3 Payroll & Labour Costing (The Highest Risk Area)
*   **Timesheets:** Mandatory for *any* staff not 100% dedicated to the grant.
    *   *Best Practice:* Fortnightly timesheets, approved by Supervisor *and* Project Manager, coded to Grant Cost Centre.
    *   *Trap:* "Estimated" percentages (e.g., "Admin spends 20% on Grant X") are rejected by auditors without contemporaneous timesheet evidence.
*   **On-Costs:** Must use actuals (Super, Workers Comp, Payroll Tax, Leave Accruals) or a pre-approved fixed percentage rate. Cannot invent a rate.

### 4.4 Asset Management & Capital Expenditure
*   **Asset Register:** Assets >$5k (or $10k threshold) purchased with grant funds must be on a separate Grant Asset Register.
*   **Title/Ownership:** Government grants often stipulate Crown/State retains title; NGO is custodian. Philanthropy usually transfers title to NGO.
*   **Disposal:** Cannot sell/scrap grant-funded assets without funder written approval. Proceeds usually return to funder or reinvested in project.

### 4.5 GST & Tax Treatment
*   **Government Grants:** Usually **GST-Free** (Non-taxable supply) if recipient is a Govt entity or endorsed charity/DGR. *Input Tax Credits (ITCs)* can be claimed on expenses.
*   **Philanthropy:** Often **GST-Free** (Donation), but Sponsorship/Service Agreements may be **Taxable Supply + GST**.
*   **Acquittal Reporting:** Report *Net* expenditure (Excl GST) if claiming ITCs. Report *Gross* (Incl GST) if not registered for GST or grant is GST-inclusive. **Consistency with BAS is non-negotiable.**

## 5. Record-Keeping Architecture: Building the Evidentiary Chain

The "Golden Rule": **If it isn't documented contemporaneously, it didn't happen.** Auditors do not accept retrospective reconstructions.

### 5.1 The Chart of Accounts (CoA) – The Compliance Backbone
Do not use generic codes. Structure your CoA for **Grant Dimensionality**.
*   **Bad:** `6000 - Salaries`
*   **Good:** `6000-DEPT-PROJ-GRANT` (e.g., `6000-CD-YOUTH-DSS2024`).
*   **Dimensions Required:**
    1.  Natural Account (Salary, Travel, Consumables).
    2.  Cost Centre / Department.
    3.  **Project / Grant Code (Mandatory).**
    4.  Funder Source (Govt/Philanthropy/Unrestricted).
*   *Result:* One-click GL extract for any acquittal.

### 5.2 The Document Hierarchy & Retention Policy
| Document Class | Examples | Minimum Retention (AU Std) | Storage Protocol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Source Documents** | Invoices, Receipts, Timesheets, Contracts, Bank Statements | **7 Years** post-acquittal acceptance (ATO/ASIC) / **State Archival Acts** may require longer. | **Digital First:** PDF/A format. OCR searchable. Named: `YYYYMMDD_VENDOR_GRANTCODE_INV123.pdf` |
| **Process Evidence** | Quote comparisons, COI forms, Evaluation panels, Variation approvals emails. | 7 Years post-acquittal. | Linked in Grant Management System (GMS) or ERP to transaction. |
| **Reporting Outputs** | Submitted Acquittals, Funder Acceptance Letters, Audit Management Letters. | Permanent / 15+ Years. | Board Portal / Secure Cloud Archive. |
| **Correspondence** | Key emails approving scope change, budget variation approvals. | 7 Years. | Saved out of Outlook into Project Folder (`.msg` or PDF). |

### 5.3 Digital Workflow: The "No Paper" Standard
1.  **Purchase Order (PO) Raised** in ERP $\rightarrow$ Auto-checks Budget Availability.
2.  **Goods Received / Service Entry Sheet** $\rightarrow$ Project Manager verifies delivery.
3.  **Invoice Matched (3-Way Match):** PO + Receipt + Invoice.
4.  **Payment Batch** $\rightarrow$ ABA File $\rightarrow$ Bank.
5.  **Auto-Reconciliation:** Bank feed matches Payment $\rightarrow$ GL Posted.
6.  **Acquittal Generation:** GL Trial Balance by Grant Code $\rightarrow$ Maps to Acquittal Template.

**Critical Control:** *No payment without a PO (except petty cash <$200). No PO without Grant Code.*

### 5.4 Version Control for Budget Variations
Funders require tracking of **Approved Budget $\rightarrow$ Current Budget $\rightarrow$ Actuals**.
*   Maintain a **Budget Variation Register**.
*   *Columns:* Date, Variation Type (Internal Reallocation / Funder Approved / Scope Change), Amount, Line Items Affected, Funder Approval Ref (Email/Letter Date), Updated Budget Figure.
*   Acquittal must reconcile to the *Final Approved Budget*, not the original application budget.

## 6. The Audit Continuum: From Desk Review to Forensic Examination

Understanding the *type* of audit dictates your preparation depth.

### 6.1 Level 1: Desk-Based Acquittal Review (100% of Grants)
*   **Who:** Funder Program Officer / Grants Admin.
*   **Scope:** Arithmetic check (Does Income = Expenditure + Surplus/Deficit?), Budget variance >10% explained, KPIs ticked off, Signed declaration present.
*   **Prep:** Ensure the "Variance Commentary" column in your acquittal template is pre-filled *before* the accountant signs off. "Underspend on Travel ($5k) due to COVID pivot to Zoom; funds reallocated to IT licenses per Variation Approval #VA-04 dated 12/05/24."

### 6.2 Level 2: Compliance Audit / Certification Audit (Targeted / Random ~5-10%)
*   **Who:** Funder Internal Audit / Contracted External Firm (e.g., KPMG, PwC, specialist grant auditors).
*   **Scope:** **Sample Testing.** "Provide source docs for 20 transactions." "Verify 5 staff timesheets for Q3." "Show procurement file for Contractor X."
*   **Standard:** *ASAE 3100 (Compliance Engagements)* or *ASAE 3500 (Performance Engagements)*.
*   **Materiality:** Often low ($0 materiality for probity breaches; $5k for financial misstatement).

### 6.3 Level 3: Performance Audit (Auditor-General / OAG)
*   **Who:** State/Federal Auditor-General.
*   **Scope:** **Economy, Efficiency, Effectiveness (3 Es).** Not just "did you spend it right?" but "did you achieve outcomes efficiently?" "Was the grant program design flawed?"
*   **Powers:** Compulsory production of documents, witness summons. Public report tabled in Parliament.
*   **Prep:** Requires **Program Logic** evidence. Not just "we ran 10 workshops," but "workshops led to 20% employment uplift per longitudinal survey."

### 6.4 Level 4: Forensic Investigation / Fraud Audit
*   **Trigger:** Whistleblower (PID Act), Data analytics anomaly (Benford’s Law on invoices), Journalist tip-off.
*   **Scope:** Full digital forensics (email discovery, deleted file recovery, bank forensic tracing).
*   **Outcome:** Criminal referral (CDPP / Police), Civil recovery, Debarment.
*   **Defense:** **Whistleblower Policy**, **Segregation of Duties**, **Immutable Audit Trails** (Blockchain/Write-Once-Read-Many logs).

## 7. Anatomy of Failure: The Top 10 Compliance Pitfalls & Mitigation Strategies

Based on analysis of ANAO reports, State Auditor-General findings, and sector feedback.

### Pitfall 1: The "Budget Drift" (Unauthorized Variations)
*   **Failure:** Spending $15k from "Travel" on "Equipment" without written funder approval. Acquittal shows variance; funder claws back $15k.
*   **Root Cause:** Project staff spend to "need," Finance reports to "budget," no reconciliation loop.
*   **Mitigation:** **Hard Budget Locks in ERP.** System prevents coding invoice to Grant Travel code if budget exhausted. Requires "Budget Transfer Form" workflow *before* PO creation.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you need to ask "Is this allowed?", the answer is "Not without approval."

### Pitfall 2: The "Phantom FTE" (Payroll Allocation Without Timesheets)
*   **Failure:** CEO claims 20% FTE on Grant ($40k). Auditor asks for timesheets. CEO says "I'm too busy." **Full $40k disallowed.**
*   **Root Cause:** Senior staff exemption culture; poor timesheet UX.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Mandatory Timesheets for ALL** staff on grants (including CEO).
    *   Use **Mobile/App based timesheets** (pre-populated with "Grant X - Admin" button).
    *   Monthly "Timesheet Compliance Report" to CEO/Board. Zero tolerance for >2 weeks late.

### Pitfall 3: Procurement "Mates Rates" / Missing COI
*   **Failure:** Org hires Director’s spouse’s consulting firm for $45k (under $80k threshold, so "only 1 quote needed"). No COI declared. **Fraud finding. Reputational destruction.**
*   **Root Cause:** Thresholds seen as "hurdles to avoid" not "controls to follow."
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **COI Declaration at Induction & Annually.**
    *   **Automated COI Check:** ERP flags if Vendor ABN matches Staff/Board Related Party register.
    *   **Policy:** *All* Related Party transactions require Board Minute approval *regardless of value*.

### Pitfall 4: The "GST Mismatch" (BAS vs Acquittal)
*   **Failure:** Acquittal reports Net expenditure ($100k). BAS shows Gross ($110k) claimed as ITCs. Funder reconciles: "You claimed GST back from ATO *and* kept the cash from us." **Clawback of $10k GST component.**
*   **Root Cause:** Finance team prepares Acquittal; Tax Agent prepares BAS. No cross-check.
*   **Mitigation:** **Reconciliation Control:** `Acquittal Net Expenditure + GST Claimed on BAS (for Grant) = Gross Expenditure in GL`. Sign-off by Tax Agent *and* Grant Accountant.

### Pitfall 5: Capital Expenditure Expensed (or Vice Versa)
*   **Failure:** Buying a $15k Vehicle. Expensed to "Motor Vehicles" in P&L. Funder says: "Asset >$5k threshold. Should be Balance Sheet. Depreciation only allowed." **Acquittal rejected.**
*   **Root Cause:** AP Clerk codes to "Expense" account default.
*   **Mitigation:** **Fixed Asset Register Integration.** PO for Asset $\rightarrow$ Auto-creates Asset Shell in Register $\rightarrow$ Capitalizes on Goods Receipt. Expense accounts *blocked* for Asset categories.

### Pitfall 6: Missing "In-Kind" Valuation Evidence
*   **Failure:** Application promised $50k Volunteer In-Kind. Acquittal claims $50k. Auditor asks: "Show calculation." Org: "We estimated." **$50k Disallowed.**
*   **Standard:** *Australian Accounting Standards / ACNC* require **Fair Value** measurement.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   Volunteer Sign-in Sheets (Hours).
    *   **Rate Card:** Pre-approved rates (e.g., "Admin Volunteer = $30/hr", "Specialist Legal Volunteer = $200/hr").
    *   Calculation: `Hours x Rate = Value`. Signed off by Volunteer Coordinator.

### Pitfall 7: The "Late Acquittal" Cascade
*   **Failure:** Final report due 30 June. Submitted 15 August. Funder withholds *next year's* funding tranche. Org cashflow crisis.
*   **Root Cause:** "Year-end audit" prioritized over "Grant acquittal."
*   **Mitigation:** **Internal "Soft Close" Deadline:** Acquittal draft due **Day 15** post-period. Review Day 20. Submit Day 25. Treat Funder Deadline as "Late."

### Pitfall 8: Program Logic Disconnect (Activity vs Outcome)
*   **Failure:** KPI: "Reduce youth unemployment." Report: "We ran 50 resume workshops." Auditor: "So what? Did unemployment drop?"
*   **Mitigation:** **Data Collection Plan** embedded in Grant Agreement negotiation.
    *   Define *Proxy Indicators* if long-term outcomes unmeasurable during grant (e.g., "Job interviews secured," "Certifications gained").
    *   Build CRM/Case Management to capture this *operationally*, not at report time.

### Pitfall 9: Unspent Funds / Surplus Mismanagement
*   **Failure:** $50k surplus at project end. Org absorbs into general funds. Funder demands return (Deed says "Unexpended funds repayable").
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Monthly Burn Rate Tracking:** `Forecast Spend to End Date` vs `Cash Remaining`.
    *   **Variation Request Early:** If surplus predicted at Month 9 of 12, request scope extension (buy extra laptops, extend staff contract) *before* year end.
    *   **Segregated Bank Account:** For large grants (>$500k), use a dedicated sub-account. Prevents "borrowing" grant cash for payroll.

### Pitfall 10: Institutional Knowledge Loss (The "Single Point of Failure")
*   **Failure:** Grant Manager resigns 2 weeks before acquittal. Passwords gone. Filing system unintelligible. Funder deadline missed.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Grant Handover Protocol:** Mandatory 2-week overlap.
    *   **Centralized Grant Workspace (SharePoint/Teams/GMS):** Not personal OneDrive/Email.
    *   **Documented Procedures:** "How to Acquit Grant X" SOP updated annually.

## 9. Real-World Case Studies: Lessons from the Field

### Case Study 1: The "Reasonable Allocation" Trap (University Research Admin)
**Context:** NHMRC Grant ($2M over 5 years). University charged "Central Admin Overhead" at 30% of direct costs per university policy.
**Failure:** NHMRC caps "On-Costs/Overheads" at a specific rate (e.g., 20% or specific items only). University applied its *internal* rate, not the *funder's* rate.
**Result:** **$400k Disallowed** over 3 years. University had to refund from unrestricted funds.
**Lesson:** **Funders' Guidelines Trump Internal Policy.** The Grant Agreement is the tax law for that project. Build a "Funder Rate Card" in the ERP for each grant.

### Case Study 2: The "Missing Timesheet" Clawback (Community Health NGO)
**Context:** State Health Dept Grant ($800k/yr). 15 staff split across 3 programs.
**Failure:** Staff used paper timesheets. Boxes archived off-site. Auditor (3 years later) requested Q2 Timesheets for Program B. Boxes water-damaged/mouldy. Unreadable.
**Result:** **$220k Salary Costs Disallowed.** NGO lost funding for following year due to "Governance Failure."
**Lesson:** **Digital, Contemporaneous, Backed Up.** Paper is a liability. If you must use paper: Scan *daily* to cloud. Retain digital 7 years; destroy paper after 1 year (if policy allows).

### Case Study 3: The "Scope Creep" Capital Build (Local Government / Community Hall)
**Context:** Federal "Building Better Regions" Grant ($1.5M). Council manages build.
**Failure:** Council Project Manager approved "Variations" with builder (better tiles, extra parking) verbally. Total variations = $300k. Grant budget fixed at $1.5M. Council paid builder from own funds but coded invoices to Grant Code to track.
**Result:** Acquittal showed $1.8M expenditure on Grant Code. Auditor: "You spent grant money on unapproved scope." **$300k Clawback.** Council wore the cost.
**Lesson:** **Change Control is Contractual.** No variation (verbal or written) is valid without Funder Deed of Variation. Internal "Project Contingency" $\neq$ Grant Budget Flexibility.

### Case Study 4: The Philanthropic "Impact Washing" Reputational Hit
**Context:** Major Foundation Grant ($500k over 3 years) for "Systems Change."
**Failure:** Annual reports highlighted "10,000 people reached" (Output). Foundation commissioned independent evaluation (Year 3). Found zero policy change, zero sustained behavior change (Outcome). Grantee had no outcome data.
**Result:** Foundation declined renewal. Publicly cited "lack of evidence base" in sector newsletter. Grantee lost 3 other funders who read the newsletter.
**Lesson:** **Philanthropy Audits Outcomes, Not Just Receipts.** Invest 5-10% of grant budget in M&E (Monitoring & Evaluation) infrastructure *at start*.

### Case Study 5: The "Related Party" Procurement Fraud (Arts Organization)
**Context:** Multi-year Govt Operational Funding ($400k/yr).
**Failure:** Artistic Director's partner owns a Production Company. Org engages them for 5 productions/year ($50k each). COI forms signed *annually* but "Management Plan" was "Director recuses from decision." Board minutes show Director *chaired* the meeting where contract approved.
**Result:** **ICAC / Corruption Commission Referral.** Director charged. Org placed on "High Risk" funding list (monthly reporting, no advance payments) for 5 years.
**Lesson:** **Recusal = Absence.** You cannot chair the meeting. You cannot influence the spec. The COI Register must be checked *at PO creation* by Finance, not just HR at induction.

## 10. Conclusion: Compliance as Organizational Strategy

Grant compliance is frequently viewed through a **deficit lens**—a cost center, a risk, a distraction from "real work." This is a strategic error.

**Mature organizations flip the lens:**
1.  **Compliance = Fundraising.** A clean audit history is the #1 due diligence document for major donors and government tenders. It *is* the business case.
2.  **Compliance = Operational Excellence.** The controls required for acquittal (timesheets, procurement, budget monitoring) are the *exact same controls* required for good management. You are not building a "grant system"; you are building a **management system** that happens to satisfy funders.
3.  **Compliance = Trust Capital.** In a sector defined by trust, the ability to say "We can prove every dollar, every hour, every outcome" is your most valuable currency.

### The Final Checklist: Are You "Acquittal Ready" Today?
*   [ ] Can I produce a **Trial Balance by Grant Code** for any funder within 1 hour?
*   [ ] Does every transaction >$1k have a **PDF invoice/receipt attached** in the ERP?
*   [ ] Are **Timesheets** for the last pay period **approved and locked**?
*   [ ] Is the **Conflict of Interest Register** current for all decision-makers?
*   [ ] Do I have **Funder Approval Emails** for every budget variation >10%?
*   [ ] Is the **Asset Register** reconciled to the General Ledger?
*   [ ] Has the **GST/BAS Reconciliation** been signed off for the last quarter?
*   [ ] Are **Unspent Funds** forecasted and variation requests drafted?
*   [ ] Is the **Grant Register** (Master Schedule) reconciled to the Bank/GL?

**If you answered "No" to any:** You have a control gap. Close it *before* the auditor asks for it. The cost of prevention is 1/10th the cost of cure.

### Appendix: Glossary of Key Terms
*   **Acquittal:** Formal certification of expenditure and performance closing a grant.
*   **Clawback:** Recovery of funds by funder due to breach/underspend.
*   **COI (Conflict of Interest):** Real, perceived, or potential conflict between private interest and public duty.
*   **Deed of Variation:** Legal document amending the Grant Agreement (scope, budget, time).
*   **EL1/EL2 / SES:** Government delegation levels for financial approval (Critical: know your org's *Instrument of Delegation*).
*   **Funder:** Generic term for Grantor (Government, Foundation, Corporate).
*   **GMS (Grant Management System):** Software managing lifecycle (e.g., SmartyGrants, Fluxx, Salesforce NPSP, custom ERP module).
*   **PGPA Act:** *Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013* (Cth) – The "Bible" for Commonwealth money.
*   **Probity:** Procedural integrity, fairness, honesty in procurement.
*   **Related Party:** Key Management Personnel, Close Family, Controlled Entities (AASB 124).
*   **Statutory Declaration:** Legal document signed before authorized witness
