Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. It is encouraging to hear about the valuable work your community garden is doing, and I am happy to provide clarity on the eligibility criteria and application requirements before you commit to preparing a full submission.

First, to address your primary question: **unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspicing arrangement with an eligible legal entity—such as a local council—are eligible to apply**, provided the auspicing organisation meets the program’s eligibility requirements (e.g., incorporated not-for-profit, local government authority, or other approved entity). The council would need to sign the funding agreement, assume legal and financial responsibility for the grant, and provide a letter of support confirming the auspicing arrangement, including details of insurance coverage and governance oversight.

Regarding **grant amounts**, this round offers a minimum of $5,000 and a maximum of $50,000 per project. Your proposed budget for rainwater tanks and a shade structure would fall within this range, assuming the total project cost (including any in-kind contributions) does not exceed the cap.

**Infrastructure of the kind you describe is considered an eligible cost**, provided it is directly linked to the project’s resilience outcomes—such as climate adaptation, water security, and community wellbeing. Eligible expenditure includes materials, contractor fees, permits, and reasonable project management costs. Ongoing operational costs (e.g., utilities, maintenance) are not funded.

For **supporting documents**, please prepare the following:
- A signed auspicing agreement or letter from the council confirming their role, ABN, insurance coverage, and willingness to enter a funding agreement.
- Your group’s constitution, rules, or terms of reference (even if informal), demonstrating not-for-profit status and community benefit.
- A detailed project budget with at least two quotes for major capital items.
- Evidence of land tenure or permission to install infrastructure on the site.
- A brief project plan outlining timelines, risk management, and community engagement.
- Most recent financial statement or income/expenditure summary (showing the ~$40,000 turnover).
- Letters of support from community stakeholders or partner organisations.

Applications close at 5:00 pm AEST on 30 June. I recommend starting the documentation early, particularly the council’s formal endorsement, as this can take time to finalise.
