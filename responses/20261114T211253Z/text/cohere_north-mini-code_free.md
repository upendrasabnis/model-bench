# Best Practices in Nonprofit Grant Writing  
*A comprehensive guide from funder research to post‑award reporting*

---

## 1. Introduction  

Grant writing is both an art and a science. For nonprofits, a well‑crafted grant can mean the difference between sustaining programs, expanding services, and closing critical gaps in community support. This article walks you through the entire grant‑writing cycle, offering concrete examples, practical checklists, and actionable tips you can apply to every proposal.  

---

## 2. Understanding the Grant Landscape  

### 2. Brest Types of Grants  

| Grant Type | Typical Funding Source | Typical Use |
|------------|----------------------|-------------|
| **Program‑Specific Grants** | Foundations, government agencies | Funding a defined program or service |
| **Capacity‑Building Grants** | Large foundations, corporate CSR | Strengthening organizational infrastructure |
| **Emergency/Disaster Grants** | Humanitarian NGOs, federal disaster relief | Rapid response, immediate relief |
| **Research/Policy Grants** | Think tanks, universities | Data collection, policy analysis |
| **Endowment/Endow‑Grant** | Wealthy donors, memorial funds | Long‑term investment, interest‑bearing |

### 2.2 Key Funders’ Characteristics  

- **Mission Alignment** – Does the funder’s mission mirror yours?  
- **Geographic Focus** – Local, regional, national, or international?  
- **Funding Priorities** – Look for “priority areas” in the RFP.  
- **Funding Cycle** – Annual, semi‑annual, or ad‑hoc?  
- **Reporting Frequency** – Quarterly, semi‑annual, or annual?

## 3. Researching Funders  

### 3.1 Sources to Explore  

1. **Foundation Directory Online (FDO)** – Comprehensive database of U.S. foundations.  
2. **Guidestar** – IRS‑required filings (Form 990) for transparency.  
3. **Foundation Center (now Candid)** – Grant listings, trends, and analytics.  
4. **Government portals** – Grants.gov (U.S.), Canada.ca (Canada), etc.  
5. **Funder Websites** – Look for “Grants,” “Funding Opportunities,” or “Call for Proposals.”  
6. **Professional associations** – Nonprofit network groups often share funder newsletters.  

### 3.2 Steps to Build a Funder Profile  

| Step | Action | Tools/Resources |
|------|--------|-----------------|
| **1** | Identify potential funders | FDO, Candid, sector‑specific directories |
| **2** | Review past grants & reports | Form 990, annual reports, grant databases |
| **3** | Note eligibility criteria | Funder websites, contact info |
| **4** | Capture deadlines & RFI dates | Spreadsheet or grant‑management software |
| **5** | Document contact person & email | CRM (e.g., Salesforce, Airtable) |
| **6** | Summarize mission alignment | Side‑by‑side comparison matrix |

### 3.3 Example Funder Research Sheet  

```
Funder: The Robert Wood Johnson Foundation
Mission: Improve health and wellbeing in the U.S.
Focus Areas: Childhood obesity, mental health
Eligibility: 501(c)( userdata, community‑based orgs
Grant Size: $50,000 – $2M
Deadlines: Rolling (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4)
Reporting: Annual progress + financial
Contact: Sarah Miller, Program Officer (smiller@rwjf.org)
Notes: Preference for data‑driven interventions
```

### 3.4 Checklist: Funders Research  

- [ ] Funder’s mission statement reviewed  
- [ ] Eligibility criteria verified  
- [ ] Past grant awards analyzed (type, size, focus)  
- [ ] Application deadlines and RFI dates logged  
- [ ] Contact information saved  
- [ ] Alignment score (1‑5) assigned
