**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our funding guidelines allow organisations that are not incorporated as a separate legal entity to apply, provided they are formally auspiced by a recognised government body — such as a local council — and can demonstrate that the council holds the appropriate insurance and governance arrangements on their behalf. The key requirement is that the auspicing council must be listed as the primary applicant on the application form, and the project must be clearly identified as a community‑led initiative under the council’s auspices. In your case, because the council already provides insurance coverage and you operate under its banner, you meet the eligibility criteria for this round.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant offers awards ranging from a minimum of $5,000 to a maximum of $50,000 per project. Applications that request an amount outside this band will be considered ineligible, so I recommend aiming for a figure that reflects the scope of your proposed rain‑water tanks and shade structure while staying within the $5,000–$50,000 range. If your project budget exceeds $50,000, you may need to consider a co‑funding approach or a subsequent funding round.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure that directly supports climate‑resilience outcomes is eligible, including the installation of rain‑water harvesting systems, shade structures, and related site improvements. However, the costs must be “eligible project costs” as defined in the grant guide — meaning they are essential to the resilience objective, reasonable, and supported by obtainable quotes. Capital equipment that is not directly tied to the resilience function (e.g., general garden tools) would not be covered. Therefore, the rain‑water tanks and shade structure you describe are fully eligible, provided you can supply detailed quotations and a clear cost breakdown.

**Suggested supporting documents**

4. A budget spreadsheet that aligns with the grant’s maximum allowable amount and clearly separates eligible from non‑eligible costs.  
5. Evidence of your current financial statements or an audited financial summary showing the $40,000 annual turnover, to demonstrate fiscal transparency.  
6. Proof of any required local government approvals for the proposed works, such as building permits or development consent.
