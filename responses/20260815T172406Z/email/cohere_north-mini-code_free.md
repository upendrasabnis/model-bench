Thank you for reaching out and for the clear description of your garden’s aims and operations. I’m pleased to provide the following information to help you determine whether your project aligns with the Community Resilience Grant programme.  

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines state that an applicant may be either a registered charitable organisation or an unincorporated group that is formally **auspiced** by a local government body. The key requirement is that the auspicing council must act as the legal signatory for the grant agreement, provide the necessary insurance coverage, and assume financial accountability for the project. Because you are operating under the auspices of your local council, which already holds the required insurances, you meet this criterion. However, please note that the council will need to sign the grant agreement and, where required, provide a copy of its own insurance policy as evidence of coverage.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, the funding level is determined by the scope, impact, and cost‑effectiveness of the proposed project. Typical awards for infrastructure‑focused initiatives range from $15,000 to $75,000, but applications that demonstrate strong community benefit and clear cost‑sharing arrangements may be funded outside this band. Our programme encourages applicants to propose the most appropriate budget that fully addresses the project’s objectives while maintaining fiscal responsibility.  

**Eligibility of rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes – infrastructure such as **rainwater harvesting systems (tanks, gutters, and associated plumbing)** and **climate‑resilient shade structures** are expressly listed as eligible costs under the “Climate Adaptation and Resilience” category of the grant. These items must be directly linked to the project’s resilience goals, such as reducing water consumption, mitigating heat stress, and ensuring continuous operation during extreme weather events. We recommend that the budget line items for these components be detailed, with quotations that break down equipment, installation, and any ancillary works (e.g., foundations, access pathways).  

**Supporting documents to prepare**  

1. **Project Narrative** – A concise (2–3 page) description of the problem you are addressing, the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline for implementation.  
2. **Budget and Budget Narrative** – A line‑item budget that includes the cost of the tanks, shade structure, and any other supplies or labour. Include a brief explanation of each line item and any cost‑sharing contributions.  
3. **Proof of Insurance** – A copy of the council’s insurance certificate confirming that it covers the project activities and any public liability associated with the new infrastructure.  
4. **Financial Statements** – For the most recent fiscal year (or a simplified statement if you are not required to file audited accounts). This should demonstrate that your current turnover of approximately $40,000 is derived from the sources you have identified.  
5. **Letters of Support** – At least two letters from community stakeholders (e.g., local businesses, resident groups, or other NGOs) confirming the need for the project and their willingness to assist with its operation or maintenance.  
6. **Council Auspice Agreement** – A formal letter from your local council stating that it will act as the legal sponsor of the grant, will sign the agreement, and will retain the insurance coverage for the duration of the project.  
7. **Site Plans and Drawings** – Simple sketches or a site plan indicating where the rainwater tanks and shade structure will be located, along with any existing utilities or access constraints.
