Dear Jamie,

Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out before investing time in a full application, and I am happy to address each of your questions in turn.

First, regarding your organisational structure: unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspicing arrangement with a local government authority are absolutely eligible to apply, provided the auspicing body (in this case, your local council) is willing to enter into a legally binding funding agreement on your behalf. The council would assume full legal and financial accountability for the grant, including acquittal responsibilities, so it is essential that you have a written Memorandum of Understanding or Auspicing Agreement in place prior to submission.

Second, the current grant round offers a minimum request of $5,000 and a maximum of $50,000 per project. Given your annual turnover of approximately $40,000, your proposed budget should fall comfortably within this range. Please note that we generally expect applicants to demonstrate some level of co-contribution—whether cash, in-kind, or volunteer labour—though this is not a strict eligibility criterion for this round.

Third, infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience to climate impacts is a priority funding area. Rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures that enable continued operation during extreme heat events are explicitly listed as eligible capital expenditure in the program guidelines. Costs for materials, professional installation, necessary permits, and associated site preparation are all allowable. Ongoing maintenance costs, however, are not eligible.

Finally, regarding supporting documentation, you should prepare the following for a complete application:
1. A signed letter from the council CEO or authorised delegate confirming the auspicing arrangement and insurance coverage.
2. Your group’s most recent financial statement (a simple income and expenditure report is sufficient given your turnover).
3. A detailed project budget with at least two quotes for major infrastructure items.
4. A project plan outlining timelines, risk management, and how the infrastructure will benefit the wider community.
5. Evidence of land tenure or permission to install infrastructure on the garden site.
6. Letters of support from community stakeholders (e.g., local schools, neighbourhood houses, or health services) are highly regarded but not mandatory.

The guidelines and application form are available on our website, and I strongly recommend attending the applicant information webinar scheduled for next Tuesday.
