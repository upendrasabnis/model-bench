Thank you for reaching out and for the thoughtful description of the work your community garden is undertaking. I’m pleased to provide clarification on the points you raised, and I hope the information below helps you decide whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. An unincorporated group that operates under the auspices of a local council – and where the council holds the necessary insurances, contracts and legal responsibilities on the group’s behalf – is indeed eligible to apply. The key requirement is that the applicant demonstrate effective governance and financial oversight, which a council’s support typically satisfies. However, you will still need to provide a simple organisational structure document (e.g., a constitution or set of operating guidelines) that outlines the roles of volunteers, decision‑making processes, and how the council’s auspice functions in practice.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum award. Funding levels are determined on a case‑by‑case basis, reflecting the scope, impact and budget of the proposed project. Past awards have ranged from approximately $5,000 to $50,000, but applications that fall outside this range are also considered if the justification is robust. For your project – which includes rainwater tanks and a shade structure – a grant in the $15,000‑$30,000 range would likely align with the anticipated costs, though the final amount will depend on the detailed budget you submit.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures is expressly listed as an eligible cost category under the “Climate‑Resilient Community Assets” component of the grant. These assets must demonstrably enhance the sustainability and resilience of community operations, particularly in the face of climate‑related challenges such as extreme heat. The proposal should include a clear justification of how each piece of infrastructure directly supports continued garden operation and benefits local residents.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following materials:

1. **Project Narrative** – A concise (max 2 pages) description of the problem, objectives, implementation plan, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – Itemised costs for equipment, installation, contingency, and any staffing or consultancy fees, with sources for each line item.  
3. **Organizational Profile** – A one‑page overview of your group, its volunteer base, operating history, and the council’s auspice agreement.  
4. **Proof of Insurance** – A copy of the council’s insurance certificate confirming coverage for public liability and any relevant risks associated with the project.  
5. **Letters of Support** – Brief endorsements from the council, local businesses, or community members that demonstrate broad backing for the initiative.  
6. **Financial Statements** – Most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple income‑and‑expense summary) showing the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
7. **Site Plans or Drawings** – Sketches or CAD files indicating where the rainwater tanks and shade structure will be placed.  
8. **Risk Assessment** – A basic assessment of health‑ and safety‑related risks associated with the installation and use of the new infrastructure.  

We also advise that you review the full grant guidelines (available on our website) for any additional requirements, such as the need for a project timeline, monitoring and evaluation framework, or a matching contribution. Should you decide to move forward, our grants team is happy to provide a brief pre‑application review once you have drafted an initial concept note.
