**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. While we preferentially support registered charities or incorporated not‑for‑profit entities, unincorporated groups that operate under the formal auspices of a local council are also eligible, provided that the council can demonstrate its endorsement and assume legal responsibility for any grant‑related obligations (such as insurance coverage and financial accountability). In your case, because the council already holds the necessary insurances on your behalf and acts as the contracting authority, you should be able to submit an application under the council’s banner. We will, however, require a signed letter of support from the council confirming that it will act as the primary recipient of funds and that it will oversee the implementation of the project.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum award. Instead, funding levels are determined on a case‑by‑case basis, typically ranging from $5,000 to $50,000 for projects of the scale you describe. Your total budget of approximately $40,000 aligns well with this range, and we would expect a grant request of roughly 60‑80 % of the total project cost, leaving the remainder to be covered by in‑kind contributions, council support, or other funding sources.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes—capital infrastructure that enhances community resilience to climate‑related challenges is explicitly listed as an eligible cost. Rainwater harvesting systems and durable shade structures are considered “climate‑adaptation” measures that directly support the continuity of essential community services (in your case, the garden’s ability to operate during hot summers). We do require that such items be justified within the project’s overall objectives, accompanied by detailed specifications, cost breakdowns, and evidence that they will deliver measurable resilience benefits.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend compiling the following materials:

1. **Project Narrative** – A concise description of the problem you are addressing (increasing summer heat impact), the proposed solution, and the expected outcomes (e.g., reduced water costs, extended growing season, community health benefits).  
2. **Budget and Financial Plan** – Itemised costs for rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary expenses, together with a funding matrix showing the grant request versus other contributions.  
3. **Council Endorsement Letter** – A formal letter from your local council confirming that it will act as the contracting body, that it holds the requisite insurances, and that it supports the project’s objectives.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – Even though you are unincorporated, a profile that outlines your volunteers, governance structure, and any previous community projects you have delivered.  
5. **Insurance and Risk Management Documentation** – A copy of the council’s insurance certificate (or a letter confirming coverage) and a brief risk‑assessment plan for the installation work.  
6. **Community Support Evidence** – Letters of support from local residents, user agreements, or attendance records from the weekly produce stall to demonstrate demand and community engagement.  
7. **Implementation Timeline** – A Gantt‑style schedule indicating key milestones (procurement, installation, testing, and operational launch).  
8. **Monitoring and Evaluation Plan** – Outline how you will measure success (e.g., water usage reduction, temperature differentials, user satisfaction surveys) and report back to funders.
