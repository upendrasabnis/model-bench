Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out to confirm eligibility before investing time in a full application, and I am happy to address each of your questions in turn.

First, regarding organisational structure: unincorporated groups that operate under a formal auspicing arrangement with an eligible legal entity—such as a local council—are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing organisation meets the program’s eligibility criteria (e.g., is an incorporated not-for-profit, local government body, or other eligible entity) and agrees to accept legal and financial responsibility for the grant. Your council’s willingness to hold insurances and act as auspice is a positive indicator; we will require a signed auspicing agreement or letter of support from the council confirming this arrangement as part of the application.

Second, the Community Resilience Grant offers funding between $5,000 and $50,000 per project. There is no minimum project budget, but the grant amount requested must fall within this range and represent no more than 80% of total eligible project costs; the remaining 20% can be met through cash or in-kind contributions, including volunteer labour valued at the standard rate.

Third, infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures is considered an eligible capital cost, provided the items are directly related to building community resilience against climate impacts—in your case, adapting to hotter, drier summers. Costs for purchase, delivery, installation, and any necessary site preparation (e.g., plumbing, footings) are all allowable. Ongoing maintenance costs, however, are not eligible.

Regarding supporting documentation, please prepare the following:
- A signed auspicing agreement or letter from the council confirming their role, ABN, and insurance coverage.
- Your group’s most recent financial statement or a simple income-and-expenditure summary showing the ~$40,000 annual turnover.
- Quotes from at least two suppliers for each major infrastructure item (tanks, shade structure, installation).
- A brief project plan outlining timelines, risk management, and how the infrastructure will sustain garden operations during extreme heat.
- Evidence of community need or support (e.g., letters from members, local health service, or council).
- If available, your group’s constitution, rules, or terms of reference demonstrating not-for-profit status.

Your annual turnover of $40,000 is well within the program’s thresholds, and the fact that income is generated through a produce stall and membership fees does not affect eligibility, provided all funds are reinvested in the garden’s community purposes.
