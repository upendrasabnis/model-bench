Thank you for reaching out and for your interest in the Community Resilience Grant. I’m pleased to provide clarification on the points you raised, and I hope the information below helps you decide whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. Unincorporated groups that operate under the auspices of a local council are indeed eligible, provided the council acts as the primary point of contact for contractual and financial matters and holds any required insurances, tax registrations, and public liability coverage. In practice, we require the council to sign a “supporting partner” agreement confirming that it will oversee the project’s financial reporting, procurement, and compliance with any applicable council policies. If your council can provide such an undertaking, your group meets the organisational‑structure criteria.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to be flexible. Applicants may request any amount between **$5,000 and $150,000** for a single project cycle. The final award is determined by the merit of the proposal, the demonstrated community impact, and the availability of funds within the current round. While many projects receive funding in the $20,000‑$60,000 range, larger capital investments—such as those involving substantial infrastructure—are not excluded, provided the budget aligns with the project’s scope and outcomes.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Capital infrastructure that directly supports the long‑term resilience and sustainability of community activities is fully eligible. Specifically, the following costs are considered allowable under the “Infrastructure and Assets” category:  

1. **Rainwater tanks** – including purchase, delivery, installation, and any necessary plumbing or storage facilities.  
2. **Shade structures** – permanent or semi‑permanent shelters designed to protect garden spaces, participants, and produce from extreme heat.  
3. Associated site works such as foundations, footings, and minor earthworks that are essential to the installation.  

Please note that ordinary operating expenses (e.g., consumables, staffing, or routine maintenance) are covered under separate budget lines and should be distinguished from capital items in your budget breakdown.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To ensure a smooth review process, we recommend assembling the following documentation:  

- **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem you are addressing (increasing summer heat), the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and how the project aligns with the grant’s resilience objectives.  
- **Budget Table** – a line‑item breakdown separating capital costs (tanks, shade structure, installation) from operating costs, with quotations or provisional invoices where available.  
- **Proof of Council Support** – a formal letter or memorandum of understanding from your local council confirming that it will act as the fiscal sponsor, hold insurances, and assume liability for the project.  
- **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page overview of your garden’s mission, volunteer base, current activities, and annual turnover (the $40,000 figure you mentioned is useful for context).  
- **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for very small organisations, a simplified profit‑and‑loss statement and balance sheet).  
- **Risk Assessment and Safety Plan** – documentation outlining how the installation will be managed safely, including any necessary permits or council approvals.  
- **Community Engagement Evidence** – minutes from recent community meetings, sign‑up sheets, or promotional materials demonstrating local support.
