Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for your interest in the Community Resilience Grant. I appreciate the work you and your volunteers are doing to strengthen your community garden, and I am happy to clarify the eligibility criteria and application requirements for you.

**Auspice Arrangements**  
Unincorporated groups that operate under a formal auspice agreement with a local government entity are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing organisation (in your case, the local council) agrees to act as the legal applicant and to assume responsibility for the grant funds, reporting, and any contractual obligations. You will need to submit a signed auspice letter from the council confirming their support, the scope of the project, and their willingness to manage the grant on your behalf.

**Grant Size**  
The Community Resilience Grant programme has a minimum award of $5,000 and a maximum award of $50,000 per project. Applications are assessed on the merit of the proposed resilience outcomes, so the requested amount should be commensurate with the scale of the activities and the demonstrated need.

**Eligible Costs**  
Capital infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience—such as rainwater harvesting systems, shade structures, and related installation works—is explicitly listed as an eligible expense. Costs may include the purchase of tanks, fittings, shading materials, labour for installation, and any necessary engineering or design fees. Ongoing operational costs (e.g., routine maintenance, utilities) are not covered, but you may include a modest contingency (up to 10 % of the total budget) for unforeseen installation expenses.

**Supporting Documentation**  
To strengthen your application, please prepare the following items:

1. **Auspice Agreement** – a signed letter or memorandum of understanding from the local council outlining their role as the auspicing body.  
2. **Project Plan** – a concise description (max 2 pages) of the proposed rainwater tanks and shade structure, including site plans, timelines, and how the infrastructure will improve resilience to extreme heat and water scarcity.  
3. **Budget Breakdown** – itemised costs for each component (tanks, fittings, shade materials, labour, design, contingency) and any in‑kind contributions (e.g., volunteer labour, council‑provided site access).  
4. **Evidence of Need** – brief data or anecdotal information demonstrating the increasing heat stress on the garden and the current limitations of water supply.  
5. **Organisational Information** – although you are not a registered charity, provide a short profile of the garden (mission, volunteer base, weekly stall turnover, membership fees) and any relevant insurance certificates held by the council.  
6. **Letters of Support** (optional but recommended) – from community members, local schools, or other stakeholders who would benefit from the enhanced garden facilities.
