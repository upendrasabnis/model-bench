Thank you for reaching out and for the clear description of your project. I’m pleased to provide the following clarification on the points you raised, and I hope it will help you decide whether to proceed with a full application.  

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our Community Resilience Grant is designed to support projects that deliver clear public benefit, regardless of whether the applicant is a registered charity. Unincorporated groups are eligible provided they have a clearly identified “responsible entity” that can enter into a grant agreement, hold the funds, and be accountable for compliance. In practice, many successful applicants use a council or a parent organisation as the auspicing body for this very reason. Because your local council already provides the necessary insurances and can act as the fiscal sponsor, you should be able to submit an application under the council’s legal umbrella. The only stipulation is that the council must sign the grant agreement and be prepared to demonstrate oversight of the project.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
There is no fixed minimum or maximum grant size for this round. The grant pool is allocated on a project‑by‑project basis, and awards are determined by the overall merit of the proposal, the relevance to the grant’s objectives, and the proportion of eligible costs that the applicant can realistically fund. Historically, awards have ranged from roughly $5,000 to $50,000, but applications that demonstrate strong community impact and cost‑effectiveness can receive larger amounts if justified.  

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, capital infrastructure that directly supports the sustainability and operational resilience of community‑focused activities is an eligible cost. Rainwater tanks, shade structures, and associated installation work are considered permissible expenditures as they address the core challenge you identified—maintaining garden operations during hotter summer periods. In addition to the physical assets, you may also claim reasonable project‑management fees, contingency allowances, and monitoring‑evaluation costs.  

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:  

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem, objectives, timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Budget and Narrative Breakdown** – itemising each eligible cost (e.g., tank purchase, delivery, installation, design fees) with supporting quotations where possible.  
3. **Organizational Profile** – a brief overview of your group, its volunteer base, and the council’s role as auspice (including any partnership agreement).  
4. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or a set of accounts if unaudited) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
5. **Insurance Certificate** – confirmation that the council’s insurance covers the project activities, as required by the grant terms.  
6. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from local businesses, community organisations, or council representatives that attest to the project’s community value.  
7. **Risk Assessment and Management Plan** – outlining potential safety, environmental, or operational risks and how they will be mitigated.  
8. **Monitoring and Evaluation Plan** – a simple framework for measuring success (e.g., water savings, increased usage hours, community feedback).  

If you decide to move forward, please ensure that the council’s senior management signs the “Grant Agreement – Responsible Entity” form, which formalises their commitment to oversee the grant on your behalf.
