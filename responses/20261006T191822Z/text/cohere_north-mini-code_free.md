**Best Practices in Nonprofit Grant Writing**  
*A comprehensive guide from funder research to post‑award reporting*  

---

## 1. Introduction  
Grant writing is both an art and a science. Successful proposals demonstrate clear need, realistic goals, strong capacity, and rigorous evaluation. This article walks you through every stage of the grant‑writing cycle, offering concrete examples, checklists, and practical tips you can apply to any funding opportunity.

---

## 2. Understanding the Grant Landscape  

### 2.1 Why Funder Research Matters  
- **Alignment** – Matching your mission to a funder’s priority prevents wasted effort.  
- **Credibility** – Knowing a funder’s past grants shows you understand their language and expectations.  
- **Competitive edge** – Insight into a funder’s current priorities helps you tailor your story.  

### 2.2 How to Research Funders  
| Step | Action | Tools/Resources |
|------|--------|-----------------|
| **Identify** | Use the Foundation Center (now Candid) database, grant portals, and the nonprofit’s own funding calendar. | Candid.org, GrantStation, Instrumentl |
| **Review** | Examine recent grant announcements, annual reports, and strategic priorities. | Funder websites, PDFs of prior RFPs |
| **Map** | Create a matrix linking funder priorities to your program areas. | Excel/Google Sheets |
| **Network** | Attend webinars, conferences, and local grant‑maker meetings. | Local United Ways, Philanthropy Southwest |
| **Document** | Record deadlines, required formats, and special instructions. | Shared drive or project‑management tool (e.g., Asana) |

#### Practical Checklist: Funders Research  

- [ ] List all active RFP cycles (next 6‑12 months)  
- [ ] Note required documents (budget template, logic model, evaluation plan)  
- [ ] Capture funder’s preferred contact person and email  
- [ ] Summarize funder’s mission, priority themes, and geographic focus  
- [ ] Flag any “restricted” funding (e.g., only for certain states)  

### 2.3 Example: Funder Research Summary  

```
Funder: The Robert Wood Johnson Foundation – “Healthiest Communities”
Priority Areas: Reducing childhood obesity, expanding access to healthy foods
Recent Grants: $2.5M to “Urban Garden Projects” (2022), $1.8M to “School Nutrition Programs” (2023)
Application Deadline: March 15, 2025
Contact: Jane Doe, Program Officer, j.doe@rwjf.org
Special Requirements: Must include a logic model and a detailed evaluation plan.
```

## 3. Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement  

### 3.1 Elements of a Strong Needs Statement  
1. **Data‑Driven Evidence** – Use recent statistics, local health department reports, or peer‑reviewed studies.  
2. **Human Impact** – Brief anecdotes or case studies illustrate the problem beyond numbers.  
3. **Scope & Scale** – Clearly define the geographic area, population, and magnitude of the need.  
4. **Urgency** – Explain why the problem must be addressed now (e.g., rising rates, funding cuts).  

### 3.2 Example Needs Statement  

> “In City X, 28% of children ages 6‑11 are classified as obese—well above the national average of 18%. This trend is projected to increase 15% over the next five years without intervention. Our target community, the Riverside neighborhood, lacks safe walking routes to parks and affordable fresh‑produce markets, contributing to limited physical activity and poor nutrition. Immediate action is needed to prevent long‑term health complications and reduce future healthcare costs.”  

### 3.3 Checklist: Needs Statement  

- [ ] Cite the most recent data (within last 2 years)  
- [ ] Include at least one local statistic and one national benchmark  
- [ ] Provide a concise human story (1‑2 sentences)  
- [ ] Define the geographic and demographic scope  
- [ ] Explain why timing matters (trends, policy changes, etc.)

## 4. Defining Goals & Objectives  

### 4.1 SMART Objectives  
- **Specific** – Clear, unambiguous outcomes.  
- **Measurable** – Quantifiable indicators.  
- **Achievable** – Realistic given resources.  
- **Relevant** – Directly tied to the need.  
- **Time‑bound** – Defined period for completion.  

### 4.2 Example Objectives  

| Goal | Objective (SMART) | Indicator | Baseline | Target | Timeline |
|------|-------------------|-----------|----------|--------|----------|
| Increase physical activity among children | 80% of participating children will attend after‑school activity sessions at least 3 times per week. | Attendance logs | 0% | 80% | 6 months |
| Improve nutrition knowledge | 75% of parents will correctly answer ≥4 of 5 nutrition‑knowledge quiz items after the program. | Quiz scores | 30% correct | 75% correct | 4 months |
| Build community partnerships | Establish 3 new partnerships with local schools, parks, and grocery stores. | Partnership agreements signed | 0 | 3 | 3 months |

### 4.3 Checklist: Goals & Objectives  

- [ ] Each goal links directly to the needs statement  
- [ ] Objectives are SMART and quantifiable  
- [ ] Baseline data is documented  
- [ ] Targets are realistic and stretch the organization  
- [ ] Timeline aligns with grant period

## 5. Developing the Logic Model  

### 5.1 Logic Model Components  

1. **Inputs** – Resources (staff, budget, supplies).  
2. **Activities** – Concrete actions (workshops, outreach, trainings).  
3. **Outputs** – Immediate results (number of participants, materials distributed).  
4. **Outcomes** – Short‑ and long‑term changes (knowledge gain, behavior shift).  
5. **Impact** – Broader community effect (reduced obesity rates).  

### 5.2 Example Logic Model (Table)  

```
Inputs                     Activities                Outputs                         Outcomes                              Impact
$50,000 grant              Hire certified trainer    Conduct 8 after‑school          80% of children attend ≥3 sessions  15% reduction in childhood
2 staff FTE                Develop curriculum       programs                        Parents score 75% on nutrition quiz   obesity in Riverside by Year 2
Community center space     Distribute take‑home kits 1,200 take‑home kits            Children increase daily steps by 500   Improved community health metrics
```

### 5.3 Checklist: Logic Model  

- [ ] List all inputs (personnel, cash, in‑kind)  
- [ ] Define each activity clearly (who does what)  
- [ ] Quantify outputs (numbers, copies, contacts)  
- [ ] Specify outcome indicators (knowledge, behavior, attitudes)  
- [ ] Describe the ultimate impact (community‑level change)  
- [ ] Ensure the model is visual (flowchart or simple table) for reviewers

## 6. Program Description  

### 6.1 Key Sections  

| Section | Content | Tips |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Program Overview** | One‑paragraph summary of the problem and solution. | Use plain language; avoid jargon. |
| **Target Population** | Demographics, size, geographic location. | Provide a map or demographic chart if possible. |
| **Program Components** | Detailed description of each activity. | Use bullet points or numbered steps. |
| **Innovation** | What makes this approach unique. | Reference research, partnerships, or technology. |
| **Sustainability** | How the program will continue after the grant. | Include plans for funding, staffing, and community ownership. |

### 6.2 Example Program Description  

> **Program Overview:** The “Healthy Horizons” initiative addresses childhood obesity in Riverside by combining after‑school physical‑activity sessions with nutrition education.  
>   
> **Target Population:** 120 children ages 6‑11 from low‑income households in the Riverside neighborhood (population 4,500).  
>   
> **Program Components:** (1) Weekly 60‑minute activity sessions led by a certified PE instructor; (2) Bi‑weekly nutrition workshops for parents; (3) Distribution of weekly “Healthy Box” kits containing fresh produce and recipes.  
>   
> **Innovation:** The program integrates a mobile “food‑bank” truck that delivers produce directly to participants’ homes, reducing transportation barriers.  
>   
> **Sustainability:** After the grant ends, the program will be housed within the existing community center, with a modest fee‑based model and a volunteer parent‑lead team.  

### 6.3 Checklist: Program Description  

- [ ] Provide a clear, concise overview (≤150 words)  
- [ ] Identify target population size and characteristics  
- [ ] List each program component with frequency/duration  
- [ ] Highlight any unique features or partnerships  
- [ ] Outline post‑grant sustainability plan

## 8. Budget Development  

### 8.1 Budget Categories  

| Category | Description | Typical Line Items |
|----------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Personnel** | Salaries, benefits, fringe | Salary + 20% benefits |
| **Travel** | Transportation, mileage, participant travel | $0.56/mile, bus rentals |
| **Supplies** | Materials, kits, printed resources | $5 per take‑home kit |
| **Equipment** | Purchases, leases | $2,000 treadmill |
| **Overhead/Indirect** | Facility rent, utilities, admin costs | 10% of total budget |

### 8.3 Checklist: Budget

- [ ] Include a clear “direct vs. indirect” breakdown

- [ ] Provide a narrative explanation for each line item  
- [ ] Double‑check calculations and total consistency  
- [ ] Ensure budget aligns with funder’s maximum allowable indirect rate

## 10. Evaluation Plan  

### 10.1 Core Evaluation Components  

1. **Indicators** – What will you measure (knowledge, behavior, attendance)?  
2. **Data Sources** – Surveys, focus groups, administrative records.  
3. **Methods** – Pre‑/post‑tests, quantitative (statistics) and qualitative (themes).  
4. **Timeline** – When data will be collected (baseline, mid‑point, end).  
5. **Reporting** – Format and frequency of reports to the funder.  

### 10.2 Example Evaluation Plan  

> **Goal 1:** Increase nutrition knowledge among parents.  
> - **Indicator:** % of parents scoring ≥4/5 on a knowledge quiz.  
> - **Data Source:** Pre‑ and post‑program surveys.  
> - **Method:** 20‑question multiple‑choice quiz administered via SurveyMonkey.  
> - **Timeline:** Baseline (Month 1), Post‑program (Month 6).  
>   
> **Goal 2:** Increase physical activity participation.  
> - **Indicator:** % of children attending ≥3 sessions per week.  
> - **Data Source:** Attendance logs.  
> - **Method:** Automated tracking in a spreadsheet.  
> - **Timeline:** Ongoing, reported quarterly.  
>   
> **Goal 3:** Community impact.  
> - **Indicator:** Change in childhood obesity rate (measured via local health department data).  
> - **Data Source:** External health statistics.  
> - **Method:** Comparative analysis of pre‑ and post‑program data.  
> - **Timeline:** Baseline (Year 0), Follow‑up (Year 2).  

### 10.3 Checklist: Evaluation Plan  

- [ ] Each goal has a clear indicator  
- [ ] Data sources are realistic and accessible  
- [ ] Methods are described (survey tool, interview guide, etc.)  
- [ ] Collection schedule aligns with program timeline  
- [ ] Reporting format matches funder’s requirements (e.g., PDF, Excel)

## 11. Writing the Narrative (Storytelling & Alignment)  

### 11.1 Structure of the Narrative  

| Section | Content | Tips |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Opening Hook** | A vivid story or statistic that grabs attention. | Use a personal anecdote; keep it under 50 words. |
| **Problem Statement** | Expanded needs statement with local context. | Cite data, then human impact. |
| **Solution Overview** | Brief description of the program and why it works. | Emphasize evidence‑based practices. |
| **Implementation Plan** | Summarize activities, timeline, and capacity. | Reference the logic model. |
| **Evaluation & Impact** | Highlight expected outcomes and measurement. | Show how success will be tracked. |
| **Sustainability & Legacy** | Explain long‑term vision. | Connect to community ownership. |
| **Closing Call‑to‑Action** | Reiterate funding need and gratitude. | End with a strong, specific request. |

### 11.2 Example Narrative Excerpt  

> “In Riverside, one in four children battles obesity—a rate that has risen 12% over the past five years. Meet 9‑year‑old Maya, who once avoided vegetables because the only fresh produce she saw was wilted at the corner store. With our ‘Healthy Horizons’ program, Maya will receive weekly after‑school activity sessions and a take‑home “Healthy Box” stocked with crisp apples and carrots, while her parents attend nutrition workshops that equip them with practical cooking skills. By partnering with the community center, local schools, and a mobile produce truck, we will reach 120 families, increase physical‑activity participation to 80% of children, and boost parental nutrition knowledge by 45% within six months. Your investment today will set Maya and 119 peers on a healthier path—creating a ripple effect that reduces childhood obesity by 15% in Riverside within two years.”  

### 11.3 Checklist: Narrative Writing  

- [ ] Begin with a compelling hook (story or striking data)  
- [ ] Clearly state the problem and its magnitude  
- [ ] Describe the solution in plain language  
- [ ] Show how your organization is uniquely qualified  
- [ ] Reference the logic model and timeline briefly  
- [ ] Outline evaluation metrics and expected impact  
- [ ] End with a concise funding request and gratitude  
- [ ] Proofread for tone, voice, and readability (aim for 8th‑grade level)

## 12. Applying for Grants (Submission Guidelines & Deadlines)  

### 12.1 Common Application Requirements  

- **Executive Summary** (optional but useful)  
- **Organization Profile** (IRS status, 501(c)(3) certification)  
- **Budget Narrative** (detailed explanation of line items)  
- **Letters of Support** (partner agencies, community leaders)  
- **Compliance Documents** (certifications, conflict-of-interest forms)  

### 12.2 Example Submission Checklist  

- [ ] Complete all required fields in the online portal  
- [ ] Attach PDFs in the exact file naming convention (e.g., “Budget_HealthyHorizons_2025.pdf”)  
- [ ] Include a cover letter addressing the program officer by name  
- [ ] Proofread for spelling, grammar, and formatting consistency  
- [ ] Submit at least 24 hours before the deadline (to avoid technical glitches)  

### 12.3 Tips for Meeting Deadlines  

1. **Create a master calendar** with reminder dates for each funder’s deadline.  
2. **Batch similar tasks** (e.g., data collection, budget drafting) to avoid multitasking fatigue.  
3. **Use a “grant writing sprint” schedule** (e.g., outline day 1, draft day 2, review day 3).  
4. **Keep a “grant archive”** of previous successful proposals for quick reference.

## 13. Post‑Award Management  

### 13.1 Grant Agreement Essentials  

- **Scope of Work** – What activities are funded and any restrictions.  
- **Budget Terms** – Allowed expenditures, required invoices, and indirect cost caps.  
- **Reporting Obligations** – Frequency and format of progress and final reports.  
- **Audit Requirements** – Federal grants may require external audits.  

### 13.2 Financial Management Best Practices  

| Practice | Why It Matters | How to Implement |
|----------|----------------|------------------|
| **Separate Grant Bank Account** | Prevents commingling funds. | Open a dedicated checking account. |
| **Detailed Invoice Tracking** | Ensures compliance with funder rules. | Use accounting software (QuickBooks, NPO Corner). |
| **Monthly Budget Reviews** | Early detection of overspending. | Compare actuals vs. budget; adjust if needed. |
| **Receipt Retention** | Required for audit trails. | Scan and store all receipts in a secure folder. |

### 13.3 Reporting Timeline  

| Report Type | Due Date | Content Highlights |
|-------------|----------|--------------------|
| **Quarterly Progress Report** | End of each quarter | Activities completed, milestones achieved, challenges, budget status. |
| **Mid‑Term Review** | 6 months into project | Evaluation data update, revised outcomes, recommendations. |
| **Final Report** | End of grant period | Full narrative, outcomes, lessons learned, recommendations for scaling. |
| **Financial Report** | Usually with each progress report | Expense summary, actual vs. budget, receipts attached. |

### 13.4 Example Quarterly Report Structure  

1. **Executive Summary** – One‑page snapshot of progress.  
2. **Activities Implemented** – Bullet list with dates.  
3. **Milestones Achieved** – Reference timeline checklist.  
4. **Evaluation Data** – Updated indicators (e.g., attendance % after Q2).  
5. **Budget Status** – Total expenditures, remaining funds.  
6. **Challenges & Solutions** – Brief note on any obstacles and mitigation.  
7. **Next Steps** – Preview of upcoming quarter’s focus.  

### 13.5 Checklist: Post‑Award Management  

- [ ] Read and sign the grant agreement within the required timeframe  
- [ ] Set up a dedicated grant calendar (deadlines, reporting dates)  
- [ ] Implement financial controls (separate account, receipt policy)  
- [ ] Prepare quarterly reports in advance (leave buffer for reviewer feedback)  
- [ ] Conduct an internal audit before the funder’s audit deadline  
- [ ] Document lessons learned for future grant applications
