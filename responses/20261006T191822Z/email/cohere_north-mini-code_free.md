Thank you for reaching out and for the thoughtful description of the wonderful work your community garden is doing. I’m pleased to provide the following clarifications in response to the points you raised, and I hope they will help you decide whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from both incorporated not‑for‑profit entities and unincorporated groups that are formally auspiced—or “hosted”—by a local government body. When a council holds the necessary insurances, holds any required permits, and acts as the legal signatory for the project, the group can be considered an eligible applicant. In such cases, the council’s endorsement should be demonstrated through a formal letter of support that confirms it will assume liability for the grant funds and any project‑related obligations. Please note that the council’s involvement must be documented in the application’s organisational structure section.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not impose a strict minimum or maximum award size; instead, we assess each proposal against the merit of its outcomes, sustainability, and alignment with program priorities. Past awards have ranged from roughly $5,000 to $150,000, depending on the scale and complexity of the project. Your anticipated costs for rainwater tanks and a shade structure will be evaluated against the overall budget of the proposal, and we are happy to discuss realistic funding expectations during the application review stage.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes, capital infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures is expressly covered under the “Environmental Sustainability” component of the grant. These items are considered eligible provided they directly support the project’s resilience objectives—in your case, enhancing water security and reducing heat stress for garden users. We recommend that any infrastructure items be clearly linked to measurable outcomes (e.g., reduced water usage, increased crop yields during heat events) in your project narrative.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we suggest assembling the following materials:

1. **Project Proposal Form** – completed with all required fields, including a detailed timeline, budget breakdown, and outcome indicators.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – a brief description of the garden’s history, volunteer base, and community impact, accompanied by the council’s auspice letter.  
3. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and cash flow summary) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
4. **Insurance Confirmation** – a copy of the council’s insurance certificate confirming coverage for public liability and any works‑related risks.  
5. **Site Plans / Drawings** – sketches or scaled diagrams showing where the rainwater tanks and shade structure will be located.  
6. **Community Support** – letters of support from local residents, businesses, or other stakeholder groups, as well as any media coverage highlighting the garden’s role in the community.  
7. **Risk Assessment & Safety Plan** – documentation outlining how you will manage installation, operation, and maintenance safety, particularly given the council’s auspice.  

We also recommend reviewing the full grant guidelines on our website (Section III – Eligibility & Requirements) to ensure that any additional criteria—such as matching funds or reporting obligations—are addressed.
