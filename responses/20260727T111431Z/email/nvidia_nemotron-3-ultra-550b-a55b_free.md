Subject: Re: Question about eligibility for the Community Resilience Grant

Dear Jamie,

Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate you reaching out to confirm eligibility before investing time in a full application—this is exactly the kind of due diligence we encourage. Below, I address each of your questions in turn.

**Auspicing Arrangements & Eligibility**  
Yes, unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspicing agreement with an eligible legal entity—such as a local council—are eligible to apply. The council must be the legal applicant, sign the grant agreement, and assume financial and reporting responsibility for the project. Your arrangement, where the council holds the relevant insurances, meets this requirement provided a written auspicing agreement is in place that clearly outlines the roles, responsibilities, and liability provisions for both parties.

**Grant Amount Limits**  
For this round, the minimum grant request is $5,000 and the maximum is $50,000. Given your projected project scope, your request should fall comfortably within this band. Please note that we cannot fund more than 80 per cent of total eligible project costs; the remaining 20 per cent (or more) must be sourced from your own funds, in-kind contributions, or other grants.

**Eligible Infrastructure Costs**  
Capital infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience is an eligible cost category. Rainwater tanks and a permanent shade structure designed to mitigate heat impacts and ensure continued operation during extreme weather events are both considered eligible, provided they are fixed assets, comply with relevant Australian Standards and local planning requirements, and are supported by quotes from licensed suppliers. Portable or temporary structures (e.g., pop-up gazebos) are not eligible.

**Supporting Documents to Prepare**  
To avoid delays, please have the following ready for upload with your application:
1. Signed auspicing agreement between your group and the council (on council letterhead).  
2. Council’s current public liability and workers’ compensation certificates of currency.  
3. Two written quotes for each major infrastructure item (rainwater tanks, shade structure).  
4. Evidence of land tenure or a letter of support from the landowner confirming permission to install the infrastructure.  
5. A simple project budget showing total costs, the grant amount sought, and confirmed co-contributions.  
6. Your group’s most recent financial statement (even if unaudited) demonstrating the ~$40,000 annual turnover.  
7. A brief risk management plan addressing installation, maintenance, and ongoing operational risks.

The application portal opens on 1 June and closes at 5:00 pm AEST on 30 June. I strongly recommend starting the draft early so the council’s finance and governance teams have ample time to review and approve the submission.
