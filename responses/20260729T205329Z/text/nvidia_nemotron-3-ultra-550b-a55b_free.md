NONPROFIT GRANT WRITING BEST PRACTICES  
A COMPREHENSIVE GUIDE FROM RESEARCH TO POST‑AWARD REPORTING  

---  

SECTION 1 – UNDERSTANDING THE GRANT LANDSCAPE  

1.1 TYPES OF FUNDERS  
- Federal agencies (e.g., NIH, NSF, USDA)  
- State and local government programs  
- Private foundations (family, corporate, community)  
- Corporate giving programs  
- Individual donors and crowdfunding platforms  

1.2 FUNDING CYCLES  
- Annual, biennial, rolling deadlines  
- Letter of inquiry (LOI) vs. full proposal  
- Multi‑year awards and renewal processes  

1.3 KEY TERMINOLOGY  
- RFP (Request for Proposals)  
- NOFA (Notice of Funding Availability)  
- CFDA (Catalog of Federal Domestic Assistance) number  
- Indirect cost rate, cost share, matching funds  

CHECKLIST – QUICK FUNDER SNAPSHOT  
- Funder name and mission  
- Geographic focus  
- Programmatic priorities (current year)  
- Average award size and duration  
- Eligibility requirements (501(c)(3), fiscal sponsor, etc.)  
- Application format (online portal, PDF, paper)  
- Deadline dates (LOI, full proposal, reporting)  
- Contact person and preferred communication method  

---  

SECTION 2 – RESEARCHING AND QUALIFYING FUNDERS  

2.1 BUILD A FUNDER DATABASE  
- Use Foundation Directory Online, GrantStation, Guidestar, USAspending.gov  
- Tag each funder with: mission alignment, geographic fit, funding history, average grant size, application complexity  

2.2 ALIGNMENT MATRIX  
Create a spreadsheet with rows = funders, columns = your program areas. Score 1‑5 on fit. Prioritize scores ≥4.  

2.3 DEEP DIVE ON TOP 10 TARGETS  
- Read the last three years of awarded grants (abstracts, budgets)  
- Attend funder webinars, office hours, or site visits  
- Network with current grantees for insider tips  

2.4 QUALIFYING QUESTIONS (YES/NO)  
- Does the funder support the specific population we serve?  
- Is the grant size sufficient to cover core costs?  
- Are we eligible for indirect cost recovery?  
- Does the timeline match our program launch?  
- Is the reporting burden manageable?  

CHECKLIST – FUNDER QUALIFICATION  
[ ] Mission match ≥80%  
[ ] Geographic eligibility confirmed  
[ ] Award size meets minimum budget need  
[ ] Indirect cost policy compatible  
[ ] Reporting frequency ≤ quarterly  
[ ] Application deadline ≥ 8 weeks away  

---  

SECTION 3 – CRAFTING A COMPELLING NEEDS STATEMENT  

3.1 PURPOSE OF THE NEEDS STATEMENT  
- Demonstrates urgency and relevance  
- Connects data to the funder’s priorities  
- Sets the stage for your solution  

3.2 DATA SOURCES  
- Census Bureau, American Community Survey  
- State health department dashboards  
- Local school district reports  
- Peer‑reviewed literature (cite DOI)  
- Your own program evaluation data  

3.3 STRUCTURE  
1. Hook – a vivid anecdote or striking statistic (1‑2 sentences)  
2. Scope – prevalence, demographics, trends (3‑4 sentences)  
3. Gap – what existing services miss (2‑3 sentences)  
4. Consequence – impact on individuals/community if unaddressed (2 sentences)  
5. Alignment – explicit link to funder’s stated goals (1 sentence)  

3.4 EXAMPLE  
Hook: “In 2023, 1 in 4 children in Riverbend County entered kindergarten without basic literacy skills.”  
Scope: “County‑wide assessments show a 15% decline in early‑literacy scores over five years, disproportionately affecting low‑income families (U.S. Census, 2022).”  
Gap: “Only two community‑based tutoring programs exist, serving 120 children annually, leaving an estimated 1,200 eligible children unserved.”  
Consequence: “Research links early‑literacy deficits to a 30% higher high‑school dropout rate (National Center for Education Statistics, 2021).”  
Alignment: “The Smith Foundation’s 2024 Early Childhood Education Initiative prioritizes scalable, evidence‑based literacy interventions in underserved rural counties.”  

CHECKLIST – NEEDS STATEMENT QUALITY  
[ ] Opens with a compelling hook  
[ ] Uses at least three recent, credible data points  
[ ] Clearly defines the service gap  
[ ] States measurable consequences  
[ ] Directly references funder’s priority language  
[ ] ≤ 350 words (or funder’s limit)

SECTION 4 – DEVELOPING PROGRAM DESIGN: LOGIC MODEL & THEORY OF CHANGE  

4.1 LOGIC MODEL COMPONENTS  
- Inputs (resources)  
- Activities (what you do)  
- Outputs (tangible products)  
- Outcomes (short, medium, long term)  
- Impact (systemic change)  

4.2 BUILDING THE MODEL  
1. List all inputs (staff, volunteers, space, technology, cash).  
2. Map each activity to an input.  
3. Define measurable outputs (e.g., “120 tutoring sessions delivered”).  
4. Write outcome statements using “increase/decrease” + metric + timeframe.  
5. Connect outcomes to the funder’s impact goals.  

4.3 THEORY OF CHANGE NARRATIVE  
- One‑paragraph story linking inputs → activities → outcomes → impact.  
- Include assumptions and external factors.  

4.4 EXAMPLE LOGIC MODEL (TEXTUAL)  
Inputs: 2 FTE literacy coaches, 500 books, $30,000 grant, community center space.  
Activities: Recruit 30 volunteer tutors; conduct bi‑weekly 1‑hour tutoring; host monthly family literacy nights.  
Outputs: 720 tutoring hours delivered; 30 family events held; 150 children enrolled.  
Short‑term Outcomes (0‑6 months): 80% of enrolled children show ≥10% improvement on DIBELS benchmark.  
Medium‑term Outcomes (6‑18 months): 60% of participants meet grade‑level reading proficiency.  
Long‑term Outcomes (18‑36 months): 20% reduction in district‑wide kindergarten readiness gap.  
Impact: Community achieves sustained literacy gains, informing county policy.  

CHECKLIST – LOGIC MODEL VALIDATION  
[ ] Every activity has a corresponding input  
[ ] Outputs are countable and time‑bound  
[ ] Outcomes are SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time‑bound)  
[ ] Assumptions are explicitly listed  
[ ] Model fits on one page (or funder’s required format)

SECTION 5 – WRITING THE PROJECT NARRATIVE  

5.1 COMMON SECTIONS (FUNDER‑SPECIFIC)  
- Executive Summary  
- Organizational Capacity  
- Problem Statement / Needs Assessment  
- Goals & Objectives  
- Methods / Implementation Plan  
- Evaluation Plan  
- Sustainability Plan  
- Budget Narrative  

5.2 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (ONE PAGE)  
- One sentence mission alignment  
- One sentence problem  
- One sentence solution  
- Total request amount and period  
- Key outcomes (2‑3 bullet points)  

5.3 ORGANIZATIONAL CAPACITY  
- Governance (board expertise)  
- Staff qualifications (include bios ≤ 150 words each)  
- Past grant performance (highlight 2‑3 relevant awards)  
- Financial health (audit opinion, reserve ratio)  

5.4 GOALS & OBJECTIVES  
- Goal: broad, visionary statement.  
- Objectives: 3‑5 SMART objectives per goal.  

5.5 METHODS / IMPLEMENTATION PLAN  
- Timeline (Gantt style or table) with milestones.  
- Staffing plan (who does what, % FTE).  
- Partnerships (MOUs, letters of support).  
- Risk mitigation (table: risk, likelihood, impact, mitigation).  

5.6 EVALUATION PLAN  
- Formative (process) indicators: attendance, fidelity checks.  
- Summative (outcome) indicators: pre/post tests, surveys.  
- Data collection tools, responsible staff, analysis schedule.  
- Use of external evaluator if required.  

5.7 SUSTAINABILITY PLAN  
- Diversified revenue (earned income, individual giving, other grants).  
- Institutionalization (policy adoption, integration into school curriculum).  
- Capacity building (staff training, volunteer pipeline).  

CHECKLIST – NARRATIVE COMPLETENESS  
[ ] Executive summary ≤ 1 page  
[ ] All required headings present  
[ ] Objectives are SMART and numbered  
[ ] Timeline covers entire grant period  
[ ] Evaluation includes both process and outcome measures  
[ ] Sustainability addresses at least two revenue streams  
[ ] Language mirrors funder’s terminology

SECTION 6 – BUDGET DEVELOPMENT AND NARRATIVE  

6.1 BUDGET CATEGORIES (COMMON)  
- Personnel (salaries, fringe)  
- Fringe benefits (health, retirement)  
- Travel (local, conference)  
- Equipment (≥ $5,000 per item)  
- Supplies (consumables)  
- Contractual / Subawards  
- Other (rent, utilities, printing)  
- Indirect Costs (if allowed)  

6.2 BUILDING THE BUDGET  
1. Start with the logic model – each activity drives a cost line.  
2. Use actual salary scales, current fringe rates, and documented quotes.  
3. Apply the funder’s indirect cost rate or negotiated rate.  
4. Include a 5‑10% contingency only if permitted.  
5. Ensure total matches the request amount exactly.  

6.3 BUDGET NARRATIVE TIPS  
- Explain each line item in plain language.  
- Justify any cost > $5,000 or > 10% of total budget.  
- Show cost‑share or matching funds separately.  
- Reference the narrative sections (e.g., “Personnel costs support Objective 1.2”).  

6.4 EXAMPLE BUDGET NARRATIVE EXCERPT  
Personnel – Project Director (0.5 FTE, $45,000/yr): Oversees all program operations, ensures fidelity to the logic model, and serves as primary liaison with the funder.  
Fringe – 30% of salaries: Includes health insurance, retirement, payroll taxes per organizational policy.  
Supplies – 500 leveled readers @ $12 each = $6,000: Directly supports tutoring sessions (Activity 2).  

CHECKLIST – BUDGET ACCURACY  
[ ] All line items tie to activities in the logic model  
[ ] Salaries reflect current organizational pay scale  
[ ] Fringe rate matches approved rate or funder’s cap  
[ ] Indirect cost rate applied correctly  
[ ] No unexplained “miscellaneous” line > 5% of total  
[ ] Total request equals sum of line items (no rounding errors)

SECTION 7 – PREPARING SUPPORTING DOCUMENTS  

7.1 LETTERS OF SUPPORT / MEMORANDA OF UNDERSTANDING  
- One page each, on partner letterhead.  
- State specific role, commitment (e.g., “Provide 200 sq. ft. space at no cost”).  
- Signed by authorized representative.  

7.2 ORGANIZATIONAL DOCUMENTS  
- IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter  
- Most recent audited financial statements  
- Board roster with affiliations  
- Current strategic plan (excerpt)  

7.3 RESUMES / BIOS  
- Limit to 2 pages per key staff.  
- Highlight grant‑relevant experience.  

7.4 EVALUATION TOOLS  
- Survey instruments, assessment rubrics, data‑sharing agreements.  

CHECKLIST – ATTACHMENTS COMPLETE  
[ ] All required letters attached (minimum 3)  
[ ] IRS letter current (≤ 5 years old)  
[ ] Audited financials ≤ 12 months old  
[ ] Bios for all personnel listed in budget  
[ ] Evaluation instruments appended

SECTION 8 – SUBMISSION STRATEGY  

8.1 TIMELINE MANAGEMENT  
- 12 weeks before deadline: finalize logic model, assign writers.  
- 8 weeks: draft narrative sections, circulate for internal review.  
- 6 weeks: complete budget, obtain finance sign‑off.  
- 4 weeks: collect letters, finalize attachments.  
- 2 weeks: full proposal review (content, compliance, formatting).  
- 1 week: submit early to avoid portal issues.  

8.2 COMPLIANCE CHECK  
- Page limits, font, margins, file naming conventions.  
- Required certifications (e.g., lobbying, debarment).  
- Electronic signature requirements.  

8.3 INTERNAL REVIEW PANEL  
- Program director, finance officer, evaluation lead, executive director.  
- Use a rubric aligned with funder’s scoring criteria.  

8.4 SUBMISSION LOG  
- Record date, time, confirmation number, portal screenshot.  

CHECKLIST – PRE‑SUBMISSION  
[ ] All narrative sections within page limits  
[ ] Budget totals match request amount  
[ ] All required signatures obtained  
[ ] Files named per funder convention (e.g., “OrgName_ProjectName_Budget.pdf”)  
[ ] Confirmation receipt saved  

---  

SECTION 9 – POST‑AWARD MANAGEMENT  

9.1 AWARD ACCEPTANCE  
- Review award agreement for special conditions.  
- Sign and return within funder’s deadline.  
- Set up grant‑specific accounting code.  

9.2 FINANCIAL TRACKING  
- Separate ledger for grant funds.  
- Monthly reconciliation against budget.  
- Track cost‑share contributions in real time.  

9.3 PROGRAMMATIC MONITORING  
- Weekly staff check‑ins on activity milestones.  
- Quarterly data pull for evaluation indicators.  
- Adjust work plan with funder approval if needed.  

9.4 REPORTING REQUIREMENTS  
- Financial reports (usually quarterly).  
- Performance reports (semi‑annual or annual).  
- Final report (narrative + financial + outcomes).  

9.5 REPORT WRITING BEST PRACTICES  
- Use the same logic model language.  
- Present data in tables/figures with clear captions.  
- Highlight deviations, lessons learned, and corrective actions.  
- Include success stories (quotes, photos with releases).  

9.6 CLOSE‑OUT  
- Submit final financial report with zero balance.  
- Return any unexpended funds if required.  
- Archive all grant documents for 3‑7 years per funder policy.  

CHECKLIST – POST‑AWARD COMPLIANCE  
[ ] Grant accounting code established  
[ ] Monthly financial reconciliation completed  
[ ] Quarterly program data uploaded to funder portal  
[ ] Reports submitted by due dates (no late submissions)  
[ ] All communications with funder logged  
[ ] Close‑out package prepared 30 days before grant end  

---  

SECTION 10 – CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT & KNOWLEDGE MANAGEMENT  

10.1 DEBRIEF AFTER EACH GRANT CYCLE  
- Convene “lessons learned” meeting (30‑45 minutes).  
- Capture: what worked, what didn’t, timeline bottlenecks, funder feedback.  

10.2 UPDATE TEMPLATES  
- Revise logic model template, budget workbook, narrative boilerplate.  
- Store in shared drive with version control.  

10.3 BUILD A GRANT LIBRARY  
- Archive successful proposals (redacted).  
- Tag by funder, program area, award size.  

10.4 STAFF DEVELOPMENT  
- Annual grant‑writing workshop (internal or external).  
- Cross‑train program staff on budget basics.  

10.5 METRICS FOR GRANT OFFICE PERFORMANCE  
- Proposals submitted vs. funded (win rate).  
- Average time from RFP release to submission.  
- Reporting on‑time rate.  

CHECKLIST – CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT  
[ ] Debrief scheduled within 2 weeks of award decision  
[ ] Templates updated and versioned  
[ ] Grant library searchable by keyword  
[ ] Training calendar published for next fiscal year  
[ ] Performance dashboard reviewed quarterly by leadership
