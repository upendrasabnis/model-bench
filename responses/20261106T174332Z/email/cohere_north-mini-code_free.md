**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines state that applicant organizations must be either incorporated non‑profit entities or unincorporated groups that have a formal auspicing arrangement with a public or private entity capable of acting as the fiscal sponsor. An unincorporated group that operates “under the auspices of our local council” generally meets this criterion, provided the council can demonstrate that it will hold legal responsibility for the grant funds, maintain the required insurances, and submit the necessary reporting on your behalf. To confirm eligibility, we will need a letter from the council’s senior management confirming the auspicing relationship, outlining the council’s authority to administer grant funds, and stating that all required insurances are held in the council’s name. If such a letter is not already prepared, please ask the council to draft one and include it in your application package.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a single fixed award size. Instead, grants are awarded on a competitive basis within a typical range of **$5,000 to $50,000**, depending on the scope of the proposed work, the demonstrated community impact, and the availability of funds in the current round. While many successful projects fall between $10,000 and $30,000, applications that clearly justify higher expenditures are also considered. There is no upper ceiling beyond the total budget allocated for the round, but proposals that exceed $50,000 will require additional justification and may be subject to a more rigorous review.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs (rainwater tanks and shade structure)**  
Infrastructure that directly enhances the long‑term resilience and operational capacity of a community facility is fully eligible under the “Community Resilience” theme. Specifically, rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures that reduce water consumption, mitigate heat stress, and enable year‑round garden operations are considered allowable expenditures. However, the costs must be itemized in the project budget, and you should be prepared to demonstrate how each component aligns with the grant’s objectives (e.g., reduced reliance on municipal water, increased safety for volunteers and visitors, and climate‑adaptation benefits). If any portion of the infrastructure is intended for commercial sale or profit‑generating activities, those elements will need to be excluded from the grant request.

**Supporting documents we recommend preparing**  
1. **Project Narrative** – A concise description of the problem, proposed solution, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – Itemized costs for the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary supplies, with a clear distinction between capital and recurring expenses.  
3. **Proof of Insurance** – A copy of the council’s insurance certificate confirming coverage for public liability and equipment.  
4. **Auspice Letter** – As mentioned, a formal letter from the council confirming the sponsorship arrangement and fiscal responsibilities.  
5. **Financial Statements** – Most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a set of simplified financial statements) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover and the sources of income.  
6. **Letters of Support** – At least two letters from community stakeholders, local businesses, or other organizations endorsing the project.  
7. **Site Plans or Sketches** – Visual representations of where the tanks and shade structure will be installed.  
8. **Risk Assessment and Safety Plan** – Documentation outlining how the installation will be managed safely, particularly given the volunteer nature of the group.
