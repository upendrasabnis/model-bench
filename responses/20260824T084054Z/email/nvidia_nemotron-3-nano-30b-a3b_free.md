Thank you for reaching out and for the detailed overview of your community garden project. I’m pleased to provide clarification on the points you raised regarding the Community Resilience Grant (CRG).  

**1. Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The CRG accepts applications from organisations that are “formally auspiced” by a local government authority, provided the auspicing council assumes responsibility for the group’s financial and legal obligations. While the group itself does not need to be a registered charity, it must be listed on the council’s official auspice register and have a written agreement that outlines the council’s oversight, insurance coverage, and reporting requirements. If your garden is already covered under such an arrangement, you meet the eligibility criteria.  

**2. Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The current funding round allocates grants ranging from **$5,000 to $25,000**. Applications that request less than $5,000 are considered ineligible, while projects seeking up to $25,000 may be funded, subject to the overall budget and competitive assessment. Your projected request for rainwater tanks and a shade structure falls comfortably within this band.  

**3. Eligibility of the proposed infrastructure**  
Infrastructure that enhances the resilience of community facilities—such as water‑capture systems, shading, and ventilation improvements—is expressly eligible under the CRG’s “Built Environment” category. Both rainwater tanks and a shade structure are considered permissible capital works, provided they are directly linked to climate‑adaptation outcomes (e.g., water security during heat events).  

**4. Supporting documentation**  
To strengthen your application, please include the following:  

- A copy of the council’s auspice agreement or letter confirming oversight.  
- Evidence of your current insurance coverage (the council’s policy details are sufficient).  
- A detailed project budget that breaks down costs for the tanks, structure, site preparation, and any professional fees.  
- A risk‑assessment and maintenance plan that demonstrates how the works will improve resilience.  
- Letters of support from at least two community stakeholders (e.g., the council, local schools, or neighbouring businesses).
