**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. Unincorporated groups that operate under the auspices of a local government entity are indeed eligible, provided that the council can demonstrate its support through a formal auspice agreement (or equivalent documentation) and that it assumes responsibility for any legal or financial liabilities, including insurance coverage. In your case, the council’s provision of insurance should satisfy this requirement. We also recommend that you include a letter from the council confirming the auspice relationship, as this will be a key element of the eligibility evidence we review.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed grant size. Awards are determined on the basis of the overall project budget, the degree of community benefit, and the availability of funds in the current round. Historically, grants have ranged from approximately $5,000 to $50,000, but applications with larger capital needs are considered on a case‑by‑case basis. If your total project cost (including the rainwater tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary works) falls within the overall funding envelope for the round, there is scope for a grant that reflects a substantial portion of the expenditure.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, capital infrastructure that directly supports the sustainability and operational continuity of community assets is an eligible cost. Specifically, rainwater harvesting equipment (tanks, gutters, filtration, and associated plumbing) and permanent shade structures (e.g., pergolas, shade sails, or covered seating areas) are explicitly listed under the “Environmental Resilience” and “Infrastructure Improvement” categories of our guidelines. These items must be essential to the project’s objectives, reasonably priced, and installed in accordance with relevant building and environmental regulations.

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem you are addressing (e.g., heat stress, water scarcity), the proposed solution, and the expected outcomes for the community.  
2. **Budget and Financial Statements** – a detailed breakdown of all costs (materials, labour, contingency), together with the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and cash‑flow summary). Your current annual turnover of $40,000 will be useful for demonstrating financial viability.  
3. **Auspice Agreement** – a formal letter from the council confirming the partnership, outlining the council’s responsibilities regarding insurance, governance, and any required reporting.  
4. **Insurance Certificate** – confirmation that the council holds appropriate public liability and property insurance covering the project site.  
5. **Site Plans and Technical Specifications** – scaled drawings or photographs of the garden layout, together with technical data sheets for the proposed tanks and shade structure (size, capacity, materials, compliance certifications).  
6. **Community Support** – letters of endorsement from local residents, businesses, or other stakeholder groups, and evidence of volunteer involvement (e.g., a brief profile of key volunteers).  
7. **Implementation Timeline** – a Gantt‑style chart or bullet‑point schedule outlining key milestones, from procurement through installation to post‑completion monitoring.  
8. **Sustainability and Maintenance Plan** – a brief outline of how the garden will maintain and monitor the new infrastructure over the long term, including any training or education components.
