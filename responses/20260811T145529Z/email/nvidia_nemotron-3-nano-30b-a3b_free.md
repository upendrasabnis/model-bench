Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the detailed overview of your community garden project. I am pleased to confirm that your organisation may be eligible for the Community Resilience Grant, and I will address each of your questions in turn.

**1. Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our funding guidelines allow applications from unincorporated community groups provided they are formally auspiced by a local government authority that holds the necessary insurances and can demonstrate fiscal oversight. Since your garden operates under the auspices of the regional council and the council holds the requisite public liability coverage, you meet the primary eligibility criterion. However, the application must be submitted on the council’s official letterhead, signed by an authorised council officer, and must include a copy of the council’s insurance certificate.

**2. Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant offers tiered funding: the minimum award is **$5,000**, and the maximum is **$25,000** per project. Projects that demonstrate a clear link to climate‑adaptation or disaster‑risk reduction may be considered for the upper end of the range, especially when the budget includes matching contributions from other sources.

**3. Eligibility of proposed infrastructure**  
Both rainwater harvesting systems and shade structures are classified as eligible capital expenditures under the “Infrastructure for Resilience” category, provided they are directly related to mitigating climate‑related risks (e.g., water security during heatwaves). Detailed cost estimates, supplier quotations, and a maintenance plan are required to substantiate these items.

**4. Supporting documentation**  
To strengthen your application, please include the following:  

- A signed letter of endorsement from the council confirming the auspice arrangement and insurance coverage.  
- A copy of the council’s public liability insurance certificate.  
- A detailed project budget (including line‑item costs for tanks, shade structure, installation, and contingency).  
- Quotes or pro‑forma invoices from suppliers.  
- A risk‑assessment and maintenance plan that outlines how the infrastructure will be sustained after the grant period.  
- Evidence of your annual turnover and financial statements for the past two fiscal years.  
- A concise project narrative (maximum 1,000 words) that explains the community need, expected outcomes, and how the project aligns with the grant’s resilience objectives.
