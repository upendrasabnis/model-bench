## 2. What Is Acquittal Reporting?

### Definition, Purpose, and Scope

An **acquittal**, also called a **grant closeout report** or **final report**, is a formal submission that confirms the completion of a project and the sound use of its funds. It typically includes:

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Narrative | Project goals, outcomes, and lessons learned |
| Financial | Detailed accounting of expenditures versus budget |
| Technical | Evidence of deliverables, performance metrics, and data |
| Compliance | Confirmation of policy adherence (e.g., conflict‑of‑interest, procurement) |

### When Does an Acquittal Begin?

- **Mid‑Project:** Many funders require interim reports; these are precursors to the final acquittal.
- **Project End:** The formal acquittal is due within a specified window after the project’s conclusion (often 30–90 days).

## 6. Audit Processes

### 6.1 Internal Audits

- **Frequency**: Quarterly or semi‑annual.
- **Scope**: Financial accuracy, internal controls, compliance.
- **Outcome**: Audit report with findings and corrective actions.

### 6.2 External Audits

- **Independent Auditors**: CPA firms, government auditors.
- **Scope**: Full financial statements, compliance checks.
- **Requirement**: Often a prerequisite before submitting an acquittal.

### 6.3 Government‑Directed Audits

- **Agency Audits**: e.g., U.S. Office of Management and Budget (OMB) or Canada’s Treasury Board.
- **Focus**: Cost‑share verification, compliance with federal regulations.
- **Documentation**: Provide “audit trail” spreadsheets, supporting documents, and a signed audit opinion.

## 7. Common Compliance Failures

| Category | Example | Why It Happens |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| **Cost Misallocation** | Treating a community outreach event as a direct program expense | Lack sano understanding of categorization rules |
| **Missing Receipts** | Claiming a vendor payment without a signed receipt | Overreliance on oral agreements or digital invoices |
| **Untracked Sub‑Awards** | Failing to report a subcontractor’s expenses | No system to capture sub‑award financials |
| **Late Submission** | Sending an acquittal 45 days after deadline | Poor project close‑out planning |
| **Inadequate Narrative** | Generic “project was successful” with no data | Underestimating the importance of evidence |
| **Data Privacy Violations** | Sharing unredacted participant data in the report | Lack of data‑protection policy |
| **Inconsistent Currency Conversion** | Mixing USD and CAD without conversion rates | No standardized conversion process |

### Lessons Learned

1. **Start Early:** Begin the close‑out process at the project’s midpoint.
2. **Centralize Records:** One repository for all financial and narrative documents.
3. **Automate Alerts:** Set calendar reminders for key dates.
4. **Train Staff:** Regular workshops on funder requirements and internal controls.

## 9. Real‑World Case Studies

### 9.1 Case Study 1: Federal Health Initiative (USA)

- **Funder:** NIH
- **Project:** Community‑Based Hypertension Screening
- **Issue:** Failure to properly classify “community outreach” expenses as indirect costs.
- **Outcome:** NIH required a corrective action plan and deducted the misclassified amount.
- **Lesson:** Understand the OMB circulars and indirect cost rate agreements.

### 9.2 Case Study 2: State Education Grant

- **Funder:** California Department of Education
- **Project:** STEM After‑School Program
- **Issue:** Late submission of the final report due to a data migration error.
- **Outcome:** The grant was still honored, but the institution was flagged for future compliance monitoring.
- **Lesson:** Implement a data migration checklist before final submission.

### 9.3 Case Study 3: Philanthropic Foundation

- **Funder:** Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation
- **Project:** Rural Water Infrastructure
- **Issue:** Inadequate evidence of sustainability plan in the narrative.
- **Outcome:** Foundation withheld a portion of the final payment pending a revised plan.
- **Lesson:** Embed sustainability metrics from project inception.

### 9.4 Case Study 4: Corporate Sponsorship

- **Funder:** Salesforce Community Foundation
- **Project:** Digital Literacy Workshops
- **Issue:** Incorrect use of brand assets in the final report (violated brand guidelines).
- **Outcome:** Rebranding required; no penalty but lost goodwill.
- **Lesson:** Review brand guidelines meticulously before submission.
