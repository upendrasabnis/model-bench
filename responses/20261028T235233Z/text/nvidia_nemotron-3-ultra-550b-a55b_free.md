# The Architecture of Accountability: A Master Guide to Grant Compliance & Acquittals

## Part I: Foundations — Defining the "Acquittal" and the Compliance Lifecycle

### 1.1 What Is an Acquittal? Beyond the Dictionary Definition
In the grants ecosystem, **acquittal** is the formal, evidentiary process by which a recipient demonstrates to a funder that:
1.  **Funds were spent** strictly in accordance with the approved budget and grant agreement.
2.  **Activities were delivered** as described in the project plan (scope, timeline, geography).
3.  **Outcomes were achieved** (or valid reasons for variance are documented).
4.  **Legal and regulatory obligations** (insurance, WHS, Modern Slavery, Indigenous procurement targets) were met.

**Crucial Distinction:** An acquittal is not a receipt dump. It is a **forensic reconciliation** linking three distinct ledgers:
*   **The Financial Ledger** (General Ledger trial balance for the grant cost center).
*   **The Activity Ledger** (Milestone reports, KPI dashboards, participant attendance logs).
*   **The Evidence Ledger** (Invoices, contracts, timesheets, bank statements, photos, third-party verification).

### 1.2 The Compliance Lifecycle: A 5-Stage Model
Compliance is not an end-of-year event. It is a continuous control environment.

| Stage | Phase | Core Compliance Action | Risk if Skipped |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Pre-Award** | Negotiate achievable KPIs; clarify "eligible expenditure" definitions; set up Chart of Accounts (CoA) mapping. | "Mission drift" budget; ineligible costs baked in. |
| **2** | **Onboarding / Setup** | Configure finance system (project codes, dimension tags); draft *Compliance Calendar*; assign RACI matrix. | Data fragmentation; missed reporting deadlines. |
| **3** | **Active Delivery** | Monthly "shadow acquittals" (internal reconciliation); variance analysis; document retention discipline. | Year-end surprise deficits; lost receipts. |
| **4** | **Reporting / Acquittal** | Final financial statement prep; performance narrative drafting; independent audit engagement (if required). | Qualified audit opinion; clawback demands. |
| **5** | **Post-Acquittal** | Lessons learned workshop; archive retention trigger start; funder relationship maintenance. | Repeat errors; reputational damage for future rounds. |

## Part II: The Taxonomy of Requirements — Government vs. Philanthropic

While the *principles* of accountability are universal, the *mechanics* differ radically. Understanding the "Funding DNA" dictates your compliance architecture.

### 2.1 Government Funders (Federal, State/Provincial, Local)
**DNA:** Statutory authority, public money stewardship, auditability by Supreme Audit Institutions (e.g., ANAO, GAO, NAO).

#### Common Non-Negotiable Requirements:
1.  **Legislative Frameworks:** Grants are often governed by specific Acts (e.g., *Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013 (PGPA Act)* in Australia; *Uniform Guidance 2 CFR 200* in US Federal awards).
2.  **Standard Grant Agreements:** Rigid, boilerplate clauses (Cl. 10–15 typically cover reporting, audit rights, IP, privacy, insurance).
3.  **Acquittal Formats:** Mandatory templates (Excel workbooks, portal-based forms). *Deviation is usually rejected automatically.*
4.  **Audit Thresholds:**
    *   **US (2 CFR 200.501):** Single Audit required if >$750k federal expended/year.
    *   **Australia:** Tiered: Tier 1 (>$500k) = Independent Audit Report; Tier 2 ($50k–$500k) = Accountant’s Review; Tier 3 (<$50k) = Statutory Declaration.
    *   **UK:** Charity Commission thresholds / Grant maker specific.
5.  **Eligible Expenditure Rules:** Strict definitions (e.g., "No capital expenditure," "Admin capped at 10%," "No alcohol/gifts"). *Real World:* A US nonprofit lost $120k in federal funding for charging "staff morale pizza parties" to a grant where "food" was restricted to "client-facing workshop catering only."
6.  **Procurement Rules:** Mandatory competitive tendering thresholds (e.g., 3 quotes >$10k; open tender >$80k). Conflict of Interest (COI) registers mandatory for all procurement.
7.  **Specific Policy Mandates:** Indigenous Procurement Policy (IPP), Local Jobs First, Modern Slavery Act reporting, Child Safe Standards, Work Health Safety (WHS) compliance flow-down.

#### Real-World Scenario: The "Indirect Cost" Trap
> **Entity:** Mid-sized NGO receiving a $2M State Government service contract.
> **Failure:** They applied their federally negotiated NICRA (Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate Agreement) of 28% to the State grant.
> **Clause:** State agreement explicitly stated: *"Overheads/Administration capped at 7% of direct salary costs."*
> **Result:** $420k in claimed indirect costs disallowed. Organization had to repay from unrestricted reserves. **Lesson:** Never assume portability of indirect cost rates. Read the *Specific Conditions* schedule.

---

### 2.2 Philanthropic & Corporate Funders (Foundations, Trusts, CSR)
**DNA:** Donor intent, strategic alignment, relationship-based, flexibility (within guardrails).

#### Common Requirements:
1.  **Grant Agreement / Letter of Agreement:** Shorter, often negotiable. Focus on *outcomes* over *line-item compliance*.
2.  **Narrative-Heavy Reporting:** "Impact Reports" > Financial Acquittals. They want stories of change, photos, case studies.
3.  **Flexible Budgeting:** "Budget variations >10% require notification" (vs. government "prior written approval").
4.  **Restricted vs. Unrestricted:** Critical distinction.
    *   *Project Grant:* Acquit against project budget.
    *   *General Operating Support (GOS):* Acquit against organizational audit/990/Annual Report. **No project-level acquittal required.**
5.  **Multi-Year "Check-ins":** Instead of annual acquittals, often require "Progress Reports" to release Year 2/3 tranches.
6.  **Evaluation Requirements:** Increasingly common: "Participatory Evaluation," "Developmental Evaluation," or funding an external evaluator (budgeted separately).
7.  **Recognition/Branding:** Logo usage, annual report listing, site visit hosting.

#### Real-World Scenario: The "Funder Collaboration" Conflict
> **Entity:** Arts organization funded by three foundations for a single festival.
> **Funder A:** Requires separate bank account for grant funds.
> **Funder B:** Requires funds deposited into main operating account for cash flow.
> **Funder C:** Requires acknowledgment as "Principal Partner" exclusive of other funders.
> **Resolution:** Organization negotiated a **Master Funding Agreement** side letter. Funds pooled in main account (satisfying B), but internal sub-ledger tracking created "virtual sub-accounts" for A. Funder C received "Lead Artistic Partner" title (distinct from "Principal"). **Lesson:** Philanthropic compliance is a negotiation; document the *agreed* workaround in writing.

### 2.3 The Hybrid Nightmare: Blended Funding
Most complex organizations run blended models.
**The Golden Rule:** **Cost Allocation Methodology (CAM) must be documented, board-approved, and consistently applied.**
*   *Direct Costs:* Charged 100% to specific grant (e.g., Project Officer salary @ 1.0 FTE).
*   *Shared Direct Costs:* Allocated via driver (e.g., Rent allocated by % of FTE headcount per program).
*   *Indirect/Overhead:* Allocated via approved rate (e.g., 15% of Total Direct Costs).

**Failure Mode:** "Double-dipping" overhead. Charging rent as a direct cost to Grant A *and* including rent in the overhead base for Grant B.
**Control:** Maintain a **Cost Allocation Policy Manual** updated annually. Test it during monthly shadow acquittals.

## Part III: Record-Keeping — The Evidence Engine

### 3.1 The "Golden Thread" Principle
Every dollar reported in the Final Acquittal Financial Statement must be traceable in **< 3 clicks** from the summary cell to the source document (Invoice -> Bank Statement -> GL Entry -> Acquittal Schedule).

### 3.2 Document Taxonomy & Retention Matrix

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Source Financial** | Invoices, Receipts, Bank Statements, Payroll Journals, Timesheets | **7 Years** (Standard: US IRS, AU ATO, UK HMRC) | Digital (PDF/A), OCR'd, tagged with Grant Code + Account Code |
| **Contractual** | Grant Agreement, Variations, Sub-contractor Agreements, MOUs | **7 Years** post-contract expiry | Contract Management System (e.g., Gatekeeper, Ironclad) + Drive backup |
| **Governance** | Board Minutes approving budget/acquittal, COI Registers, Delegation Registers | **Permanent / 7 Years** | Board Portal (Diligent, BoardPro) |
| **Operational / Activity** | Attendance sheets, Survey raw data, Workshop photos, Training materials | **5–7 Years** (Check funder spec) | Project Mgmt Tool (Asana, Monday) / SharePoint structured folders |
| **Audit & Compliance** | Audit Management Letters, Audit Reports, Funder Correspondence | **7 Years** | Secure "Audit Vault" folder (Read-only for finance/auditors) |
| **HR / Personnel** | Employment contracts (grant-funded roles), Police Checks, WWCC/Blue Cards | **7 Years** post-employment end | HRIS (BambooHR, Employment Hero) — *Privacy Act/GDPR compliant* |

## Part IV: The Audit — Surviving the External Scrutiny

### 4.1 Audit Types & Triggers
| Audit Type | Trigger | Scope | Who Performs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Statement Audit** | Org revenue > threshold (e.g., $1M AU / $500k US) | Whole Entity FS | CPA/CA Firm |
| **Grant-Specific Acquittal Audit** | Funder mandate (Tier 1/High Value) | Single Grant Cost Center | CPA/CA Firm (often specified by funder) |
| **Single Audit (US) / OMB A-133** | >$750k Federal Expenditure | Major Programs + Internal Controls | CPA Firm (Yellow Book standards) |
| **Performance/Value-for-Money Audit** | Funder / Auditor General discretion | Efficiency, Effectiveness, Economy | Supreme Audit Institution / Internal Audit |
| **Probity/Compliance Review** | High risk / Whistleblower / Random | Specific clauses (Procurement, COI, WHS) | Funder Internal Audit / Specialist Firm |

### 4.2 Preparing the "Audit Pack" (PBC List)
Auditors issue a **Prepared by Client (PBC)** list. Treat this as a project plan.
**Standard PBC Items for a Grant Acquittal Audit:**
1.  **Final Trial Balance** for Grant Cost Center (mapped to Acquittal template).
2.  **General Ledger Detail** (Full transaction dump for period).
3.  **Reconciliation Workpapers:** Bank Rec, Payroll Rec, Sub-ledger to GL Rec.
4.  **Sample Selection Support:** Population lists for Payroll, Procurement, Travel (Auditor picks samples; you pull files).
5.  **Fixed Asset Register** (if CapEx purchased).
6.  **Commitments/Accruals Schedule** (Goods received not invoiced).
7.  **Related Party Transaction Register.**
8.  **Budget vs. Actual Variance Analysis** (with commentary for >10% variances).
9.  **Funder Correspondence File** (Approvals for variations, extensions).
10. **Management Representation Letter** (Draft for auditor review).

### 4.3 The "Management Letter" — Your Improvement Roadmap
The **Management Letter (or "Points Raised" letter)** is more valuable than the Audit Opinion.
*   **Clean Opinion + Heavy Management Letter** = High risk of future failure.
*   **Track every point:** Assign Owner, Due Date, Status in a **Audit Findings Register**.
*   **Root Cause Analysis:** Don't just fix the instance (e.g., "missing receipt for $45"). Fix the *system* (e.g., "Implemented mandatory receipt capture via Expensify app before reimbursement").

## Part V: The "Hall of Shame" — Top 10 Compliance Failures & Mitigation Strategies

Based on audit reports (ANAO, GAO, Charity Commission) and funder feedback sessions, these are the recurring catastrophic failures.

### Failure 1: The "Salary Sacrifice / On-Cost" Black Hole
*   **The Error:** Budgeting "Salary $80k" but actual cost is $80k + 10.5% Super + 1.5% Workers Comp + 4.75% Payroll Tax + Leave Accruals = **$93.4k**. Grant runs 15% over budget on personnel.
*   **Root Cause:** Budget built on "Base Salary" only; Payroll system posts on-costs to different GL codes not mapped to grant.
*   **Fix:** **Budget on "Total Cost of Employment (TCE)."** Map *all* payroll GL codes (Gross, Super, WC, Tax, Leave Provision) to the Grant Cost Center dimension.

### Failure 2: Timesheet Theatre (The "100% Allocation" Myth)
*   **The Error:** Program Manager works 50% Grant A / 50% Grant B. Timesheets show 100% Grant A for 6 months, then 100% Grant B. Or, timesheets submitted monthly but approved quarterly.
*   **Funder View:** Fraud indicator. *2 CFR 200.430* requires "After-the-fact" records reflecting *actual* activity.
*   **Fix:**
    *   Implement **daily/weekly timesheet entry** (not monthly recall).
    *   Use **system-enforced allocation** (e.g., Deputy, Tanda, ADP) where % split is pre-set but editable daily.
    *   **Monthly Payroll Reconciliation:** Payroll Officer verifies Timesheet Hours = Paid Hours = Grant Allocation % *before* journal posting.

### Failure 3: Procurement "Splitting" to Avoid Tender Thresholds
*   **The Error:** Need $90k website. Split into 3 invoices: $30k Design, $30k Build, $30k SEO. All awarded to same vendor without tender. Threshold is $50k.
*   **Legal Risk:** Criminal offense in some jurisdictions (e.g., *Crimes Act* provisions on fraudulent schemes); automatic disallowance.
*   **Fix:** **Procurement Plan** approved at project start. Aggregate spend tracking per vendor per project. "Single Source Justification" form required *before* spend if competitive process not used.

### Failure 4: The "In-Kind" Valuation Fantasy
*   **The Error:** Claiming $50k "Volunteer Time" at $50/hr (corporate rate) for unskilled packing boxes. Claiming "Free Rent" at commercial market rate for a dilapidated shed.
*   **Standard:** **Fair Value / Replacement Cost.** Volunteers valued at *replacement cost* (what you'd pay to hire someone), not *opportunity cost*. Rent valued at *market rent for comparable condition/location*.
*   **Fix:** **In-Kind Valuation Policy.** Pre-approve rates with funder. Use Volunteering Australia / Independent Sector (US) standard hourly rates. Get independent valuation for rent/goods >$10k.

### Failure 5: Capital Expenditure Disguised as Operating
*   **The Error:** Buying a $15k vehicle, $8k laptop suite, or building a shed under "Equipment Hire" or "Materials."
*   **Rule:** Asset capitalization threshold (e.g., >$5k / >$10k) + Useful Life > 1 year = **Capital**. Requires specific CapEx approval. Depreciation ≠ Acquittal Expense.
*   **Fix:** **Pre-Purchase Approval Workflow.** Any PO >$1k routed to Finance for "CapEx vs OpEx" classification *before* order placed.

### Failure 6: The "Unspent Funds" Liability Mismanagement
*   **The Error:** Grant ends June 30. $50k cash left in bank. Org spends it on general ops in July. Funder demands return in August.
*   **Accounting Standard:** Unspent restricted funds = **Contract Liability (Deferred Revenue)**, not Revenue.
*   **Fix:** **Monthly Liability Reconciliation.** Track "Funds Received vs. Expenditure Incurred." Cash ≠ Revenue. Forecast burn rate monthly. Apply for variation/extension *before* year-end if underspent.

### Failure 7: Sub-Recipient / Sub-Contractor Blindness
*   **The Error:** Passing $200k to a partner org. No sub-agreement, no flow-down clauses (audit rights, insurance, compliance), no monitoring. Partner misspends. Prime recipient liable.
*   **Regulatory:** **2 CFR 200.331 (US) / PGPA Rule 11 (AU)** – Pass-through entity responsible for sub-recipient compliance.
*   **Fix:** **Sub-Recipient Management Framework:**
    1.  Risk Assessment (High/Med/Low).
    2.  Sub-Agreement with Flow-Down Clauses.
    3.  Monitoring: Desk review of *their* acquittal/audit; Site visit (High risk); Financial capacity check.

### Failure 8: Conflict of Interest (COI) "File and Forget"
*   **The Error:** Board member owns IT firm. Org awards $40k contract without tender. COI declared in annual register, but *not* at procurement decision point. No minutes showing recusal.
*   **Fix:** **Real-Time COI Protocol.**
    *   Register updated *before* every procurement >$5k.
    *   Meeting minutes must record: "Director X declared interest, left room, did not vote."
    *   Independent probity advisor for high-value/related-party procurements.

### Failure 9: Data Integrity — The "Spreadsheet Spaghetti"
*   **The Error:** Acquittal prepared in Excel. Hard-coded numbers. Broken links to source. Version 12_Final_v3_REAL.xlsx used. Numbers don't tie to GL.
*   **Fix:** **Single Source of Truth (SSOT).**
    *   Acquittal template **linked live to GL** (PowerQuery / ODBC / API) OR
    *   Strict **Control Total Reconciliation** page: `GL Total = Acquittal Schedule Total = Bank Payments Total`. Variance must = $0.00.

### Failure 10: Narrative / Financial Disconnect
*   **The Error:** Financial acquittal shows $100k spent on "Workshops." Narrative reports "10 workshops delivered." Unit cost = $10k. Funder knows market rate is $2k. Or: Narrative claims "500 participants," attendance sheets show 50.
*   **Fix:** **Integrated Reporting Workshop.** Finance + Program Managers co-draft acquittal. Cross-reference every KPI to a financial line item. *Unit Cost Analysis* mandatory for final report.

## Part VI: Operationalizing Compliance — The "Shadow Acquittal" Methodology

### 6.1 What is a Shadow Acquittal?
A **monthly internal rehearsal** of the final acquittal. You populate the funder’s exact acquittal template (or your master internal version) with Year-to-Date (YTD) actuals every month.

### 6.2 The Monthly Shadow Acquittal Workflow (5-Day Close)

| Day | Activity | Owner | Output |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **D+1** | **Hard Close:** All AP invoices posted; Payroll finalized; Bank recs done. | Finance Team | Clean GL |
| **D+2** | **Data Pull:** Export Grant TB; Export Payroll detail; Export CRM KPI data. | Finance / Data Analyst | Raw Data Pack |
| **D+3** | **Reconciliation:** Map GL to Acquittal Lines. Flag variances >10% or $5k. Check "Unbilled/Accrued" items. | Grant Accountant | **Reconciliation Workpaper** (Signed off) |
| **D+4** | **Narrative Draft:** Program Manager writes YTD progress against KPIs; explains variances. | Program Manager | **Variance Commentary Doc** |
| **D+5** | **Review & Sign-off:** Finance Manager reviews financials; CEO/ED reviews narrative. File saved to Audit Vault. | Finance Mgr / ED | **Monthly Shadow Acquittal Pack** (Archived) |

### 6.3 Benefits
1.  **No Year-End Crunch:** Final acquittal = Copy/Paste June Shadow Acquittal + Final Audit Adjustments.
2.  **Early Warning:** Detect underspend/overspend in Month 3, not Month 12.
3.  **Audit Ready:** Auditors can test *monthly* controls, not just year-end balance.
4.  **Funder Confidence:** You can send a "Quarterly Health Check" to funder proactively (builds trust for variations).

## Part VIII: Special Scenarios — Advanced Compliance Challenges

### 8.1 Grant Variations & Scope Changes
*   **Never** spend outside scope hoping for retrospective approval.
*   **Process:** Variation Request Form -> Budget Reforecast -> Funder Written Approval (Email/Letter/Portal) -> **Update Shadow Acquittal Budget Column** -> Proceed.
*   **Trap:** "No-cost extensions" (Time only). Still requires formal deed of variation. Insurance/Workers Comp must cover extended period.

### 8.2 Foreign Currency & International Grants
*   **Exchange Rate Policy:** Spot rate on invoice date? Rate on payment date? Monthly average? **Pick one, document it, apply consistently.**
*   **Bank Fees:** International transfer fees ($25–$50) are often *eligible* admin costs. Capture them.
*   **Sanctions Screening:** Mandatory OFAC (US), DFAT (AU), UK Sanctions List checks on all foreign vendors/partners *before* payment.

### 8.3 Disaster / Crisis Funding (e.g., COVID, Floods, Bushfires)
*   **Speed vs. Compliance:** Funders often relax *procurement* rules (emergency sole source) but **tighten *reporting* rules** (weekly sit-reps).
*   **Segregation:** **Separate Cost Center / Bank Account** for disaster funds. Do not mix with BAU.
*   **Eligibility Window:** Strict "Incident Period" dates. Costs incurred 1 day outside = Ineligible.

### 8.4 Capital Grants / Infrastructure (The 10-20 Year Liability)
*   **Acquittal ≠ End.** Capital grants often carry **Asset Register Obligations** for 10–20 years.
*   **Requirements:** Asset cannot be sold/leased/demolished without funder consent. Insurance must note funder interest. Annual "Asset Condition Report" may be required.
*   **Accounting:** Recognize Revenue over Asset Useful Life (Deferred Capital Grant Liability), not on receipt.

*   Anonymous channel (e.g., YourCall, Stopline, internal email) for reporting grant fraud, coercion to falsify timesheets, or procurement corruption.
*   **Protected Disclosure Policy** must be visible and trusted.

## Part X: The Final Checklist — "Ready to Submit" Protocol

Before hitting "Submit" on the portal or emailing the PDF, the **Accountable Officer (CEO/Finance Director)** must physically tick these:

**Financial Integrity**
- [ ] **Trial Balance = Acquittal Schedule** (To the cent).
- [ ] **Bank Statements** reconcile to "Grant Receipts" and "Grant Payments" lines.
- [ ] **Payroll Reconciliation** complete: Gross Pay + On-costs = Acquittal Salary Lines.
- [ ] **No Double Counting:** Expenses not claimed elsewhere (e.g., other grants, core funding).
- [ ] **Capital Items** correctly treated (Expensed vs. Capitalized per policy).
- [ ] **In-Kind** valued per agreed policy; supported by letters/valuations.
- [ ] **Unspent Funds** correctly shown as Liability (not Revenue); Return/Variation plan attached.

**Evidence Completeness**
- [ ] **Sample Test:** Can I find the invoice for Line 402 (Travel) in < 60 seconds?
- [ ] **Procurement Files:** Quotes/Contracts/COIs exist for all spend >$5k (or funder threshold).
- [ ] **Sub-Recipient Acquittals** received, reviewed, and consolidated.
- [ ] **Insurance Certificates** current for grant period (PL, PI, WC, Volunteer).

**Narrative & KPI Alignment**
- [ ] **Output KPIs** match financial scale (e.g., $100k spent / 10 workshops = $10k unit cost logic holds).
- [ ] **Variance Commentary** provided for *every* line >10% variance (Underspend *and* Overspend).
- [ ] **Qualitative Data** de-identified (Privacy Act/GDPR compliant).
- [ ] **Funder Recognition** evidence attached (Photos of signage, screenshots of socials, annual report page).

**Governance & Legal**
- [ ] **Board Minutes** record approval of Final Acquittal (if required by constitution/funder).
- [ ] **Authorized Signatory** matches Grant Agreement Schedule.
- [ ] **Audit Report** attached (if threshold met) — *Unqualified opinion preferred.*
- [ ] **Management Letter** response attached (if prior year findings existed).
- [ ] **Privacy / Data Sovereignty** check: No client PII in acquittal submitted to portal.

**Housekeeping**
- [ ] **File Naming:** `Funder_GrantID_FinalAcquittal_YYYYMMDD_v1_SIGNED.pdf`
- [ ] **Backup:** Copy saved to Audit Vault / Offsite / Cloud Archive.
- [ ] **Calendar:** Next reporting deadline / Audit engagement letter date diarized.

## Conclusion: Compliance as a Strategic Asset

Grant compliance is frequently viewed as a "cost center" — a burden of bureaucracy. This is a strategic error.

**Organizations that master the "Architecture of Accountability" unlock three compounding advantages:**

1.  **Risk Immunity:** They survive audits, investigations, and funding audits without reputational scarring or financial clawbacks.
2.  **Funder Trust Capital:** Funders talk. A reputation for "clean, early, insightful acquittals" leads to **unrestricted funding, multi-year renewals, and invitation-only rounds.** Funders fund *low administrative burden* partners.
3.  **Operational Intelligence:** The discipline of monthly shadow acquittals, rigorous cost allocation, and integrated narrative/financial reporting produces **better management decisions.** You know *exactly* what your programs cost per unit of impact.

The acquittal is not the receipt for the money spent. It is the **certificate of competence** for the organization. Build the systems, enforce the discipline, and treat every grant — whether $5,000 or $5,000,000 — as a contract of trust that your systems are designed to honor automatically.

### Appendix: Quick Reference Glossary

*   **Acquittal:** Formal verification of expenditure and performance against grant terms.

*   **Deferred Revenue / Contract Liability:** Unspent grant funds sitting on Balance Sheet (not P&L).
*   **Eligible Expenditure:** Costs explicitly allowed by funder guidelines/agreement.
*   **Flow-Down Clauses:** Terms in Prime Grant Agreement mandated to be included in Sub-contracts.
*   **General Ledger (GL) / Trial Balance (TB):** Core accounting records.
*   **In-Kind Contribution:** Non-cash contributions (volunteers, donated goods, rent-free space) valued at Fair Value.
