Dear Jamie,  

Thank you for reaching out and for the opportunity to clarify the eligibility criteria for our Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate the thoughtful nature of your inquiry, and I am happy to address each of the points you raised.  

**1. Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. Unincorporated groups that operate under the auspices of a local government entity are indeed eligible, provided that the council can demonstrate its support for the project (for example, through a formal letter of endorsement, a memorandum of understanding, or a statement confirming that the council will hold the relevant insurances and liabilities). The council’s involvement is often viewed positively, as it indicates an established relationship with local authorities and can help mitigate risk for the grant‑making agency.  

**2. Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award amount. Instead, we assess each proposal on its merits, the clarity of its outcomes, and the proportion of the requested funds relative to the overall project budget. Historically, awards have ranged from approximately $10,000 to $150,000, but we are prepared to consider requests outside this band if the justification is compelling and the funding is proportionate to the project’s scale and impact.  

**3. Eligibility of infrastructure costs such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Infrastructure that enhances the sustainability, resilience, or operational capacity of a community project is expressly eligible. Rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures clearly align with the grant’s objectives of building community resilience in the face of climate‑related challenges (e.g., water security and heat stress). These capital items are considered allowable costs, provided they are directly attributable to the project’s aims and are adequately justified in the budget breakdown.  

**4. Suggested supporting documents**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend preparing the following documentation:  

- **Project description and business plan** – a concise narrative outlining the problem you aim to address, the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline for implementation.  
- **Budget and cost breakdown** – a detailed spreadsheet itemising each expense, including purchase, installation, and any consultancy fees.  
- **Letter of endorsement from the council** – confirming that the council will act as the auspicing body, hold insurance, and support the project’s implementation.  
- **Organizational profile** – a brief overview of your group’s volunteers, activities, and any past projects, even if the group is unincorporated.  
- **Financial statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and cash flow summary) demonstrating your $40,000 annual turnover.  
- **Risk assessment and mitigation plan** – outlining potential challenges (e.g., site access, maintenance) and how you will manage them.  
- **Community support evidence** – letters of support or attendance records from local residents, businesses, or stakeholder groups.  

We also advise reviewing the full grant guidelines on our website, as they contain additional requirements such as reporting obligations, monitoring visits, and any matching‑fund expectations. Should you decide to proceed with a full application, we will be happy to provide a checklist to ensure no essential component is omitted.
