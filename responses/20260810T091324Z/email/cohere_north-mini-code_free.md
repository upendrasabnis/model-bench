Thank you for reaching out with your questions about the Community Resilience Grant. I’m pleased to provide clarification on the points you raised, and I hope this information helps you decide whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility for unincorporated groups**  
Our grant programme is open to a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations, and unincorporated groups are indeed eligible provided they have an appropriate auspice arrangement. In your case, having your local council act as the fiscal host and holder of the required insurances satisfies the requirement that the applicant be a legally‑recognised entity capable of entering into contractual agreements and accounting for public funds. The council’s involvement will be documented in a formal auspice agreement, which should outline the responsibilities of each party, the use of grant funds, and the reporting obligations. If you have not yet drafted such an agreement, we can share a template that many successful applicants have used.

**Grant amount – minimum and maximum**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a minimum award; however, we typically see applications ranging from $5,000 to $75,000. The maximum amount that can be requested is $75,000 per fiscal year, and the final award will be determined by the merit of the project, its alignment with programme objectives, and the availability of funding. Your projected costs for rainwater tanks and a shade structure appear to fall comfortably within this range, and we would expect a request of roughly $30,000–$40,000 to be competitive.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Both rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures are considered eligible capital improvements under the “Climate‑Resilient Community Infrastructure” component of the grant. The key criteria are that the assets are permanent, directly support the organisation’s core activities (in your case, the continuation of the garden during hot periods), and provide measurable resilience benefits. We have funded similar projects in the past, and the cost breakdown should include the tank capacity, installation, any necessary plumbing, and the structural design, materials, and foundations for the shade canopy.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend compiling the following documentation:

1. **Project Narrative** – a clear description of the problem (increasing summer heat), the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and how the project aligns with the grant’s resilience objectives.  
2. **Budget and Cost Breakdown** – itemised quotes for tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary costs (e.g., permits, landscaping).  
3. **Letters of Support** – at least two endorsements from community members, local businesses, or partner organisations, confirming the need and anticipated impact.  
4. **Auspice Agreement** – a signed agreement with your council detailing their role as fiscal sponsor, insurance coverage, and reporting responsibilities.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for a small operation, a simple cash‑flow summary) demonstrating your $40,000 turnover and the sources of income.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Insurance Certificate** – confirmation that the council’s insurance meets the programme’s liability requirements.  
7. **Implementation Timeline** – a Gantt‑style schedule outlining key milestones, from procurement to completion, with realistic dates.  

We also advise that you review the full grant guidelines on our website (specifically the “Eligibility and Application Requirements” section) to ensure you meet any additional criteria, such as demonstration of community participation or alignment with local climate‑adaptation plans.
