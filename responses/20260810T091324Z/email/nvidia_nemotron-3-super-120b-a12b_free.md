**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The Community Resilience Grant is open to incorporated not‑for‑profit organisations, registered charities, and unincorporated groups that are formally auspiced by an eligible entity such as a local government council, a state‑based peak body, or another incorporated organisation that can accept legal and financial responsibility for the grant. Your arrangement—operating under the auspices of your regional council, which already holds the necessary public liability and volunteer insurances—meets this requirement. You will need to provide a letter of auspice from the council confirming that they agree to act as the legal and financial holder of any funds awarded and that they will oversee acquittal reporting.

**Grant size**  
For the current funding round, the minimum grant amount is $5,000 and the maximum is $50,000 (GST exclusive). Applications are assessed on a merit basis, and the award size is matched to the scope and demonstrated need of the proposed project. Given your estimated turnover of $40,000 and the nature of the works you describe, a request in the mid‑range (e.g., $15,000–$25,000) would be typical, but you are welcome to propose any amount within the stated limits that reflects the true cost of the rainwater tanks and shade structure.

**Eligible costs**  
Capital infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience to climate‑related stresses is an eligible expense. This includes the purchase and installation of rainwater harvesting tanks, associated plumbing, and the construction or purchase of a shade structure (e.g., a pergola, shade sail, or permanent roofing). Operational costs such as ongoing maintenance, utilities, or staff salaries are not covered, but you may include a modest contingency (up to 10 % of the total budget) for unforeseen installation expenses.

**Supporting documentation**  
To substantiate your application, please prepare the following items:

1. **Auspice letter** from the council (on official letterhead) stating their agreement to act as the legal and financial sponsor, confirming insurance coverage, and nominating a council contact person.  
2. **Project plan** (max 2 pages) outlining the objectives, anticipated climate‑resilience benefits, a timeline for procurement and installation, and how the garden will continue to operate during hotter months.  
3. **Detailed budget** (using the template provided in the application portal) showing itemised costs for tanks, fittings, labour, shade structure, any required council permits, and the contingency amount. Include quotes or indicative prices from at least two suppliers where possible.  
4. **Evidence of community support** (e.g., letters of endorsement from regular volunteers, a summary of membership numbers, or photos of the weekly produce stall) to demonstrate the garden’s ongoing activity and local impact.  
5. **Financial statement** of the garden’s recent income and expenditure (a simple profit‑and‑loss summary covering the last 12 months) to show the $40,000 turnover figure you mentioned.  
6. **Proof of council insurance** (certificate of currency) covering public liability and volunteer injury for the garden’s activities.
