Thank you for reaching out and for the opportunity to clarify the eligibility criteria for our Community Resilience Grant. I’m pleased to share that unincorporated community groups that operate under the auspices of a local council are indeed eligible to apply. The council’s involvement can serve as the primary applicant, with the garden’s steering committee acting as the implementing partner, and the council’s existing insurance and risk‑management arrangements typically satisfy the grant’s liability requirements.  

Regarding funding limits, our Community Resilience Grant is designed to be flexible: applications may request any amount up to $150,000, though the majority of awards fall within the $30,000‑$80,000 range for projects of this scale. There is no stipulated minimum grant size; however, we generally expect applicants to demonstrate that the requested amount represents a substantial proportion of the total project cost.  

Infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting tanks and permanent shade structures is expressly listed as an eligible expense under the “Climate‑Resilient Infrastructure” category of the grant. These items must be directly attributable to the project’s resilience objectives, provide measurable benefits (e.g., reduced water consumption, increased operating days during heat events), and be installed on the garden’s premises.  

When preparing your supporting documentation, we recommend the following:  

1. **Project Plan** – a one‑page narrative outlining the problem, proposed solution, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Budget Breakdown** – itemized costs for tanks, shade structure, installation, and any ancillary supplies, with quotations where possible.  
3. **Council Support Letter** – a formal statement from your local council confirming that they will retain insurance coverage and any necessary permits.  
4. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and cash‑flow summary) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover figure.  
5. **Organizational Overview** – a brief description of the garden’s volunteer composition, governance structure, and any past community impact.  
6. **Risk Assessment** – a concise document outlining how the project will be managed in terms of health, safety, and environmental risk.  

Please note that while we can provide this guidance, final eligibility will be confirmed during the formal application review process. If you decide to proceed, the full application portal will walk you through the exact documentation required and any additional forms that may be needed.  

We encourage you to submit a draft application for our review before the final deadline; this can help ensure that your proposal aligns with our funding priorities and maximises its chances of success.
