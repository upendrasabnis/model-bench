## 1. Introduction: The Lifecycle of Trust

Grant funding is not a gift; it is a contract. Whether the source is a federal agency distributing taxpayer dollars, a state government administering a community infrastructure program, or a private foundation deploying endowed assets, the fundamental transaction is identical: **Resources are exchanged for specific, measurable outcomes within a defined legal and ethical framework.**

**Acquittal reporting** is the formal mechanism by which the recipient (the grantee) demonstrates to the provider (the grantor) that this contract has been honored. It is the closing of the loop. Yet, in the non-profit, research, and public sectors, acquittal is frequently treated as an administrative afterthought—a frantic scramble of receipts and narrative summaries conducted in the two weeks before a deadline.

This reactive approach is dangerous. It exposes organizations to financial liability (clawbacks), reputational damage (debarment or public findings), and operational paralysis (funding freezes on current or future awards).

This article treats compliance not as a burden, but as a core operational competency. We will dissect the anatomy of the acquittal, contrast the divergent worlds of government and philanthropic reporting, build the infrastructure for "audit-ready" record-keeping, and provide a strategic roadmap to avoid the compliance failures that sink organizations.

## 2. Defining the Acquittal: More Than a Receipt

### 2.1 Etymology and Legal Basis
The term "acquit" derives from the Old French *acquiter* (to settle a claim) and Latin *quietare* (to set at rest). In grant management, **to acquit is to formally discharge a liability.** The liability is the obligation to spend funds *exactly* as described in the Grant Agreement (or Deed of Grant, Contract, Award Letter).

An acquittal is a **legal attestation**. When an authorized officer signs an acquittal report, they are making a sworn declaration—often under penalty of perjury or specific statutory false-statement provisions—that:
1.  The financial statement is true and complete.
2.  Funds were used solely for the approved purpose.
3.  All conditions precedent (milestones, matching funds, insurance, permits) were met.
4.  Assets purchased with grant funds are accounted for and titled correctly.

### 2.2 The Three Dimensions of Acquittal
A complete acquittal addresses three distinct dimensions. Failure in any one dimension constitutes a compliance breach.

| Dimension | Core Question | Primary Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial (Probity)** | *Was the money spent legally and accurately?* | General Ledger (GL) extracts, bank statements, invoices, payroll registers, asset registers. |
| **Performance (Fidelity)** | *Did we do what we said we would do?* | KPI dashboards, milestone reports, evaluation data, beneficiary counts, deliverables (reports, software, buildings). |
| **Governance (Regularity)** | *Did we follow the rules of the road?* | Conflict of interest registers, procurement policies, board minutes, insurance certificates, sub-grant agreements. |

### 2.3 Acquittal vs. Progress Reporting
A critical distinction often blurred by grantees:
*   **Progress Reports** are *formative*. They occur mid-stream (quarterly, semi-annually). They inform the funder of trajectory and allow for course correction (variance approvals, scope changes).
*   **Acquittal Reports** are *summative*. They occur at the end (or at defined milestone gates). They are final, binding legal documents. **You cannot "correct" an acquittal easily once submitted.** A progress report says "We are on track"; an acquittal says "We have arrived."

## 3. The Regulatory Landscape: Government vs. Philanthropic Frameworks

The compliance burden varies radically by funder type. Understanding the *source of authority* dictates the rigor of your response.

### 3.1 Government Grants (Federal/National, State/Provincial, Local)
**Authority:** Statute, Regulation, Uniform Guidance (e.g., 2 CFR 200 in US), Financial Management Acts, Procurement Acts.
**Philosophy:** **Stewardship of Public Funds.** The default answer to "Can I do this?" is "No, unless explicitly permitted."
**Key Characteristics:**
*   **Cost Principles:** Strict definitions of Allowable, Allocable, Reasonable, and Consistent treatment (e.g., 2 CFR 200 Subpart E).
*   **Indirect Costs (F&A):** Negotiated rates (NICRA) or de minimis (10% MTDC). Strict separation of direct vs. indirect.
*   **Procurement Standards:** Full and open competition thresholds (e.g., micro-purchase <$10k, simplified acquisition <$250k, formal procurement >$250k in US). Sole source justifications require heavy documentation.
*   **Audit Mandates:** Single Audit Act (US) / Uniform Guidance Subpart F (expenditure >$750k/yr triggers entity-wide audit).
*   **Flow-Down Clauses:** Prime recipients *must* push federal clauses (Davis-Bacon, Buy America, FFATA reporting, Suspension/Debarment checks) to sub-recipients and contractors.
*   **Consequence of Failure:** Disallowances (clawbacks), suspension/debarment (exclusion from future funding), False Claims Act liability (treble damages + penalties), criminal referral.

**Real-World Example: The "Unallowable Cost" Trap**
A US university receives an NIH grant. The PI charges a $5,000 "celebration dinner" for the lab team to the grant as "Meals/Entertainment." During a Single Audit, the auditor disallows the cost. Because the university’s indirect cost rate proposal includes this department’s base, the finding cascades: the cost is removed from the direct base, the indirect recovery on that $5k is recalculated, and the university owes the federal government ~$7,500 (Direct + Indirect). The finding is reported on the Schedule of Findings and Questioned Costs (SF-SAC), publicly accessible via the Federal Audit Clearinghouse.

### 3.2 Philanthropic Grants (Private Foundations, Corporate Foundations, High-Net-Worth Individuals)
**Authority:** Contract Law (Grant Agreement), IRS Code (Private Foundation Excise Tax rules / Expenditure Responsibility), Donor Intent.
**Philosophy:** **Impact Alignment & Donor Intent.** The default answer is "Yes, if it advances the mission and follows the agreement."
**Key Characteristics:**
*   **Flexibility:** Often allow "General Operating Support" (unrestricted) or flexible line items. Budget deviations >10-15% usually require a simple email, not a formal amendment.
*   **Expenditure Responsibility (US Context):** If a private foundation grants to a non-501(c)(3) (e.g., a for-profit social enterprise, a foreign NGO), the foundation *must* conduct pre-grant inquiry, require a written agreement, require reports, and verify spending. The grantee bears the burden of proof for the foundation’s compliance.
*   **Narrative Weight:** Philanthropic acquittals weigh "Story of Change" heavily. Quantitative metrics matter, but qualitative narrative (case studies, photos, lessons learned) is often the currency of renewal.
*   **Restricted vs. Unrestricted:** Tracking restricted funds (Time, Purpose, Capital) is the primary compliance risk. Commingling restricted funds with general operating reserves is a major finding.
*   **Consequence of Failure:** Rarely legal action. Usually: Non-renewal, reputational damage in the philanthropic ecosystem (donors talk), requirement to return unspent funds, decline of future proposals.

**Real-World Example: The "Mission Drift" Finding**
A community health nonprofit receives a $250k grant from a family foundation for a specific "Mobile Dental Van" program (Capital + 2 years Ops). In Year 2, the ED uses $40k of the van's operating budget to cover a shortfall in the main clinic's rent (unrestricted deficit). The acquittal shows the van ran fewer days. The foundation program officer flags this during the site visit. The grantee must repay the $40k or demonstrate equivalent unrestricted revenue replacement. The trust is broken; the foundation declines a $500k capacity-building grant the following year.

### 3.3 International / Multi-Lateral Funding (EU, UN, World Bank, Global Fund)
**Authority:** Treaty, Financial Regulations, Specific Grant Contract General Conditions.
**Philosophy:** **Harmonization & Fraud Prevention.**
**Unique Features:**
*   **Eligibility Rules:** Strict nationality/origin rules for goods/services (e.g., EU "Rule of Origin").
*   **Per Diems/Unit Costs:** Heavy reliance on simplified cost options (SCOs) – per diems, unit costs, lump sums – rather than actuals. Acquittal focuses on *output verification* (did the training happen?) not receipt verification (what was the hotel bill?).
*   **Ex-Post Audits:** High probability of retrospective audit by European Court of Auditors or OLAF (EU Anti-Fraud Office) years after project close.
*   **Currency/Exchange Rate Risk:** Specific rules on which exchange rate to use (contract date, invoice date, monthly average).

## 4. Anatomy of an Acquittal Report: Core Components

While templates vary, a "Gold Standard" acquittal package contains the following sections. Treat this as your master checklist.

### 4.1 Cover Sheet / Certification Page
*   **Legal Entity Name & Tax ID / Charity Number.**
*   **Grant Agreement Number / Reference.**
*   **Reporting Period:** Start Date – End Date (must match contract).
*   **Authorized Signatory:** Name, Title, Signature, Date. *Must be the person authorized in the Grant Agreement (often CEO/ED/CFO/Board Chair).*
*   **Certification Language:** "I certify that to the best of my knowledge and belief, the report is true, complete, and accurate..."

### 4.2 Financial Statement (The "Face Sheet")
Usually a prescribed template (e.g., SF-425 Federal Financial Report in US, or Funder-specific Excel template).
*   **Columns:** Approved Budget | Cumulative Prior Expenditures | Current Period Expenditures | Cumulative Total | Variance ($ and %).
*   **Cost Categories:** Personnel, Fringe, Travel, Equipment, Supplies, Contractual, Construction, Other, Indirect Costs.
*   **Matching / Cost Share:** Separate tracking column for Third-Party In-Kind, Cash Match, Program Income.
*   **Cash Position:** Cash Received vs. Cash Expended (Cash on Hand). *Government funders hate large cash balances; they imply poor cash forecasting.*

### 4.3 General Ledger (GL) Reconciliation / Schedule of Expenditures
The "Backup" to the Face Sheet.
*   **Mapping:** Every line on the Face Sheet must trace to a sum of GL account codes.
*   **Crosswalk Document:** A mapping table showing: *Grant Budget Line Item -> Internal Chart of Accounts Code(s).*
*   **Variance Analysis Narrative:** Required for any line item variance >10% (or threshold set by funder).
    *   *Bad:* "Spent less on travel."
    *   *Good:* "Travel underspent by 22% ($14k) due to COVID-19 travel restrictions March-June. Funds reallocated to 'Supplies' (Zoom licenses, home office stipends) via Prior Approval Request #G-2023-04 approved 04/15/2023."

### 4.4 Performance / Narrative Report
*   **Logic Model Alignment:** Report against the *exact* Outputs and Outcomes in the approved Logic Model/Theory of Change.
*   **Quantitative Metrics:** Target vs. Actual (e.g., "Target: 500 youth served. Actual: 485. Variance: -3%.").
*   **Qualitative Analysis:** Explanation of variance. Barriers, enablers, unexpected outcomes.
*   **Deliverables Inventory:** List of all required deliverables (reports, datasets, software, buildings, curricula) with status (Submitted/Delivered/Pending) and location (URL, Drive path, Physical address).

### 4.5 Asset Inventory & Disposition Plan
For any equipment (> $5k/unit typically) or real property purchased.
*   **Fields:** Description, Serial #, Acquisition Date, Cost, % Federal/Foundation Share, Location, Condition, Title Holder.
*   **Disposition Request:** If project ends, request instructions: Retain? Transfer? Sell (return % share to funder)?

### 4.6 Sub-Recipient / Contractor Monitoring Summary
If you passed funds down:
*   List of Sub-awards/Contracts > Threshold (e.g., $25k/$30k).
*   Status of *their* audits (Single Audit received? Management Letter?).
*   Status of your monitoring (Desk reviews, site visits conducted).
*   **Flow-down verification:** Did you check SAM.gov (US) / Debarment lists for every vendor >$25k?

### 4.7 Program Income / Earned Revenue Report
If the grant generated revenue (fees, sales, rent).
*   **Treatment:** Additive (add to budget), Deductive (reduce grant draw), or Cost Sharing (match). *Must follow the specific grant terms.*

## 5. Financial Acquittal: The General Ledger as Evidence

The General Ledger (GL) is the single source of truth. If the GL is messy, the acquittal will fail.

### 5.1 Chart of Accounts (CoA) Design for Grants
**The Golden Rule:** Your CoA must map 1:1 to the funder’s budget categories *without* requiring manual journal entries to "fix" the mapping at month-end.

**Best Practice Structure (Segmented CoA):**
`Fund - Function - Cost Category - Project/Grant - Location`
*   *Example:* `2100 (Fed Grant) - 5000 (Program Svcs) - 6100 (Personnel) - G-2023-04 (Grant ID) - 01 (Main Site)`

**Why this works:** You can run a trial balance filtered by `Fund=2100` AND `Project=G-2023-04` and instantly generate the Schedule of Expenditures. No spreadsheets required.

### 5.2 Cost Allowability: The Four Pillars (2 CFR 200 Subpart E / General Principles)
Every charge to the grant must pass four tests. **Document the test at the time of transaction.**

| Test | Definition | Documentation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Allowable** | Permitted by Cost Principles & Grant Terms. Not explicitly unallowable (e.g., lobbying, alcohol, entertainment, fines, penalties, first-class airfare). | **Pre-approval workflow:** Flag unallowable codes in AP system. Require "Allowability Checklist" sign-off by Grant Manager for non-standard expenses. |
| **Allocable** | Assignable to the grant in proportion to benefit received. If a cost benefits 3 grants + general ops, it must be split via a documented methodology (e.g., FTE %, Square Footage, Direct Labor Hours). | **Allocation Methodology Policy:** Written, board-approved policy. Annual certification that methodology hasn't changed. Timesheets for personnel; square footage logs for rent. |
| **Reasonable** | Would a prudent person pay this price? Comparable market rates. | **Procurement File:** 3 quotes, sole source justification, or GSA/State contract reference. |
| **Consistent** | Treated the same way across all funding sources. You cannot charge rent as Direct Cost to Grant A and Indirect Cost to Grant B. | **Cost Accounting Standards (CAS) / Disclosure Statement (DS-2):** For large orgs. For small orgs: Consistent written policy manual. |

### 5.3 Personnel Costs: The Highest Risk Area
Salaries and wages are usually 60-80% of grant budgets. They are the #1 audit target.

**The "After-the-Fact" Timesheet Myth:**
Many organizations use "After-the-Fact" (ATF) records (e.g., "I certify I worked 100% on Grant X last semester"). **Under 2 CFR 200.430(i), this is only acceptable for employees who work 100% on a single cost objective.**
*   **Split-funded employees MUST have contemporaneous timesheets** (daily/weekly entry) reflecting actual activity distribution.
*   **Payroll Certification:** Semi-annual certification by PI/Supervisor is required for *all* grant-funded staff.

**Real-World Finding: The "Ghost" FTE**
A non-profit charges a Program Director at 1.0 FTE to a Federal Grant for 12 months.
*   *Audit:* Review of Outlook calendar/email metadata shows Director spent 40% time on fundraising gala, 20% on Board meetings, 10% on unrelated program.
*   *Result:* 70% of salary ($84k) disallowed. Indirect costs on that salary also disallowed. Total finding ~$110k.
*   *Fix:* Implement **Activity-Based Time Reporting** (not just "Grant vs Non-Grant" but "Grant A / Grant B / Fundraising / Admin"). Use tools like Toggl, Harvest, or ERP modules (Workday, Oracle, Sage Intacct) with mandatory daily entry.

### 5.4 Indirect Costs (Facilities & Administrative - F&A)
*   **Negotiated Rate (NICRA):** Use the rate current at the time of expenditure. If rate expires, use provisional rate; do not invent a rate.
*   **De Minimis (10% MTDC):** Only if you have *never* had a negotiated rate. MTDC = Modified Total Direct Costs (excludes equipment, capital expenditures, patient care, rental costs, tuition remission, sub-awards >$25k each).
*   **Common Error:** Charging Indirect Costs on **Equipment** ($5k+ unit cost) or **Sub-award amounts > $25k**. These are excluded from the MTDC base.

### 5.5 Procurement & The "Paper Trail"
**Micro-purchase (<$10k US / <$3k-5k typical elsewhere):** No competition required, but must distribute equitably among suppliers. *Document: "Price deemed reasonable based on catalog price / prior purchase."*
**Simplified Acquisition ($10k - $250k US):** Requires 3 quotes (written/email). *Document: Quote comparison matrix.*
**Formal Procurement (>$250k):** Sealed bids (IFB) or Competitive Proposals (RFP). Public advertising. Evaluation committee minutes. Contract file.
**Sole Source:** Only permitted if: 1) Single source, 2) Emergency, 3) Funder authorization, 4) Inadequate competition. **Requires written justification signed by CFO/ED *before* purchase.**

**Contractor vs. Sub-recipient Determination (Critical):**
*   **Sub-recipient:** Determines eligibility, has programmatic decision making, adheres to compliance requirements, uses funds for a program. -> **Flow-down clauses apply. Monitoring required. Sub-recipient audit required.**
*   **Contractor:** Provides goods/services for the recipient’s own use, operates in competitive market, provides similar goods to many buyers. -> **Procurement rules apply. No flow-down of program compliance.**

*Misclassifying a sub-recipient as a contractor to avoid monitoring/audit requirements is a "Material Weakness" finding.*

## 6. Performance Acquittal: Proving Impact, Not Just Activity

Funders increasingly pay for *outcomes*, not just *outputs*. The performance acquittal must bridge this gap.

### 6.1 The Logic Model as Contract
Your approved Logic Model (Inputs -> Activities -> Outputs -> Outcomes -> Impact) is the scoring rubric for the performance acquittal.

| Level | Definition | Acquittal Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Outputs** | Direct products (Countable). "Number of workshops held." | Sign-in sheets, LMS completion reports, event photos, deliverable submission receipts. |
| **Outcomes (Short)** | Changes in Knowledge, Skills, Attitudes (KSA). "Participants increased financial literacy." | Pre/Post surveys (statistically significant sample), assessment scores, certification rates. |
| **Outcomes (Long)** | Changes in Behavior, Condition, Status. "Participants open savings accounts." | Bank data sharing agreements (with consent), credit report pulls, longitudinal tracking database. |
| **Impact** | Population-level change. "Poverty rate reduced in County X." | Census data, government stats (attribution is hard; contribution is the claim). |

### 6.2 Data Quality & Verification
**The "Denominator Problem":**
*Report:* "We served 1,000 unique clients."
*Audit:* "Show me the Unique Client ID list. Show me the intake forms. Show me the discharge summary."
*Failure:* Spreadsheet has duplicates (John Smith / J. Smith). 200 clients lack signed consent forms. 50 clients counted twice across two programs.

**Fix: Case Management System (CMS) / CRM as System of Record.**
*   Apricot, Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud, CaseWorthy, ETO, Custom SQL.
*   **Required Fields:** Unique ID, Demographics, Enrollment Date, Service Log (Date, Type, Duration, Staff), Exit Date, Outcome Status.
*   **Acquittal Button:** A report in the CMS that auto-generates the "Clients Served" metric with filters matching the grant definition (e.g., "Active during reporting period," "Resident of Target Zip Codes").

### 6.3 Handling Variance: The "Variance Narrative"
You will miss targets. The acquittal is not a pass/fail test; it is a test of **organizational honesty and learning**.

**Structure of a Strong Variance Narrative:**
1.  **State the Variance:** "Target: 500 job placements. Actual: 380 (-24%)."
2.  **Contextualize (External):** "Major employer (Auto Plant) closed Q2, removing 150 entry-level slots. Local unemployment rose 3%."
3.  **Analyze (Internal):** "Our job developer caseload ratio was 1:60 (industry std 1:30). Outreach relied on walk-ins; digital marketing budget cut in Year 1."
4.  **Corrective Action (Implemented):** "Hired 1.5 FTE Job Developers (Oct). Shifted to Salesforce Marketing Cloud for targeted employer outreach (Nov)."
5.  **Revised Projection:** "Based on Q4 pipeline, projecting 520 placements for Year 2 (exceeding target)."
6.  **Lesson Learned:** "Real-time labor market data subscription now budgeted for Year 2."

**Red Flag Language to Avoid:** "We did our best," "Staff turnover," "COVID," "The target was unrealistic" (without proposing a formal modification *earlier*).

### 6.4 Qualitative Data: The "Story" with Rigor
Foundations love stories. Auditors love evidence. Combine them.
*   **Case Study Protocol:** Standardized template: Pseudonym, Context, Intervention, Outcome Quote, Staff Reflection, Data Point Link.
*   **Media Releases:** Photos/Videos require **Media Release Forms** signed by subjects (parents for minors). File these *with* the digital asset.

## 7. Record-Keeping Infrastructure: Building the Audit Trail

**"If it isn't documented, it didn't happen."** — Every Auditor, Ever.

### 7.1 The Document Retention Matrix
Retention requirements vary. The strictest wins.
*   **Federal (US):** 3 years from submission of *final* expenditure report (Single Audit extends this to 3 years from audit report acceptance). **Real property/Equipment:** 3 years after final disposition.
*   **State/Local:** Often 5-7 years.
*   **Private Foundations:** Typically 4-7 years (IRS Form 990-PF statute of limitations).
*   **Litigation Hold:** Indefinite (overrides all).

**Policy:** Adopt a **"7-Year Minimum / Permanent for Legal/Capital"** policy organization-wide to simplify.

### 7.2 Digital vs. Physical: The "Trusted Digital Repository"
Moving paperless is not scanning shoeboxes. It requires **Information Governance**.

**Core Requirements for Digital Records:**
1.  **Immutability / Write-Once-Read-Many (WORM):** Audit trails on the files themselves (SharePoint/Google Drive version history is usually sufficient; blockchain overkill).
2.  **Metadata Tagging:** Every file *must* have: `Grant_ID`, `Fiscal_Year`, `Doc_Type` (Invoice, Timesheet, Contract, Report), `Vendor_Name`, `Amount`.
3.  **Naming Convention:** `G2023-04_FY24_Invoice_AcmeCorp_12500_20240315.pdf`
4.  **Access Control (Least Privilege):**
    *   Finance: Read/Write Financials.
    *   Program: Read/Write Performance.
    *   ED/Board: Read All.
    *   Auditors: Read All (Time-limited Guest Link).
5.  **Backup & Disaster Recovery:** Geo-redundant (Cloud + Offsite). Test restore annually.

## 8. The Audit Event: Surviving Scrutiny

An audit is not an investigation; it is a verification procedure. Treat it as a project.

### 8.1 Types of Audits
1.  **Single Audit (US) / Statutory Audit (UK/AU/CA):** Entity-wide. Opinion on Financial Statements + Compliance (Major Programs). Threshold: $750k Federal Expenditures (US).
2.  **Program-Specific Audit:** Funder hires auditor to audit *just* their grant. Common in philanthropy and state pass-throughs.
3.  **Desk Review / Monitoring Visit:** Funder staff review documents remotely or on-site. Lower rigor than audit, but findings are binding.
4.  **Inspector General (IG) / OIG Audit:** Law enforcement arm. Fraud focus. **Legal counsel required immediately.**
5.  **Sub-recipient Monitoring (Your audit of them):** You are the auditor. You issue a management decision on their findings.

### 8.2 The PBC (Prepared by Client) List: The Battle Plan
Auditors send a "PBC List" (Request List). **Do not wait for it.** Maintain a "Rolling PBC" folder updated monthly.
*   **Standard PBC Items:**
    *   Trial Balance (Grant Fund only) -> GL Mapping.
    *   Bank Reconciliations (All months).
    *   Payroll Registers + Timesheet Samples (Auditor picks samples).
    *   Procurement Files (Top 5 vendors by $ + 3 random).
    *   Sub-recipient Agreements + Monitoring Docs.
    *   Board Minutes (Grant approval, Conflict of Interest policy review).
    *   Insurance Certificates (Workers Comp, General Liability, Auto, D&O).
    *   Equipment Inventory Listing.
    *   Prior Year Audit Findings Status (Corrective Action Plan implementation proof).

### 8.3 Managing the Audit Fieldwork
*   **Single Point of Contact (SPOC):** Assign one staff member (Grant Manager/Controller) as Auditor Liaison. **No one else talks to auditors directly.** This prevents conflicting answers.
*   **Secure Data Room:** Use a VDR (Virtual Data Room) or permissioned SharePoint folder. **Never email spreadsheets with PII/SSNs.**
*   **Daily Stand-up:** 15-min sync with Auditor Lead: "What did you finish? What do you need today? Any preliminary findings?"
*   **Preliminary Findings (Exit Conference):** Auditors *must* discuss findings before finalizing. This is your chance to provide missing docs or correct misunderstandings. **Take notes. Do not argue. Say: "We will provide a written response by [Date]."**

### 8.4 The Audit Finding: Anatomy and Response
**Finding Structure (Yellow Book / GAGAS):**
1.  **Criteria:** The rule (Statute, Regulation, Grant Term, Policy).
2.  **Condition:** What actually happened (The facts).
3.  **Cause:** Why it happened (Root cause).
4.  **Effect:** The impact (Questioned costs, non-compliance, risk).
5.  **Recommendation:** What the auditor says you should do.

**Your Management Response (Corrective Action Plan - CAP):**
*   **Concur / Partially Concur / Do Not Concur.** (Rarely "Do Not Concur" without legal advice).
*   **Specific Corrective Action:** "We will implement X."
*   **Responsible Party:** Name/Title.
*   **Target Date:** Specific Date.
*   **Evidence of Implementation:** What you will send the auditor/funder to prove it's fixed.

*Example:*
> **Finding 2024-001: Unallowable Costs – Alcohol.**
> **CAP:** Concur. CFO to update AP Policy Section
