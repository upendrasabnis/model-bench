**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our Community Resilience Grant programme is open to a broad spectrum of applicants, including unincorporated community groups, provided that the group operates under a legally recognised “auspicing” arrangement. In practice this means that the primary legal responsibility for governance, financial management and compliance rests with the auspicing body—in your case, the local council. As long as the council holds the necessary insurances, holds any required licences or permits, and can act as the primary point of contact for grant purposes, your group meets the eligibility criteria. You will still need to demonstrate that the council supports the project and is prepared to sign the appropriate grant agreement, which we will include in the application pack.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The grant programme does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, funding is allocated on a case‑by‑case basis, reflecting the scope, impact and cost of the proposed activity. Past awards have ranged from approximately $5,000 to $150,000, depending on the scale of the initiative and the proportion of eligible costs. For a rainwater‑tank and shade‑structure project of the type you describe, a typical award would fall between $15,000 and $45,000, but the final amount will be determined by the completeness of your budget and the strength of your outcomes case.  

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure that directly enhances the resilience and sustainability of community operations is fully eligible. Both rainwater tanks (including purchase, delivery, installation and any necessary plumbing work) and permanent shade structures (such as pergolas or polytunnels) qualify as “capital assets” under the grant’s cost categories. However, please note that we require evidence of durability (e.g., manufacturer specifications, warranty information) and that the assets will remain the property of the community group (or the council, if title is held by the auspicing body). Routine consumables such as soil amendments, seeds or seasonal maintenance costs are not covered.  

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To present a strong application, we recommend assembling the following documents:  

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem you are addressing (e.g., heat stress on garden productivity), the proposed solution, and the expected outcomes.  
2. **Budget and Cash‑flow Statement** – line‑item costs for the tanks, shade structure, installation, and any contingency. Include quotations or provisional invoices for major items.  
3. **Proof of Insurance** – a copy of the council’s insurance certificate confirming coverage for the project activities and the proposed assets.  
4. **Council Support Letter** – an official letter from the council confirming its role as auspice, its willingness to sign the grant agreement, and any relevant permits or approvals.  
5. **Organizational Profile** – a brief constitution or set of operating guidelines, membership list, and a recent bank statement demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
6. **Evaluation Plan** – outline how you will measure success (e.g., increased water‑independence, reduced temperature in the garden, number of community members benefiting).  
7. **Partnerships and Volunteer Contributions** – any letters of support from partner organisations, local businesses, or volunteer groups that can add credibility.  

We also advise you to review the full guidelines on our website (section “Eligibility and Application Requirements”) for any additional forms, such as the Environmental Impact Assessment checklist that may be required for water‑related installations.  

If you decide to move forward, the next step is to complete the online pre‑application form, which will generate a detailed checklist of required attachments.
