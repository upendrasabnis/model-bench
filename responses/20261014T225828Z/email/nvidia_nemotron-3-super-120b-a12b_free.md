Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the valuable work you are doing with the community garden. I am happy to clarify the eligibility requirements for the Community Resilience Grant and to outline the information you will need to prepare a strong application.

**Auspicing arrangements**  
The grant program expressly allows unincorporated groups to apply when they are auspiced by an eligible organisation, such as a local council, a registered charity, or an incorporated association. In your case, the council’s auspice satisfies this requirement, provided you can submit a formal letter of support from the council confirming that it will act as the legal and financial auspice for the project, that it holds the necessary public liability and volunteer insurance, and that it will be responsible for acquitting any grant funds awarded.

**Grant size**  
The Community Resilience Grant offers funding ranging from a minimum of $5,000 to a maximum of $50,000 per project. There is no prescribed minimum or maximum turnover for applicants; the assessment focuses on the project’s relevance to resilience outcomes, the capacity of the auspicing organisation to manage the funds, and the demonstrated community need.

**Eligible costs**  
Infrastructure investments that directly enhance the group’s ability to withstand climate‑related stresses—such as rainwater harvesting tanks, shade structures, irrigation upgrades, or flood‑mitigation works—are explicitly listed as eligible capital costs. Please ensure that any items you propose are new purchases (not replacements of existing assets) and that you provide itemised quotes or vendor estimates to substantiate the budget.

**Supporting documentation**  
To accompany your application, we recommend preparing the following:

1. **Auspice letter** – signed by an authorised officer of the local council, outlining their role, insurance coverage, and willingness to manage the grant.  
2. **Proof of council insurance** – a certificate of currency for public liability and volunteer insurance.  
3. **Project plan** – a brief description (max 500 words) of how the rainwater tanks and shade structure will improve resilience, including timelines and maintenance arrangements.  
4. **Budget sheet** – itemised costs, showing requested grant amount, any co‑contributions (in‑kind or cash), and quotes for each major item.  
5. **Organisational information** – a simple governance outline (e.g., meeting minutes showing volunteer coordination) and a summary of recent activities (produce stall reports, membership figures).  
6. **Letters of community support** – optional but helpful, from residents, local schools, or other stakeholders endorsing the project’s benefits.
