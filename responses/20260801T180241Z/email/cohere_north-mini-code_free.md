**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines state that an applicant may be either a registered charity, a not‑for‑profit incorporated entity, or an unincorporated group that is formally **auspiced or sponsored by a local government body**. The council’s sponsorship must be evidenced by a written agreement (or a letter of support) that confirms the council will provide necessary insurance, hold any required permits, and act as the fiscal host for the grant funds. Because you operate under the auspices of your local council, which already holds the relevant insurances on your behalf, you meet this criterion, provided you can supply the council’s endorsement letter when you submit the application.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to fund projects ranging from **$5,000 to $150,000**. There is no strict minimum award; however, projects that request less than $5,000 are generally encouraged to seek alternative community funding sources, as the administrative overhead of processing very small grants can outweigh the benefit to the applicant. Your proposed rainwater‑tank and shade‑structure installation appears to fall comfortably within the typical mid‑range of $30,000–$80,000, depending on the scale of the infrastructure.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes—capital infrastructure such as rainwater harvesting systems, shade structures, and associated site works are explicitly listed as eligible expenses under the “Climate‑Resilient Community Assets” category. Eligible costs include:

* Purchase and delivery of rainwater tanks (including any necessary pumps and filtration).  
* Fabrication, delivery, and installation of shade structures (e.g., pop‑up canopies, permanent pergolas).  
* Site preparation (excavation, foundations, drainage).  
* Project‑specific engineering or design fees.  
* Contingency funds (typically capped at 10 % of the total budget) to cover unforeseen site conditions.

**Supporting documents we recommend preparing**  

1. **Letter of Support/Auspice Agreement** – A formal letter from your local council confirming that they will act as fiscal host, provide insurance coverage, and oversee the project on your behalf.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – A brief overview of your garden’s mission, volunteer base, and current operations (you already supplied a summary of turnover and activities, which is useful).  
3. **Financial Statements** – For the most recent fiscal year, include a profit‑and‑loss statement, balance sheet, and cash flow summary. Because your turnover is $40,000, a simple set of accounts prepared by your accountant or the council’s finance team will suffice.  
4. **Budget and Narrative** – A detailed line‑item budget that aligns with the eligible cost categories above, plus a narrative describing the problem you are addressing (increasing summer heat), the solution (rainwater tanks and shade), and the anticipated outcomes (improved water security, reduced heat stress, extended growing season).  
5. **Project Timeline** – A Gantt‑style or milestone chart outlining key activities, responsible parties, and target dates leading up to the grant’s implementation period.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Management Plan** – Demonstrating that you have identified potential risks (e.g., water quality, structural safety) and have mitigation strategies in place.  
7. **Evaluation Plan** – A simple framework for measuring success (e.g., water usage logs, temperature monitoring, user satisfaction surveys) and reporting back to the community.  
8. **Proof of Insurance** – Although the council holds the insurance, a copy of the policy or a certificate confirming that your project will be covered under the council’s liability policy is often requested.
