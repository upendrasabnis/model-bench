## 1. Introduction: The Lifecycle of Trust

Grant funding is not a gift; it is a contract. Whether the funder is a federal agency distributing taxpayer dollars, a state government administering a community infrastructure program, or a private foundation deploying endowment assets, the underlying mechanism is identical: **resources are exchanged for specific, measurable outcomes.**

The "acquittal" is the formal closure of this contract. It is the moment the grantee stands before the funder and says, *"We received $X. We spent $X on Y activities. We achieved Z outcomes. Here is the evidence."*

Yet, across the non-profit, research, and public sectors, the acquittal is frequently treated as an administrative afterthought—a frantic scramble of receipts and spreadsheets two weeks before a deadline. This mindset is dangerous. In an era of increasing scrutiny—driven by Royal Commissions, Inspector General reports, and heightened public demand for transparency—compliance failures result in more than just rejected reports. They trigger **clawbacks (recovery of funds)**, **debarment from future funding**, **reputational destruction**, and in severe cases, **criminal prosecution**.

This article serves as a definitive operational manual. It moves beyond definitions to provide a systems-level view of acquittal management: the regulatory architecture, the financial mechanics, the evidentiary standards, the technology stack, and the organizational culture required to turn compliance from a liability into a competitive advantage.

## 2. Defining the Acquittal: More Than a Receipt

### 2.1 Etymology and Legal Basis
The term "acquit" derives from the Old French *acquiter* (to settle a claim) and Latin *quietare* (to put at rest). In grant management, to **acquit** is to discharge a legal obligation. The Grant Agreement (or Deed of Grant) creates a conditional liability: the grantee holds funds *in trust* (or as a restricted asset) until the conditions of the agreement are met. The acquittal is the legal instrument that extinguishes that liability.

### 2.2 The Three Pillars of Acquittal
A complete acquittal rests on three distinct but interconnected pillars. Failure in any one pillar constitutes a compliance failure.

| Pillar | Core Question | Primary Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial (Probity)** | Was the money spent legally, accurately, and only on approved budget lines? | General Ledger extracts, bank statements, invoices, payroll records, asset registers. |
| **Performance (Output/Outcome)** | Were the agreed activities delivered and did they achieve the intended impact? | KPI dashboards, milestone reports, evaluation reports, beneficiary data, photos/case studies. |
| **Governance (Process)** | Were the funds managed under appropriate internal controls and procurement rules? | Board minutes, conflict of interest registers, procurement policies, insurance certificates, audit reports. |

### 2.3 Types of Acquittals
*   **Progress/Interim Acquittal:** Required at set intervals (quarterly, bi-annually, or at project milestones). Often triggers the next payment tranche. Focus: "Are we on track?"
*   **Final Acquittal:** Submitted at project completion. Focus: "Did we finish the job and spend all the money correctly?"
*   **Special Purpose / Capital Acquittal:** For infrastructure/asset purchases. Requires independent quantity surveyor reports, certificates of occupancy, and asset valuation.
*   **Unexpended Funds Acquittal:** Specific reporting when a project finishes under budget or is terminated early. Dictates the legal process for returning funds (interest calculations, consolidation into consolidated revenue).

## 3. The Regulatory Landscape: Government vs. Philanthropic Frameworks

While the principles of accountability are universal, the *regulatory weight* differs significantly. Understanding the "Master" regulations governing your funder is step one.

### 3.1 Government Funders (Federal, State/Provincial, Local)
**The Golden Rule:** Public money is governed by **Public Finance Acts**, **Financial Management Acts**, and **Audit Acts**. Compliance is not optional; it is statutory.

*   **United States (Federal):** Governed by **2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance)**. This is the "Bible" for US federal awards. It covers Cost Principles (Subpart E), Audit Requirements (Subpart F - Single Audit), and Administrative Requirements.
    *   *Key Concept:* **Allowability** (Is the cost necessary, reasonable, allocable, and consistent with policies?).
*   **Australia (Commonwealth):** Governed by the **Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013 (PGPA Act)** and the **Commonwealth Grants Rules and Guidelines (CGRGs)**.
    *   *Key Concept:* **Value with Relevance** – achieving outcomes efficiently, effectively, economically, and ethically.
*   **United Kingdom:** Governed by **Managing Public Money (HM Treasury)** and the **Grant Funding Agreements** specific to departments (e.g., UKRI, NHS, DLUHC).
*   **European Union:** Governed by the **Financial Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2018/1046** and specific programme guides (Horizon Europe, Erasmus+). Heavy emphasis on **eligible costs** and **beneficiary methodology**.

**Consequence of Failure:** Financial penalties, interest on misspent funds, referral to Auditor-General/Inspector General, criminal referral (fraud), legislative debarment.

### 3.2 Philanthropic & Corporate Funders
**The Golden Rule:** Governed by **Contract Law** and **Trust Law** (for foundations), and the funder’s own **Grantmaking Guidelines**.

*   **Flexibility:** Generally more flexible on budget variations (e.g., moving 10-15% between lines without prior approval).
*   **Focus:** Heavily weighted toward **Impact Measurement** and **Storytelling** (qualitative data) alongside financial probity.
*   **Legal Recourse:** Breach of contract, reputational damage, "clawback" clauses in grant agreements. Private foundations (US 501(c)(3)) have strict IRS "Expenditure Responsibility" rules if granting to non-public charities (e.g., for-profits, foreign orgs), requiring rigorous pre-grant inquiry and annual reports until funds are spent.

### 3.3 The "Pass-Through" Complexity
Many organizations are **Sub-recipients** (Sub-awardees).
*   *Flow-down clauses:* The prime recipient passes the prime award terms (2 CFR 200, CGRGs) down to the sub-recipient.
*   *Compliance Stack:* A sub-recipient must comply with: 1. Their own policies, 2. The Prime Recipient’s policies, 3. The Federal/State Funder’s regulations.
*   *Risk:* The "strictest standard wins" principle applies. If the Prime requires 3 quotes for procurement >$5k, but the Funder requires 3 quotes >$10k, the sub-recipient must follow the $5k rule.

## 4. Anatomy of an Acquittal Report: Core Components

A standard acquittal package submitted to a government department typically includes the following distinct documents. Treat this as a "bundle of evidence," not a single form.

### 4.1 The Certification / Declaration (The Legal Instrument)
This is the only page requiring a **wet signature (or advanced digital signature)** from an Authorized Officer (CEO, CFO, Accountable Authority).
*   **Content:** "I certify that the attached financial statement presents fairly the expenditure... that funds were used for approved purposes... that all conditions of the agreement have been met."
*   **Legal Weight:** Signing a false certification is perjury / fraud.
*   *Real-World Tip:* Never let a junior finance officer sign this. The signatory must have **delegated authority** under the organization’s Instrument of Delegation.

### 4.2 Statement of Receipts and Expenditure (The Financial Core)
*   **Format:** Usually a funder-prescribed template (Excel/PDF).
*   **Columns Required:** Approved Budget (Original) | Approved Budget (Current/Varied) | Actual Expenditure (Year to Date) | Actual Expenditure (Cumulative) | Variance ($) | Variance (%).
*   **Reconciliation:** Must tie exactly to the General Ledger (GL) Trial Balance for the project cost center.

### 4.3 Notes to the Financial Statement (The Narrative Context)
This is where you explain the numbers. Mandatory disclosures usually include:
1.  **Accounting Policy:** Cash vs. Accrual basis (Government usually demands Accrual).
2.  **Variance Explanations:** Any line item >10% (or $10k) variance requires a written explanation.
3.  **Capital Expenditure Treatment:** Depreciation policy vs. Expensing policy (critical for asset purchases).
4.  **In-Kind Contributions:** Valuation methodology (fair market value) and evidence (volunteer logs, donor letters).
5.  **Interest Earned:** Did the grant bank account earn interest? Most government agreements require interest to be reported and often returned or used for project purposes.

### 4.4 Performance Report (The Logic Model in Action)
*   **Outputs:** Quantitative counts (e.g., "15 workshops delivered," "500 participants trained").
*   **Outcomes:** Qualitative/Quantitative change (e.g., "Employment rate of participants increased from 20% to 65%").
*   **Evidence Links:** Explicit references to attachments (e.g., "See Attachment A: Workshop Attendance Sheets"; "See Attachment B: Independent Evaluation Report").

### 4.5 Independent Audit Report (The External Verification)
*   **Thresholds:** US Federal: $750k+ federal expenditure triggers Single Audit. Australia: Often >$500k or high-risk profile. Philanthropy: Often >$100k or mandatory for multi-year.
*   **Standard:** ISAE 3000 (Assurance Engagements Other Than Audits) or ISA 800/805 (Special Purpose Frameworks).
*   **Opinion Types:** Unmodified (Clean) | Qualified (Except for...) | Adverse | Disclaimer. *Anything other than Unmodified triggers immediate funder scrutiny.*

## 5. Financial Acquittal: The General Ledger as Evidence

The General Ledger (GL) is the single source of truth. If the acquittal template doesn't match the GL, the acquittal is wrong. Period.

### 5.1 Chart of Accounts (CoA) Design for Grants
**Failure Point:** Using a single "Grant Expense" code.
**Best Practice:** Mirror the **Approved Budget Structure** in the CoA.
*   *Example Structure:*
    *   `41000-GRANT-REV` (Revenue)
    *   `61100-GRANT-PERS-SAL` (Personnel - Salary)
    *   `61110-GRANT-PERS-ONC` (Personnel - On-costs/Super)
    *   `62100-GRANT-TRAV-DOM` (Domestic Travel)
    *   `62200-GRANT-TRAV-INTL` (International Travel)
    *   `63100-GRANT-EQUIP-CAP` (Capital Equipment >$5k)
    *   `63200-GRANT-EQUIP-EXP` (Minor Equipment <$5k)
    *   `64100-GRANT-SUPP-CONS` (Consumables)
    *   `65100-GRANT-SUB-ORG` (Sub-contractors)
    *   `66100-GRANT-ADMIN-OH` (Admin Overhead / Indirect Costs)

### 5.2 Cost Allowability: The "Necessary, Reasonable, Allocable" Test (2 CFR 200.403 / PGPA Equivalent)
Every transaction posted to the grant code must pass four tests:
1.  **Necessary:** Required for the project scope? (e.g., Buying a coffee machine for the office kitchen is rarely necessary for a research grant).
2.  **Reasonable:** Would a prudent person pay this price? (Benchmarking against market rates).
3.  **Allocable:** Can it be assigned to the project in proportion to benefit? (Shared costs like rent/insurance require a documented allocation methodology—e.g., FTE % or Floor Space %).
4.  **Consistent:** Treated the same as other activities (cannot charge rent to Grant A as direct cost and Grant B as indirect).

### 5.3 Direct vs. Indirect Costs (Overhead/Administration)
*   **Direct Costs:** Identifiable specifically with the project (Project Manager salary, project-specific software, travel to project site).
*   **Indirect Costs (F&A / Overhead):** Shared costs (Finance team, HR, IT, Rent, Utilities, Audit fees).
*   **The Negotiated Rate:** US Federals use a **Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate Agreement (NICRA)**. Australia/UK often use a capped percentage (e.g., 10-15% of direct costs) or a specific methodology.
*   *Compliance Trap:* **Double Dipping.** Charging the Finance Manager’s salary as a Direct Cost (Line 61100) *and* claiming an Overhead percentage that includes Finance salaries. You must choose one methodology per cost pool.

### 5.4 Procurement & Probity: The Paper Trail
For every expense > Procurement Threshold (often $5k, $10k, or $80k depending on jurisdiction):
1.  **Need Identification:** Brief/RFP document.
2.  **Market Approach:** 3 Quotes / Open Tender / Panel Contract.
3.  **Evaluation:** Scorecards, Conflict of Interest (COI) declarations from evaluators.
4.  **Contract:** Signed agreement with scope, deliverables, price, insurance requirements.
5.  **Invoice Verification:** Purchase Order (PO) matching to Invoice (3-way match: PO + Goods Receipt + Invoice).
*   *Real-World Example:* A university research center purchased a $45,000 microscope via a sole-source justification ("only vendor with this spec"). The auditor found the spec was written *by the vendor*. **Finding:** Procurement breach. **Result:** Cost disallowed; $45k liability to the university.

### 5.5 Payroll & Timekeeping: The Highest Risk Area
Salaries are usually 60-80% of grant budgets. They are the #1 audit target.
*   **Requirement:** **After-the-fact** certification (not budget estimates).
*   **Standard:** **Time and Effort Reporting** (US: 2 CFR 200.430; Australia: PGPA/Institutional Policy).
*   **Mechanism:**
    *   *100% Grant Funded:* Semi-annual certification by PI/Supervisor ("I confirm Dr. Smith worked 100% on Grant X").
    *   *Split Funded:* **Monthly** timesheets or effort certification systems (e.g., ClickCommerce, Huron, manual timesheets) showing % effort per project code.
*   **Red Flag:** "Budget-based" charging (Charging 50% to Grant A and 50% to Grant B because the budget says so, regardless of actual work done). This is **fraud**.

## 6. Performance & Impact Acquittal: Proving the "So What?"

Financial acquittal proves you spent the money honestly. Performance acquittal proves you spent it *wisely*.

### 6.1 The Logic Model Framework
Funders evaluate performance against the **Logic Model** agreed upon at application:
*   **Inputs** -> **Activities** -> **Outputs** -> **Outcomes (Short/Med/Long)** -> **Impact**.
*   *Acquittal Focus:* Primarily **Outputs** and **Short/Medium-term Outcomes**. Long-term impact is rarely acquittable within the grant period but requires a "sustainability plan."

### 6.2 Setting SMART KPIs at Contracting (Prevention > Cure)
Most acquittal failures originate in the **Grant Agreement negotiation**.
*   *Bad KPI:* "Improve community wellbeing." (Unmeasurable).
*   *Better KPI:* "Deliver 10 mental health workshops."
*   *Best KPI (Acquittable):* "Deliver 10 mental health workshops (Output) to 200 unique participants (Output) resulting in a 15% increase in K10 psychological distress scores (Outcome) measured via pre/post survey."

### 6.3 Data Collection Infrastructure
You cannot report in Month 24 what you didn't measure in Month 1.
*   **Case Management Systems (CMS):** Essential for service delivery (Client ID, Service Type, Date, Outcome Flag). *Examples: Penelope, Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud, Custom SQL.*
*   **Survey Tools:** Qualtrics, REDCap (research), SurveyMonkey (simple). Must capture **Unique Identifiers** to prevent double counting.
*   **Digital Evidence:** Geo-tagged photos (capital works), Screen recordings (software dev), GitHub commits (code), LMS completion reports (training).

### 6.4 Handling Underperformance (The "Variance Narrative")
If you missed a KPI (e.g., only 8 of 10 workshops delivered), **do not hide it**.
*   **The "Variance Narrative" Structure:**
    1.  **Fact:** "Delivered 8/10 workshops."
    2.  **Cause:** "Venue flooding in Q3 (Attach insurance claim/photos); Key facilitator resigned (Attach HR record)."
    3.  **Mitigation:** "Rescheduled 2 workshops for Month 25 (No-cost extension requested/approved)."
    4.  **Impact Assessment:** "Delayed outreach to Cohort B; projected outcome achievement delayed by 3 months."
*   *Funder Psychology:* Funders manage *risk*. A transparent variance narrative builds trust. Silence destroys it.

### 6.5 Qualitative Evidence: The "Case Study" Protocol
Numbers need narrative. Funders use case studies for Ministerial briefings and Annual Reports.
*   **Protocol:** De-identify (Privacy Act/GDPR). Structure: **Context -> Intervention -> Change -> Quote**.
*   *Example:* "Participant 'J' (34, long-term unemployed) engaged in Job Ready program (Context). Completed Cert III in Aged Care + 120hr placement (Intervention). Secured permanent part-time role; reports improved housing stability (Change). 'This course didn't just give me a cert, it gave me a routine.' (Quote)."

## 7. Record-Keeping Infrastructure: Building the Audit Trail

"If it isn't documented, it didn't happen." — *Every Auditor, Ever.*

### 7.1 The Legal Retention Requirements
*   **US Federal (2 CFR 200.334):** 3 years from submission of *final* expenditure report (not project end). **Exceptions:** Real property/equipment = 3 years after final disposition. Litigation/Audit findings = until resolved.
*   **Australia (Archives Act 1983 / State Records Acts):** Minimum 7 years for financial records after final action. Grant agreements often specify "7 years after acquittal acceptance."
*   **UK (HMRC/Charity Commission):** 6 years minimum.
*   **Golden Rule:** Adopt the **longest applicable retention period** across all your funders as your organizational policy.

### 7.2 The "Digital First" Evidence Locker
Move beyond the "shoebox of receipts" or the "shared drive folder named 'Grant Stuff'."

#### 7.2.1 Folder Architecture (Standardized Naming Convention)
`/Grants/[FUNDER_CODE]-[GRANT_ID]-[PROJECT_NAME]/`
├── `01_Agreement/` (Signed Deed, Variations, Correspondence)
├── `02_Financial/`
│   ├── `01_Budget/` (Approved, Revised)
│   ├── `02_Ledger_Exports/` (Monthly GL dumps, named `YYYY-MM_GL.csv`)
│   ├── `03_Invoices_Receipts/` (Sub-folders by Budget Line: `61100_Salaries`, `62100_Travel`)
│   ├── `04_Payroll/` (Timesheets, Effort Certs, Payroll Journals)
│   ├── `05_Procurement/` (RFP, Quotes, Eval Scores, Contracts, POs)
│   ├── `06_Bank/` (Statements, Reconciliations, Interest Calcs)
│   └── `07_Acquittal_Workpapers/` (Reconciliation spreadsheets, Variance calcs)
├── `03_Performance/`
│   ├── `01_KPI_Tracker/` (Live dashboard exports)
│   ├── `02_Raw_Data/` (Survey exports, CMS reports, Attendance sheets)
│   ├── `03_Evaluation/` (Reports, Ethics approvals)
│   └── `04_Case_Studies/` (Consent forms, Drafts, Final)
├── `04_Governance/`
│   ├── `01_Board_Minutes/` (Relevant extracts)
│   ├── `02_COI_Register/` (Project specific)
│   ├── `03_Insurance/` (Certificates of Currency)
│   └── `04_Risk_Register/` (Project risk log)
├── `05_Audit/`
│   ├── `01_Engagement_Letter/`
│   ├── `02_Auditor_Queries/` (PBC - Prepared By Client lists)
│   └── `03_Final_Report/` (Management Letter, Signed Opinion)
└── `06_Correspondence/` (Emails re: variations, approvals, extensions)

### 7.3 Document Integrity Controls
*   **Immutability:** Use "Write Once, Read Many" (WORM) storage or Version Control (SharePoint/Google Drive version history) so auditors see the *exact* file submitted.
*   **Naming Convention:** `YYYY-MM-DD_DOCUMENT-TYPE_DESCRIPTION_v#.ext` (e.g., `2023-10-15_INVOICE_AcmeConsulting_WebDev_v1.pdf`).
*   **OCR & Searchability:** All scanned PDFs must be OCR'd. Auditors search by ABN/EIN, Amount, Date.

### 7.4 The "Prepared By Client" (PBC) List
Auditors issue a PBC list (usually 30-50 items). **Do not wait for the auditor.** Maintain a "Rolling PBC" folder. Every month, drop the current:
*   Bank Reconciliation
*   GL Trial Balance (Project Filter)
*   Payroll Register (Project Filter)
*   Fixed Asset Register (Project Filter)
*   KPI Dashboard Snapshot
When the auditor arrives, you hand them a USB/Link: "Here is 90% of your PBC list, pre-organized." This reduces audit fees (hourly rates) and findings.

## 8. The Audit Event: Preparation, Execution, and Response

### 8.1 Types of Audits
1.  **Financial Statement Audit:** Opinion on the org's whole FS (includes grant note disclosure).
2.  **Single Audit / Program Specific Audit (US):** Compliance + Internal Controls over Major Programs ($750k+ threshold).
3.  **Acquittal Audit / Grant Certification:** Specific engagement to certify the *acquittal statement only* (Common in Australia/UK/Philanthropy). Cheaper than full audit.
4.  **Funder Site Visit / Monitoring Review:** Program officers visit. Not a formal audit, but findings are binding.
5.  **Forensic Audit:** Triggered by suspicion of fraud. Lawyers lead; privilege applies.

### 8.2 The Audit Cycle: A Project Management Approach

#### Phase 1: Planning (T-60 Days)
*   **Engagement Letter:** Review scope. Does it cover *all* funder requirements? (e.g., "Auditor to verify compliance with CGRG Clause 5.4").
*   **Entrance Conference:** Meet auditor. Agree on timeline, key contacts, data transfer method (Secure portal > Email).
*   **Risk Assessment:** Provide auditor with: Org Chart, Delegations Register, Risk Register, Prior Year Management Letter.

#### Phase 2: Fieldwork (T-30 to T-5)
*   **Sample Selection:** Auditor picks transactions (Statistical or Judgmental).
    *   *Typical Sample:* 25-60 transactions (Payroll, Procurement, Travel, Assets).
*   **The "PBC" Dance:** Auditor requests -> You provide -> Auditor tests -> Auditor queries -> You respond.
*   **Critical Control:** **Single Point of Contact (SPOC).** All auditor queries go through the Grant Manager/Finance Lead. **No direct auditor-staff contact** without SPOC knowledge. Prevents contradictory answers.

#### Phase 3: Reporting (T+14 to T+30)
*   **Draft Management Letter / Findings:** Review for factual accuracy. Dispute *facts*, not *judgments*.
*   **Exit Conference:** Discuss findings. Negotiate "Recommendation" wording.
*   **Final Report:** Signed Opinion + Management Letter.

#### Phase 4: Remediation (The Most Important Phase)
*   **Management Action Plan (MAP):** For every finding:
    *   Finding Reference
    *   Root Cause (5 Whys analysis)
    *   Corrective Action (Specific, Measurable)
    *   Responsible Officer
    *   Target Date
    *   Status (Open/Closed/Verified)
*   **Board/Audit Committee Oversight:** MAP must be a standing agenda item until all "High/Medium" items closed.

## 9. The Top 10 Compliance Failures and How to Avoid Them

Based on analysis of Auditor-General reports (AU, US, UK, CA) and Inspector General alerts over the last decade.

### Failure 1: The "Budget Drift" (Unauthorized Variations)
*   **Scenario:** Project needs change. Staff move money from "Travel" to "Equipment" without asking. Final acquittal shows $50k overspend on Equipment.
*   **Rule:** Most deeds require **prior written approval** for variations >10% or >$10k (or moving funds *into* Capital/Admin lines).
*   **Fix:** **Monthly Budget vs. Actual Review.** Flag variances >5% immediately. Submit Variation Request *before* spending. Automate alerts in Finance System.

### Failure 2: Ineligible Costs – The "Grey Zone" Spending
*   **Scenario:** Charging "Entertainment" (staff Christmas party), "Lobbying" (meeting Minister), "Alcohol," "Fines/Penalties," "Donations," or "Costs incurred before start date / after end date."
*   **Rule:** **2 CFR 200.420-475 (Selected Items of Cost)** or PGPA "Proper Use" provisions. Explicit unallowable lists exist.
*   **Fix:** **Pre-Payment Checklist** for Accounts Payable. "Is this cost allowable per Grant Agreement Clause X?" checkbox on Payment Authorization Form.

### Failure 3: Timekeeping Fraud / "Budget Charging"
*   **Scenario:** PI works 10% on Grant A, 90% on Grant B. Payslip splits 50/50 because "that's the budget."
*   **Rule:** **After-the-fact, actual effort.** (2 CFR 200.430(i)).
*   **Fix:** Implement **Effort Certification Software** (or rigorous manual process). Monthly certification for split-funded staff. Semi-annual for 100% funded. Train PIs: "Certifying false effort is research misconduct/fraud."

### Failure 4: Procurement Breaches – Sole Source Abuse
*   **Scenario:** "We always use Vendor X." No quotes. Vendor X is Director's cousin (undeclared COI).
*   **Rule:** Open competition thresholds. COI declarations *before* evaluation.
*   **Fix:** **Procurement Gateway Process.** No PO raised without attached: 1. Quotes/Eval, 2. COI Forms, 3. Delegation Approval. System-enforced (ERP workflow).

### Failure 5: The "Missing In-Kind" Valuation
*   **Scenario:** Grant requires $200k In-Kind (volunteer labor, donated space). Acquittal says "Volunteers helped." No hours, no rate, no sign-in sheets.
*   **Rule:** In-Kind must be **verifiable, auditable, and valued at Fair Market Value**.
*   **Fix:** Treat In-Kind like Cash. **Volunteer Timesheets** (signed by supervisor). **Market Rate Schedule** (e.g., "Admin Volunteer = $30/hr based on Award Rate"). Rent valuation = Independent valuation or comparable lease.

### Failure 6: Interest Income "Leakage"
*   **Scenario:** Grant sits in high-interest saver for 6 months. Org earns $15k interest. Keeps it in general revenue.
*   **Rule:** Most gov deeds: Interest = Property of Crown/Funder (or must be spent on project).
*   **Fix:** **Segregated Bank Account** (or Sub-ledger tracking). Monthly interest calculation journal: `Dr Bank / Cr Grant Revenue (Liability)` or `Cr Project Income` (if allowed). Report in Acquittal.

### Failure 7: Asset Management Failures (Capital Equipment)
*   **Scenario:** Buys $100k vehicle. No asset tag. Not on Asset Register. Sold 2 years later; proceeds kept.
*   **Rule:** Assets > Threshold ($5k/$10k) must be tagged, registered, insured, and **disposal requires funder approval** (proceeds often returned pro-rata).
*   **Fix:** **Capitalization Policy.** Asset Register fields: Grant ID, % Funded, Location, Condition, Disposal Date/Proceeds/Authority.

### Failure 8: Sub-recipient / Contractor Misclassification
*   **Scenario:** Hires "Consultant" who works 9-5, uses org email, managed by PI, no ABN/Business structure. Actually an employee (Sham Contracting). Payroll tax/Super/WorkCover unpaid.
*   **Rule:** **Control Test / Integration Test / Economic Reality Test.** (ATO PSI rules, IRS 20-Factor, UK IR35).
*   **Fix:** **Classification Checklist** at engagement. If "Employee" -> Put on Payroll. If "Sub-recipient" (carries programmatic risk) -> Flow-down clauses, Sub-recipient Monitoring (Risk Assessment, Audit Review).

### Failure 9: Late / Non-Submission of Acquittal
*   **Scenario:** Project ended June 30. Acquittal due Sept 30. Submitted Dec 15.
*   **Consequence:** Funder freezes *all* payments to org (including other grants). "Overdue" flag on CRM. Reputational hit.
*   **Fix:** **Acquittal Calendar.** Treat due date as **Internal Due Date = Funder Due Date minus 4 Weeks**. Build "Acquittal Sprint" into project plan (Week 1: Data dump; Week 2: Reconcile; Week 3: Draft; Week 4: Review/Sign/Submit).

### Failure 10: Data Integrity / "Version Control Chaos"
*   **Scenario:** Auditor asks for "Final Acquittal Workpaper." Finance sends v12. Program sends v9. Numbers don't match. Auditor expands sample size (costs more, finds more).
*   **Fix:** **Single Source of Truth (SSOT).** One Master Reconciliation Workbook (Excel/PowerBI/Adaptive) locked down. All supporting schedules feed *into* it. Version control: `v1.0_DRAFT`, `v1.1_MGR_REVIEW`, `v2.0_FINAL_SIGNED`.
