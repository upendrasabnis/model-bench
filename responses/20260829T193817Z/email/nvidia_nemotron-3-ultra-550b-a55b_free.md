Thank you for your enquiry regarding the Community Resilience Grant round. It is encouraging to hear about the valuable work your community garden is doing in a regional setting, and I am happy to provide clarification on the eligibility criteria and application requirements to assist with your planning.

**Eligibility of Auspiced Groups**
Unincorporated groups operating under a formal auspicing arrangement with an eligible legal entity—such as a local council—are eligible to apply, provided the auspicing organisation meets the program’s eligibility requirements (e.g., incorporated not-for-profit, local government authority, or other eligible entity). The council must sign the grant agreement, assume legal and financial responsibility for the project, and hold the required insurances (public liability, workers’ compensation, etc.). Please ensure you have a written auspicing agreement in place that clearly outlines the roles, responsibilities, and financial arrangements between your group and the council prior to submitting an application.

**Grant Amounts**
For this round, the minimum grant request is $5,000 and the maximum is $50,000. Your proposed project budget should fall within this range. Given your annual turnover of approximately $40,000, please ensure the requested amount is proportionate to your organisational capacity and the scale of the project.

**Eligible Costs – Infrastructure**
Capital infrastructure that directly supports community resilience—such as rainwater tanks and permanent shade structures—is considered an eligible cost, provided the assets remain with the auspicing organisation or the project site for the useful life of the infrastructure. Costs for site preparation, installation, and necessary permits are also eligible. Ongoing maintenance costs, however, are not funded under this program.

**Supporting Documents to Prepare**
To avoid delays, please have the following ready for upload with your application:
1. Signed auspicing agreement between your group and the council.
2. Council’s current Certificate of Currency for public liability and workers’ compensation insurance.
3. Quotes (minimum two) for the rainwater tanks and shade structure, including installation.
4. Evidence of land tenure or a letter of support from the landowner (if the council does not own the site).
5. Your group’s most recent financial statement (income/expenditure) and a simple project budget.
6. A brief project plan outlining timelines, risk management, and how the infrastructure will enhance resilience during extreme heat events.

The application portal opens on 1 May and closes at 5:00 pm AEST on 31 May. I strongly recommend starting the draft early so you have time to gather the required documents and seek feedback from your council’s grants officer.
