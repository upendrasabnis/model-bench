**Eligibility of unincorporated groups**  
Our grant programme welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based organisations. Unincorporated groups that are formally **auspiced or sponsored by a local government or other eligible entity** are indeed eligible, provided the auspicing body can demonstrate that it will be responsible for the grant funds, maintain the required insurances, and act as the primary point of contact for reporting purposes. In your case, the fact that your council already holds the relevant insurances and can act as the fiscal sponsor satisfies this requirement. Please note that you will still need to submit a brief auspice agreement (or a letter of support) from the council confirming that it will oversee financial reporting and compliance.

**Grant amount – minimum and maximum**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award amount. Instead, we assess proposals on the basis of **cost‑effectiveness, community impact, and alignment with programme objectives**. Typical awards have ranged from $5,000 to $150,000, but we have funded projects at both lower and higher price points depending on the scope and urgency of the need. For your rainwater‑tank and shade‑structure project, a realistic budget would likely fall within the $20,000–$60,000 band, though we are open to reviewing any detailed cost estimate you provide.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure that directly supports **resilience to climate‑related challenges** is fully eligible under the grant. Specifically, the installation of rainwater harvesting systems, permeable paving, shade structures, and other climate‑adaptive features are considered acceptable expenditures. The key is that the infrastructure must be **directly linked to the project’s resilience objectives** (e.g., reducing water consumption during heat events, providing safer working conditions for volunteers, and ensuring continuity of food‑production activities). Please ensure that each line item in your budget is clearly tied to these outcomes.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
When you move forward with a full application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:

1. **Project Proposal Form** – a concise narrative (maximum 2 pages) describing the problem, proposed solution, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Budget and Narrative Breakdown** – a table itemising all costs, with brief justification for each line item.  
3. **Auspice/Letter of Support** – an official letter from your local council confirming its sponsorship, insurance coverage, and willingness to act as fiscal agent.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – a one‑page overview of your group, including volunteer numbers, governance structure (even if informal), and any previous community projects.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss summary and cash‑flow statement) covering the last fiscal year.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Management Plan** – outlining potential environmental, safety, and operational risks associated with the installation work and how they will be mitigated.  
7. **Community Engagement Evidence** – minutes or summaries from public meetings, letters of support from neighbouring residents, or promotional material demonstrating local backing.  
8. **Site Plans or Sketches** – a simple diagram showing the proposed location of rainwater tanks and the shade structure within the garden.
