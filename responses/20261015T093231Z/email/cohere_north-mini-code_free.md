Thank you for reaching out and for the enthusiasm you have for enhancing your community garden’s resilience. I’m pleased to provide the following clarification on the points you raised, and I hope it helps you to decide confidently whether to proceed with a full application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups**  
Our Community Resilience Grant program welcomes applications from both incorporated not‑for‑profit entities and unincorporated groups that are formally auspiced by a public‑sector body such as your local council. When an organisation is auspiced, the council assumes legal responsibility for the grant funds and any related liabilities, and it also provides the required insurances. Therefore, your garden’s current arrangement—where the council holds the relevant insurances on your behalf—should satisfy the eligibility requirement for “unincorporated groups.” We do request a letter of auspice from the council confirming that they will act as the primary recipient of the grant and that they will manage the funds in accordance with our guidelines.

**Grant amount – minimum and maximum**  
The program does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award; instead, we assess each proposal against its scope, impact, and alignment with the program’s objectives. Typical awards for community‑infrastructure projects in the recent rounds have ranged from roughly $5,000 to $75,000, though larger amounts can be considered for more extensive initiatives. Your projected costs for rainwater tanks and a shade structure appear to fall well within this band, but we will evaluate the full budget in the context of the overall community benefit.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes—capital assets such as rainwater harvesting systems and durable shade structures are expressly listed as eligible expenditures under the “Infrastructure and Equipment” category. We require that these items be essential to the project’s core objectives, demonstrate good value for money, and have a useful life of at least five years. Any ancillary costs (e.g., installation, permits, and contingency) may also be included, provided they are justified in the budget breakdown.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend compiling the following materials:

1. **Project Narrative** – a clear description of the problem you are addressing (e.g., heat stress during summer), the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and how the project aligns with the grant’s resilience themes.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – itemised costs for tanks, shade structure, installation, permits, and any other expenses, together with a brief justification for each line item.  
3. **Letter of Auspice** – an official statement from your council confirming their support and their role as fiscal host.  
4. **Insurance Certificate** – a copy of the council’s liability insurance policy (or a letter confirming coverage) that names your garden as a covered activity.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and balance sheet) for the year ending [date]; for unincorporated groups, a cash‑flow summary is acceptable.  
6. **Member/Volunteer Signatures** – a brief endorsement signed by at least three of your core volunteers or committee members, demonstrating community backing.  
7. **Site Plans or Sketches** – a simple diagram showing where the tanks and shade structure will be located.  
8. **Letters of Support** – optional but valuable, such as endorsements from local businesses, schools, or other community organisations.

We strongly encourage you to review the full grant guidelines on our website (particularly the “Eligibility” and “What We Fund” sections) before submitting your application. If any of the above documents require additional clarification, please feel free to ask—our program officers are happy to assist.

Should you decide to move forward, please submit your application through the online portal no later than the closing date. We look forward to reviewing your compelling proposal and supporting the garden’s efforts to become more resilient for the whole community.
