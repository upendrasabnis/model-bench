Thank you for reaching out and for the enthusiasm you have for strengthening the resilience of your community garden. I’m pleased to provide the following clarification on the eligibility criteria and application requirements for the upcoming Community Resilience Grant round.  

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines expressly permit applications from unincorporated community groups that operate under the formal auspices of a local government body. In such cases, the council’s legal standing and existing insurance coverage satisfy the liability and risk‑management requirements that we normally expect of registered charities. Provided that the council can confirm in writing that it will administer the grant funds on the group’s behalf and that all activities are aligned with the council’s community‑service objectives, your organization is eligible to apply.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to be flexible, and there is no preset minimum amount. Funding decisions are based on the scope, impact, and alignment with program priorities rather than a fixed ceiling. Historically, successful projects have received between $5,000 and $75,000, but we have approved grants both below and above this range when the justification is compelling. If your preliminary budget for the rainwater tanks, shade structure, and associated installation costs is significantly higher, we can discuss scaling the proposal to fit the available funds.  

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, capital infrastructure that enhances environmental sustainability and operational continuity is fully eligible under the “Climate‑Resilience and Adaptation” category of the grant. Specifically, rainwater harvesting equipment (tanks, collection systems, and plumbing) and permanent shade structures (e.g., pergolas, canopies) are considered acceptable expenditures because they directly support water security and reduce heat stress on both volunteers and produce. We recommend that you clearly articulate how each component will mitigate climate‑related risks (e.g., water shortage, extreme heat) in your narrative.  

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To streamline the assessment process, we suggest assembling the following documents in a single PDF file:  

1. **Project Description** – a concise overview (max. 2 pages) that outlines the problem you are addressing, the proposed solution, timeline, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Budget and Finance Sheet** – a detailed breakdown of all costs, including equipment quotes, installation labor, contingency, and any in‑kind contributions.  
3. **Organizational Profile** – a short profile of your garden, its volunteer base, and the role of the council as your auspicing body, including the council’s letter of support (if not already provided).  
4. **Insurance & Liability Confirmation** – a copy of the council’s insurance certificate confirming coverage for the project activities, or a letter stating that coverage will extend to the new infrastructure.  
5. **Partnership Agreements** – any memoranda of understanding with local schools, businesses, or other stakeholders that will contribute to the project.  
6. **Evaluation Plan** – a simple framework describing how you will measure success (e.g., water usage reduction, increased produce yield, volunteer satisfaction).  

We also recommend reviewing the full grant guidelines on our website (section “Eligibility & Guidelines”) to ensure that any additional requirements—such as environmental impact assessments or compliance with local building codes—are addressed.  

Finally, please note that the application portal will open two weeks from today, and we strongly advise submitting a draft to your council’s administration office for review before the official deadline. Early internal vetting can help identify any gaps that might otherwise delay a successful submission.
