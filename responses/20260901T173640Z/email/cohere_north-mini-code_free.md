Thank you for reaching out and for the opportunity to clarify the eligibility criteria for our Community Resilience Grant round. I appreciate your diligence in reviewing the requirements before investing time in a full application, and I am happy to address each of the points you raised.

**1. Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme welcomes applications from both incorporated not‑for‑profit organisations and unincorporated community groups, provided they operate under a formal auspice arrangement with a recognised entity such as a local council. In your case, the fact that the council holds the necessary insurances on your behalf is generally acceptable; the council’s involvement can serve as evidence of governance and risk management. However, we do require a written agreement (or a letter of support) from the council confirming that it will act as the fiscal sponsor for any awarded funds. This document should outline the council’s responsibilities regarding fund disbursement, reporting, and any required accountability measures. If your group is not yet a registered charity, the council’s auspice can satisfy the “legal entity” requirement, but you will still need to demonstrate that the council is willing and able to administer the grant on your behalf.

**2. Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award amount. Instead, we assess each proposal against the anticipated impact, the proportion of the total project cost that the grant would cover, and the availability of other funding sources. Typical awards for infrastructure projects in the $30‑$80 k range have been successful, but we are open to considering larger or smaller requests as long as the budget is realistic and well‑justified. Please note that the grant can fund up to 50 % of eligible project costs; the remaining balance must be secured through other means (e.g., council contributions, in‑kind donations, or member fundraising).

**3. Eligibility of rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Infrastructure of the type you describe—rainwater harvesting systems and protective shade structures—is explicitly listed as eligible costs under the “Climate‑Resilient Community Infrastructure” component of the grant. Both items must directly support the project’s objective of enhancing operational resilience during hot summer periods, and they should be justified with technical specifications, cost estimates, and evidence of need (e.g., historical water usage data, heat‑stress impact assessments). It is important to ensure that any proposed equipment meets relevant Australian standards and local building regulations.

**4. Recommended supporting documents**  
To strengthen your application, we suggest preparing the following documentation:

* **Project plan and budget** – a clear timeline, breakdown of costs (including contingency), and explanation of how each cost aligns with grant criteria.  
* **Council auspice agreement** – a formal letter or memorandum of understanding confirming that the council will act as fiscal sponsor, detailing insurance coverage, procurement procedures, and reporting obligations.  
* ** organisational profile** – a brief constitution or set of guiding principles, even if the group is unincorporated, to demonstrate its community purpose and governance structure.  
* **Financial statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for unincorporated groups, the latest set of financial statements prepared by the council) showing an annual turnover of approximately $40 k.  
* **Insurance certificate** – confirmation that the council’s insurance policy covers the activities that will be conducted with the new infrastructure.  
* **Community support** – letters of support from local residents, businesses, or partner organisations, as well as any membership sign‑ups or volunteer commitments.  
* **Technical specifications** – product datasheets, supplier quotes, and installation plans for the rainwater tanks and shade structure.  
* **Sustainability and maintenance plan** – outlines how the garden will maintain, monitor, and evaluate the new infrastructure over the long term.
