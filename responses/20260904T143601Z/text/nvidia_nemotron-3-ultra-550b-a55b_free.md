## 1. Introduction: The Currency of Trust

In the social sector, **trust is the only currency that matters.** Money is merely the vehicle for that trust.

When a government agency awards a $5 million contract for workforce development, or a family foundation grants $50,000 for a pilot literacy program, they are not simply purchasing services. They are investing in a hypothesis: *This organization, with this leadership, using this methodology, will achieve this specific public good.*

**Grant compliance and acquittal reporting are the mechanisms by which that hypothesis is tested and verified.**

Too often, organizations treat compliance as a bureaucratic hurdle—a "back-office" function to be rushed through at the 11th hour before a deadline. This is a strategic error of the highest order. Compliance is not the opposite of program delivery; it is the **evidence base** for program delivery. An organization that cannot account for its funds cannot credibly claim to have achieved its outcomes.

This article moves beyond the "how-to" of filling out forms. It provides an architectural blueprint for a compliance infrastructure that satisfies the most rigorous auditors, satisfies the most demanding philanthropists, and—critically—protects the organization’s reputation and future funding viability.

## 2. Defining the Acquittal: More Than a Receipt

### 2.1 What is an Acquittal?
Technically, an **acquittal** (often used interchangeably with *financial report*, *expenditure report*, *closeout report*, or *certification of expenditure*) is the formal process by which a grantee demonstrates to a funder that:
1.  **Funds were spent:** The money left the bank account.
2.  **Funds were spent *correctly*:** Expenditures align with the approved budget categories (Chart of Accounts).
3.  **Funds were spent *allowably*:** Costs adhere to the funder’s specific guidelines (e.g., no alcohol, no lobbying, specific salary caps).
4.  **Funds were spent *reasonably*:** Costs pass the "prudent person" test (fair market value, necessary for the project).
5.  **Outcomes were achieved:** The programmatic deliverables promised in the proposal were met (or variances explained).

### 2.2 The Two Pillars: Financial vs. Programmatic
Most funders bifurcate the acquittal into two distinct but linked workstreams:

| Pillar | Core Question | Primary Evidence | Typical Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Acquittal** | "Did you spend the money exactly as budgeted?" | General Ledger (GL) extracts, bank statements, payroll registers, invoices, receipts, timesheets. | Quarterly / Annually / End-of-Grant |
| **Programmatic Acquittal** | "Did you do what you said you would do?" | KPI dashboards, beneficiary lists, case studies, evaluation reports, photos, published papers. | Quarterly / Semi-Annually / End-of-Grant |

### 2.3 The "Certification" Moment
The acquittal culminates in a **certification**—a legal sign-off by an authorized representative (usually CEO, CFO, or Board Treasurer).
> *"I certify that the expenditures reported are accurate, allowable, and allocable to the award, and that programmatic outcomes have been achieved per the agreement."*

**This is a personal legal liability.** Signing a false acquittal constitutes fraud. This reality must drive the internal control environment.

## 3. The Compliance Lifecycle: From Award to Closeout

Compliance is not an event; it is a continuous cycle. Failure at any stage compounds exponentially.

```mermaid
graph LR
    A[Award Notification] --> B[Kickoff & Budget Load]
    B --> C[Monthly: Transaction Coding & Timesheets]
    C --> D[Quarterly: Interim Reporting & Reforecasting]
    D --> E[Annual: Audit / Single Audit]
    E --> F[End of Grant: Final Acquittal Package]
    F --> G[Closeout & Document Retention]
    G --> H[Lessons Learned / Next Proposal]
```

### Phase 1: Post-Award Setup (Days 1–30) — *The "Golden Window"*
*   **Read the Agreement *Line by Line*:** Not just the budget. Read the *Special Conditions*, *Flow-down Clauses*, *Intellectual Property rights*, *Publication restrictions*, and *Termination for Convenience* clauses.
*   **Budget Load:** Enter the approved budget into your accounting system (QuickBooks, Sage Intacct, NetSuite, Xero, Adaptive) as a **Project/Grant Dimension**. Do not rely on spreadsheets.
*   **Chart of Accounts Mapping:** Map funder budget categories (e.g., "Personnel," "Fringe," "Travel," "Equipment," "Indirect Costs") to your internal GL codes.
*   **Delegation of Authority:** Define *who* can approve what. Can a Program Manager approve a $5,000 subcontractor invoice? Does the CFO need to sign off on travel >$500?
*   **Subrecipient vs. Contractor Determination:** Critical distinction (see Section 9). If passing funds to another entity, execute a Subaward Agreement *before* money moves.

### Phase 2: Operational Execution (Monthly)
*   **Timesheets:** The #1 audit finding globally. Staff working on *multiple* grants must complete **after-the-fact** timesheets (Personnel Activity Reports - PARs) showing actual effort % per grant. Estimates/budgets are not evidence.
*   **Invoice Coding:** Every Accounts Payable (AP) invoice must be coded to: **Grant Code + Budget Category + Cost Center**.
*   **Reconciliation:** Monthly bank reconciliation *per grant* (or pooled cash reconciliation with grant-level sub-ledger).

### Phase 3: Interim Reporting (Quarterly/Semi-Annually)
*   **Variance Analysis:** Don't just report numbers. Explain *why* Personnel is 15% underspent (vacancy) or Supplies 20% overspent (price surge).
*   **Reforecasting:** Submit a revised budget projection if variances exceed 10-20% (thresholds vary by funder).
*   **Cash Management:** Report cash on hand (Federal: minimize time between drawdown and disbursement per 2 CFR 200.305).

### Phase 4: Year-End / Single Audit (Annually)
*   **SEFA Preparation:** Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (or State/Philanthropic equivalent).
*   **Indirect Cost Rate:** Finalize provisional rate or negotiate final rate (NICRA).

### Phase 5: Final Acquittal & Closeout (Grant End Date + 90–120 Days)
*   **Final Financial Report (FFR):** Cumulative actuals vs. Budget.
*   **Refund/Remittance:** Return unspent funds (unless carryover approved).
*   **Property Disposition:** Report on equipment purchased (>$5k usually); request disposition instructions.
*   **Final Program Report:** Narrative summary, data sets, publications list.
*   **Record Retention Trigger:** Start the clock (usually 3–7 years post-closeout).

## 4. Common Requirements Across Funder Types

While the *principles* are universal (allowability, allocability, reasonableness), the *mechanics* differ wildly.

### 4.1 Government Funders (The Compliance Heavyweight)
**Key Frameworks:** **2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance)** – The "Bible" for US Federal funds. State/Local often adopt "flow-down" versions.

#### A. Cost Principles (Subpart E)
*   **Allowability:** Cost must be *necessary*, *reasonable*, *allocable*, *consistent with policies*, *adequately documented*, and *not prohibited*.
*   **Selected Items of Cost (200.420–.475):** The "Green Book" of specific rules.
    *   *Travel:* Fly America Act (US flag carriers), per diem limits (GSA rates), no first class.
    *   *Entertainment/Alcohol:* **Strictly Unallowable.** (Even a glass of wine at a donor dinner charged to the grant = finding).
    *   *Lobbying:* Unallowable. Requires separate tracking (SF-LLL form).
    *   *Salary Caps:* NIH Salary Cap, Statutory caps (e.g., $212,100 for certain DOE grants in 2024).
    *   *Equipment:* Capitalization threshold ($5k or $10k federal; lower for some states). Title usually vests in grantee *but* federal interest remains.

#### B. Indirect Costs (F&A / Overhead)
*   **Negotiated Rate (NICRA):** If you have one, you *must* use it.
*   **De Minimis Rate:** 10% of Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC) if never negotiated a rate. **Electing this rate is binding for the life of the grant.**
*   **MTDC Base:** Excludes: Equipment (>5k), Capital Expenditures, Patient Care, Tuition Remission, Rental of Facilities, Scholarships, Subawards >$25k each, Participant Support Costs.

#### C. Procurement Standards (200.317–.327)
*   **Micro-purchase (<$10k):** No quotes needed, but must be reasonable/distributed equitably.
*   **Simplified Acquisition ($10k–$250k):** Obtain price quotes from "adequate number" of vendors (usually 3).
*   **Formal Procurement (>$250k):** Sealed bids (IFB) or Competitive Proposals (RFP).
*   **Sole Source Justification:** Heavy documentation required (only one source, emergency, public exigency).
*   **Domestic Preference:** Buy American / Build America Buy America Act (BABAA) for infrastructure.

#### D. Subrecipient Monitoring (200.331)
*   **Risk Assessment:** Pre-award questionnaire, financial stability review, Single Audit review.
*   **Flow-down:** All 2 CFR 200 requirements flow down to subrecipients.
*   **Monitoring:** Site visits, desk reviews, invoice sampling during the grant.

#### E. Reporting
*   **SF-425 (Federal Financial Report):** Standard form. Cash basis or Accrual basis (must be consistent).
*   **SAM.gov / FAPIIS:** Registration maintenance, performance reporting (PPR), integrity reporting.

---

### 4.2 Philanthropic Foundations (The Relationship Managers)
**Key Philosophy:** "Trust-based philanthropy" is trending, but **accountability remains.** Requirements are defined by the *Grant Agreement*, not a federal code.

#### A. Budget Flexibility
*   **Line-item flexibility:** Often 10–25% variance allowed without prior approval.
*   **No Indirect Cost Rate negotiation:** Foundations often pay a flat % (10–15%) or "Full Cost" model. Some restrict overhead entirely (requiring "direct cost only" budgets).
*   **Restricted vs. Unrestricted:** The acquittal for *unrestricted* funds is often just a narrative report + audited financials. *Restricted* funds require project-level tracking.

#### B. Reporting Cadence
*   **Narrative Heavy:** Less focus on GL extracts; more on "Stories of Change," logic model updates, DEI metrics, and learning.
*   **Multi-year Grants:** Annual "check-ins" vs. formal acquittals. Final report at end of term.

#### C. Common Unique Requirements
*   **Lobbying/Advocacy Tracking:** Private foundations (US) cannot fund lobbying (IRS 4945). Grantees must certify funds not used for lobbying. Requires separate tracking codes.
*   **Intellectual Property:** Foundations increasingly require **Open Access** (Creative Commons licensing) for research/outputs.
*   **Participatory Grantmaking:** Grantees may be asked to evaluate *the foundation*.

#### D. "Trust-Based" Nuance
*   *Example:* The **MacArthur Foundation** or **Mackenzie Scott** gifts often have *no* formal reporting requirements. **Best Practice:** Self-impose a rigorous internal acquittal. Produce a "Stewardship Report" voluntarily. It builds relational capital for renewal.

---

### 4.3 International & Multilateral Donors (The Complexity Multipliers)
**Key Players:** USAID, DFAT (Australia), FCDO (UK), Global Fund, Gavi, EU (PRAG), World Bank.

#### A. USAID (2 CFR 700 / ADS 303)
*   **Branding & Marking:** Strict logo placement rules on *everything* (vehicles, buildings, t-shirts, social media).
*   **Source/Nationality Rules:** **Code 937 / 941.** Commodities/services must originate from US or developing country (waivers are hard).
*   **Fly America Act:** Strict enforcement.
*   **Cost Share / Leverage:** Mandatory tracking of "in-kind" and cash match. Valuation rules are strict (fair market value, volunteer rates).
*   **Pipeline/Accrual Accounting:** USAID often requires **Accrual-based** reporting (obligations + expenditures), not just cash.

#### B. UK FCDO / DFAT
*   **Logframes & Theory of Change:** Rigid adherence to approved Logframe indicators. Changes require formal "Change Request" (CR) forms.
*   **Value for Money (VfM):** Explicit analysis required: Economy, Efficiency, Effectiveness, Equity (4 Es).
*   **Fraud/Whistleblowing:** Mandatory reporting of *any* suspected fraud to funder within 24–48 hours.

#### C. EU / Global Fund
*   **Eligibility Rules:** Very specific definitions of "Eligible Expenditure" (e.g., depreciation policies, VAT treatment).
*   **Ex-ante / Ex-post Controls:** Pre-verification of procurement files before payment (Ex-ante) or intense post-audit (Ex-post).
*   **Double Funding Checks:** Cross-referencing beneficiary lists across grants.

## 5. Financial Acquittal Mechanics: The General Ledger as Evidence

The acquittal *is* the General Ledger (GL) filtered for a specific Grant Code + Date Range. If your GL is messy, your acquittal will be wrong.

### 5.1 The Chart of Accounts (CoA) Design: The Foundation
**Bad Design:** One "Grant Expense" account. Details in Excel.
**Good Design:** Multi-dimensional segmentation.

| Segment | Example Values | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fund** | `FED-DOE-2024`, `FORD-FDN-2023`, `UNRESTRICTED` | Legal entity / Funding Source |
| **Project/Grant** | `PRJ-WORKFORCE-01`, `PRJ-LITERACY-PILOT` | Specific Award |
| **Activity/Task** | `ACT-TRAINING`, `ACT-ADMIN`, `ACT-EVAL` | Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) |
| **Natural Account** | `5010-Salaries`, `5200-Travel`, `5500-Subcontract` | Nature of Expense |
| **Cost Category** | `DIRECT`, `INDIRECT`, `COST-SHARE`, `UNALLOWABLE` | Compliance Tagging |

**Result:** You can run a "Trial Balance by Grant by Category" that *is* the SF-425 or Foundation Financial Report.

### 5.2 Personnel Costs: The High-Risk Zone (60–80% of budgets)
**The Golden Rule:** *Payroll systems do not speak "Grant." You must translate.*

#### A. After-the-Fact Certification (PARs / Effort Reporting)
*   **Who:** Anyone paid *partially* from a grant (or multiple grants).
*   **When:** At least monthly (best practice) or semi-annually (federal minimum for some entities).
*   **Format:** % Effort per Grant/Project. Total = 100%.
*   **Sign-off:** Employee *and* Supervisor (who has first-hand knowledge).
*   **System:** Paper forms die. Use **HRIS integration** (BambooHR, Workday, UKG, Paylocity) or Grant Management Systems (Fluxx, Foundant, Salesforce NPSP, AmpliFund) with timesheet modules.

#### B. Fringe Benefits
*   **Pool Rate vs. Actuals:** Most orgs use a **Fringe Rate** (e.g., 32% of salary).
*   **Reconciliation:** At year-end, compare Actual Fringe Pool Costs vs. Fringe Charged to Grants.
    *   *Over-recovery:* Must refund funders or reduce future rates.
    *   *Under-recovery:* Organization absorbs loss (usually).
*   **Components:** FICA, Workers Comp, Health, Retirement, Tuition, Leave accruals.

#### C. Salary Caps & "Extra Service Pay"
*   **NIH Cap:** $212,100 (2024). If PI earns $300k, only $212,100 is chargeable to NIH. The "over-cap" $87,900 *must* be paid from unrestricted funds. **Mechanism:** Cost transfer or separate "Unallowable" GL code.
*   **Academic Year vs. Summer:** 9-month faculty charging summer salary = "Extra Service." Must be documented as *additional* work, not just "more time on same project."

### 5.3 Subrecipients vs. Contractors (The Classification Minefield)
**Misclassification = Major Audit Finding.**

| Characteristic | **Subrecipient** (Subaward) | **Contractor** (Vendor/Procurement) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Relationship** | Collaborative partner; performs *programmatic* work. | Vendor; provides *goods/services* for operations. |
| **Decision Making** | Has programmatic discretion; shapes approach. | Follows specs/SOW provided by you. |
| **Compliance** | Subject to 2 CFR 200 / Single Audit. | Subject to Procurement Standards only. |
| **Flow-down** | All prime terms flow down. | Only specific contract clauses apply. |
| **Risk** | High (Monitoring required). | Low (Invoice verification). |
| **Examples** | Partner NGO doing case management; University lab doing analysis. | Audit firm; Cloud hosting (AWS); Office supply co; Janitorial. |

**The "But they are a 501(c)(3)!" Trap:** Legal status ≠ Role. A law firm (LLC) can be a subrecipient if they are *co-designing* a legal aid model. A university (nonprofit) is a contractor if they just run a lab assay per your protocol.

### 5.4 Indirect Cost (IDC) Calculation Mechanics
**MTDC Base Calculation (Monthly/Quarterly):**
1.  Total Direct Costs YTD.
2.  **Less:** Equipment (> $5k/10k).
3.  **Less:** Capital Expenditures.
4.  **Less:** Patient Care Costs.
5.  **Less:** Tuition Remission.
6.  **Less:** Rental Costs of Leased Facilities.
7.  **Less:** Scholarships/Fellowships.
8.  **Less:** Participant Support Costs (Stipends, Travel for trainees).
9.  **Less:** Subaward amounts **exceeding $25k per subaward** (First $25k *is* in base).
10. **= MTDC.**
11. **IDC Charge = MTDC x Negotiated Rate %.**

*Automation Tip:* Configure your ERP to calculate MTDC dynamically. Manual Excel calculation = Error risk.

### 5.5 Cost Transfers (The "Correction" That Looks Like Fraud)
Moving an expense from Grant A to Grant B *after* the fact.
*   **Policy:** Must be done within **90 days** (best practice) or **120 days** (federal max).
*   **Documentation Required:**
    1.  Why was it charged incorrectly originally? (Error, late award, reclassification).
    2.  Why is the *new* grant correct? (Benefit received).
    3.  PI/Dept Head + Grants Manager + Controller signatures.
*   **Red Flag:** Transfers *to* a grant ending soon, or *from* an overspent grant. Auditors sample these heavily.

## 6. Performance & Impact Reporting: Proving the "So What?"

Financial compliance keeps you out of jail; programmatic compliance gets you refunded.

### 6.1 The Logic Model as Reporting Backbone
Your acquittal narrative must map 1:1 to the Logic Model in the approved proposal.

| Logic Model Column | Acquittal Evidence Required |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Inputs** (Budget) | Financial Report (Covered above) |
| **Activities** (What you did) | **Output Metrics:** # Workshops held, # Patients seen, # Curricula developed. *Must match approved targets.* |
| **Outputs** (Direct products) | **Deliverables:** Reports submitted, Toolkits published, Software code pushed to GitHub. |
| **Outcomes** (Changes) | **Outcome Metrics:** % Job placement rate, Reading level increase, Policy changed. *Requires data collection systems.* |
| **Impact** (Long-term) | Usually beyond grant period. Report on *trajectory* or longitudinal study design. |

### 6.2 Quantitative Reporting: Data Integrity
*   **Source Systems:** Case Management (Apricot, Social Solutions, Salesforce), LMS (Canvas, Moodle), Survey Tools (Qualtrics, KoboToolbox).
*   **De-duplication:** Unique Client IDs across systems. A person served in Q1 and Q2 counts as **1 Unique Individual**, 2 Service Units.
*   **Disaggregation:** Funders increasingly require data by Race, Ethnicity, Gender, Age, Geography, Disability Status. Build this into intake forms *now*.
*   **Variance Narrative:**
    *   *Target:* 100 jobs. *Actual:* 65.
    *   *Bad:* "Hard to find employers."
    *   *Good:* "Local manufacturing plant closure (Major Employer X) removed 200 entry-level roles in Q2. Pivoted to healthcare sector (MOU signed with Hospital Y). Projecting 90 by Year 2."

### 6.3 Qualitative Reporting: The "Color"
*   **Case Studies:** 1–2 page stories. **Anonymize** (HIPAA/FERPA). Structure: Challenge -> Intervention -> Result -> Quote.
*   **Photos/Media:** High-res, signed **Media Release Forms** on file. Caption: Date, Location, Activity, Funder Acknowledgement.
*   **Lessons Learned / Failure Reporting:** Funders value honesty. "We failed to recruit rural participants because transport voucher value ($15) < actual cost ($40). Increasing voucher in Year 2."

### 6.4 Evaluation Reporting
*   **Internal vs. External:** If budget >$500k, external evaluator often mandated.
*   **IRB:** Human subjects research requires Institutional Review Board approval *before* data collection. Acquittal must include IRB determination letter.
*   **Data Sharing:** Funders (NIH, NSF, USAID, Gates) increasingly require **Data Management Plans (DMP)** and deposition in repositories (Dryad, ICPSR, Figshare). Acquittal includes DOI links to datasets.

## 7. Record-Keeping Infrastructure: Building an Audit-Ready Organization

**"If it isn't documented, it didn't happen."** — Every Auditor, Ever.

### 7.1 The Document Hierarchy
1.  **Source Documents (Primary Evidence):** Original invoices, receipts, timesheets (signed), bank statements, cancelled checks, contracts, grant agreements.
2.  **Accounting Records (Secondary):** GL, Sub-ledgers, Journal Entries, Bank Recs, Payroll Registers.
3.  **Compliance Workpapers (Tertiary):** Indirect cost calc sheets, Cost share tracking, Subrecipient monitoring checklists, Procurement scorecards, PAR certifications.
4.  **Correspondence:** Email approvals for variances, waivers, prior approvals (equipment, foreign travel, scope change).

### 7.2 Digital vs. Physical: The "Paperless" Standard

### 7.3 Retention Schedules: The Legal Minimums
| Record Type | Federal (2 CFR 200.334) | Best Practice | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Records** | 3 years from **Final Report Submission** | **7 Years** | Covers IRS, State, most Foundation reqs. |
| **Property Records** | 3 years post-disposition | **7 Years post-disposition** | Equipment >$5k. |
| **Personnel / Timesheets** | 3 years | **7 Years** | ERISA/FLSA may require longer. |
| **Subrecipient Docs** | 3 years from *Sub's* final report | **7 Years** | Prime is liable for Sub's compliance. |
| **Audit Workpapers** | N/A (Auditor retains) | **7 Years** | Keep your copy of PBC (Prepared by Client) items. |
| **Emails (Grant-related)** | 3 years | **7 Years** | Use Litigation Hold / Retention Policies in M365/Google Vault. |

**Critical Nuance:** The "3 years" clock starts at **Final Report Submission to the Federal Agency**, not grant end date. A 5-year grant + 1 year closeout = 9 years minimum retention.

### 7.4 Disaster Recovery & Cybersecurity
*   **Backup:** 3-2-1 Rule (3 copies, 2 media types, 1 offsite/immutable).
*   **Ransomware:** Immutable backups (AWS S3 Object Lock, Wasabi, Veeam).
*   **Access Control:** Least privilege. Program staff *read* only their grant folders. Finance *write* to financial folders. ED/Controller *admin*.

## 8. The Audit Gauntlet: Preparing for and Surviving Scrutiny

### 8.1 Types of Audits
1.  **Single Audit (Uniform Guidance):** Required if **$750,000+** in Federal expenditures/year. Covers *Compliance* + *Internal Controls* + *Financial Statements*.
2.  **Financial Statement Audit (GAAS):** Annual opinion on org-wide financials. Required by most foundations >$1M revenue.
3.  **Funder-Specific / Desk Review:** Funder staff review your reports + sample 5–10 transactions remotely.
4.  **Site Visit / Monitoring Visit:** Funder (or Prime) comes on-site. Reviews files, interviews staff, observes operations.
5.  **OIG / Inspector General Audit:** Criminal/Civil fraud investigation. **Lawyer up immediately.**

### 8.2 The PBC (Prepared by Client) List: Your Battle Plan
Auditors send a "PBC List" 2–4 weeks before fieldwork. **Do not wait.**
*   **Standard Items:** Trial Balance (by Fund), GL Detail (by Fund), Bank Recs, Payroll Register, SEFA, Org Chart, Policies & Procedures Manual, Board Minutes, Prior Year Audit + Management Letter.
*   **Grant-Specific:** Grant Agreements, Budgets, Subrecipient Agreements, Procurement Files (sampled), Equipment Inventory, Time & Effort Reports (sampled), Indirect Cost Rate Proposal.

### 8.3 The "Sample" Logic: What Auditors Actually Test
Auditors use **Statistical Sampling** (e.g., Monetary Unit Sampling) or **Judgmental Sampling** (high risk/large $).
*   **Transaction Testing:** They pick 25–60 transactions. For *each*, they demand:
    1.  Invoice/Receipt.
    2.  Proof of Payment (Bank statement/Cancelled check).
    3.  Approval Email/Signature (Delegation of Authority).
    4.  Grant Coding (GL Screenshot).
    5.  Allowability Check (Is it in budget? Is it unallowable per 200.4xx?).
    6.  Allocability (Timesheet / Square footage / Headcount basis).

### 8.4 Common Audit Findings & Remediation
| Finding Type | Severity | Example | Remediation (Corrective Action Plan - CAP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Material Weakness (IC)** | Critical | No segregation of duties: ED approves invoices, signs checks, reconciles bank. | Hire Controller; implement dual-control in banking; Board Treasurer reviews bank rec. |
| **Significant Deficiency (IC)** | High | Timesheets approved *after* payroll run; missing supervisor sigs. | Implement workflow: Timesheet -> Supervisor Approve -> Payroll Process. Automate reminders. |
| **Non-Compliance (Allowability)** | High | $12k Alcohol charge on Federal grant. | Refund $12k to grant; update Policy; train staff; add "Unallowable" code block in AP workflow. |
| **Procurement Violation** | Med/High | $150k IT contract sole-sourced without justification. | Document retrospective justification (if valid) or disallow cost; update Procurement Policy; train PMs. |
| **Subrecipient Monitoring** | High | No risk assessment done; no site visit for high-risk sub. | Complete risk assessments retroactively; schedule visits; hire monitor consultant. |
| **Reporting Error** | Low/Med | SF-425 cash basis vs Accrual mismatch; Math error on MTDC. | Resubmit corrected report; implement peer-review checklist for reports. |

### 8.5 The Exit Conference & Management Letter
*   **Attend:** ED, CFO, Controller, Grant Manager.
*   **Dispute Factually:** "The $5k travel charge *was* allowable; here is the prior approval email from Program Officer dated X." Do not argue opinion.
*   **CAP Deadline:** Usually 6 months from audit report date. **Track CAP implementation in Board Minutes.**
