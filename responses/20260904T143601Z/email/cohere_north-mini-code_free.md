**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program welcomes applications from a broad spectrum of community‑based entities, including unincorporated groups that operate under the formal auspices of a local government. When an unincorporated group is “auspiced” by a council, the council typically assumes responsibility for certain statutory obligations such as public liability insurance, risk management, and compliance with local planning regulations. This arrangement is considered compatible with our eligibility criteria, provided that the council can confirm in writing that it will act as the primary point of contact for the grant, will hold any required insurance, and will be responsible for the disbursement of funds to the project’s beneficiaries. If your council has a formal partnership or memorandum of understanding with your garden, please include that document in your application package.

**Grant amount – minimum and maximum**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to fund projects that deliver measurable resilience benefits, and we do not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award amount. Instead, we assess funding based on the overall project cost, the degree of community impact, and the proportion of the budget that the applicant can secure from other sources. In recent rounds, awards have ranged from approximately $5,000 to $150,000, though many successful projects fall between $20,000 and $80,000. We encourage you to outline the total budget for the rainwater‑tank and shade‑structure component, and we can then discuss how much of that may be appropriate for grant support.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes—capital infrastructure that directly enhances community resilience is an eligible cost. Specifically, the grant supports:  

1. **Water‑harvesting infrastructure** (e.g., rainwater tanks, gutters, storage vessels) that reduces reliance on municipal water supplies and mitigates the impacts of drought or water‑restriction periods.  
2. **Climate‑adaptation structures** (e.g., shade sails, pergolas, windbreaks) that protect participants from extreme heat, extend the usable season of the garden, and improve overall safety.  

Both categories align with the grant’s objective of building capacity to cope with increasingly frequent climate‑related challenges. Please note that any construction work must comply with local building codes and, where required, obtain the necessary permits. Our program can provide guidance on what permits are typically needed for small‑scale installations.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  

1. **Project Narrative** – A concise description of the problem you are addressing (e.g., heat stress, water scarcity), the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and how the project contributes to broader community resilience goals.  
2. **Budget and Budget Narrative** – Itemized costs for tanks, shade structures, installation, any professional fees, and a brief explanation of each line item.  
3. **Organizational Profile** – A one‑page overview of your garden, its mission, volunteer base, and the role of the council as auspice. Include any existing partnership agreements or memoranda of understanding.  
4. **Proof of Insurance / Council Confirmation** – A letter from your local council confirming that it holds public liability insurance covering the project activities, and that the council will act as the fiscal host for any grant funds.  
5. **Community Support** – Letters of support from local residents, schools, or other stakeholder groups, as well as any evidence of participation (e.g., sign‑up sheets, social‑media engagement metrics).  
6. **Site Plans / Drawings** – Simple sketches or CAD diagrams showing the intended location of the rainwater tanks and shade structure on the garden site.  
7. **Financial Statements** – For an unincorporated group, recent bank statements or cash‑flow summaries that demonstrate the $40,000 annual turnover you mentioned, together with sources of income (produce stall, membership fees).  
8. **Sustainability and Maintenance Plan** – An outline of how the garden will maintain and operate the new infrastructure over the long term, including any volunteer training or cost‑sharing arrangements.
