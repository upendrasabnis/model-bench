Thank you for reaching out and for the clear, concise overview of your organisation and the project you have in mind. I’m pleased to provide the following information to help you determine your eligibility and to outline what you will need to include in your application.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme welcomes applications from both incorporated not‑for‑profit entities and unincorporated community groups, provided that the group operates under a formal auspicing arrangement with a public‑sector body such as a local council. In such cases the council must act as the fiduciary agent for the grant, holding any required insurance, managing financial reporting, and assuming legal responsibility for the awarded funds. Because your garden is already hosted by the council and the council holds the necessary insurances on your behalf, you meet this criterion. Please note that the council will be required to sign a separate auspice agreement as part of the application package; a draft of this document is included in the guidelines we provide to successful applicants.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed award size. Instead, we assess each proposal against the program’s strategic objectives and the overall merit of the budget. Typical grants in the recent round have ranged from $15,000 to $75,000, but we are prepared to consider applications that fall outside this band if the justification and cost‑effectiveness are compelling. Your projected costs for two rainwater tanks and a shade structure appear to be within a reasonable range for the grant’s upper‑mid tier.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure that directly enhances the resilience and sustainability of community assets is fully eligible. Specifically, rainwater harvesting equipment (tanks, gutters, filtration where required) and permanent shade structures that protect both crops and volunteers are considered allowable expenses. Please ensure that any costs are broken out clearly in the budget section, and that you include a brief rationale linking each item to the program’s resilience objectives (e.g., water security during drought, reduced heat stress for volunteers, and continuity of food‑production activities).

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application we recommend assembling the following documents:

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem you are addressing, the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline with milestones.  
2. **Detailed Budget** – a line‑item breakdown that distinguishes capital items (tanks, shade structure, installation) from operating costs, with quotations or provisional invoices where possible.  
3. **Auspice Agreement Draft** – a formal letter from your council confirming that it will act as the fiscal host, maintain insurance coverage, and be responsible for any reporting or audit requirements.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – a short description of your group’s volunteer base, governance (even if informal), and any previous community projects you have delivered.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and balance sheet) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Safety Plan** – documentation outlining how the installation will be managed safely, including any necessary permits or council approvals.  
7. **Letters of Support** – endorsements from local businesses, community organisations, or council representatives that validate the community need and the project’s relevance.  

We also advise you to review the full grant guidelines on our website, as they contain detailed eligibility criteria, permissible activities, and the exact format required for each document.
