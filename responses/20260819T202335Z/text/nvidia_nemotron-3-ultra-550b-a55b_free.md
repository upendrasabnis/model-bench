## Introduction  

Grant writing is both an art and a science.  Nonprofits that consistently win funding share a disciplined workflow: **research → align → design → write → review → manage**.  This guide walks you through each step, provides concrete examples, and supplies printable checklists you can paste into your project management tool.  Whether you’re a seasoned development director or a first‑time grant writer, the practices below will raise your success rate and reduce the “last‑minute scramble” that burns staff time and goodwill.  

> **Key Principle:** *Every funder is a partner, not a check‑writer.*  Treat the proposal as a mutually beneficial contract that outlines shared impact, clear accountability, and transparent communication.

### 1.4 Relationship‑Building Before the Ask  

1. **Introductory Email** – 150‑word “mission alignment” note, attach a one‑pager.  
2. **Request a 15‑min Call** – Ask about funding priorities, not money.  
3. **Invite to Site Visit / Event** – Show impact first‑hand.  
4. **Send Quarterly Impact Updates** – Even if not funded yet.

## Phase 2 – Building a Winning Proposal Structure  

### 2.1 Standard Sections (Most Funders Require)  

| Section | Typical Length | Core Purpose |
|---------|----------------|--------------|
| Cover Letter | 1 page | Personalize, reference prior contact |
| Executive Summary | 1‑2 pages | Hook, problem, solution, ask |
| Organizational Background | 1‑2 pages | Credibility, track record |
| Needs Statement / Problem Statement | 1‑2 pages | Data‑driven urgency |
| Program Design (Logic Model) | 1‑2 pages | Theory of change, activities |
| Goals, Objectives, Outcomes | 1 page | SMART metrics |
| Evaluation Plan | 1‑2 pages | How you’ll prove impact |
| Sustainability Plan | ½‑1 page | Post‑grant funding, integration |
| Budget & Budget Narrative | 2‑3 pages | Transparent, justified costs |
| Appendices | As needed | Letters of support, IRS determination, audited financials |

### 2.2 Tailor the Outline to Each Funder  

- **Read the RFP** line‑by‑line; create a “requirements checklist” (see §10).  
- **Map each required element** to your master outline; add or delete sections accordingly.  
- **Use the funder’s terminology** (e.g., “outcomes” vs. “impact”).

## Phase 3 – Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement  

### 3.1 The “Problem‑Solution” Arc  

1. **Quantify the Problem** – Use local, state, national data.  
2. **Humanize** – Add a brief story or quote.  
3. **Show Gaps** – What existing services miss.  
4. **Link to Funder Priorities** – Explicitly name the funder’s focus area.  

### 3.2 Data Sources & Citation Style  

| Source | Example | Citation Format |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| U.S. Census ACS | % of households below poverty line in ZIP 60612 | (U.S. Census Bureau, 2023) |
| State Health Dept. | Asthma ER visits per 1,000 children | (IL Dept. of Public Health, 2022) |
| Peer‑Reviewed Study | Effectiveness of after‑school STEM | (Smith et al., 2021) |
| Internal Program Data | 68 % of participants lack broadband | (Org. Internal Report, 2024) |

*Always footnote or use parenthetical citations; keep a master bibliography.*  

### 3.3 Example Needs Statement (≈250 words)  

> **Community Context** – In the Near West Side of Chicago (ZIP 60612), 38 % of families live below 150 % of the federal poverty level (U.S. Census Bureau, 2023).  The neighborhood’s only public library closed in 2019, leaving 2,300 school‑age children without free internet access.  
>   
> **Health & Education Gap** – The Illinois Department of Public Health reports a 27 % higher asthma‑related ER visit rate for children in this ZIP compared with the city average (2022).  Research links poor indoor air quality—exacerbated by lack of ventilation in overcrowded housing—to both asthma exacerbations and reduced cognitive performance (Smith et al., 2021).  
>   
> **Current Services** – Two after‑school programs serve 150 youth combined, but neither offers STEM curricula nor air‑quality monitoring.  A 2023 survey of 120 parents (Org. Internal Report) found 71 % would enroll children in a program that provides “hands‑on science + healthy‑home workshops.”  
>   
> **Alignment** – The Greenfield Foundation’s 2024 “Environmental Justice & Youth STEM” priority explicitly seeks “community‑driven interventions that reduce indoor environmental hazards while expanding STEM pathways for low‑income youth.”  

### 3.4 Checklist – Needs Statement  

- [ ] **Data‑driven** (≥ 3 independent sources)  
- [ ] **Local relevance** (zip‑code, county, school district)  
- [ ] **Human story** (1‑2 sentences)  
- [ ] **Gap analysis** (what’s missing)  
- [ ] **Funder‑specific language** (≥ 2 keywords)  
- [ ] **Citations formatted** per funder guidelines

## Phase 4 – Designing Logic Models & Theory of Change  

### 4.1 Logic Model Template  

| **Inputs** | **Activities** | **Outputs** | **Short‑Term Outcomes** | **Intermediate Outcomes** | **Long‑Term Impact** |
|------------|----------------|------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|----------------------|
| $150k grant, 2 FTE staff, 5 volunteers, partner org. space | 1. Recruit 30 youth (ages 12‑15) 2. Deliver 12 weekly STEM workshops 3. Conduct 4 home‑air‑quality assessments per family | 30 youth enrolled; 12 workshops delivered; 120 home assessments completed | ↑ STEM self‑efficacy (pre/post survey) ↑ Knowledge of indoor pollutants | ↑ School science grades (↑0.5 GPA) ↓ Asthma ER visits (↓15 %) | Community‑wide reduction in environmental health disparities; pipeline to STEM careers |

### 4.2 Theory of Change Narrative (≈150 words)  

> *If* we provide low‑income youth with hands‑on STEM experiences **and** equip families with actionable indoor‑air‑quality data, *then* youth will develop scientific confidence and families will adopt low‑cost mitigation strategies (e.g., ventilation, low‑VOC cleaning). *Because* improved home environments reduce asthma triggers, children miss fewer school days, leading to higher academic performance. *Ultimately*, this creates a replicable model for environmental justice that can be scaled across similar Midwest neighborhoods.  

### 4.3 Visual Logic Model Tips  

- Use **Microsoft Visio, Lucidchart, or Google Drawings**.  
- Keep it **one page**, landscape, with color‑coded arrows (inputs → activities = blue; outputs → outcomes = green).  
- Include **measurement indicators** next to each outcome box (e.g., “Pre/Post STEM Self‑Efficacy Survey, n=30”).  

### 4.4 Checklist – Logic Model  

- [ ] All **inputs** listed with dollar values.  
- [ ] **Activities** are specific, time‑bound, and assigned to staff.  
- [ ] **Outputs** are countable (units, sessions, participants).  
- [ ] **Outcomes** are **SMART** (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time‑bound).  
- [ ] **Indicators** and **data sources** attached to each outcome.  
- [ ] **Assumptions** and **external factors** documented (e.g., “School district permits after‑school use”).

## Phase 5 – Developing Realistic Budgets & Budget Narratives  

### 5.1 Budget Categories (Standard Nonprofit)  

| Category | Typical Line Items | % of Total (Rule‑of‑Thumb) |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| Personnel | Salaries, fringe, payroll taxes | 45‑55 % |
| Fringe Benefits | Health, retirement, FICA | 15‑20 % of personnel |
| Travel | Local mileage, conference travel | 2‑5 % |
| Equipment | Laptops, air‑quality sensors | 3‑7 % |
| Supplies | Curriculum kits, PPE, printing | 3‑6 % |
| Contractual / Consultants | Evaluation, data analysis | 5‑10 % |
| Indirect / Overhead | Rent, utilities, admin (if allowed) | 10‑15 % (or funder‑capped) |
| Other | Stipends, incentives, insurance | 1‑3 % |

### 5.2 Sample Budget (12‑Month, $150,000)  

| Line Item | Qty | Unit Cost | Total | Narrative Note |
|-----------|-----|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Program Director (0.5 FTE) | 1 | $45,000 | $22,500 | Oversees curriculum, partner coordination |
| STEM Educator (1 FTE) | 1 | $38,000 | $38,000 | Delivers workshops, mentors youth |
| Community Health Worker (0.5 FTE) | 1 | $30,000 | $15,000 | Conducts home assessments |
| Fringe (30 % of salaries) | — | — | $22,650 | Health, retirement, FICA |
| Air‑Quality Sensors (10) | 10 | $120 | $1,200 | Low‑cost PM2.5/CO₂ monitors |
| Workshop Supplies (kits, 30) | 30 | $45 | $1,350 | Hands‑on kits per youth |
| Travel (mileage 2,000 mi @ $0.58) | — | — | $1,160 | Staff home visits |
| Evaluation Consultant | 1 | $12,000 | $12,000 | Pre/post surveys, data analysis |
| Indirect (10 % of direct) | — | — | $11,380 | Rent, utilities, admin |
| **Total** | | | **$150,000** | |

### 5.3 Budget Narrative Essentials  

1. **Justify each line** – “Air‑quality sensors ($1,200) are essential for Objective 2: collect real‑time indoor pollutant data for 30 families.”  
2. **Show cost‑share** – “Organization contributes $15,000 in-kind space (valued at $1,250/mo).”  
3. **Explain fringe rate** – “30 % reflects actual employer costs per FY2024 audited financials.”  
4. **Address funder caps** – “Indirect costs limited to 10 % per Greenfield Foundation policy.”  

### 5.4 Common Budget Pitfalls & Fixes  

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| **Over‑estimating personnel time** | Use a **time‑allocation matrix** (staff × activity × %FTE). |
| **Missing indirect costs** | Verify funder policy; if not allowed, shift to direct line items. |
| **Rounding errors** | Keep spreadsheet formulas; round only final totals. |
| **Unallowable expenses** (e.g., lobbying) | Cross‑check with funder’s “unallowable costs” list. |
| **No contingency** | Add 3‑5 % contingency line (if permitted). |

### 5.5 Checklist – Budget  

- [ ] All **direct costs** mapped to logic‑model activities.  
- [ ] **Fringe** calculated on actual rates.  
- [ ] **Indirect** rate matches funder policy.  
- [ ] **Cost‑share / in‑kind** documented with valuation letters.  
- [ ] **Narrative** references each budget line to a specific objective.  
- [ ] **Spreadsheet** version‑controlled (e.g., “Budget_v3_FINAL.xlsx”).

## Phase 6 – Writing the Narrative: Goals, Objectives, Activities, Evaluation  

### 6.1 Goal vs. Objective vs. Activity  

| Level | Definition | Example |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **Goal** | Broad, long‑term change | “Reduce environmental health disparities for youth in Near West Side.” |
| **Objective** | SMART, measurable, time‑bound | “By Month 12, 80 % of enrolled youth will increase STEM self‑efficacy score by ≥ 15 %.” |
| **Activity** | Concrete action to achieve objective | “Deliver 12 weekly 2‑hour STEM workshops (Month 2‑13).” |

### 6.2 Writing SMART Objectives – Template  

> **Objective 1:** *By 12/31/2026, 80 % of the 30 youth participants will demonstrate a ≥ 15 % increase in STEM self‑efficacy as measured by the validated **STEM‑SE Survey** (pre‑test Month 1, post‑test Month 12).*  

- **Specific** – STEM self‑efficacy, 30 youth.  
- **Measurable** – ≥ 15 % increase, survey score.  
- **Achievable** – Prior pilot showed 18 % avg. gain.  
- **Relevant** – Directly ties to funder’s “Youth STEM” priority.  
- **Time‑bound** – 12‑month grant period.  

### 6.3 Activity Detailing (Work Plan)  

| Month | Activity | Lead | Output | Monitoring Tool |
|-------|----------|------|--------|-----------------|
| 1‑2 | Recruit & enroll 30 youth | Program Dir. | Enrollment list (30) | Enrollment tracker |
| 2‑13 | Weekly STEM workshops (2 hrs) | STEM Educator | 12 workshops × 30 youth | Attendance sheets |
| 3‑4 | Baseline home air‑quality assessments | CHW | 30 assessment reports | Sensor data upload |
| 5‑12 | Quarterly family workshops (healthy‑home) | CHW | 4 workshops × 30 families | Sign‑in sheets |
| 12 | Post‑program evaluation | Eval Consultant | Final report | Survey data, ER visit data |

### 6.4 Evaluation Plan – Mixed Methods  

| Evaluation Question | Indicator | Data Source | Method | Timeline | Responsible |
|---------------------|-----------|-------------|--------|----------|-------------|
| **Did STEM self‑efficacy improve?** | Δ STEM‑SE score | Pre/Post survey | Paired t‑test | Month 1 & 12 | Eval Consultant |
| **Did indoor air quality improve?** | PM2.5 reduction ≥ 10 % | Sensor logs | Descriptive stats + GIS mapping | Month 3, 6, 9, 12 | CHW + Eval |
| **Did asthma ER visits decline?** | ER visit rate per 1,000 | Hospital discharge data (with consent) | Interrupted time series | Baseline 12 mo vs. grant 12 mo | Eval Consultant |
| **Program fidelity** | % of workshops delivered as planned | Attendance & curriculum checklist | Fidelity checklist | Ongoing | Program Dir. |

### 6.5 Sustainability Narrative (≈150 words)  

> *Beyond the grant period, the program will transition to a **fee‑for‑service model** with the local school district (MOU signed 2024) that reimburses $5,000 per semester for after‑school STEM enrichment.  The community health worker position will be absorbed into the organization’s existing **Healthy Homes Initiative**, funded by a recurring CDC grant (awarded 2023‑2027).  Air‑quality sensors become organizational assets for future community‑based research, generating modest rental income ($200/yr per sensor).  A **donor‑cultivation plan** targets 10 new major donors ($5k each) identified during the grant’s final showcase event.*  

### 6.6 Checklist – Narrative  

- [ ] **Goal** aligns with funder’s mission statement.  
- [ ] **≥ 3 SMART objectives** (one per major outcome).  
- [ ] **Activities** mapped 1‑to‑1 to objectives.  
- [ ] **Work plan** includes month, lead, output, monitoring tool.  
- [ ] **Evaluation** mixes quantitative & qualitative; includes baseline.  
- [ ] **Sustainability** cites specific revenue streams or policy changes.

## Phase 8 – Post‑Award Management & Reporting  

### 8.1 Award Acceptance & Kick‑Off  

1. **Sign Grant Agreement** – Review legal clauses (indemnification, IP, termination).  
2. **Set Up Grant‑Specific Accounting** – Separate fund code in accounting software (e.g., QuickBooks “Grant‑GF‑2026”).  
3. **Kick‑Off Meeting (Week 1)** – Review logic model, assign responsibilities, set up shared drive (Google Drive/SharePoint).  

### 8.2 Financial Management  

| Task | Frequency | Tool |
|------|-----------|------|
| **Drawdown Requests** | Per funder schedule (monthly/quarterly) | Grant management module |
| **Expense Tracking** | Real‑time (receipt upload) | Expensify / Bill.com |
| **Budget vs. Actual Review** | Monthly | Excel dashboard (variance > 10 % flagged) |
| **Audit Prep** | Quarterly | Reconcile to general ledger |

### 8.3 Programmatic Monitoring  

- **Monthly Dashboard** (Google Data Studio) showing: enrollment, attendance, sensor data uploads, survey completion rates.  
- **Quarterly Narrative Report** – Use the **“Progress Report Template”** (see Appendix A).  
- **Mid‑Term Evaluation** (Month 6) – External evaluator conducts focus groups; adjust activities if fidelity < 80 %.  

### 8.4 Reporting to Funder  

| Report Type | Due | Content | Format |
|-------------|-----|---------|--------|
| **Quarterly Narrative** | 30 days after quarter end | Activities, outputs, outcome data, challenges, adjustments | PDF (2‑3 pages) |
| **Annual Financial Report** | 90 days after grant year | Expenditures by line item, indirect cost reconciliation | Excel + PDF |
| **Final Report** | 60 days after grant close | Cumulative outcomes, lessons learned, sustainability plan | PDF (5‑7 pages) + data appendix |
| **Ad Hoc Requests** | As needed | Specific data (e.g., sensor raw files) | CSV/Excel |

**Tip:** Draft each report **one week before due date**; circulate to program & finance leads for sign‑off.  

### 8.5 Relationship Maintenance  

- **Thank‑You Letter** (within 48 hrs of award).  
- **Quarterly Impact Snapshots** (1‑page infographic) emailed to program officer.  
- **Site Visit Invitation** (mid‑grant).  
- **Public Acknowledgment** (social media, annual report) per funder’s recognition policy.  

### 8.6 Close‑Out Checklist  

- [ ] All **financial reconciliations** completed; final drawdown submitted.  
- [ ] **Final narrative & data** uploaded to funder portal.  
- [ ] **Equipment inventory** (sensors, laptops) transferred to org asset list.  
- [ ] **Staff debrief** – capture lessons learned in a “Grant Close‑Out Memo.”  
- [ ] **Donor stewardship** – send impact report, invite to next event.
