### 2.3.  Matching Logic Checklist

1. **Mission Alignment** – Does the funder’s purpose match your program’s goal?  
2. **Geographic Scope** – Are you within the funder’s region?  
3. **Target Pop** – Does the demographic fit?  
4. **Programmatic Focus** – Is your activity a known priority?  
5. **Funding Level** – Is your budget within the typical range?  
6. **Application Window** – Do you have the capacity to meet the deadline?  

> **Concrete Example**  
> *Your nonprofit serves low‑income families in Houston with after‑school tutoring. The *Houston Community Foundation* funds “Education & Youth Empowerment” projects up to $15k. You match all six criteria.*

## 3.  Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement

### 3.1.  The “Problem‑Solution” Structure

| Section | What to Include | Sample Sentence |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Context** | Demographics, statistics, policy backdrop | “In 2023, 47% of Houston’s 5–7‑year‑olds lived below the poverty line.” |
| **Gap** | Specific unmet need | “Only 12% had access to after‑school tutoring.” |
| **Impact** | Consequences of the gap | “These children miss 20% of learning time.” |
| **Opportunity** | Why now is the right time | “New state funding for STEM education is rolling out.” |

### 3.2.  Needs‑Statement Checklist

| ✔ | Item | Why It Matters |
|---|------|----------------|
| 1 | Data‑driven | Credibilityافة |
| 2 | Concise (≤ 200 words) | Funder can read fast |
| 3 | Human story | Adds emotional resonance |
| 4 | Clearly linked to mission | Shows alignment |
| 5 | Quantifiable outcomes | Sets measurable goals |

### 3.3.  Example

> **Context**: “Houston’s Westside districts have a combined 52% high school dropout rate.”  
> **Gap**: “Without after‑school programming, at-risk youth lack bophelo.”  
> **Impact**: “These gaps translate into/class‑wide lower test scores.”  
> **Opportunity**: “The Texas Department of Education recently allocated $2 million for after‑school STEM initiatives.”

## 4.  Writing the Narrative: The Heart of the Proposal

### 4.1.  Introduction

- **Hook**: Start with a striking fact or anecdote.  
- **Mission Alignment**: “Our organization, dedicated to equitable education, seeks to…”
- **Request Summary**: “We request $12,000 to fund one semester of after‑school tutoring.”

### 4.2.  Program Description

| Component | Key Questions | Practical Tips |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|
| **Goals** | What do you want to achieve? | Use “SMART” (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time‑bound) |
| **Activities** | What will you do? | Break into weekly tasks, staff roles |
| **Timeline** | When will it happen? | Gantt chart or table |
| **Staffing** | Who will run it? | Bios, relevant experience |
| **Partnerships** | Collaborations? | Schools, local businesses |

### 4.3.  The “What योगदान” Section

| Item | How to Present | Example |
|------|----------------|--------|
| **Impact** | Connect to needs statement | “We expect to improve test scores by 5%.” |
| **Sustainability** | Post‑grant plan | “We’ll train volunteers to keep the program running.” |
| **Innovation** | Unique angle | “We’ll use a blended learning model with AI tutoring.” |

### 4.4.  Narrative Checklist

| ✔ | Section | Focus |
|---|---------|-------|
| 1 | Introduction | Hook + mission |
| 2 | Needs | Data + impact |
| 3 | Goals | SMART |
| 4 | Activities | Clear, actionable |

## 6.  Logic Models: Visualizing Impact

### 6.1.  The Five‑Column Model

| **Inputs** | **Activities** | **Outputs** | **Outcomes** | **Impact** |
|------------|----------------|-------------|--------------|------------|
| Staff, funds, materials | Deliver tutoring | 200 hours of tutoring | 10% rise in test scores | Reduced dropout rate |

### 6.2.  Creating a Logic Model Checklist

| ✔ | Item | How to Build |
|---|------|--------------|
| 1 | Collect baseline data | Use school reports |
| 2 | Define short‑term outcomes | e.g., “Increased engagement.” |
| 3 | Set intermediate outcomes | e.g., “Improved grades.” |
| 4 | Align outcomes to impact | e.g., “Higher graduation rates.” |
| 5 | Include assumptions & external factors | e.g., “Stable funding.” |

### 6.3.  Sample Logic Model (Text Version)

> **Inputs**: $12k grant, 2 part‑time tutors, 50 students.  
> **Activities**: 3 pname tutoring sessions per week.  
> **Outputs**: 180 tutoring hours, 150 worksheets distributed.  
> **Outcomes**: 15% increase in 6‑month test scores.  
> **Impact**: 5% reduction in district dropout rate over 3 years.

## 7.  The Proposal Skeleton: Formatting & Submission

| Section | Page Limit | Key Points |
|---------|------------|------------|
| Cover Letter | 1 page | Personalize, mention prior contact |
| Executive Summary | 1 page | Problem, solution, ask |
| Organizational Profile | 1–2 pages | Mission, history, board |
| Needs Statement | 1–2 pages | Data, narrative |
| Goals & Objectives | 1 page | SMART |
| Activities & Timeline | 1–2 pages | Gantt chart |
| Staffing & Management | 1 page | Bios, roles |
| Evaluation Plan | 1 page | Metrics, tools |
| Budget | 1 page | Table + narrative |
| Sustainability | 1 page | Post‑grant plan |
| Appendices | as needed | Supporting docs |

> **Formatting Checklist**  
> 1. **Font**: 12‑pt Times New Roman or Garamond.  
> 2. **Margins**: 1″ all sides.  
> 3. **Spacing**: 1.5 line spacing.  
> 4. **Headers**: Page numbers, proposal title.  
> 5. **File Type**: PDF (unless portal requests Word).
