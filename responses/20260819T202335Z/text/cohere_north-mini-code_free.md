# Best Practices in Nonprofit Grant Writing  
*An in‑depth guide covering funder research, compelling needs statements, budgets, logic models, and post‑award reporting*  

---  

## 1. Introduction  

Grant writing is both an art and a science. Successful proposals combine rigorous research, clear storytelling, precise financial planning, and robust evaluation. This article walks you through every stage of the grant‑writing cycle, offering concrete examples, practical checklists, and actionable tips you can start using today.  

---  

## 2. Understanding the Funding Landscape  

### 2.1 Researching Funders  

| Step | What to Do | Tools & Resources |
|------|------------|-------------------|
| **Identify potential funders** | Use databases like **Foundation Center (Candid)**, **GrantStation**, **Fluxx**, and **National Philanthropic Trust**. | Candid.org, Fluxx.com |
| **Read the funder’s mission** | Review annual reports, strategic plans, and recent grant guidelines. | Funders’ websites, newsletters |
| **Check past grants** | Search the **grant database** for similar projects, geographic focus, or issue areas. | Candid’s GrantSearch, Google Advanced Search |
| **Note eligibility & geographic restrictions** | Create a spreadsheet with columns: Funder, Eligibility, Geographic Focus, Funding Range, Deadline. | Excel/Google Sheets |
| **Understand application requirements** | List required documents: project narrative, budget, logic model, evaluation plan, letters of support. | Funder PDFs, FAQs |
| **Set up alerts** | Subscribe to **GrantStation alerts**, **Fluxx notifications**, or **Foundation Center’s eNews**. | Email alerts |
| **Visit webinars & conferences** | Attend **AFP (Association of Fundraising Professionals)** or **Council on Foundations** events. | Event calendars |

#### Example: Funder Research Sheet  

```
Funder: The Robert Wood Johnson Foundation
Mission: Improve public health and health equity
Eligibility: U.S.-based nonprofits, community-based organizations
Geographic Focus: Nationwide (priority to underserved areas)
Funding Range: $50,000 – $500,000
Previous Grants: “Healthy Food Access,” “Youth Mental Health”
Application Requirements: Project Narrative (max 10 pages), Budget Narrative, Logic Model, Evaluation Plan, 2 Letters of Support
Deadline: Quarterly (Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct)
Website: rwjf.org
Notes: Preference for data‑driven interventions
```

### 2.2 Checklist: Funder Research  

- [ ] Identify 5–10 potential funders for your project.  
- [ ] Record eligibility, geographic focus, and funding range.  
- [ ] Review at least three past grants to understand language and format.  
- [ ] Note required documents and any special instructions.  
- [ ] Set calendar reminders for application deadlines.  
- [ ] Subscribe to funder newsletters for updates.  

### 2.3 Building Relationships with Funders  

1. **Attend funder informational sessions** (in person or virtual).  
2. **Introduce yourself early** – send a brief “thank you” after a grant award or conference.  
3. **Stay informed** – share relevant reports or data that align with their priorities.  
4. **Be transparent** – if you need clarification, ask politely and quickly.  
5. **Follow up** – after a grant decision, request feedback and maintain a professional tone.

## 3. Developing the Proposal Core  

### 3.1 Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement  

A needs statement answers: *Who needs help? How do we know? Why is it urgent?*  

#### Example Needs Statement  

> **Community X**, a town of 12,000 residents located in a rural county, experiences a **30% higher rate of childhood asthma hospitalizations** compared to the state average. Local schools report **four asthma‑related absences per student per year**, and families lack access to affordable inhalers and asthma education. This unmet need creates a cycle of missed school days, reduced academic achievement, and increased healthcare costs for families and the community.  

#### Checklist: Needs Statement  

- [ ] Identify the target population and geographic area.  
- [ ] Provide quantitative data (statistics, rates, trends).  
- [ ] Cite reputable sources (CDC, local health department, peer‑reviewed studies).  
- [ ] Explain why the problem matters now (urgency, equity, cost).  
- [ ] Connect the problem to your organization’s mission and values.  
- [ ] Keep the statement **150–250 words** (most funders have a limit).  

### 3.2 Writing the Program Description  

- **What** you will do (activities).  
- **How** you will do it (methods, partners).  
- **Who** will deliver (staff, volunteers).  

#### Example Program Description (Excerpt)  

> **Program Goal:** Reduce asthma triggers in 150 households within Community X.  
> **Activities:**  
> 1. Conduct home‑environment assessments (trained volunteers).  
> 2. Provide low‑cost air filters and hypoallergenic bedding (procured through local suppliers).  
> 3. Deliver two community workshops on asthma management (hosted at the local library).  
> 4. Establish a peer‑support network via monthly “Asthma Buddy” meetings.  

### 3.3 Defining Goals and Objectives  

- **Goals** are broad, long‑term outcomes (e.g., “Improve asthma health outcomes”).  
- **Objectives** are SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time‑bound.  

#### Example Objectives  

| Goal | Objective | Metric | Timeline |
|------|-----------|--------|----------|
| Reduce asthma triggers in 150 households | Install air filters in 150 homes | Number of homes completed | 6 months |
| Increase asthma knowledge among families | 80% of participants pass post‑workshop quiz | Quiz score ≥80% | 3 months |
| Decrease emergency visits | Reduce asthma ER visits by 25% among participants | ER visit count | 12 months |

### 3.4 Logic Model Development  

A logic model visualizes the connection between resources, activities, outputs, outcomes, and impact.  

#### Template (Excel/Google Sheet)  

| Input (Resources) | Activities (What we do) | Output (Products/Services) | Outcome (Short‑term) | Impact (Long‑term) |
|-------------------|--------------------------|----------------------------|----------------------|--------------------|
| Grant $75,000; 2 staff FTE; community partners | Home assessments, filter distribution, workshops, support groups | 150 homes assessed; 150 filters installed; 4 workshops; 6 support groups | 80% of families report reduced symptoms; 90% pass knowledge quiz | 25% reduction in ER visits; improved school attendance |

#### Checklist: Logic Model  

- [ ] List all **inputs** (funding, staff, in‑kind donations).  
- [ ] Define **activities** (concrete actions).  
- [ ] Identify **outputs** (number of services delivered).  
- [ ] Specify **short‑term outcomes** (knowledge gain, behavior change).  
- [ ] Define **long‑term impact** (health improvements, systemic change).  
- [ ] Ensure each level is **measurable**.  

### 3.5 Evaluation Plan  

- **Formative evaluation** (during implementation) – check progress, adjust.  
- **Summative evaluation** (after completion) – measure impact.  

#### Sample Evaluation Questions  

1. **Process:** How many households received filters? Did participants attend workshops?  
2. **Outcome:** What is the change in asthma symptom frequency (pre/post survey)?  
3. **Impact:** Did ER visits decline? What were the cost savings?

## 4. Financial Planning and Budget Development  

### 4.1 Core Budget Categories  

| Category | Typical Items | Tips |
|----------|---------------|------|
| **Personnel** | Salaries, benefits, fringe rates | Use **salary benchmarks**; include benefits (≈30% of salary). |
| **Travel** | Participant mileage, conference fees, site visits | Use **IRS mileage rate**; get receipts. |
| **Materials/Supplies** | Air filters, educational materials, PPE | Itemize quantities and unit costs. |
| **Rent/Overhead** | Space for workshops, storage | Allocate a **reasonable overhead %** (e.g., 10% of direct costs). |
| **Equipment** | Portable air quality monitors, computers | Include purchase vs. lease options. |
| **Evaluation** | Survey software, data analysis, external evaluator | Distinguish between internal and external costs. |
| **Communications** | Brochures, website updates, social media ads | Align with campaign goals. |

### 4.2 Budget Narrative: Why Each Cost Matters  

- **Explain** how each line item directly supports the program.  
- **Show** how costs were derived (e.g., “Air filters at $150 each for 150 households = $22,500”).  
- **Justify** any indirect costs with a clear methodology.  

#### Example Narrative (excerpt)  

> **Personnel (2 FTEs – Program Manager & Community Organizer):** Salaries of $55,000 and $48,000 respectively, including 25% benefits. These staff will oversee home assessments, coordinate filter distribution, and facilitate workshops, ensuring project milestones are met.  

### 4.3 Budget Templates & Checklists  

#### Template (Google Sheet)  

```
Item                Quantity   Unit Cost   Total Cost   Notes
Air filters         150        $150        $22,500     Purchased from local supplier, bulk discount
Workshop materials  4          $200        $800        Includes handouts, supplies, printing
Travel (mileage)    500 miles   $0.56       $280        Based on 2022 IRS rate
Personnel (FTE)     2           $55,000     $110,000    Includes benefits
Overhead (10%)      —          —           $22,000     Applied to direct costs
Total Direct Costs  —          —           $155,580
Total Indirect      —          —           $22,000
Grand Total         —          —           $177,580
```

#### Checklist: Budget Review  

- [ ] Verify **calculations** (use spreadsheet formulas).  
- [ ] Ensure **budget aligns** with logic model activities.  
- [ ] Include **contingency** (5–10% of total).  
- [ ] Double‑check **salary + benefits** calculations.  
- [ ] Label **direct vs. indirect** costs clearly.  
- [ ] Attach **receipts or quotes** for large purchases.  
- [ ] Run a **budget vs. prior year** comparison (if applicable).  

### 4.4 Common Budget Pitfalls (and How to Avoid Them)  

| Pitfall | Why It Happens | Fix |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| **Over‑inflating salaries** | Misunderstanding fringe rates | Use **actual salary + 20‑30% benefits**. |
| **Missing indirect costs** | Assuming “overhead” is not allowed | Clarify with funder; include a **reasonable overhead** line. |
| **Rounding errors** | Manual calculations | Use **spreadsheet formulas** and lock cells. |
| **Unclear expense descriptions** | “Miscellaneous” line items | Break down into **specific categories** (e.g., “Printing – workshop handouts”). |
| **Ignoring inflation** | Using last year’s costs | Update costs to **current market rates**. |

## 5. Writing the Narrative: Best Practices  

### 5.1 Clear, Concise Language  

- **Write at an 8th‑grade reading level** (or lower for broader audiences).  
- **Use active voice** (“We will train volunteers” vs. “Volunteers will be trained by us”).  
- **Avoid jargon** unless it’s essential and defined.  

### 5.2 Using Data and Stories  

| Element | How to Include | Example |
|---------|----------------|---------|
| **Statistics** | Cite numbers that quantify the need. | “Asthma rates are 30% higher in Community X.” |
| **Quotes** | Include a parent or teacher’s perspective. | “My child’s asthma is now under control thanks to the filter program.” |
| **Case Study** | Brief snapshot of a beneficiary. | “Family of three, ages 7, 9, and 11, saw a 70% reduction in inhaler use after filter installation.” |
| **Visuals** | Graphs, photos, maps (if allowed). | Simple bar chart showing symptom reduction. |

### 5.3 Tailoring to Funder Priorities  

1. **Read the request for proposals (RFP) carefully.** Highlight keywords: “community engagement,” “evidence‑based,” “sustainability.”  
2. **Mirror the funder’s language** in your narrative (e.g., if they emphasize “health equity,” incorporate that term).  
3. **Align your organization’s mission** with the funder’s strategic goals.  

#### Example Alignment  

> **Funder priority:** “Promote health equity in underserved communities.”  
> **Your proposal:** “Our program targets Community X, where asthma disparities are 30% above the state average, directly addressing health equity.”  

### 5.4 Editing and Proofreading  

- **First draft:** Focus on content, not grammar.  
- **Second draft:** Polish language, tighten word count.  
- **Third draft:** Grammar/spelling check, format compliance.  

#### Proofreading Checklist  

- [ ] All **headings** match required format.  
- [ ] **Page numbers** and **section titles** are consistent.  
- [ ] **Budget** is attached with narrative.  
- [ ] **Logic model** is clear and legible.  
- [ ] **References** are formatted correctly.  
- [ ] **Signature page** is completed (if required).

## 6. Application Logistics and Submission  

### 6.1 Required Forms and Documents  

| Document | Typical Content | Tips |
|----------|----------------|------|
| **Project Narrative** | Full proposal (often 10–20 pages) | Use the outline from Section 3. |
| **Budget** | Detailed spreadsheet + narrative | Ensure formatting matches funder template. |
| **Logic Model** | Visual diagram + brief explanation | Include a caption describing each component. |
| **Evaluation Plan** | Methodology, indicators, data sources | Show how you will measure success. |
| **Letters of Support** | 2–3 letters from partners, community leaders | Ask supporters early; give them a copy of your proposal. |
| **Organization Profile** | IRS status, annual report, staff list | Keep up‑to‑date on your website. |
| **Compliance Documents** | Non‑discrimination policy, conflict of interest | Attach as separate PDFs. |

### 6.2 Checklist: Submission Process  

- [ ] Verify **deadline** and **time zone**.  
- [ ] Complete **all required forms** exactly as instructed.  
- [ ] Double‑check **page limits** and **font requirements**.  
- [ ] Run a **spell‑check** and **readability check** (e.g., Hemingway, Grammarly).  
- [ ] Attach **all documents** in the correct order.  
- [ ] Save each file with a **consistent naming convention** (e.g., “OrgName_ProjectNarrative.pdf”).  
- [ ] Send a **confirmation email** to the funder’s grants office acknowledging receipt.  
- [ ] Keep a **submission log** (date, contact person, status).

## 7. Post‑Award Management  

### 7.1 Grant Accounting and Budgeting  

- **Separate bank account** for grant funds (if required).  
- **Track expenses** weekly using accounting software (e.g., QuickBooks, Xero).  
- **Reconcile** monthly with funder’s financial reports.  

#### Sample Expense Log (Spreadsheet)  

```
Date   | Expense Category | Description                     | Amount | Budget Remaining
--------|------------------|--------------------------------|--------|-----------------
01/10   | Personnel        | Salary – Program Manager       | $5,500 | $64,500
01/12   | Supplies         | Air filters (150 units)        | $22,500| $42,000
01/15   | Travel           | Mileage – site visits          | $140   | $41,860
...
```

### 7.2 Reporting Requirements  

| Report Type | Frequency | Key Elements | Tips |
|-------------|-----------|--------------|------|
| **Financial Report** | Quarterly (or as specified) | Expenditures, budget status, audit trail | Use funder’s template if provided. |
| **Progress Report** | Monthly/Bimonthly | Activities completed, milestones, challenges | Include photos, participant quotes. |
| **Evaluation Report** | Mid‑term & final | Outcome data, lessons learned, recommendations | Use logic model to map results. |
| **Compliance Report** | As required | Non‑discrimination, conflict of interest | Keep documentation ready for audit. |

### 7.3 Communicating with Funders  

- **Prompt acknowledgments** for each report.  
- **Transparent updates** on challenges (e.g., supply chain delays).  
- **Celebrate successes** early (e.g., first 50 filters installed).  

#### Example Email Template – Progress Update  

```
Subject: Monthly Progress Update – Community Asthma Relief (Grant #12345)

Dear [Funder Contact],
I am pleased to share our progress report for the period [Month, Year]. Highlights include:
- Completed home assessments for 120 households.
- Installed air filters in 95 homes.
- Conducted two asthma education workshops (30 participants each).

We have encountered a delay in filter procurement due to supplier constraints, but we are securing an alternate vendor.

Please find the attached financial summary and activity log.

Thank you for your continued support.
Best regards,
[Your Name]
Program Director
```

### 7.4 Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E)  

- **Set up a dashboard** (Google Data Studio, Power BI) to track key indicators in real time.  
- **Conduct quarterly reviews** with your team to assess progress against the logic model.  
- **Document lessons learned** after each milestone to improve future proposals.  

### 7.5 Renewals and Future Funding  

- **Prepare a “renewal package”** early (6–8 weeks before expiration).  
- **Gather evidence** of impact (statistics, testimonials, cost‑benefit analysis).  
- **Showcase sustainability plans** (e.g., diversified funding, community ownership).  

#### Checklist: Preparing for Renewal  

- [ ] Review funder’s **renewal guidelines** (if any).  
- [ ] Compile **final evaluation report** and any supplemental data.  
- [ ] Update **logic model** to reflect new objectives (if expanding).  
- [ ] Draft a **sustainability plan** (e.g., fundraising strategy, in‑kind support).  
- [ ] Secure **letters of support** from new partners.  
- [ ] Align the **budget** with any new scope or inflation adjustments.

**Remember:** Grant writing is not a one‑time task; it’s an ongoing relationship built on transparency, accountability, and shared vision. Treat each proposal as a partnership opportunity, and let data‑driven storytelling be your bridge to sustainable funding.
