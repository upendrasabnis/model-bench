**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines expressly permit applications from unincorporated community groups that are formally auspiced—or “hosted”—by a local government entity. The council’s auspice satisfies the requirement for organisational stability and accountability, and it also means that the council holds the necessary public‑liability and other insurances on your behalf. Consequently, your group meets the organisational‑structure criterion even though you are not a registered charity.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum award. Instead, we assess each proposal on its merits, considering the scope of the activities, the level of community benefit, and the proportion of the requested funds relative to the overall project cost. Typical awards for infrastructure projects in the $30 k‑$80 k range are common, but we are open to larger requests where the justification is robust. If you anticipate a total project cost that exceeds the usual range, we recommend discussing the budget early in the application process so we can confirm feasibility.

**Eligibility of rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, capital infrastructure that directly supports the long‑term sustainability and climate‑adaptation goals of a community project is an eligible cost. Rainwater tanks and shade structures clearly fall within this category because they reduce water consumption, mitigate heat stress, and enable the garden to remain operational during hot summer periods. Both items must be justified with clear specifications, cost breakdowns, and evidence of how they will achieve measurable outcomes (e.g., water‑usage reduction, temperature differentials, extended growing season).

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:

1. **Project Narrative** – A concise description of the problem (increasing summer heat), the proposed solution (tanks + shade), and the expected impacts on garden operations and the wider community.  
2. **Budget Table** – Detailed line‑item costs for equipment, installation, any professional fees, and a contingency (typically 5‑10 %). Include quotations or provisional invoices for the tanks and shade structure.  
3. **Organizational Profile** – A brief overview of your group’s purpose, volunteer base, and the council’s auspice agreement (a copy of the memorandum of understanding or hosting letter is helpful).  
4. **Financial Statements** – Most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple income‑and‑expense summary) demonstrating the $40 k turnover you mentioned.  
5. **Insurance Confirmation** – A letter from the council confirming that public liability and any required professional indemnities are in place for the project duration.  
6. **Community Support** – Letters of endorsement from local residents, schools, or other stakeholder groups, as well as any signed agreements with the council for site use.  
7. **Implementation Timeline** – A Gantt‑style chart or milestone list outlining the phases from procurement through installation to operational testing.  
8. **Monitoring and Evaluation Plan** – Clear metrics (e.g., litres of water saved per season, temperature reduction inside the shade structure) and a simple reporting schedule for post‑grant monitoring.
