Thank you for reaching out and for the important work your community garden is doing. I’m pleased to provide the information you need to decide whether the Community Resilience Grant (CRG) is a suitable fit for your organisation.

**1. Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The CRG is open to “community-based organisations” that are **not** registered charities but that can demonstrate a formal governance structure and a clear link to a local authority. An unincorporated group that operates under the auspices of an elected council, with the council providing the necessary insurance and administrative support, meets this criterion. In practice this means you will need to supply:

- A copy of the council’s letter confirming their role as your sponsor and the extent of their support (e.g., insurance, premises, or other resources).  
- Evidence of a written operating agreement or constitution that sets out your group’s governance, decision‑making processes, and financial controls.

**2. Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The CRG offers awards between **$5,000 and $20,000**. The exact amount awarded is assessed on a case‑by‑case basis, taking into account the project’s scope, the expected impact on community resilience, and the applicant’s financial capacity. For a project such as rainwater tanks and a shade structure, a grant in the $10,000–$15,000 range is typical, but this is not guaranteed.

**3. Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure that enhances the community’s resilience to climate change is explicitly listed as an eligible cost. This includes:

- Installation of rainwater tanks, catchment systems, and associated plumbing.  
- Construction or procurement of shade structures, pergolas, or other protective facilities that improve the garden’s operational viability during extreme heat events.  

These items must be directly linked to the proposed resilience outcomes and justifiable in terms of cost. The application should demonstrate how the infrastructure will reduce vulnerability to extreme weather and support continued food production for the local community.
