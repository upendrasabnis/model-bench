Thank you for reaching out and for the opportunity to clarify the eligibility requirements for our Community Resilience Grant. I appreciate the thoughtfulness with which you are approaching the application process, and I am happy to address each of the points you raised.

**1. Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant program is designed to support projects that deliver measurable community benefit, and we do accept applications from unincorporated groups provided that the group operates under a formal auspicing arrangement with an eligible entity. In your case, the fact that your garden is hosted by the local council—and that the council holds the necessary insurances on your behalf—generally satisfies this requirement. The council can act as the fiscal sponsor and primary applicant, which is a common model for community‑based initiatives that are not yet incorporated or charitable. We do require a written auspice agreement (or a letter of support from the council confirming its role) to be included with the application. If you have not yet secured such an agreement, I recommend discussing the formal arrangement with the council’s community‑services department, as this will streamline the review process.

**2. Minimum or maximum grant amount**  
There is no preset minimum or maximum grant size for the Community Resilience Grant; awards are determined on a case‑by‑case basis according to the project’s scope, anticipated impact, budget realism, and the availability of funds in the current round. Historically, grants in this program have ranged from roughly $5,000 to $75,000, but we have funded projects both smaller and larger than this range when the proposal demonstrates exceptional value. Your projected budget for rainwater tanks and a shade structure will be evaluated against these criteria, and we can provide guidance on how to align your request with typical award levels.

**3. Eligibility of infrastructure costs (rainwater tanks and shade structures)**  
Yes—capital infrastructure that enhances the long‑term resilience and operational capacity of a community asset is fully eligible under the grant. Rainwater tanks directly address water security during hot summers, and a durable shade structure supports both participant comfort and the continuity of garden operations under extreme weather. Both items should be documented as part of the project’s capital budget, with clear justification linking them to the grant’s resilience objectives (e.g., reduced water costs, increased year‑round usability, mitigation of heat‑related risks).

**4. Supporting documents to prepare**  
To ensure a smooth assessment, we recommend assembling the following materials:

- **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem, objectives, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
- **Detailed Budget** – line‑item costs for equipment, installation, and any ancillary expenses, with a note on how each line aligns with eligible activities.  
- **Budget Narrative** – explanations for each budget line, especially for capital items such as tanks and structures.  
- **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed accounts (or, for a small operation, a simple profit‑and‑loss statement and cash flow summary) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you referenced.  
- **Letter of Support/Auspice Agreement** – a formal letter from the council confirming its role as fiscal sponsor, insurance coverage, and any required indemnities.  
- **Insurance Certificate** – proof that the project will be covered under the council’s liability policy (or, if you obtain separate coverage, that documentation).  
- **Letters of Support** – endorsements from local stakeholders, neighboring organisations, or community members.  
- **Site Plans or Drawings** – schematic layouts indicating where the tanks and shade structure will be placed.  
- **Evaluation Plan** – an outline of how you will measure success (e.g., water savings, increased usage metrics, participant satisfaction) post‑implementation.
