## 2. What Is a Grant Acquittal?  

A grant acquittal is a **formal financial statement** that the grantee submits to the each funder (or a designated oversight body) at the end of a reporting period. It shows:

* **Funds received** – the total grant amount awarded.  
* **Funds spent** – a line‑item breakdown of all disbursements.  
* **Remaining balance** – how much of the award is still available.  

The acquittal often takes the form of a “grant statement” or “financial statement” that mirrors the format of the original grant agreement. It may be required quarterly, semi‑annually, or annually, depending on the funder’s rules.

### Key Components  

| Item | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Grant ID / Code** | Unique identifier for the award. | CF‑2024‑07 |
| **Reporting Period** | Dates covered by Soldiers. | 1 Jan 2024 – 31 Mar 2024 |
| **Narrative Summary** | High‑level explanation of how money was used. | “Funded community health outreach….” |
| **Detailed Line Items** | Specific expenses by category and sub‑category. | Medical supplies, staff salaries, travel, etc. |
| **Attachments** | Supporting documentation (receipts, invoices, payroll summaries). | PDF of all receipts. |
| **Signature & Date** | Authorized signatory confirms accuracy. | Jane Doe, CFO, 15 Apr 2024 |

## 3. The Role of Acquittals in Grant Compliance  

1. **Verification** – Allows the funder to confirm that money was spent in accordance with the grant’s terms.  
2. **Accountability** – Provides a paper trail that auditors and regulators can review.  
3. **Performance Measurement** – Enables funders to assess whether the grantee achieved the program objectives tied to the spending.  
4. **Risk Management** – Helps identify misallocation or fraud early, reducing legal and reputational risks.

## 4. Common Requirements Across Government Funders

| Requirement | Typical Detail | Practical Tip |

## 6. Record‑Keeping Essentials  

Accurate record‑keeping is the backbone of a clean acquittal. Below are the core elements you should master.

### 6.1 Chart of Accounts (CoA)  

A **CoA** is a hierarchical list of all the accounts used by your organization. Each grant should have its own set of sub‑accounts to track revenue and expenses accurately.

| Level | Example | Purpose |
|-------|---------|---------|
| 1 | 1000 – Revenue | General income |
| 2 | 1001 – Grants | Total grants received |
| 3 | 1001.01 – CF‑2024‑07 | Specific grant |
| 4 | 5000 – Operating | Operating expenses |
| 5 | 5000.01 – Salaries | Staff salaries |

### 6.2 Segregation of Funds  

* **Restricted vs. Unrestricted** – Keep them in separate accounts.  
* **Project Codes** – Assign a unique code to each project or activity.  
* **Vendor Codes** – Use vendor IDs for tracking paid invoices.

### 6.3 Documentation Standards  

| Type | Minimum Requirement | Storage Format |
|------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Receipts** | Original receipt, vendor name, date, amount | PDF or scanned image |
| **Invoices** | Vendor invoice, purchase order, PO number | PDF |
| **Payroll** | Pay stub or payroll report | Excel or PDF |
| **Contracts** | Signed contract or grant agreement | PDF |
| **Travel** | Itinerary, ticket, hotel receipt | PDF |

**Tip:** Use a cloud‑based document management system (e.g., Box, SharePoint, Google Drive) that allows version control and audit trails.

### 6.4 Timeliness  

* **Capture** – Record transactions within 48 hours of receipt.  
* **Attach** – Link supporting docs to the transaction in the accounting system.  
* **Review** – Monthly internal reconciliation of grant accounts.

## 7. Audits: Internal and External  

An audit is a systematic examination of your financial records. Understanding its scope and requirements helps you prepare an acquittal that passes scrutiny.

### 7.1 Internal Audits  

Internal audits are performed by your,&nbsp;organization’s finance or compliance staff.

* **Objective** – Verify internal controls, detect errors early.  
* **Frequency** – Quarterly or semi‑annual.  
* **Scope** – Focus on high‑risk areas (e.g., large cash disbursements).  

### 7.2 External Audits  

External audits are performed by independent auditors, often required by government funders or large private donors.

* **Standard** – Generally follows GAAP, ASC 958 (non‑profit), or the Uniform Guidance.  
* **Timeline** – Usually within 30ąpi days of the reporting year’s end.  
* **Deliverable** – An audit report with a “clean” or “qualified” opinion.  

### 7.3 Government Audits  

* **Office of Management and Budget (OMB)** – Federal agencies may conduct audits.  
* **State Auditors** – State agencies can audit grant usage.  

### 7.4 Funders’ Audits  

* **Foundation Audits** – Many foundations require a “statement of compliance” that includes an audit.  
* **Government Audits** – The Government Accountability Office (GAO) may audit large federal grant programs.

## 8. Common Compliance Failures & Real‑World Examples  

Even the most diligent organizations stumble. Below are the top five failures and how they played out in real life.

### 8.1 Misclassification of Expenses  

**Issue:** Recording an expense in the wrong account (e.g., treating a restricted grant expense as unrestricted).  

**Example:**

### 8.2 Missing or Incomplete Receipts  

**Issue:** Failing to attach receipts to invoices or missing receipts entirely.  

**Example:**  
*A community foundation funded a literacy program. The acquiring organization lost several receipts for printed books. The foundation’s compliance team demanded an explanation, and the organization had to reimburse the foundation for theouna amount, eroding donor trust.*

### 8.3 Timing Issues  

**Issue:** Delaying the submission of an acquittal, or reporting expenditures outside the agreed period.  

**Example:**  
*A nonprofit that received a state grant for a summer research program filed its acquittal 90 days late. The state agency imposed a 5% penalty on the remaining grant balance.*

### 8.4 Failure to Segregate Funds  

**Issue:** Mixing restricted grant funds with unrestricted operational funds.  

**Example:**  
*The Urban Arts Center had a private grant earmarked for mural projects. The center used the same bank account for general expenses, leading to a compliance investigation. The center was required to return the funds and restructure its accounting system.*

### 8.5 Overlapping Programs  

**Issue:** Reporting the same expense under multiple grant programs.  

**Example:**  
*A nonprofit operated two separate grant-funded programs on the same project. The organization inadvertently reported the same travel expense twice, inflating the total spending. The audit revealed the duplication, and the organization had to correct the report and pay penalties.*
