Dear Jamie,

Thank you for reaching out and for the enthusiasm you and your volunteer team have for enhancing the garden’s capacity to withstand the increasingly hot summers in your regional town. I’m pleased to provide the following clarifications in response to the points you raised.

**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our Community Resilience Grant is open to a broad spectrum of applicants, including unincorporated community groups, provided that the group operates under the formal auspices of a local government entity that can demonstrate legal responsibility for the project. In practice, this means that if your council can act as the “auspicing organization”—holding the necessary insurances, providing fiscal oversight, and endorsing the application on your behalf—your group meets the eligibility criteria. We do require a written letter of support from the council confirming this auspice, together with any relevant memoranda of understanding between the council and your volunteers.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The grant program does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, we assess each proposal against the overall budget available for the round, the scope of the activities, and the demonstrated need. Typical awards for infrastructure projects of the nature you describe range from $15,000 to $75,000, but we are happy to consider applications that fall outside this band if the justification and cost‑effectiveness are clearly articulated. Please note that the total funding request should be proportionate to the impact you anticipate and the resources you will contribute.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes, the installation of rainwater tanks and a shade structure are fully eligible under the “Climate‑Resilient Infrastructure” component of the grant. Both items directly support the garden’s ability to operate during periods of extreme heat and align with the program’s broader objective of enhancing community resilience to climate‑related challenges. We encourage you to itemise each component—e.g., tank capacity, material specifications, installation labour, and any ancillary works such as plumbing or foundation work—so that reviewers can assess the technical robustness and cost‑effectiveness of your proposal.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend compiling the following documents:

1. **Letter of Support from the Council** – confirming the auspice arrangement, insurance coverage, and any financial or administrative assistance the council will provide.  
2. **Organizational Profile** – a brief overview of your garden’s mission, volunteer composition, and operating history (even without charitable registration).  
3. **Budget and Narrative** – a detailed breakdown of all costs, including equipment quotations, contractor estimates, and any in‑kind contributions.  
4. **Project Plan** – timelines, milestones, and a description of how the rainwater tanks and shade structure will be implemented and maintained.  
5. **Financial Statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed statements (or, for a small operation, a set of simplified cash‑flow statements) demonstrating the $40,000 annual turnover and the sources of income.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Insurance** – documentation that the council’s insurance covers public liability and that you have a plan for ongoing maintenance and risk management.  
7. **Community Impact Statement** – evidence of local demand (e.g., membership numbers, produce stall attendance, community surveys) and how the project will benefit residents, particularly during heat events.

If you can provide these materials, we will be able to evaluate your proposal more comprehensively and address any additional information we may require. Should you have any further questions about the application process, the technical specifications of the equipment, or the documentation format, please do not hesitate to contact me directly.
