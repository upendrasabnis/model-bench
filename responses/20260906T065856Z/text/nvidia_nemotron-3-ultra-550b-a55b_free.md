## 1. Introduction  

Grant writing is both an art and a science.  
Successful proposals blend storytelling with data, align with funder priorities, and demonstrate organizational capacity.  
This guide walks you through every stage—from funder research to post‑award reporting—using concrete examples, templates, and checklists you can copy into your workflow.

## 2. Understanding the Grant Landscape  

| Grant Type | Typical Size | Typical Funder | Key Characteristics |
|------------|--------------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Foundation Grants** | $5K‑$5M | Private, family, corporate foundations | Often program‑specific, high touch, require relationship building |
| **Government Grants** | $10K‑$100M+ | Federal, state, local agencies | Highly regulated, strict compliance, long review cycles |
| **Corporate Giving** | $1K‑$500K | Businesses, CSR departments | May include in‑kind, employee‑matching, branding opportunities |
| **Crowdfunding / Community Grants** | $500‑$50K | Platforms, community foundations | Public‑facing, storytelling‑driven, shorter timelines |

**Takeaway:** Identify which grant types match your mission, capacity, and timeline before investing hours in a proposal.

### 3.2 Research Techniques  

1. **Foundation Directory Online (FDO) / Candid** – Search by keyword, geography, grant size.  
2. **IRS Form 990‑PF** – Review past grants, board members, giving trends.  
3. **Funder Websites & Annual Reports** – Look for “Funding Priorities,” “Application Guidelines,” “FAQ.”  
4. **Network & Peer Intelligence** – Attend funder briefings, join Grant Professionals Association (GPA) chapters.  
5. **Social Media & News Alerts** – Set Google Alerts for funder name + “grant.”  

### 3.3 Prioritization Matrix  

| Score (1‑5) | Criteria |
|------------|----------|
| **Mission Alignment** | 5 = Directly matches your core program |
| **Funding Fit** | 5 = Grant size & duration match your need |
| **Likelihood of Success** | 5 = Strong past relationship or clear fit |
| **Capacity to Manage** | 5 = Reporting & compliance within your bandwidth |
| **Strategic Value** | 5 = Opens doors to other funders or policy influence |

*Action:* Funders scoring ≥ 18/25 become “Tier 1” targets for the current cycle.

## 5. Crafting a Compelling Needs Statement  

### 5.1 Anatomy of a Needs Statement  

1. **Problem Definition** – What is the issue?  
2. **Scope & Scale** – How many people/communities affected?  
3. **Evidence** – Data, research, local statistics.  
4. **Consequences** – What happens if nothing changes?  
5. **Gap** – Why current services are insufficient.  
6. **Urgency** – Time‑sensitive factors (policy windows, demographic shifts).  

### 5.2 Example: Youth STEM After‑School Program  

> **Problem Definition:** In the Riverbend School District, only 12 % of 6th‑8th graders meet state proficiency standards in mathematics and science.  
> **Scope & Scale:** 3,450 middle‑school students across 7 schools; 68 % qualify for free/reduced lunch.  
> **Evidence:** State Department of Education 2023 Report Card; National Assessment of Educational Progress (NAEP) 2022 shows a 15‑point achievement gap for low‑income students.  
> **Consequences:** Without intervention, students are 3× more likely to drop out of STEM pathways, limiting future earnings by an estimated $1.2 M per cohort.  
> **Gap:** Existing after‑school tutoring serves 150 students (4 % of need) and lacks hands‑on STEM labs.  
> **Urgency:** The district’s new “STEM‑Ready 2025” initiative allocates matching funds only for programs launching by Fall 2026.  

### 5.3 Writing Tips  

- **Lead with a powerful statistic** (e.g., “1 in 4 children…”)  
- **Use local data** – funders love community‑specific evidence.  
- **Quote stakeholders** – a teacher, parent, or youth voice adds authenticity.  
- **Avoid jargon** – write for a smart layperson.  
- **Keep it ≤ 500 words** for most foundations; government RFPs may allow more.

## 6. Designing a Logic Model / Theory of Change  

### 6.1 Core Components  

| Component | Description | Example (STEM After‑School) |
|-----------|-------------|------------------------------|
| **Inputs** | Resources invested | $150K grant, 2 STEM coordinators, 10 volunteer mentors, lab kits |
| **Activities** | What you do | Weekly 2‑hr hands‑on labs, monthly field trips, parent STEM nights |
| **Outputs** | Direct products/services | 180 lab sessions, 12 field trips, 4 parent nights, 300 student‑hours |
| **Short‑Term Outcomes** | Immediate changes | ↑ Student confidence in STEM (pre/post survey +15 %); ↑ attendance (90 %+) |
| **Intermediate Outcomes** | Behavioral/skill changes | ↑ Math/science grades (average +0.5 GPA); ↑ enrollment in AP STEM courses |
| **Long‑Term Impact** | Systemic change | ↑ College STEM majors from district (target 20 % by 2030); ↓ workforce gap |

### 6.2 Visual Template (ASCII)  

```
Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Short‑Term Outcomes → Intermediate Outcomes → Long‑Term Impact
```

### 6.3 Checklist for a Strong Logic Model  

- [ ] All inputs are quantified (dollars, FTE, materials).  
- [ ] Activities are specific, time‑bound, and directly linked to outputs.  
- [ ] Outputs are countable (sessions, participants, materials distributed).  
- [ ] Outcomes are **SMART** (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time‑bound).  
- [ ] Arrows show clear causal pathways; no “magic” leaps.  
- [ ] Includes **assumptions** and **external factors** (e.g., school calendar).  
- [ ] Reviewed by program staff, evaluator, and finance.

## 7. Writing Goals, Objectives, and Outcomes  

| Level | Language | Example |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **Goal** | Broad, aspirational | “All Riverbend middle‑school students have equitable access to high‑quality STEM learning.” |
| **Objective** | Specific, measurable, time‑bound | “By June 2027, increase the percentage of 7th‑grade students scoring proficient on the state science assessment from 12 % to 30 %.” |
| **Outcome** | Change in condition/behavior | “Students demonstrate a 15 % increase in self‑efficacy for STEM tasks (pre/post survey).” |
| **Indicator** | Data point to track | “Number of students completing ≥ 80 % of lab sessions.” |
| **Target** | Numeric benchmark | “≥ 250 students complete ≥ 80 % of sessions in Year 1.” |

### 7.1 SMART Objective Worksheet  

| Objective | Specific | Measurable | Achievable | Relevant | Time‑Bound |
|-----------|----------|------------|------------|----------|------------|
| Increase STEM proficiency | 7th‑grade science proficiency | % proficient on state test | 30 % (baseline 12 %) | Aligns with district STEM‑Ready 2025 | June 2027 |

*Use this worksheet for every objective in the proposal.*

## 8. Developing a Realistic Budget  

### 8.1 Budget Categories (Standard Federal & Foundation)  

| Category | Typical Line Items | % of Total (Rule of Thumb) |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| **Personnel** | Salaries, fringe, consultants | 40‑55 % |
| **Fringe Benefits** | Health, retirement, payroll taxes | 15‑25 % of personnel |
| **Travel** | Mileage, airfare, per diem | 2‑5 % |
| **Equipment** | Lab kits, laptops (>$5K) | 1‑3 % |
| **Supplies** | Consumables, curriculum materials | 3‑7 % |
| **Contractual** | Sub‑grants, evaluators, trainers | 5‑15 % |
| **Other Direct Costs** | Space rental, printing, insurance | 2‑5 % |
| **Indirect Costs** | Overhead (if allowed) | 10‑15 % (or per funder rate) |

### 8.2 Sample Budget Narrative (Excerpt)  

> **Personnel – STEM Program Coordinator (1.0 FTE, $55,000/yr)**  
> *Responsibilities:* Curriculum development, mentor recruitment, data collection, quarterly reporting.  
> *Fringe (30 %):* $16,500 – health, retirement, payroll taxes.  
> **Supplies – Hands‑On Lab Kits (30 kits × $120 = $3,600)**  
> *Justification:* Each kit serves 10 students per session; aligns with output of 180 sessions.  
> **Contractual – External Evaluator (0.2 FTE, $12,000)**  
> *Scope:* Design pre/post surveys, conduct focus groups, produce annual evaluation report.  

### 8.3 Budget Checklist  

- [ ] All line items tie to a logic‑model activity or output.  
- [ ] Personnel costs reflect actual salary bands + fringe.  
- [ ] Indirect cost rate matches funder policy (or use de‑minimis 10 %).  
- [ ] No “unallowable” costs (e.g., lobbying, entertainment).  
- [ ] Budget totals match the narrative and the funder’s maximum award.  
- [ ] Include a **budget justification** for every line > $5,000.  
- [ ] Attach a **budget spreadsheet** (Excel) with formulas visible.

## 9. Creating a Strong Narrative & Supporting Documents  

### 9.1 Narrative Structure (Typical 10‑15 Page Proposal)  

| Section | Page Range | Key Content |
|---------|------------|-------------|
| **Cover Letter** | 1 | Funder‑specific hook, request amount, contact |
| **Executive Summary** | 1‑2 | Problem, solution, impact, budget snapshot |
| **Statement of Need** | 2‑3 | Data‑driven needs statement (see §5) |
| **Program Description** | 3‑6 | Logic model, activities, timeline, staffing |
| **Goals & Objectives** | 1 | SMART table |
| **Evaluation Plan** | 1‑2 | Indicators, data sources, analysis, reporting |
| **Sustainability** | 1 | Diversified funding, capacity building |
| **Organizational Capacity** | 1 | Governance, staff, past performance |
| **Budget & Budget Narrative** | 2‑3 | Detailed spreadsheet + narrative |
| **Attachments** | — | IRS letter, audited financials, letters of support, resumes, logic model graphic |

### 9.2 Writing Style Tips  

- **Active voice** – “We will deliver…” not “The program will be delivered…”  
- **Concrete verbs** – *design, implement, measure, scale*  
- **Avoid “we hope to”** – use “we will achieve” with targets.  
- **Use headings & bullet points** for readability.  
- **Quote funder language** – mirror key phrases from RFP.  

### 9.3 Supporting Documents Checklist  

- [ ] 501(c)(3) determination letter (or equivalent)  
- [ ] Most recent audited financial statements (or 990)  
- [ ] Board roster with affiliations  
- [ ] Key staff bios (≤ 150 words each)  
- [ ] Letters of support (3‑5, on letterhead, signed)  
- [ ] Memoranda of Understanding (MOUs) for partners  
- [ ] Logic model graphic (PDF, high‑res)  
- [ ] Evaluation instruments (survey, rubric)  
- [ ] Organizational chart  
- [ ] Current SAM/UEI registration screenshot

## 10. Review, Editing, and Internal Approval  

### 10.1 Multi‑Stage Review Process  

| Stage | Reviewer | Focus | Turnaround |
|-------|----------|-------|------------|
| **Self‑Edit** | Grant Writer | Clarity, compliance, completeness | 1 day |
| **Program Review** | Program Director | Logic model fidelity, feasibility | 2 days |
| **Finance Review** | CFO/Finance Manager | Budget accuracy, allowable costs | 1 day |
| **Compliance/Legal** | Legal Counsel | Certifications, lobbying, conflict‑of‑interest | 1 day |
| **Executive Sign‑Off** | ED/CEO | Strategic alignment, risk | 1 day |
| **Final Polish** | Grant Writer + Editor | Grammar, formatting, page limits | 0.5 day |

### 10.2 Editing Tools  

- **Grammarly / Hemingway** – readability score ≥ 60.  
- **Track Changes** in Word/Google Docs for audit trail.  
- **Checklist** (see §14) to verify every RFP requirement.

## 11. Submission Strategies  

| Strategy | When to Use | Tips |
|----------|-------------|------|
| **Early Bird** | Rolling deadlines, limited slots | Submit 2‑3 weeks before deadline; confirm receipt. |
| **Letter of Inquiry (LOI) First** | Foundations requiring LOI | Tailor LOI to funder’s “fit” language; attach 1‑page concept. |
| **Electronic Portal** | Federal/state grants (Grants.gov, SAM) | Register early; test PDF/A compliance; keep submission receipt. |
| **Hard‑Copy** | Small family foundations | Use high‑quality paper, binder clips, include a cover letter. |
| **Follow‑Up** | All | Email program officer 1 week after submission: “Thank you, confirm receipt, offer additional info.” |

### 11.1 Submission Checklist (Day‑Of)  

- [ ] All required PDFs are PDF/A‑1b compliant.  
- [ ] File naming follows funder convention (e.g., `OrgName_ProjTitle_Narrative.pdf`).  
- [ ] Total file size < limit (often 25 MB).  
- [ ] Confirmation email saved.  
- [ ] Internal log updated with submission date, tracking number.

## 12. Post‑Award Management & Reporting  

### 12.1 Award Acceptance  

1. **Read the Award Agreement** – note reporting dates, allowable cost changes, prior‑approval thresholds.  
2. **Set Up Grant‑Specific Accounting** – separate fund code in accounting software.  
3. **Kick‑off Meeting** – program staff, finance, evaluator, funder liaison.  

### 12.2 Reporting Calendar (Example)  

| Report | Due | Content | Owner |
|--------|-----|---------|-------|
| **Quarterly Narrative** | 30 days after quarter end | Activities, outputs, challenges, stories | Program Manager |
| **Quarterly Financial** | 30 days after quarter end | Expenditures vs. budget, variance explanation | Finance |
| **Annual Performance Report** | 90 days after grant year | Outcomes vs. targets, evaluation findings, lessons learned | Evaluator + PM |
| **Final Report** | 60 days after grant close | Cumulative impact, sustainability plan, financial close‑out | ED + Finance |

### 12.3 Data Collection & Management  

- **Standardized Instruments** – pre/post surveys, attendance logs, fidelity checklists.  
- **Database** – use REDCap, Airtable, or a custom Salesforce object.  
- **Data Quality** – monthly spot‑checks, double‑entry for key metrics.  

### 12.4 Communicating Success  

- **Impact Briefs** (1‑page) for board, donors, community.  
- **Storytelling** – video testimonials, case studies.  
- **Funder Recognition** – logo on website, press release, event signage (per agreement).  

### 12.5 Compliance & Audit Prep  

- Retain **all receipts, timesheets, contracts** for 3 years post‑closeout (or per funder).  
- Conduct **internal mock audit** 60 days before final financial report.  
- Address any **findings** within 30 days; document corrective actions.

## 14. Practical Checklists (Quick‑Reference)  

### 14.1 Pre‑Proposal Checklist  

- [ ] Funder identified & prioritized (Tier 1).  
- [ ] RFP/guidelines downloaded & saved.  
- [ ] Eligibility confirmed (geography, 501(c)(3), project type).  
- [ ] Deadline entered in master calendar with buffers.  
- [ ] Internal “Go/No‑Go” decision documented.  

### 14.2 Proposal Development Checklist  

- [ ] Needs statement drafted, data‑sourced, ≤ 500 words.  
- [ ] Logic model completed, reviewed by evaluator.  
- [ ] SMART objectives table filled for each goal.  
- [ ] Budget spreadsheet built, totals match narrative.  
- [ ] Budget narrative written for every line > $5K.  
- [ ] Evaluation plan includes indicators, data sources, analysis plan.  
- [ ] Sustainability paragraph addresses diversified funding.  
- [ ] Organizational capacity section updated (staff bios, board).  
- [ ] All attachments gathered, formatted, named per convention.  
- [ ] Full proposal assembled in master document (Word/Google).  

### 14.3 Internal Review Checklist  

- [ ] Self‑edit pass (clarity, jargon, page limits).  
- [ ] Program director sign‑off on logic model & activities.  
- [ ] Finance sign‑off on budget & indirect cost rate.  
- [ ] Legal/compliance sign‑off on certifications & lobbying.  
- [ ] Executive director sign‑off on strategic fit.  
- [ ] Final proofread (grammar, formatting, page numbers).  

### 14.4 Submission Checklist  

- [ ] All files PDF/A‑1b, named correctly.  
- [ ] Total size < funder limit.  
- [ ] Submitted via required portal or mailed with tracking.  
- [ ] Confirmation receipt saved (email + screenshot).  
- [ ] Grant log updated (funder, amount, deadline, status).  

### 14.5 Post‑Award Checklist (First 30 Days)  

- [ ] Award agreement signed & returned.  
- [ ] Grant fund code created in accounting system.  
- [ ] Kick‑off meeting scheduled & minutes stored.  
- [ ] Reporting calendar entered in project management tool.  
- [ ] Data collection instruments finalized & piloted.  
- [ ] Funder contact added to CRM with reporting deadlines.  

### 14.6 Ongoing Grant Management Checklist (Monthly)  

- [ ] Update expenditure tracking vs. budget.  
- [ ] Collect program output data (attendance, sessions).  
- [ ] Note any deviations & corrective actions.  
- [ ] Prepare quarterly narrative draft (if due).  
- [ ] Communicate progress to board & key stakeholders.  

### 14.7 Close‑Out Checklist  

- [ ] Final financial report reconciled to general ledger.  
- [ ] Final performance report completed with outcome data.  
- [ ] All deliverables (reports, products) submitted to funder.  
- [ ] Equipment/inventory disposition documented.  
- [ ] Thank‑you letter & impact brief sent to funder.  
- [ ] Lessons‑learned captured in Grant Playbook.

## Closing Thoughts  

Grant writing is a **cycle**, not a one‑off event.  
Investing in systematic funder research, a living logic model, rigorous budgeting, and disciplined post‑award management turns each proposal into a stepping stone for the next.  
Use the checklists above as living documents—print them, pin them to your wall, embed them in your project‑management software.  

**Remember:** The most persuasive proposals are those that *show* you already know how to measure, learn, and sustain impact.
