**Eligibility of unincorporated, council‑auspiced groups**  
Our Community Resilience Grant is open to a broad range of applicants, including unincorporated community groups that operate under the formal auspices of a local government. As long as the council can demonstrate that it holds the necessary insurance, tax‑exempt status, and any other regulatory responsibilities on behalf of the group, the applicant is considered an eligible entity. In practice, we require a letter of support (or auspice agreement) from the council confirming that it will be responsible for the grant’s financial and administrative oversight, and that it will provide any insurance or liability coverage required by the funding agreement. Please ensure that this documentation is prepared by your council’s community services or grants team.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The grant program does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, the amount awarded is determined by the scope of the proposed project, its alignment with the program’s objectives, the quality of supporting evidence, and the overall available budget for the round. Projects similar to yours—focused on climate‑resilient infrastructure—have historically received funding in the $15,000‑$45,000 range, but applications that present a compelling case for larger capital works are also considered. We encourage you to outline the total project cost and the portion you are seeking, as this helps the assessment panel to place your request in context.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Yes, capital infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and permanent shade structures is an eligible cost under the “Climate Adaptation and Resilience” component of the grant. The key is that the items must be directly attributable to the project’s resilience objectives and must be necessary for the ongoing operation of the garden. We typically advise applicants to provide detailed specifications, cost quotations, and evidence that the items are not already owned or funded elsewhere.

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documents:  

1. **Project Narrative** – a clear description of the problem (increasing summer heat), the proposed solution, and expected outcomes.  
2. **Budget and Financial Plans** – itemised cost breakdown, quotations for tanks and shade structures, and a simple cash‑flow projection.  
3. **Organizational Profile** – a brief constitution or charter, the council auspice agreement, and proof of insurance coverage (if not held by the council, a certificate of coverage is required).  
4. **Operational History** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for unincorporated groups, a set of financial summaries) showing the $40,000 turnover you mentioned.  
5. **Community Impact** – letters of support from local residents, schools, or partner organisations, as well as any preliminary site plans or sketches.  
6. **Sustainability Plan** – a maintenance schedule and any volunteer commitment that will ensure the long‑term operation of the new infrastructure.  

We also suggest reviewing the full grant guidelines on our website, where you will find the exact application form, evaluation criteria, and any specific reporting requirements. If any of the above documents are not yet available, a brief explanation of the reason and a timeline for completion will be appreciated; the panel takes such explanations into account when assessing applications.
