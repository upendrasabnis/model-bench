**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines explicitly state that unincorporated community groups are eligible applicants **provided they are formally auspiced or sponsored by a legal entity that can assume financial and legal responsibility for the grant**. An arrangement whereby your local council holds the necessary insurances and acts as the nominating body satisfies this requirement. In practice, we have funded projects led by volunteer‑run gardens and similar organizations that operate under council auspices, so your structure should meet the eligibility criteria.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, we assess each proposal against the program’s objectives and the overall budget available for the round. Typical awards for infrastructure projects in the $30 k–$80 k range have been successful, but we are prepared to consider applications that request higher amounts if they demonstrate strong community impact and sustainable funding plans. Please note that the total project cost must be clearly articulated, and we expect the grant request to represent a reasonable proportion of that total.

**Eligibility of rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes—capital infrastructure that enhances environmental sustainability and operational resilience is fully eligible. Rainwater tanks are considered a **green‑infrastructure** component that reduces water consumption and supports year‑round garden operations during hot periods. Similarly, a durable shade structure that protects both crops and participants from extreme heat qualifies as an **essential operational asset**. Both items must be clearly linked to the project’s resilience objectives and include specifications, cost breakdowns, and justification of how they will achieve the desired outcomes.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
When you move to the full application stage, we recommend assembling the following materials:

1. **Project Narrative** – a concise description of the problem you are addressing (e.g., heat stress, water scarcity), the proposed solution, and the expected community benefits.  
2. **Budget and Financial Plan** – itemised costs for the tanks and shade structure, plus a cash‑match or in‑kind contribution statement. Because your turnover is roughly $40 k, please also include a simple profit‑and‑loss summary to demonstrate financial viability.  
3. **Organizational Profile** – a short charter or memorandum of understanding with the council confirming the auspice relationship, insurance coverage, and any governance responsibilities.  
4. **Implementation Timeline** – a Gantt‑style chart or bullet‑point schedule outlining key milestones, from procurement to installation.  
5. **Community Engagement Evidence** – minutes of public meetings, a membership list, or signage photos that illustrate local support.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Mitigation Plan** – a brief outline of potential challenges (e.g., supply chain delays, maintenance) and how you will manage them.  
7. **Staffing and Volunteer Resources** – a brief description of the team’s capabilities and any training needed for operating and maintaining the new infrastructure.

We also advise that you review the full grant guidelines on our website, paying particular attention to the **reporting requirements** (progress updates, financial statements, and a post‑implementation impact report).
