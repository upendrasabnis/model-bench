**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant guidelines expressly welcome applications from unincorporated community groups that are formally auspiced—or “hosted”—by a local government entity. The council’s role as the legal holder of insurances, tax‑exempt status, and any necessary permits satisfies the requirement for a responsible administering body. As long as the council can confirm that it will be responsible for the grant funds, that the group will operate under its policies, and that the council will provide any required reporting or accountability mechanisms, your group meets the eligibility criteria.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant is designed to fund projects of varying scale. Individual awards typically range from $5,000 to $50,000, although exceptional circumstances may warrant a larger allocation after a detailed review. There is no strict minimum; even projects that fall toward the lower end of this spectrum can be funded if they demonstrate strong community impact and feasibility. Your anticipated project costs should be presented within this context, with a clear justification for the requested amount.

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, capital infrastructure that directly enhances the resilience and sustainability of community‑based operations is an eligible cost. Rainwater tanks, shade structures, permeable paving, and related site‑work are considered permissible expenditures provided they:  

1. Serve a clear climate‑adaptation purpose (e.g., reducing water consumption during heat events).  
2. Are necessary for the continued operation of the garden under projected climate scenarios.  
3. Align with the grant’s overarching goals of building community resilience and promoting environmental stewardship.  

Documentation of how these assets will achieve the stated outcomes will be essential in the evaluation stage.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following materials:  

- **Project narrative** – a concise description of the problem, proposed solution, implementation timeline, and expected outcomes.  
- **Budget and cost breakdown** – detailed line items for each infrastructure component, including installation, permits, and contingency.  
- **Proof of auspice** – a formal letter from the council confirming its support, its role in holding insurances, and any relevant council policies that govern the partnership.  
- **Organizational profile** – a brief overview of your group’s composition, volunteer base, and operating history (e.g., minutes from council meetings, articles of association if available).  
- **Financial statements** – the most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple income‑and‑expense summary) demonstrating the $40,000 turnover you referenced.  
- **Community support** – letters of endorsement from local businesses, resident groups, or other stakeholders, as well as any attendance records from public meetings where the project was discussed.  
- **Risk assessment and insurance** – a copy of the council’s insurance certificate, plus any additional risk‑management plans you may have developed for construction work.
