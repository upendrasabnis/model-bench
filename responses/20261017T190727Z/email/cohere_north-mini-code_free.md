**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
The grant programme is open to both incorporated not‑for‑profit entities and unincorporated community groups, provided that the group’s activities are administered through an auspicing organisation that meets the programme’s fiduciary and insurance requirements. In your case, being auspiced by your local council – which holds the necessary public liability and professional indemnity insurances – satisfies the insurance criterion. The council’s involvement also serves as a reliable guarantor of financial accountability, which is viewed favourably by the assessment panel. Consequently, your group should be eligible to apply, even though you are not yet a registered charity.  

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a strict minimum or maximum award. Instead, funding levels are determined on the basis of the overall project budget, the demonstrated community impact, and the proportion of the request relative to the total cost. Historical award ranges for similar infrastructure projects have fallen between $15,000 and $75,000, but the programme is deliberately flexible to accommodate a spectrum of initiatives. We encourage you to outline the full cost of your rainwater‑tank and shade‑structure installation; the panel will assess the proportion of funding requested against the overall budget and the strength of the supporting documentation.  

**Eligibility of infrastructure such as rainwater tanks and shade structures**  
Yes, capital‑equipment items that directly enhance the sustainability and operational resilience of community spaces are considered eligible costs. Rainwater tanks are explicitly listed under the “Water‑efficiency and Climate‑Adaptation” category, while permanent shade structures fall under “Infrastructure and Facilities Improvement.” Both items must be essential to the project’s objectives, reasonably priced, and demonstrably contribute to the project’s resilience goals. Please ensure that any quotations or cost estimates clearly break out these components so that the assessors can see how each aligns with the eligible categories.  

**Supporting documents you should prepare**  
To strengthen your application, we recommend assembling the following documentation:  

1. **Project Narrative** – A concise description of the problem you are addressing (e.g., heat stress impacts), the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and a timeline for implementation.  
2. **Budget and Cost Breakdown** – Detailed quotations for the tanks and shade structure, plus line‑item estimates for installation, permits, and any ancillary costs (e.g., landscaping, signage).  
3. **Proof of Auspice** – A letter from the council confirming that it will act as the fiscal sponsor, hold insurance, and be responsible for any sub‑grant agreements.  
4. **Financial Statements** – The most recent audited or reviewed financial statements (or, for a small operation, a simple cash‑flow summary) demonstrating your $40,000 turnover and the sources of income.  
5. **Letters of Support** – Endorsements from local businesses, stakeholder groups, or community members that attest to the project’s relevance and anticipated benefit.  
6. **Risk Assessment and Insurance Certificate** – Confirmation that the council’s insurance covers the proposed activities, together with any additional risk‑mitigation plans you may have.  
7. **Site Plans or Maps** – A diagram showing the garden layout, the proposed tank locations, and the shade‑structure footprint.  

We also advise reviewing the full grant guidelines on our website (or the attached program handbook) to ensure that any other criteria—such as matching funds, community consultation requirements, or reporting obligations—are met. Should you have any further questions or need clarification on any of the above points, please do not hesitate to contact me directly or reach out to the programme officer listed in the guidelines.
