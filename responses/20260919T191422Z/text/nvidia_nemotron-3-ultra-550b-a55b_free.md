## 1. Introduction: The Lifecycle of Trust

Grant funding is not a gift; it is a contract. Whether the source is a federal agency distributing taxpayer dollars, a state government implementing policy priorities, or a private foundation executing a donor’s intent, every grant agreement establishes a fiduciary relationship. The **grant lifecycle**—pre-award, award, post-award, and closeout—is often visualized as a circle, but the **acquittal phase** (closeout) is where the circle either closes cleanly or fractures.

**Acquittal reporting** is the formal mechanism by which a grantee demonstrates that they have fulfilled the terms of that contract. It is the evidentiary bridge between *proposal* ("What we said we would do") and *reality* ("What we actually did, and what it cost").

Failure at this stage carries consequences far beyond administrative annoyance:
*   **Financial Clawbacks:** Repayment of misspent funds, often with interest.
*   **Debarment/Suspension:** Exclusion from future funding rounds (common in US Federal [SAM.gov](https://sam.gov) and EU funding portals).
*   **Reputational Damage:** Loss of credibility with boards, donors, and the public.
*   **Legal Liability:** False Claims Act violations (US), fraud statutes (UK/AU/CA), or breach of contract litigation.

This article serves as a definitive operational manual for grant managers, finance directors, executive directors, and program staff. It moves beyond "check-the-box" mentality to establish a **compliance architecture**—a system of people, processes, and technology—that turns acquittal from a year-end panic into a continuous, auditable state of readiness.

## 2. Defining the Acquittal: More Than a Receipt

### 2.1 Etymology and Legal Basis
The term "acquit" derives from the Old French *acquiter* (to settle a claim) and Latin *ad quietare* (to put at rest). In grant management, **to acquit a grant is to legally discharge the obligation**. It signifies: "The money is spent, the activities are done, the outcomes are measured, and the file can be closed."

Legally, the acquittal transforms the **Grant Agreement** (a conditional promise) into a **Final Settlement** (a discharged debt).

### 2.2 The Three Pillars of Acquittal
A complete acquittal rests on three distinct but interlocking pillars. Missing one renders the acquittal incomplete.

| Pillar | Core Question | Primary Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Acquittal** | *Did we spend the money exactly as budgeted?* | General Ledger (GL) reconciliation, bank statements, payroll records, invoices, receipts, depreciation schedules. |
| **Performance/Activity Acquittal** | *Did we do what we said we would do?* | Milestone reports, deliverables (reports, software, buildings), participant sign-in sheets, photos, GPS data, publication DOIs. |
| **Impact/Outcome Acquittal** | *Did it make the difference we promised?* | Evaluation reports, survey data (pre/post), longitudinal tracking, case studies, third-party verification. |

### 2.3 Types of Acquittal Events
1.  **Progress/Interim Acquittal:** Required quarterly, semi-annually, or at milestones. Triggers next payment tranche. *Focus: Cash flow forecasting and course correction.*
2.  **Final Acquittal:** Submitted at project end (usually 30–90 days post-end-date). *Focus: Final reconciliation, asset disposal, intellectual property transfer, sustainability.*
3.  **Special Condition Acquittal:** Triggered by specific clauses (e.g., "Acquit capital expenditure >$50k upon asset commissioning").
4.  **Deed/Title Acquittal (Real Property):** Unique to infrastructure/land grants. Requires title searches, valuation certificates, and caveat registration.

### 2.4 Real-World Distinction: "Acquittal" vs. "Reporting"
*   **Reporting:** "We held 10 workshops." (Narrative)
*   **Acquittal:** "We held 10 workshops costing $12,500 (Budget: $15,000). Attendance logs (Appendix A) show 150 unique participants (Target: 120). Venue invoices (Appendix B) reconcile to GL code 6420. Variance of $2,500 reallocated to materials per Variation Approval #VA-2023-04." (Evidence + Reconciliation + Authorization)

## 3. The Regulatory Landscape: Government vs. Philanthropic Expectations

While the *principles* of acquittal are universal (transparency, allowability, allocability), the *regulatory frameworks* differ significantly. A grantee managing a $5M NIH grant, a $200k State Health Dept grant, and a $50k Family Foundation grant simultaneously must navigate three distinct "compliance universes."

### 3.1 Federal Government (United States Focus: 2 CFR Part 200)
The "Uniform Guidance" (2 CFR 200) is the gold standard for rigor.
*   **Cost Principles (Subpart E):** **Allowability** (necessary, reasonable, allocable, consistent with policies, adequately documented). **Unallowable Costs:** Alcohol, lobbying, entertainment, fines/penalties, first-class airfare (generally).
*   **Procurement Standards (Subpart D):** Full and open competition required >$250k (Simplified Acquisition Threshold). Sole source justification documentation is mandatory.
*   **Indirect Costs (F&A):** Negotiated Rate Agreement (NICRA) vs. De Minimis 10% MTDC. *Acquittal requires applying the correct rate to the correct base.*
*   **Audit Threshold:** Single Audit (formerly A-133) required if >$750k/year in federal expenditures.
*   **Real-World Nuance:** **The "Closeout" Module in SAM.gov/PIMS.** Final FFR (Federal Financial Report) SF-425, Final Invention Statement, Tangible Personal Property Report (SF-428). Failure to submit in PIMS freezes future drawdowns.

### 3.2 State & Local Government (US/Canada/AU/UK)
*   **Flow-Down Clauses:** State grants passing through federal money inherit 2 CFR 200 requirements.
*   **State-Specific Cost Principles:** e.g., California’s *State Administrative Manual (SAM)*, Texas *Grant Management Standards (TxGMS)*, Australia’s *Commonwealth Grants Rules and Guidelines (CGRGs)*.
*   **Political Cycles:** Funds often lapse at fiscal year-end (June 30 / Sept 30 / March 31). "Use it or lose it" creates pressure for accelerated spending, increasing compliance risk.
*   **Example (Australia - CGRG):** Requires a **Grant Agreement** with specific *Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)*. Acquittal requires a **Certification by Accountable Authority** (CEO sign-off) and often an **Independent Audit Report** for grants >$500k.

### 3.3 Philanthropic & Private Foundations
*   **Governance:** IRS Form 990-PF (US) / Charity Commission Annual Return (UK) / ACNC Annual Information Statement (AU). Foundations *must* prove expenditure responsibility (US) or public benefit (UK/AU).
*   **Expenditure Responsibility (US - IRC 4945):** If granting to a non-501(c)(3) (e.g., a for-profit social enterprise or foreign NGO), the foundation *must* obtain a detailed report showing funds used for charitable purpose. This shifts acquittal burden *up* to the funder, who pushes it *down* to the grantee via contract.
*   **Flexibility vs. Rigidity:**
    *   *Restricted Grants:* Similar rigor to government (line-item budgets).
    *   *Unrestricted/General Operating Support:* Acquittal focuses on **Organizational Health** (Audit financials, 990, Board minutes, strategic plan progress) rather than project line items.
    *   *Trust-Based Philanthropy:* Movement toward "verbal acquittals" or brief narrative reports. **Risk:** Grantees often under-document, creating vulnerability if leadership changes or IRS audits the foundation.

### 3.4 International / Multilateral (EU, UN, World Bank)
*   **EU Grants (Horizon Europe, Erasmus+):** **Annotated Model Grant Agreement (AMGA)**. Strict eligibility rules (e.g., "actual costs" vs "unit costs" vs "lump sums"). **Certificate on the Financial Statements (CFS)** required for grants >€430k (threshold varies).
*   **UN/World Bank:** **IPSAS (International Public Sector Accounting Standards)** compliance. Heavy focus on **Procurement Integrity** (vendor sanctions lists, prior review thresholds).
*   **Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) / UK Bribery Act:** Acquittal must demonstrate anti-corruption controls in high-risk jurisdictions.

### 3.5 Comparative Summary Table

| Feature | US Federal (2 CFR 200) | US Foundation (Exp. Resp.) | EU Horizon Europe | Australian Gov (CGRG) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cost Basis** | Actuals (mostly) | Actuals | Actuals / Unit / Lump Sum | Actuals |
| **Indirect Costs** | NICRA / 10% De Minimis | Often capped (10-15%) or excluded | 25% Flat Rate (Indirect) | Capped % or Direct Only |
| **Procurement** | Full Competition >$250k | "Reasonable" effort | Strict EU Thresholds / Procedures | Value for Money / Probity |
| **Audit Trigger** | $750k Fed Expenditure | Funder Discretion / IRS 990 | >€430k (CFS) | >$500k (Certification) |
| **Final Report** | SF-425 + Narrative + IP | Narrative + Financials | Continuous Reporting + Periodic | Final Report + Certification |
| **Asset Title** | Federal Interest (Vests in Gov) | Grantee usually keeps | Beneficiary usually keeps | Funder may retain interest |

## 4. Anatomy of a Compliant Acquittal Report

A compliant acquittal is a **bound evidentiary package**, not an email with a spreadsheet attached. It must stand alone as a legal record 5–7 years later when the program officer and finance director have both left.

### 4.1 The Cover Package (The "Face Sheet")
1.  **Grant Identification:** Grant Number, FAIN (Federal Award Identification Number), CFDA/ALN Number, EIN/UEI, Project Title, Period of Performance.
2.  **Certification/Signature Block:**
    *   *Authorized Organizational Representative (AOR):* "I certify to the best of my knowledge..."
    *   *Chief Financial Officer / Finance Director:* Separate certification of financial accuracy.
    *   *Principal Investigator / Program Director:* Certification of technical performance.
3.  **Contact Information:** For the auditor/funder to reach the *current* responsible staff.

### 4.2 The Financial Reconciliation (The "Heart")
This is where 80% of errors occur.

#### A. Source-of-Funds / Uses-of-Funds Statement
| Category | Approved Budget (A) | Prior Period Expenditures (B) | Current Period Expenditures (C) | Total Expenditures (D = B+C) | Variance (A-D) | % Spent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Personnel | $100,000 | $60,000 | $35,000 | $95,000 | $5,000 | 95% |
| Fringe Benefits | $35,000 | $18,000 | $14,000 | $32,000 | $3,000 | 91% |
| Travel | $15,000 | $5,000 | $2,000 | $7,000 | $8,000 | 47% |
| Equipment | $50,000 | $48,500 | $0 | $48,500 | $1,500 | 97% |
| Supplies | $10,000 | $8,000 | $3,000 | $11,000 | **($1,000)** | **110%** |
| Contractual | $40,000 | $20,000 | $15,000 | $35,000 | $5,000 | 88% |
| Indirect Costs | $25,000 | $15,000 | $8,000 | $23,000 | $2,000 | 92% |
| **TOTAL** | **$275,000** | **$174,500** | **$77,000** | **$251,500** | **$23,500** | **91%** |

**Critical Compliance Checks on this Table:**
*   **Variance Explanation:** Every line item >10% variance (or $1k, whichever is lower per funder rules) requires a narrative note. *Example: "Travel underspend due to COVID-19 conference cancellations; funds reallocated to Supplies via Prior Approval PA-2023-01."*
*   **Overspend Flag:** Supplies at 110%. **Immediate Red Flag.** Requires written prior approval *before* the expense was incurred. If missing -> Cost Disallowance.
*   **Indirect Cost Base Check:** Verify MTDC (Modified Total Direct Costs) calculation excludes Equipment, Capital Expenditures, Patient Care, Tuition Remission, Subaward >$25k each.

#### B. The General Ledger (GL) Reconciliation
*The single most important document.*
*   **Trial Balance Export:** Filtered by Grant Cost Center / Project Code / Fund Code.
*   **Mapping Schedule:** Crosswalk from Chart of Accounts (Natural Accounts: 6100 Salaries, 6200 Fringe, 7100 Travel) to Budget Categories (Personnel, Fringe, Travel).
*   **Reconciling Items:** List *every* difference between GL Total and Report Total.
    *   *Timing Differences:* Accrued payroll not yet paid (valid).
    *   *Coding Errors:* $500 office supplies coded to Project A instead of Project B (must be corrected via JE).
    *   *Unallowable Costs:* $200 alcohol charge on hotel folio (must be removed manually from report).

#### C. Cash Management / Drawdown Reconciliation
*   **Total Cash Drawn:** $240,000
*   **Total Expenditures (Accrual Basis):** $251,500
*   **Cash on Hand (Advance):** -$11,500 (Grantee owes funder? No, funder owes grantee $11,500).
*   **Final Payment Request:** Included in acquittal or separate?
*   **Interest Earned:** If advance > $120k/year (US Federal), interest > $500/year must be remitted to Treasury.

### 4.3 The Performance Narrative (The "Story")
Structure: **Logic Model Alignment.**
1.  **Inputs:** Resources invested (Staff FTE, Partners engaged).
2.  **Activities (Outputs):** *Quantitative.* "Conducted 12 training sessions (Target: 10). Trained 240 nurses (Target: 200)."
3.  **Outputs (Deliverables):** *Tangible products.* "Published 3 peer-reviewed articles (DOIs listed). Developed Open-Source Software v1.0 (GitHub Link). Constructed 5km rural road (GPS Coords)."
4.  **Outcomes (Short/Long Term):** *Changes in knowledge, behavior, condition.* "Clinic vaccination rates increased from 45% to 68% (Source: EHR data)."
5.  **Deviations & Corrective Actions:** "Activity 3 (Community Forum) delayed 3 months due to permitting. Mitigation: Virtual town halls held. Approved via Amendment #2."
6.  **Sustainability / Legacy:** "Software adopted by State Health Dept. Staff positions absorbed into operating budget."

### 4.4 Required Attachments (The "Appendices")
*Do not embed these in the PDF body; label as Appendix A, B, C...*
*   **Appendix A:** Final General Ledger Detail (Transaction-level export).
*   **Appendix B:** Payroll Register / Effort Certification Reports (see Section 5.3).
*   **Appendix C:** Subrecipient Monitoring Pack (Their acquittals, your monitoring letters).
*   **Appendix D:** Procurement Files (RFP, Evaluation Scorecards, Contract, Invoices for >$25k).
*   **Appendix E:** Equipment/Asset Register (Serial #, Location, Condition, Fair Market Value at closeout).
*   **Appendix F:** Audit Report (if threshold met) or Financial Statements.
*   **Appendix G:** Prior Approval Correspondence (Emails/Letters authorizing budget changes, no-cost extensions, scope changes).

## 5. Record-Keeping Infrastructure: Building the Evidence Vault

"Acquittal happens on Day 1." The quality of your final report is determined by the systems you have in place the day the grant is awarded.

### 5.1 The Document Retention Policy (The Legal Floor)
*   **US Federal (2 CFR 200.334):** **3 years** from submission of *final* expenditure report (not project end date).
*   **Exceptions:** Real property/equipment -> 3 years after final disposition. Litigation/Audit findings -> Until resolved.
*   **State/Local:** Often 5–7 years (e.g., California = 5 years; New York = 6 years).
*   **Foundations:** Often 7 years (matching IRS statute of limitations for 990-PF).
*   **Best Practice:** **7 Years Minimum** for all grants. Electronic storage is mandatory; paper is optional but must be readable.

### 5.2 The Chart of Accounts (CoA) Design: The Compliance Backbone
If your CoA cannot isolate a grant's activity *without* manual spreadsheet manipulation, you have a structural compliance defect.

**Best Practice Structure (Segmented CoA):**
`Fund (Grant) - Department (Program) - Account (Natural) - Project (Activity) - Location (Site)`
*   *Example:* `FED-NSF-2023-001` - `RES-ONCOLOGY` - `6110-SAL-FACULTY` - `ACT-DATA-COLLECTION` - `SITE-MAIN-CAMPUS`

**Why this matters:** You can run a GL report for `FED-NSF-2023-001` and instantly see *every* transaction. No VLOOKUPs required.

### 5.3 Effort Certification (Time & Effort) – The Highest Risk Area
For salary costs (often 60-80% of budget), the **Timesheet is the Source Document**.
*   **After-the-Fact Certification (US Federal Standard):** Employee signs *after* the period (monthly/quarterly) confirming "I worked X% on Grant A, Y% on Grant B."
*   **Positive Time Reporting (PRC 001 / Some States):** Employee records *actual hours daily* on a timesheet coded to grants. Required for hourly/non-exempt staff.
*   **Institutional Base Salary (IBS) Cap:** NIH/NSF cap on salary chargeable to grants (currently ~$212,100 for NIH). Any salary above cap *must* be paid from non-federal funds (Cost Sharing). **Acquittal must prove the cap was respected.**

**Real-World Failure Mode:** Professor works 50% on Grant A, 50% on Grant B. Charges 100% to Grant A because "Grant B hasn't started yet."
*   *Audit Finding:* **Improper Cost Allocation.** Repayment required.
*   *Fix:* Interim effort reports (quarterly) reviewed by PI *and* Dept Admin. System-enforced caps (e.g., Workday, PeopleSoft Grants Module).

### 5.4 Subrecipient vs. Contractor Determination (The "Flow-Down" Trap)
This dictates your monitoring burden.
*   **Subrecipient:** Performs substantive programmatic work; has compliance requirements (audit, DUNS/UEI, SAM.gov). **You must:** Issue subaward agreement with flow-down clauses, collect *their* Single Audit, perform risk assessment, monitor technically/financially (site visits, desk reviews), report in FFATA (FSRS.gov) if >$30k.
*   **Contractor (Vendor):** Provides goods/services (lab tests, cloud hosting, catering). **You must:** Follow procurement policy, get invoice, pay. No flow-down of grant terms (except lobby/conflict of interest).
*   **Acquittal Impact:** You must submit Subrecipient Monitoring Report with your Final Acquittal. Missing a subrecipient audit = **Your Finding**.

### 5.5 Asset Management: The "Property" Acquittal
Equipment (>$5k/unit or lower threshold per policy) and Supplies (<$5k).
*   **During Grant:** Tag asset, record in Asset Register (Serial, Model, Location, Custodian, % Federal Share).
*   **At Closeout (Final Acquittal):**
    *   *Federal Interest:* Funder retains title/interest proportional to funding %.
    *   *Disposition Request:* If Fair Market Value (FMV) > $5,000 per unit, you **must** request disposition instructions from funder *before* selling/trashing.
    *   *Proceeds:* Sale proceeds = Program Income (usually) or return to funder.
    *   *Real-World Example:* University buys $50k Microscope (100% Fed funded). Project ends. Microscope FMV = $15k. University wants to move to new lab. **Action:** Submit SF-428B (Final Property Report). Request "Retain Title" for continued research use. Funder approves. University keeps asset but Federal lien remains.

### 5.6 Program Income: The Hidden Revenue Stream
Income *generated by* the grant activity (fees for services, publication sales, conference registration, rent from grant-built facility).
*   **Default (US Federal):** **Additive Method** (Add to budget, spend on allowable costs).
*   **Alternative:** **Deductive** (Deduct from allowable costs) or **Cost Sharing/Matching** (Count as match).
*   **Acquittal Requirement:** Separate tracking in GL. Report total earned, total spent, balance remaining. **Cannot be "lost" in general operating revenue.**

## 6. The Audit Gauntlet: Preparing for External Scrutiny

### 6.1 Types of Audits
1.  **Single Audit (US) / Statutory Audit (UK/AU):** Entity-wide. Covers *all* federal/major funding. Opinion on Financial Statements + Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA) + Compliance/Major Program Opinion.
2.  **Program-Specific Audit:** Only for entities with one major program (rare).
3.  **Funder-Specific Audit / Desk Review:** Funder audits *just their grant*. Common in foundations, state gov, EU (CFS).
4.  **OIG / Inspector General Audit:** Fraud/Waste/Abuse focus. High stakes.
5.  **Internal Audit:** Your own team. The "friendly" rehearsal.

### 6.2 The SEFA (Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards) – The Audit Roadmap
The auditor's first request. Must reconcile to:
1.  General Ledger (Total Federal Expenditures).
2.  Final FFRs (SF-425) submitted for each grant.
3.  Subrecipient expenditures (included in pass-through entity's SEFA).

**Common SEFA Errors:**
*   Reporting *Cash Draws* instead of *Accrual Expenditures*.
*   Omitting "Pass-Through" funds received from a State Agency (thinking "it's state money" – wrong, it's federal flow-through).
*   Incorrect ALN (Assistance Listing Number) / CFDA numbers.

### 6.3 The "Major Program" Determination (Risk-Based Approach)
Auditors don't audit every grant. They use a risk-based approach (2 CFR 200.518):
1.  **Type A Programs:** >$750k (or >$30M for large entities). *Automatically Major.*
2.  **Type B Programs:** <$750k. *Risk assessed.*
3.  **Risk Criteria:** Prior findings, complexity, new personnel, time since last audit, OIG reports.
*   **Strategy:** If you have a "High Risk" Type B program, treat it like a Major Program internally. Prepare workpapers *before* auditor asks.

### 6.4 Preparing the "Audit Binder" (PBC - Prepared By Client)
Do not make the auditor dig. Deliver a structured digital workspace (SharePoint, Box, AuditBoard).

**Standard PBC Index:**
| Tab | Content | Source System |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **01_Grant_Agreements** | Signed Award, Amendments, Special Conditions | Grant Mgmt System |
| **02_Budget_vs_Actual** | Final Budget, All Revisions, Final GL Reconciliation | ERP / Spreadsheet |
| **03_Payroll_Effort** | Effort Reports (Certified), Payroll Register, IBS Cap Calcs | HR/Payroll System |
| **04_Procurement** | Policy, RFPs >$250k, Sole Source Justifications, Contracts, Invoices | Procurement / AP |
| **05_Subrecipients** | Agreements, Risk Assessments, Monitoring Reports, Sub-Audits | Subrecipient Files |
| **06_Equipment_Assets** | Register, Disposition Requests, Title Records | Asset Module |
| **07_Program_Income** | Ledger, Invoices issued, Expenses against Income | GL Sub-ledger |
| **08_Indirect_Costs** | NICRA Letter, Rate Calculation Worksheet, Base Reconciliation | Finance |
| **09_Cash_Management** | Drawdown Schedule, Bank Stmts, Interest Calculation | Bank / Payment Mgmt |
| **10_Prior_Findings** | Prior Year Audit Findings, Corrective Action Plans, Status | Audit Tracking Log |
| **11_Correspondence** | Key emails: Prior Approvals, Scope Changes, Funder Approvals | Email Archive |

### 6.5 The Audit Finding Anatomy
If you get a finding, it follows a strict structure (Yellow Book / GAGAS):
1.  **Criteria:** The rule (e.g., "2 CFR 200.430(i) - Effort Reports required").
2.  **Condition:** What you did wrong (e.g., "3 of 10 effort reports missing signatures").
3.  **Cause:** Why (e.g., "PI on sabbatical; no delegate assigned").
4.  **Effect:** $$$ Questioned Costs (e.g., "$45,000 in unverified salary").
5.  **Recommendation:** Fix it.
6.  **Views of Responsible Officials:** Your written response (Agree/Disagree + Corrective Action Plan).

**Pro Tip:** Dispute *during* fieldwork with evidence. Once the draft report is issued, it's harder to remove a finding.

## 7. The Top 10 Compliance Failures and How to Avoid Them

Based on OIG reports, Single Audit databases (FAC), and funder feedback loops.

### Failure 1: The "Budget Drift" (Unauthorized Rebudgeting)
*   **Scenario:** PI moves $20k from Travel to Equipment without asking. "It's still the same total grant."
*   **Rule:** 2 CFR 200.308 / Most Funders: Prior written approval required for budget transfers >10% or $1k (threshold varies) *between* categories, or *any* transfer *into* Equipment/Capital.
*   **Fix:** **Automated Workflow.** ERP system blocks journal entries that move money across budget categories without an attached "Budget Revision Approval" document. Monthly "Budget vs Actual" review meeting with PI + Grants Manager.

### Failure 2: Effort Reporting "Rubber Stamping"
*   **Scenario:** Dept Admin signs PI's effort report 6 months late. PI never reviewed it.
*   **Rule:** Certification must be by *responsible person* (PI for faculty, Supervisor for staff) *after* the fact, *within* a reasonable time (usually 90-120 days).
*   **Fix:** **System-Enforced Certification.** Electronic workflow (e.g., Huron, Click, Workday) sends email -> PI logs in -> Sees % breakdown -> Clicks "Certify". System locks after deadline; auto-escalates to Dean/Provost. **No paper signatures.**

### Failure 3: Procurement "Sole Source" Abuse
*   **Scenario:** "Only Vendor X makes this widget." Reality: Vendor X is PI's friend; 3 competitors exist.
*   **Rule:** Full & Open Competition. Sole Source requires written justification: Unique capability, Emergency, Public Exigency, or Inadequate Competition (after solicitation).
*   **Fix:** **Procurement Gateway.** Any PO >$10k (or $25k) routes to Central Procurement *before* PO creation. Central Procurement owns the Sole Source form. PI provides technical specs only.

### Failure 4: Cost Sharing / Match "Phantom" Contributions
*   **Scenario:** Grant requires 1:1 match. University pledges "PI Salary 20% effort" as match. PI charges 100% to Grant. Match never materializes.
*   **Rule:** Cost Share = **Actual, Verifiable, Allowable, Not Paid by Another Federal Grant.** Must be in GL.
*   **Fix:** **Cost Share Tracking Module.** Separate GL Project Code for "Match - Grant X". Monthly reconciliation: *Pledged vs. Actual*. If lagging >20% at midpoint, trigger alert to Dean. **Valuation Rules:** Volunteer time = Independent Sector rate (or actual wage if professional service). Donated space = Fair Market Rental appraisal.

### Failure 5: Indirect Cost (F&A) Rate Misapplication
*   **Scenario:** Applying 55% On-Campus rate to Subaward >$25k (should be excluded from MTDC base). Applying rate to Equipment.
*   **Fix:** **Automated Rate Engine.** ERP calculates F&A nightly based on *current* NICRA terms and *exclusion logic* (MTDC rules). Monthly "F&A Reconciliation" by Grants Accountant.

### Failure 6: Subrecipient Monitoring "Check-the-Box"
*   **Scenario:** Send subaward. Never ask for reports. At closeout, realize sub spent $200k on unallowable costs. Prime is liable.
*   **Rule:** 2 CFR 200.332 - Monitor based on Risk Assessment. High risk = Site visit. All = Financial report review + Audit review.
*   **Fix:** **Subrecipient Management Plan (SMP)** written at Award Setup.
    *   Risk Scorecard (New? High $? Complex compliance?).
    *   Calendar: Quarterly Financials due 30 days post-quarter. Annual Audit due 9 months post-FYE.
    *   **Tracking Log:** Red/Yellow/Green status per sub. Escalation path for late reports (withhold payment).

### Failure 7: Program Income "Leakage"
*   **Scenario:** Grant-funded clinic charges patient fees. Fees go to Hospital General Revenue. Not tracked. Not reported.
*   **Fix:** **Dedicated Revenue Code.** All income generated by grant activity (even $50) posts to `Revenue-ProgramIncome-GrantX`. Quarterly sweep to verify balance.

### Failure 8: Lobbying / Unallowable Costs Charged to Grant
