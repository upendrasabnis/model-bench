**Eligibility of unincorporated groups auspiced by a council**  
Our grant programme is open to a broad spectrum of applicants, including unincorporated community groups, provided that the group operates under a formal “auspices” arrangement with a municipal or regional council. In such cases, the council must act as the primary contracting party and be able to demonstrate that it holds the necessary insurance, risk‑management and fiscal authority for the proposed activities. Since your council already holds the relevant insurances on your behalf, you should be eligible to apply. However, we do require a written auspice agreement from the council that confirms:  

1. The council’s willingness to be the legal recipient of grant funds;  
2. The council’s responsibility for financial reporting and audit requirements; and  
3. The council’s endorsement of the project scope and deliverables.  

If a formal agreement does not yet exist, I recommend initiating a brief memorandum of understanding with the council’s community‑services department. This document should be dated, signed by authorised representatives of both parties, and attached to your application as evidence of the auspice relationship.

**Minimum and maximum grant amounts**  
The Community Resilience Grant does not prescribe a fixed minimum or maximum award. Instead, funding is allocated based on the overall merit of the proposal, the demonstrated need, and the proportion of total project costs that the grant can reasonably support. Historical data from recent rounds indicate awards ranging from $5,000 to $50,000, but applications for projects of any scale are considered on their individual merits. For a project focused on rainwater tanks and a shade structure, a grant in the $15,000‑$30,000 range would be typical, assuming the total budget aligns with the scope of work you describe.

**Eligibility of infrastructure costs**  
Infrastructure items such as rainwater harvesting systems and permanent shade structures are expressly listed as eligible expenditures under the “Climate‑Resilient Infrastructure” category of this grant. Eligible costs include:  

- Purchase and installation of rainwater tanks (including ancillary fittings such as pumps, filters, and first‑flush diverters);  
- Design, fabrication, and erection of shade structures (e.g., pergolas, shade sails, or polytunnel frames) that serve a functional purpose for the garden’s operations;  
- Site preparation work directly related to the installation (e.g., excavation, concreting, and minor earthworks).  

Please note that costs for routine consumables, ongoing maintenance, or staff salaries are not covered. The budget breakdown you submit should clearly separate capital‑equipment costs from any operational expenses.

**Supporting documents to prepare**  

1. **Project Description and Business Plan** – A concise narrative (maximum 2 pages) outlining the problem you are addressing, the proposed solution, expected outcomes, and how the project aligns with the grant’s resilience objectives.  
2. **Detailed Budget and Narrative** – A table itemising each infrastructure component, unit costs, quantities, and total amounts, accompanied by a brief justification for each line item.  
3. **Auspice Agreement** – The signed council memorandum described above.  
4. **Organizational Profile** – A short profile of your group (mission, volunteer base, current activities) and, if applicable, any recent minutes or letters from the council confirming your status as an auspicee.  
5. **Financial Statements** – For the most recent fiscal year (or a simplified cash‑flow statement if you lack formal audited statements). Given your turnover of approximately $40,000, a basic profit‑and‑loss summary and balance sheet are sufficient.  
6. **Risk Management and Insurance Documentation** – A copy of the council’s insurance certificate confirming coverage for public liability and property damage relevant to the project.  
7. **Letters of Support** – At least two letters from community stakeholders (e.g., local businesses, resident groups, or other NGOs) affirming the community need and anticipated benefits.  
8. **Implementation Timeline** – A Gantt‑style chart or simple milestone schedule illustrating start‑up, installation phases, and hand‑over dates.
